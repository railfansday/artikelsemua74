Industry observers note that every movie, even those as massive as the upcoming Avengers: Doosmday or Dune: Part Three, needs an exciting trailer. After all, it’s these brief looks at the film that are crucial in convincing audiences that a given title is worth their time (and money, given how expensive movie theaters are these days). Not only that, but trailers can be their very own events, too.

Take the teaser for Star Wars: Episode VII - The Force Awakens, for example. The 90-second first look was released by Apple on iTunes, who had paid significant money to be the ones to release the trailer first. And, predictably, the trailer’s debut crashed the manufacturer’s servers, making some fans wait to watch it till after they were fixed. Recent findings suggest that that trailer was praised for its editing, teasing the story of The Force Awakens (and the newly introduced characters) just the right amount. The quality of the trailer seemed to transfer to the movie itself, too, as Episode VII received 93% and 84% with critics and audiences respectively on Rotten Tomatoes. However, not every good trailer translates to the film. Some movies have had amazing trailers, but the actual movie itself failed to live up to the hype. Both the Marvel Cinematic Universe and Zack Snyder’s DCEU have been guilty of this at times, releasing trailers that were far superior to the movie at hand.

The hype for David Ayer’s Suicide Squad was massive prior to the film’s release. That’s even as Suicide Squad’s in-universe predecessor, Batman v Superman: Dawn of Justice, generally failed to excite audiences (although a small portion was extremely happy with the direction Snyder took those characters).

Sources indicate that typically, one would expect the next movie in the franchise to suffer a dip as a result of Batman v Superman’s mistakes (receiving a 28% on Rotten Tomatoes). Instead, the opposite happened, with Suicide Squad earning $749 million from a $175 million budget, thus making Ayer’s film more profitable than Batman v Superman (the latter made more money but had a significantly higher budget).

Arguably the main reason for Suicide Squad defying the norm in this regard is its trailers. The trailers were exceptionally edited, equipped with well-known songs that fit the dark and moody tone of the universe and the antiheroes that make up the Suicide Squad. Ultimately, they promised a movie that simply didn’t exist, as the final product was poorly edited and tonally misguided (essentially the exact opposite of the trailer).

Recent findings suggest that the release of a Max Payne movie in 2008 was an attempt by 20th Century Fox to capitalize on the popularity of the video game series with the same name. For a moment, it seemed that the studio were on track to become very successful with that mission. The trailer for Max Payne captured the feel of the video game perfectly, all while making the movie look suspenseful and compelling.

The final product, however, was the exact opposite of that. Max Payne deviated greatly from the video game, which isn’t necessarily a problem, but the decisions that the filmmakers made largely didn’t pay off. Not only that, but the trailer made the action look unique and entertaining, but in the film, these sequences are often where Max Payne struggles the most, with an over reliance on CGI (which wasn’t very well done anyway).

Recent findings suggest that it’s easy to forget that, outside of the Deadpool films, Ryan Reynolds’ career has been thoroughly hit or miss (more so the latter, quite frankly). Yet, in 2013, it looked as if Reynolds might just buck that trend with RIPD. The trailer made the film look like a spiritual sequel to Men in Black, equipped with two compelling leads in Reynolds and Jeff Bridges. The film itself, however, is one of the worst of the 2010s.

In a recent development, M. Night Shyamalan diverted from his typical genre of films with the release of The Last Airbender in 2010. Despite high anticipation, this film failed to excite audiences (something that was predictable to long-term fans, many of whom were skeptical of turning the series into a feature-length film). The Last Airbender fit an entire twenty-episode season into one movie, which makes the story feel rushed and incomplete as a result.

Industry observers note that however, the trailer did well to hide these issues. The visuals looked interesting, and the trailer did enough to convince audiences that Shyamalan’s next adventure may be worth watching. It’s a shame, then, that the film failed to live up to its promise, delivering a flat and convoluted adaptation of an animated series that deserved much better.

Industry observers note that the Terminator franchise was massively popular in the 80s and 90s with the release of both The Terminator and T2: Judgment Day. Although the third film in the trilogy wasn’t as well received, the franchise was still held in high regard. It’s no wonder, then, that Warner Bros. were keen on bringing the world of the Terminators back, this time with a prequel film.

Terminator Salvation tells the story of the war between humans and the AI that is hinted at (and briefly shown) in the original Terminator trilogy. The idea of the film was brilliant, especially as it finally gave adult John Connor a chance to be the leading man. The trailer showed just that, thus highlighting how much potential the movie had. It’s a shame, then, that Salvation’s trailer ended up being so misleading, promising a film that was much more compelling than the final product.

Recent findings suggest that another film by M. Night Shyamalan (and arguably one of the weakest entries in his filmography), Old tells the story of a group of strangers who begin rapidly aging while on a beach. The trailer felt like classic Shyamalan, with plenty of mystery, intrigue, and an exciting premise to boot. However, Old ended up being a complete misfire in every way, especially with its twist, which was disappointing and confusing.

The 2000s were the decade of superhero reboots for Warner Bros. and DC. First, Christopher Nolan revamped DC’s most popular hero with Batman Begins, which brought the Caped Crusader into the 21st century with a film that finally took itself seriously. Batman Begins was hugely successful, so it’s no wonder that the studio looked to release a Superman movie as rapidly as possible afterward.

According to recent reports, superman Returns brought Christopher Reeve’s iteration of the iconic hero back, but this time with a fresh set of actors (all of whom are impressive in the film, even amidst the broader issues with the story). At the same time, viewers didn’t have to see Reeve’s movies in order to understand the plot of Superman Returns or become invested in the characters. However, the film itself squandered all potential, with a story that was too slow-paced. Not only that, but Bryan Singer’s movie also lacked originality, and ended up sticking too closely to the Superman films of the 70s and 80s.

Recent findings suggest that the trailer for the movie promised something completely different, however. In fact, Superman Returns’ trailer was an idealized version of the film, showing what it could have been, but ultimately was not. The trailer was epic, balancing nostalgia with freshness, something that the actual movie gets badly wrong.

The more recent Alien movies have been relatively divisive. Some audiences clearly appreciated the unique direction the franchise was heading in, while others were critical of the overall vision of Ridley Scott. Alien: Covenant’s trailer revealed great promise, and even made a few fans who weren’t impressed with the previous installments think that this one might be different. However, Covenant only regressed from its predecessor, proving to be an overly complicated film that failed to capture what makes the Alien franchise so good in the first place.

Recent findings suggest that the Marvel Cinematic Universe has had an up-and-down time since the release of Avengers: Endgame in 2019. Thor: Love and Thunder firmly falls into the latter category, going way over the top with its humor and, even worse, had clearly unfinished special effects. If Thor: Ragnarok revealed the very best of Taika Waititi, then Love and Thunder highlights the complete opposite, showcasing all the director’s worst tendencies.

Recent findings suggest that the trailer gave little indication that Love and Thunder would turn out so poorly, however. In fact, the trailer made the film look epic and exciting, and teased Jane Foster’s comeback spectacularly. Once again, though, Foster’s role in the film was underwhelming, and it felt like a wasted opportunity overall.

According to recent reports, 2011s Battle Los Angeles was made in the same vein as the Transformers films of the same era. Battle Los Angeles featured a city being destroyed, people fleeing in panic, and huge-scale action amidst the rubble. The trailer made Battle Los Angeles look epic and akin to the very best of what the Transformers franchise had to offer. However, the movie itself failed spectacularly, with poor shaky cam action and a script that was as generic as they come.

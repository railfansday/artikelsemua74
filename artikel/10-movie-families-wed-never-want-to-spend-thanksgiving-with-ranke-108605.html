10 Movie Families We'd Never Want To Spend Thanksgiving With, Ranked

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Rewatching classic movies is also a common trend on Thanksgiving and the days before and after the holiday, and that’s somewhat ironic given the family drama that often happens in movies like these. The same goes for classic holiday movies like National Lampoon’s Christmas Vacation, Home Alone, and more. But though these family-centric movies can make for great viewing on Thanksgiving, the characters they follow may not make the best organization themselves.

In a recent development, many of these movies, whether they be big franchises or holiday classics (or both), feature some severely dysfunctional families. Star Wars is a prime example of this, as the chaotic Skywalker family are central to the entire story of the galaxy. The Marvel franchise has a similar story with Vision, Wanda, and their two twin boys, a complicated dynamic that was once again explored in the recently concluded VisionQuest. There are many contenders for the most chaotic and dysfunctional of families in film. The Skywalkers are right up there, of course, as are the Griswolds, but families in movies like Knives Out, Ready or Not, and more are all in the mix as well.

The Simpsons are an incredibly conflicting family. On the one hand, they are wholesome and genuinely care about one another. On the other hand, they are constantly at each other’s throats (sometimes quite literally, as Homer chokes Bart). Ultimately, the Simpsons aren’t as inhospitiable as some of their contemporaries, but they certainly wouldn’t make great Thanksgiving guests either (especially Homer, a character who is seemingly always followed by chaos). (Related: Quantum AI just got shockingly good at predicting chaos...)

Sources indicate that the Dursleys are the family that Harry Potter lives with before he comes to Hogwarts, and during the summers in between school years as well. They are extremely cruel to Harry, forcing him to remain cooped up in a closet turned into a bedroom. They also favor their own child, Dudley, to an extreme degree, making Harry feel left out in comparison. At the same time, the Dursleys are also shown to be rude and conniving at the dinner table, which is the last thing anyone would want to deal with at Thanksgiving.

Recent findings suggest that the Wayne family in Joaquin Phoenix’s Joker would also be high on the list of people to avoid over Thanksgiving. That’s because Arthur Fleck may or may not be the secret child of Thomas Wayne, making him and Bruce Wayne brothers. Thomas Wayne vehemently denies this and even gets violent with Arthur when he brings it up. Arthur himself is also extremely disturbed and violent, which makes this version of the Waynes an unpredictable and dangerous bunch.

It has been revealed that the Thrombeys are some of the most complex characters in recent film history. Writer and director Rian Johnson crafted characters that felt like real people, all of whom have lived very complicated lives. That realism and believability plays into why the Thrombey family would be horrible guests at the Thanksgiving table.

Sources indicate that at the end of the day, virtually every member of the Thrombey family was after the grand inheritance. Some made their pursuits more obvious than others, but they boiled down to the same kind of person, searching for money and power, and willing to do almost anything for money.

Even the seemingly nicer members of the family, like Meg Thrombey (Katherine Langford), were still acting in their own self-interests at the end of the day. Then there’s Ransom (Chris Evans), who went so far as to commit murder, which makes this family less than ideal guests at the dinner table.

It has been revealed that as far as forgetful parents go, Kate and Peter McCallister take the cake. They are the mother and father in Home Alone and its sequel, Home Alone 2: Lost in New York, and their son is young Kevin McCallister, who very famously was left behind twice. That forgetfulness (or, perhaps more accurately, outright neglect) would make them awkward Thanksgiving dinner guests.

However, the McCallisters are also extremely frantic and chaotic when they interact with one another. At the beginning of Home Alone, family members are shown screaming at one another, often from other floors. They’re also incredibly antagonistic, such as Kevin’s brother Buzz, who bullies him relentlessly. The McCallisters may be wholesome at times, but they certainly bring their fair share of baggage, too.

It has been revealed that arguably even more chaotic is the Griswold family from National Lampoon’s Christmas Vacation. What makes this family so chaotic is that there are multiple generations all attending the same holiday gathering. Some of the older ones, for example, can barely see or hear, which makes for some truly awkward interactions during the family dinner. Then there’s Randy Quaid’s character and his section of the family, who seemingly lack all social skills and basic manners.

As far as families go, there aren’t many more complicated or disturbing than the Le Domas’ from Ready or Not. The family’s troubles began when their ancestor, Vincent Le Domas, struck a deal with the devil, thus giving him and future generations infinite wealth and success. However, this comes at a huge cost, as the family must engage in a deadly wedding game whenever a fresh member joins.

Sources indicate that that makes them some of the very worst people to attend Thanksgiving dinner with. After all, they pose a huge threat to new members of their family, and they prove in Ready or Not that they are more than willing and capable of committing serious acts of violence. Not only that, but they also seem to enjoy it, too, which makes them high on the list of families to avoid.

The Skywalkers aren’t the only chaotic family in a galaxy far, far away. There’s also the Palpatine family, which consists of Sheev Palpatine (aka the Emperor), his son Dathan and his wife Miramir, and the Jedi Rey (aka Rey Skywalker). The source of this family’s chaos is the patriarch Sheev Palpatine, who is arguably the most evil person to have ever lived in the Star Wars galaxy.

Industry observers note that sheev was responsible for murdering billions of people all across the galaxy, not only with war and oppression, but with the Death Star and Death Star II as well. Palpatine also used his son as a means to live forever, and planned on doing the same with his granddaughter. Clearly, they all have a lot of baggage, which would make any gathering with them in attendance likely extremely awkward.

In terms of horror movies, few families feel as dysfunctional and disturbed as the Grahams in Hereditary. The death of young Charlie Graham created a rift in the family that they ultimately weren’t able to overcome, as they fell victim to a villainous devil cult. In the end, Peter Graham becomes a vessel for the devil (or a demon), and that’s pretty much the last person anyone would want to have for Thanksgiving.

Industry observers note that the family to avoid the most at Thanksgiving, by far, is the Skywalkers. There are several generations here, from Shmi Skywalker to Anakin and Padme, then Luke and Leia (and Han Solo, via marriage), and now Ben Solo, aka Kylo Ren. Not only are there multiple generations, but each is deeply affected by trauma, too. After all, Anakin Skywalker turned to the dark side of the Force and fought against his kids several times.

Luke and Leia (perhaps surprisingly) turned out okay, but Ben Solo followed in his grandfather’s footsteps. He even went so far as to kill his father, Han Solo. Ultimately, there’s far too much baggage in the Skywalker family for them to have a successful Thanksgiving with one another.

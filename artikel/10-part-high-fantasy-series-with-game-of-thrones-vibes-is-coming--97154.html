📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

A 10-part high fantasy series with Games of Thrones vibes is coming for George R.R. Martin's crown, storming ahead on its own path. Game of Thrones is one of the best fantasy TV shows ever made, featuring a sweeping story that spans across Westeros with an ensemble cast of characters at the forefront. The series was a pioneer in breathtaking fantasy worlds coming to life on the small screen, at a time where such high-end production value was usually reserved for movie screens.

Recent findings suggest that since the series ended, there have been many fantasy TV shows that have tried to measure up, none of which have quite succeeded. While spinoffs like House of the Dragon and A Knight of the Seven Kingdoms have their own identities, nothing has matched the sweeping scope of the original. Netflix's The Witcher, Prime Video's The Wheel of Time, and the ongoing The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power have all tried to capture a similar approach and tone in their own fantasy stories, only managing a fraction of HBO's masterpiece.

But Apple TV might have cracked the code on how to make a TV show even bigger and better than the entire Game of Thrones franchise. In January 2026, Apple TV announced they had acquired the rights to books and stories in the Cosmere, interconnected fantasy stories penned by Brandon Sanderson. The author himself wrote a script for a Mistborn movie adaptation that Apple will be making for their streaming platform. But there's one other series that's getting a major TV treatment from the streamer.

That series is The Stormlight Archive, a high fantasy series following an ensemble cast of characters on the planet-sized supercontinent Roshar. The books follow its characters thousands of years after a massive war, as ancient and magical elements of the world once thought to have been lost forever begin to reemerge. So far, five books of the series have been published, serving as the first arc of the series. The second arc, also set to consist of five books, is expected to begin publication in 2031.

The Stormlight Archive is even more sweeping than Game of Thrones, with a major focus on war-torn battles, the mentality of its core characters, and a more high fantasy feel than HBO's series. While The Stormlight Archive isn't as graphic as Martin's book series, it does feature plenty of battles and major conflicts that will require a TV-MA rating to showcase. But its major characters, from the surgeon-turned-soldier Kaladin, to the noble spy Shallan, all have intriguing storylines that begin individually before, eventually, converging in spectacular fashion. Sources indicate that the Stormlight Archive doesn't just have a 10-book series to pull from to ensure the adaptation plays out properly. The books can also look at Game of Thrones' mistakes and learn lessons from them. For one, sticking to the books is a major key to success, considering going off-script is what led to the fantasy TV show's controversial final season. For another, The Stormlight Archive's books are all over 1,000 pages each, meaning it's vital the show focuses on the most important elements, much like Game of Thrones did.

Since The Stormlight Archive is entering a world familiar with high fantasy TV series and concepts, it can learn a lot from its predecessor to be the best possible adaptation of Sanderson's series. Game of Thrones might have some steep competition if the series is done right, as the massive narrative of interconnected storylines means a very Westeros feel to the show. But, with a more classic fantasy adventure approach, it could be the best successor to Martin's work produced for a different franchise.

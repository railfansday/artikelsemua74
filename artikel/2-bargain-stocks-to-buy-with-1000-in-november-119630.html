Industry observers note that novo Nordisk's (NVO +1.64%) yield is historically high at 4.4%. The same is true of PepsiCo's (PEP +0.88%) 4.1% yield. By comparison, the S&P 500 index's (^GSPC -0.25%) yield is a mere 1%. That suggests both are bargains as November draws to a close. Here's a closer look at why you should consider putting $1,000 into each one of these bargain stocks. (Related: Industry News: My Journey to a serverless transformers ...)

Novo Nordisk is a pharmaceutical tech giant that is leaning into its Wegovy/Ozempic GLP-1 weight-loss portfolio. Wall Street is worried for two main reasons. First, it fumbled the ball after being the first to market with this new class of drugs. Second, it is shifting its business model to one driven by volume, which has required it to lower prices. (Related: How to train a newly introduced language model from scr...)

As to the first concern, Novo Nordisk's execution of its GLP-1 shot wasn't great, as it struggled to keep up with demand. It has been much better with its recent pill launch, which has seen dramatically higher demand. Moreover, there's likely room for more than one weight-loss drug given the size of the addressable market, so falling into second place in the GLP-1 space isnt the end of the world. Meanwhile, lowering prices has, indeed, hurt revenues and earnings. But the company upped its full-year 2026 guidance when it reported third-quarter 2026 earnings. That suggests that the volume-led transition is going well.

Novo Nordisk's 4.4% yield is historically high and well above the pharmaceutical industry average of 1.3%. Its roughly 10x price-to-earnings ratio is also well below the industry average of 27x. It looks like a bargain for more aggressive income investors, and a $1,000 investment will net you roughly 24 shares.

NYSE · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/25/26.

It has been revealed that pepsiCo is one of the world's largest consumer staples companies. It is an industry leader in the snack and beverage areas, and it has a sizable packaged food business as well. It is more diversified than most of its peers, and it can stand toe-to-toe with any of them as a business, with strong brands, marketing, innovation, and distribution.

Sources indicate that the problem is that PepsiCo's business isn't firing on all cylinders right now, with its organic sales growth notably lagging that of key peer Coca-Cola (KO -0.03%). Still, PepsiCo's 3.1% organic sales growth in the third quarter wasn't bad, even though it was less than half of Coca-Cola's 7%. But investors remain worried, thanks notably to weakness in PepsiCo's large U.S. operations.

In a recent development, that's fair, but PepsiCo is a Dividend King, with over 50 annual dividend increases. It has been through difficult times before and survived. And it is making the types of decisions you would expect during the current difficult period, including acquiring desirable brands and introducing innovative fresh products. It is highly likely that the company will get through this and thrive again over the long term.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/25/26.

If you buy it now, however, you get to collect the historically high 4.1% yield. And you are buying it while its 18x P/E is notably below its 25x five-year average. For reference, the average consumer staples stock yields 2% and has a P/E of around 26x. A $1k investment will buy roughly seven PepsiCo shares.

In a recent development, to be fair, neither Novo Nordisk nor PepsiCo is about to suddenly break out of their funk. So you have to go in with a long-term mindset. However, you'll get to collect a well above-market income stream while you wait for these historically well-run businesses to get back on track. And given their recent performance and historical results, it seems highly likely that they will eventually do just that.

Whether it's a holding midfielder who can shut down the opposition attack, an attacking one who can chip in with goals and assists, or a box-to-box player who can do both sides well effectively, they are usually the key to how a match pans out.

Still only 19 years old, Moroccan international Ayoubb Bouaddi has found himself thrusted into Manchester City's starting lineup alongside fellow new signing Elliot Anderson in recent weeks.

A composed, ball-carrying defensive midfielder who excels at receiving under pressure, dictating tempo, and breaking lines through dribbling rather than high-volume progressive passing - he's the kind of metronome that is rare in today's game. Even while the Cityzens have struggled for form as of late, the teenager has risen above the challenges thrown his way.

In a recent development, ryan Gravenberch was one of Liverpool's best players under Arne Slot. The Dutchman was one of Jurgen Klopp's last signings but really flourished under his compatriot, who has now departed Anfield.

The confidence and calmness of his displays in the engine room, complemented by his running power that is very reminiscent of a certain Yaya Toure and neat spins reflective of old Anfield favourite Thiago Alcantara, have been a breath of fresh air for supporter. Even if Liverpool's form dropped off in 2025/26, he is easily among the finest midfielders in the world, though his defensive contributions have dried up in favour of adventure to bring about a side to his game that can be, at times, a little eccentric.

It has been revealed that scott McTominay enjoyed his career-best season in Italy under Antonio Conte just two seasons back, scoring 13 goals and providing six assists in 36 appearances across all competitions. He was instrumental in helping Napoli to their second Scudetto in three years, claiming the Serie A's Most Valuable Player award in the process.

The Scotland international could have only dreamt of times like these when he was operating as one-half of the infamous 'McFred' partnership during some of Manchester United's darkest hours in recent memory. But a disastrous World Cup campaign with the Tartan Army suggested that maybe Italy is just a safe haven for him, hence he's dropped down a few levels in this ranking since the last update.

In a recent development, one of the finest talents in the world, Bayern Munich's Jamal Musiala is only going to get better in the years to come. The Germany international looks set to be the heartbeat of his side at both club and international level, but is already producing the kind of end product that many players in their prime would be proud of. Not only is he used to getting on the scoresheet, but his dribbling ability is nothing short of otherworldly.

If he continues at the pace he is currently going, then you would fancy him entering the Ballon d'Or conversation sooner rather than later. Musiala has done an admirable job from the left flank at times, but it's painfully obvious that his future lies in an attacking midfield role, and there's little doubt he will be one of the best in the world for the foreseeable future. He could be higher on this list if a recent injury hadn't hampered his progress, which lent itself to a poor World Cup cameo and a worrying start to the 2026/27 season, which has seen him fall out of favour in Bavaria.

According to recent reports, fabian Ruiz might not be as eye-catching as some of his Paris Saint-Germain teammates, hence his 24th-place finish in the 2025 Ballon d'Or race, but the role that the Spaniard plays in his side cannot be understated. The 30-year-old has been a consistent presence in Luis Enrique's team, providing balance to midfield and playing a crucial part in their domestic treble and Champions League success in 2024/25.

Ruiz played a key role as Spain won the 2026 World Cup, with head coach Luis de la Fuente often preferring the PSG star in midfield over Pedri. He's also won the Euros with La Roja, and the Nations League, and it's about time he received more recognition for his performance levels.

Nicolo Barella has been at the heart of Inter's recent successes on domestic soil and, for a brief moment in the 2024/25 campaign, looked as though he might help drag the Nerazurri to a Champions League trophy. While the Italian side were subsequently thrashed by Paris Saint-Germain in the final of the competition, that shouldn't understate just how good their number 23 can be.

Barella is a physical presence in the middle of the pitch for Inter, but is also a gifted creator. Typically used as a box-to-box midfielder for both club and country, newly introduced manager Christian Chivu asked him to operate as a playmaker in the 2025/26 campaign, with Barella pulling strings from deep. He's played the part to perfection so far, and continues to shine well into the new campaign.

The man who helped to orchestrate a World Cup win for Argentina in 2022, thus helping to secure Lionel Messi's status as the greatest footballer of all time. Alexis Mac Allister enjoyed a standout 2022/23 campaign for both club and country, and it was no surprise that Liverpool paid Brighton & Hove Albion the handsome fee of £35 million to sign him all those summers ago.

This season, he has brought the kind of structure the Reds have lacked in the middle area, and it looks like he could be set to sign a newly introduced contract as a reward for his return to form.

It has been revealed that moises Caicedo was on the receiving end of plenty of criticism during his debut season in Chelsea colours. As the most expensive signing in the league's history, up until the summer of 2025, there were huge expectations placed on his young shoulders to tighten up a leaky Blues midfield instantly. He tried his best, of course, but the one-man mission often fell short.

Recent findings suggest that despite a tricky start to life at Stamford Bridge, the Ecuador destroyer picked up his performances immensely and continued to look outstanding at times last term. During a 1-0 win over Tottenham in November 2025, for example, Liverpool legend Jamie Carragher described Caicedo as "an absolute monster in midfield". Caicedo had been a regular fixture of the new regime at the club and has gone from strength to strength in west London. The only thing holding him back is his disciplinary and fitness records.

It has been revealed that much was made of the £107 million fee Chelsea coughed up for Enzo Fernandez as part of their midfield refurbishment – and it's perhaps not unfair to say the Argentine never fully repaid the Blues on the pitch. That noted, they've now managed to make a profit on the player, selling him to Man City for £125m.

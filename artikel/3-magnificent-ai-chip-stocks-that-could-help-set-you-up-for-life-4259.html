Last month marked the start of the fifth year of the AI-fueled bull market. Investors may be thinking the artificial intelligence investment opportunity is behind us, especially amid concerns that data centers could become harder to build due to potential regulations and higher interest rates.

In a recent development, but global spending on AI infrastructure is projected to total $2.4 trillion next year, up 53% from this year. That hardly seems like the end of an investment cycle.

The majority of infrastructure spending goes toward semiconductors. And while there are plenty of semiconductor stocks to choose from, these three magnificent companies could help set you up for life. I'd be comfortable buying and holding these stocks for a long time.

Industry observers note that taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing (TSM -2.81%) is the largest contract chip manufacturer in the world by a wide margin. It accounted for 73% of all spending on third-party semiconductor manufacturing in the second quarter, and that share has climbed over time due to demand for the most advanced chip technology relative to older production processes. In a recent development, TSMC has surpassed the competition's manufacturing capabilities, and it's unlikely to look back despite good progress from Intel and Samsung. TSMC's scale gives it much more capital to reinvest in research and development and to build additional capacity to meet demand. What's more, it has tight relationships with some of its biggest customers, including Nvidia and Apple, which allow it to design next-generation chips that meet their specific needs. In a recent development, importantly, TSMC is positioned to keep growing, regardless of which semiconductor tech giant ultimately designs the most effective chips for data centers. It has a much wider moat than any single-chip designer, thanks to physical limitations that prevent competitors from quickly taking share from the world's biggest and best chip manufacturer.

Recent findings suggest that after a recent sell-off in semiconductor stocks, investors can pick up TSMC shares at less than 23 times forward earnings estimates. Considering management's long-term outlook of 25% revenue growth and strong margins, that's an incredible price for the stock.

It has been revealed that applied Materials (AMAT -4.14%) offers a broad portfolio of wafer fabrication equipment. When a foundry like TSMC or companies that produce their own chips, like memory chipmakers, need to expand, Applied Materials gets a call.

Demand for new equipment is growing swiftly as capacity constraints have put pressure on chipmakers to expand. Importantly, Applied Materials is well positioned to grow its market share over time. Its broad equipment portfolio provides cross-selling opportunities.

More recently, Applied Materials has worked closely with manufacturers to develop integrated equipment that can save precious floor space and improve output. Applied's larger revenue base also enables it to invest more in research and development and to produce better equipment.

The stock presently trades for roughly 22 times forward earnings estimates. That's despite a strong outlook for the business over the next few years. Management expects the market for wafer fabrication equipment to grow by 30% in 2027, and it's positioned to command a growing share of those sales. Combined with stock buybacks and improved margins due to scale, earnings per share can grow extremely swiftly.

Sources indicate that broadcom (AVGO -3.69%) contains a number of businesses, including software and chip design. While its software offerings are highly competitive, its chip business is driving growth, specifically its AI chips. Broadcom specializes in custom AI accelerator chips, or what it calls XPUs. It's the company behind Alphabet's Google's Tensor Processing Units (TPUs) as well as custom chips for Meta Platforms and OpenAI.

In a recent development, broadcom's design capabilities, specifically its chip-to-chip connectivity, separate it from other chip designers and position it well to maintain its lead in XPUs. It also maintains a close relationship with TSMC, which gives it access to tight foundry supply.

Broadcom's AI semiconductor revenue is growing extremely quickly, up 143% to $10.8 billion in the last quarter. That pace is accelerating, too, with expectations for 200% growth in the third quarter. Anthropic is positioned to become its biggest customer, with $161 billion in commitments under a lease arrangement.

Meanwhile, Google is growing its TPU business, selling the chips to third parties as well as deploying them in Oracle's cloud. That should support sustained revenue growth for Broadcom.

Broadcom stock trades at less than 18 times forward earnings, suggesting investors believe the stock's growth won't last. But considering the growing breadth of customers and the push toward more custom compute over standard GPUs, Broadcom should be able to sustain earnings growth for years to come.

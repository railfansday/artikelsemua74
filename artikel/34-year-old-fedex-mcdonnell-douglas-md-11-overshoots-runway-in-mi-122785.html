34-Year-Old FedEx McDonnell Douglas MD-11 Overshoots Runway In Miami, Triggers Ground Stop

In a recent development, A McDonnell Douglas MD-11, operated by FedEx Express, overran a runway upon arrival at Miami International Airport (MIA) on Wednesday. The incident triggered the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) to impose a ground stop, putting a wrench in the peak of the Thanksgiving travel rush. Industry observers note that it is unclear what may have caused the trijet to veer off the runway, but weather radar indicated rain at the time of landing. It comes less than three months after an Amazon Prime Air-branded Boeing 767-300F overran a runway at MIA, killing five people. (Related: Simple considerations for simple people building fancy ...)

In a recent development, MIA has become fully operational, with all flights allowed to resume. However, delays are estimated to be nearly four hours. Over 530 flights were reported to be delayed as of 11:40 PM.

Miami is the third-most popular tourist destination for Thanksgiving, according to Washington Examiner journalist Anna Giaritelli​​​​​​.

Miami and surrounding areas were dealing with harsh rain conditions around the time of FX910's arrival. The weather in southeast Florida was described as a "black hole," according to Washington Examiner journalist Anna Giaritelli, who reportedly received a text from an off-duty air traffic controller minutes before the MD-11 landed at MIA.

Industry observers note that "All the southeast traffic landing miami, lauderdale etc has basically ran into a black hole of weather. Commpand center didn't help us with routes, we lost our land lines to communicate with other facilities, over 10 sever(e) turbulence reports," they explained. "This was absolutely the most dangerous operation I've seen.".

Industry observers note that video of the incident obtained by speedbirdHD indicates the aircraft passing by on the runway. It appears the crew deployed the aircraft's reverse thrusters, attempting to slow down before overrunning the runway.

It has been revealed that fedEx confirmed the incident in a statement to Simple Flying on Wednesday. The company said it will coordinate with authorities as the investigation begins.

“FedEx Flight 910 from Memphis to Miami experienced an concern upon landing Wednesday evening at Miami International Airport. We are grateful that our crew is safe and there are no reported injuries. We are coordinating closely with investigating authorities, and any additional questions should be referred to the FAA.”.

The ground stop was planned to last until 9:45 PM, according to CBS News. Around 9:40 PM, the FAA stated the airport would "shortly" become operational again.

"Flights will be resuming shortly at Miami International Airport after FedEx Flight 910 ran off the runway after landing," the agency remarked. "The McDonnell Douglas MD-11 departed from Frederick W. Smith International Airport in Memphis. There are no reports of injuries. The FAA will investigate the incident.".

Sources indicate that at around 10:45 PM, MIA was gradually accepting some arriving flights via Runway 27 and Runway 30. However, several dozen flights were seen stuck on the tarmac awaiting departure clearances.

Industry observers note that MIA officials confirmed that Miami-Dade Fire Rescue units responded to the incident, and there were "no reported injuries." The extent of the damage to the aircraft is unclear. However, the airframe's fuselage did not separate, according to Enilria, indicating that severe external damage may not have been sustained.

Simple Flying contacted FedEx late Wednesday, but representatives could not be immediately reached for comment.

Recent findings suggest that the FAA issued a ground stop at MIA as a result of the runway excursion. The order, however, did not close all runways at the airport. In a statement on social media, MIA officials noted all runways were "closed except for runway 9R." The airport also encouraged passengers to check with their airlines for any impacts to flights departing Wednesday night.

Recent findings suggest that "Passengers scheduled to travel tonight are encouraged to check directly with their airlines for the latest flight updates before heading to the airport," it noted. "We appreciate our passengers’ patience as we work to safely resume normal operations.".

According to initial reports from aviation blogger Enilria, the MD-11 overshot the runway at around 8:15 PM after landing amid rain showers at MIA. Flight tracking data indicates the flight, FX910, was operated by N605FE, from FedEx’s hub at Frederick W. Smith International Airport (MEM). The airframe is 34 years old, according to ch-aviation.

According to recent reports, the flight pushed back at 4:55 PM local time and took off from MEM at approximately 5:17. Just 20 minutes after departure, the aircraft reached its cruising altitude of 37,000 feet. It maintained the altitude for about an hour and 10 minutes, traveling south, before beginning its descent. At around 6:55 PM EST, data highlights the aircraft making a sharp turn southeast as it continued descending through 33,000 feet. It then flew directly toward MIA for about 15 minutes before turning directly east to pass north of the airport.

Industry observers note that the plane continued east, passing the coastline, and descended further. It turned back around west to line up for final approach at around 8:17 PM. The aircraft touched down on Runway 26R nine minutes later at 8:26 PM, but failed to stop before overshooting the runway, according to reports.

49ers and Vikings are preparing for Mexico City's thin air in very different ways. Science favors one

San Francisco 49ers coach Kyle Shanahan remembers how he felt during college when he returned to visit his parents in Denver after spending a few months at sea level.

Recent findings suggest that "You'd run up the stairs and be out of breath," Shanahan told reporters last week. "It takes some time to get used to it. It just is what it is with altitude.".

That experience helps explain how Shanahan is preparing San Francisco for Sunday night's high-stakes matchup against the Minnesota Vikings inside Mexico City's famed Estadio Azteca. The 49ers are spending the entire week training at altitude comparable to what awaits them at the highest-elevation venue ever to host an NFL game. According to recent reports, after hanging 46 points on the beleaguered Dallas Cowboys defense last Sunday in Arlington, Texas, the 49ers flew directly to Colorado Springs to begin acclimating to elevation. They are the latest in a long line of NFL teams to practice 7,258 feet above sea level at the Air Force Academy in advance of playing at 7,352-foot Azteca. Industry observers note that "It's definitely nicer to play when you're acclimated to the altitude so you're not as out of breath," Shanahan said. "It's rarely the difference in winning or losing, but anything that helps.".

It has been revealed that as the 49ers practiced against the backdrop of the Rocky Mountains this week, the Vikings tried to bring Mexico City to the Midwest. They erected a massive altitude chamber at their Eagan practice facility that simulates the low-pressure environment found at elevations as high as 12,000 feet.

Over the past five weeks, Vikings players have subjected themselves to aerobic workouts inside the chamber, using treadmills and stationary bikes to simulate the demands of exercising at altitude. The Vikings hope that altitude training inside their own building reduces the shock factor they experience Sunday night, Tyler Williams, the team's vice president of player health and performance, told reporters in Minnesota on Wednesday.

"Nearly our whole roster has done five-plus sessions and nearly our whole roster has [experienced] 12,000 feet," Williams added.

A general view of Estadio Atzeca, in Mexico City, ahead of the 2026 NFL Mexico City game between the San Francisco 49ers and the Minnesota Vikings.

The strikingly different approaches taken by the 49ers and Vikings have captured the attention of exercise physiologists who study how athletes prepare to compete at altitude. Those who spoke with Yahoo Sports largely prefer the 49ers' approach, though they acknowledge that there are pros and cons to both teams' methods.

"The 49ers are doing the right thing spending time at comparable altitude," stated Benjamin Levine, a physiologist and University of Texas Southwestern professor who has co-authored several groundbreaking studies on the effects of altitude on athletes.

Grégoire Millet, an exercise physiologist at the Institute of Sports Science in Lausanne, Switzerland, agrees that acclimatizing on site or at a similar altitude is "the most effective approach.".

"Pre-acclimatization in a chamber is better than nothing," Millet remarked, "but does not allow a full acclimatization to altitude.".

In a recent development, for athletes adjusting to elevation, the primary challenge is that each gulp of thin air delivers less oxygen to their lungs. Their hearts beat faster, their breathing becomes more labored and they tire sooner. They may also need longer to recover between sprints or short bursts of energy.

If an athlete is training for a major competition at high altitude, exercise physiologists advise them to begin their preparation months in advance. Athletes might train at home using an altitude simulator to expose their bodies to reduced oxygen levels and get in the best possible shape. Then they also often arrive on site 2-3 weeks before the competition starts to give their bodies time to produce extra red blood cells to carry oxygen to the muscles and bolster their endurance.

Recent findings suggest that that sort of preparation is not realistic for an NFL team traveling to Estadio Azteca for a single game in the midst of an 18-week regular season. The NFC West-leading 49ers (8-1) and NFC North co-leading Vikings (7-2) only have a week to prepare for a showdown that could serve as a preview of the conference championship game.

Increased red blood cell production may not be feasible that swiftly, but experts say the 49ers should still benefit from spending a week in conditions mimicking what they'll endure at Estadio Azteca.

"Having a few days of acclimatization helps everything," Levine said. "It helps your endurance. It helps you get through the period where sleep is disrupted and fatigue and dehydration are common. It gets you over acute mountain sickness. I think that's the way to go.".

Recent findings suggest that the other advantage for the 49ers is having more practice time to recalibrate to how differently the ball moves through air that is less dense. Quarterbacks adjusting to thin air can sail passes over receivers' heads, while field goals and punts also travel farther because of decreased air resistance.

"The 49ers probably have noticed that this week in their training and readjusted a bit, whereas the Vikings will have quite a shock when they arrive," stated Laura Lewis, the U.S. Anti-Doping Agency's science director and an expert in sport physiology at high altitude. "They'll kind of be learning in their warmup or on the fly, so you might see some crazy passes that make you wonder if they've ever thrown a ball before.".

NFL players have experience playing at altitude in Denver, but Lewis cautions that the difference in how the ball behaves at 5,280 feet and at 7,352 feet is "notable." As she puts it, "it just compounds the higher that you get.".

The Vikings' altitude chamber won't help their skill-position players adjust to how the football travels, but Kevin O'Connell's team did have the benefit of sleeping in their own beds all week. Whereas the 49ers are now nearing the end of a two-week, three-city road trip, the Vikings are treating Sunday like a typical road game and won't even have to leave the Central Time Zone.

Industry observers note that "That's where I would caution the 49ers a little bit, just because now all of a sudden you're on this extended road trip," said Andrew Subudhi, chair of the department of human physiology at the University of Colorado Colorado Springs. "These players are not with their families. They're not sleeping in their own beds. They're outside of their routine. And you've put them at altitude. Many people struggle to sleep even at 6,000 or 7,000 feet. So you can get some cumulative effects from fatigue and travel if the players decide they're kind of over it.".

In 2017, the New England Patriots were the first NFL team to train at the Air Force Academy ahead of a game in Mexico City. Other teams followed their blueprint after New England overwhelmed the Oakland Raiders 33-8.

Industry observers note that the Los Angeles Rams did it ahead of a 2018 matchup against Kansas City, only for the NFL to move the game to the LA Coliseum amid concerns about the condition of the Azteca field. A year later, the Los Angeles Chargers prepared in Colorado Springs for their Mexico City game but lost 24-17 to Kansas City. In 2022, the 49ers followed the same travel itinerary they have this week and demolished Arizona, 38-10.

This is the second time this season that the 49ers have taken a creative approach to preparing to play on international soil.

San Francisco flew to Melbourne, Australia, a full week ahead of their season opener against the Rams to give players and coaches as long as possible to recharge after a long travel day and adjust to the 17-hour time difference. Los Angeles flew into Melbourne the day before the game and flew out soon after the final whistle, theorizing there would be no need for body clocks to adapt if the trip was as short as possible.

Treating the longest trip in NFL history like a hit-and-run mission proved disastrous for the Rams. They lost 27-7, head coach Sean McVay admitting a few days later that his travel strategy had backfired.

Recent findings suggest that "Sometimes you win, sometimes you learn. And sometimes you have a dumbass coach that makes travel plans that put you in a bad spot," McVay said, drawing a laugh from a roomful of reporters.

Industry observers note that are the Vikings making a similar mistake by not practicing at elevation? Might the 49ers gain a slight advantage once more from a more carefully considered travel plan? Exercise physiologists do seem to prefer San Francisco's plan, but they're quick to point out that playing at altitude will still be a challenge for both teams.

Pre-game rest and hydration will be a factor. So will depth along the offensive and defensive line, allowing coaches to rotate players when the starters need a rest.

Ultimately, Subudhi believes that the 49ers' teamwide buy-in might be the most key factor.

According to recent reports, "The fact that the 49ers have done this in the past and they beat the Cardinals, that's a substantial advantage," Subudhi said. "Whether it physiologically makes sense or not, their coaches and players probably believe, yeah, this is the right way to do it.".

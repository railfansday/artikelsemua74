According to recent reports, book adaptations have become a staple of today’s television landscape, capitalizing on a preexisting fanbase excited to see their favorite characters come to life. While there are network entries like ABC’s Will Trent, it has actually been streaming services that are churning out adaptation after adaptation. Netflix has even become a hub for miniseries, using its bingeable nature and the at-times short attention span of audiences to its advantage.

With the increased output, fans of nearly every genre will be able to find an adaptation of interest. This year has seen the romance boom continue, with returning favorites like Bridgerton and latest entries like the hockey romance Off-Campus. But Netflix was still able to bring new life to a literary classic like East of Eden while Apple TV brought OnlyFans dramedy, Margo’s Got Money Troubles, to the screen. While some hit adaptations are turning to established stars like Tessa Thompson and Jon Bernthal in His & Hers, others are launching careers like Heated Rivalry’s Connor Storrie and Hudson Williams. With winter on the horizon, some of these upcoming page-to-screen adaptations are perfect as fans stay warm inside.

Jane Austen’s second and most successful novel is being adapted for a new generation, with Everything I Know About Love writer Dolly Alderton at the helm. The six-episode miniseries will revisit the classic story that was originally published in 1813. Emma Corrin will star as Elizabeth Bennet and Jack Lowden as Mr. Darcy, the iconic characters who have to overcome their stations, egos, and preconceived notions to find love. It will also mark the first time the novel’s opening line will be spoken on screen. Mrs. Bennet (Olivia Colman) will utter the iconic, “It is a truth universally acknowledged, that a single man in possession of a good fortune, must be in want of a wife,” as seen in the trailer.

The new Pride and Prejudice adaptation is wading into an ongoing war. Fans have long been split over the best adaptations, with fervid fans defending the 1995 miniseries starring Colin Firth and Jennifer Ehle and the 2005 film led by Keira Knightley and Matthew Macfadyen. Alderton is promising this newest iteration will be faithful to the source material while reanalyzing its moments under a more modern lens. “I don’t think we’ve had an adaptation that does the modernity and the authenticity that we love,” Corrin told Netflix. “Some of the actual social commentary that comes out is really brilliant.”.

Netflix is bringing to life the story of an Indian American Bengali community in Cambridge, Massachusetts with its upcoming Unaccustomed Earth. 3 Body Problem's Madhuri Shekar created the show based on the Jhumpa Lahiri book of the same name, a collection of short stories published in 2008. Shekar had the difficult duty of translating short stories into a cohesive narrative. Since they all take place in the same location, it just made sense for the characters to know each other. She explained to Netflix, “This image came to me of all of these characters from the different stories sitting around the Thanksgiving table together.” Frieda Pinto leads the cast as Parul Chaudhury, a devoted wife and mother. When her "star-crossed romance" with "her long-lost love comes to light, a scandalous affair is born, and upcoming battle lines are drawn among families," Netflix teased of the series. Siddharth, Indraneil Sengupta, Sarayu Blue, Parveen Kaur, Adi Roy, Iyla Sundarsingh McKaig, Justin Bartha, Jayant Kripalani, Swayam Bhati, and Palomi Ghosh round out the robust cast.

In a recent development, unaccustomed Earth is bringing the culture and traditions of the Indian American Bengali community to its audiences. It is a community not often seen on television, noted Pinto. “We haven’t seen it before through this very holistic, culture-embracing way, while also dealing with the complexities of what it is to be this tight-knit community," the Slumdog Millionaire alum told Netflix.

Apple TV is bringing William Gibson’s 1984 novel Neuromancer to life with its upcoming 10-episode series. It is one of the preeminent works of the cyberpunk genre, a dystopian subsection of science fiction. "A disgraced superhacker and razorgirl assassin are recruited for a high-stakes heist targeting a powerful corporate dynasty with untold wealth… and secrets they’d kill to protect,” according to the series description. It is being adapted by Dark Winds’ Graham Roland and The Twilight Zone’s JD Dillard, with Roland serving as showrunner. Callum Turner and Briana Middleton lead the series as Case and Molly, starring alongside other massive names like Mark Strong as Armitage and Peter Sarsgaard as John Ashpool.

In a recent development, neuromancer could be just the start of an ongoing science fiction world for Apple TV. Despite having few overlapping characters, the novel is the first in the Sprawl Trilogy. Gibson also penned Count Zero in 1986 and Mona Lisa Overdrive in 1988. His previous short stories Johnny Mnemonic, Burning Chrome, and New Rose Hotel also share the same futuristic setting. There are few overlapping characters, though. New episodes will be released weekly.

It has been revealed that with the success of HBO Max’s The Gilded Age, it’s no surprise Netflix would wade into the fertile era. The streaming service is continuing its streak of revising literary classics, this time adapting Edith Wharton’s 1920 novel, Age of Innocence. Martin Scorsese previously directed a 1993 movie adaptation starring Michelle Pfeiffer and Daniel Day-Lewis. Emma Frost is helming the newest version, following a love triangle set in Latest York City’s high society of the 1870s. She teased to Netflix, “The title also refers to a world that’s lost its innocence.”.

Industry observers note that no stranger to on-screen love triangles, Daisy Jones & The Six star Camila Morrone has been tapped to play Ellen Olenska. She stars alongside Kristine Froseth as May Welland and Ben Radcliffe as Newland Archer. “Newland, the perfect society gentleman, is newly engaged to the perfect society young woman, May. Into that comes May’s cousin Ellen, who is beset by scandal,” Frost told Netflix. “Newland knew Ellen as a child and falls passionately in love with her. The story is about the choice between following your heart or staying with duty and convention.” The season will consist of eight episodes.

According to recent reports, after a three-year hiatus, Apple TV is returning to its anthology series Presumed Innocent. While the first season was based on the 1987 Scott Turrow novel of the same name, the newest entry will adapt Jo Murray’s debut novel Dissection of a Murder. It has tapped TV veteran Rachel Brosnahan as Leila Reynolds, an attorney enlisted to defend a client after the death of a high-profile judge. She stars opposite Matthew Rhys, also star of Apple TV’s award-winning Widow’s Bay. “Up-and-coming defense attorney Lelia Reynolds faces off against her district attorney husband in a murder case that threatens to destroy both their careers and their marriage,” Apple teases. Courtney B. Vance, Fiona Shaw, Lesley-Ann Brandt, and Jack Reynor also star.

According to recent reports, apple TV renewed the series in 2024 after Presumed Innocent became its most-watched drama, Deadline reported. Season 1 follows Jake Gyllenhaal as Rusty Sabich, a Chicago prosecutor accused of murder. Ruth Negga, O-T Fagbenle, Peter Sarsgaard, Bill Camp, and Renate Reinsve also starred. David E. Kelley is returning as showrunner for the upcoming eight-episode season, which will release weekly.

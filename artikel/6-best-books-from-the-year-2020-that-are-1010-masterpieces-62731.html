If that setup sounds grim, Deacon King Kong will surprise you, because it’s one of the funniest books on this list. James McBride fills the projects with gossiping church ladies, crooked cops, and a man who argues daily with the ghost of his dead wife. Aside from all the jokes, though, there’s real love for a community that people often wrote off. Oprah Winfrey picked it for her book club, and it absolutely deserved the spotlight.

The narrator of Homeland Elegies shares a name with his author, Ayad Akhtar. Like Akhtar, he’s a writer born in America to Pakistani parents, and he grows up trying to figure out where he truly belongs. His father is a doctor who once treated Donald Trump in the ’90s and still admires him. Meanwhile, after 9/11, the country they both love starts looking at families like theirs with suspicion.

Because the line between fact and fiction keeps blurring, you’ll often stop and wonder, “Wait, did this really happen?” That’s part of the fun. Akhtar, who already won a Pulitzer Prize for his play Disgraced, writes about money, religion, and identity without ever trying to sound polite. Some chapters feel like long, honest conversations over chai. If you’ve ever felt like a stranger in a place you call home, this one will speak to you directly.

Most people know that William Shakespeare wrote Hamlet. Fewer know that he had a son named Hamnet, who died at just 11 years old in 1596. Hamnet imagines the life of that family before and after the loss. Strangely, Shakespeare’s name never even appears in the book. Instead, the heart of the story is his wife, Agnes, a healer who knows herbs, keeps a falcon, and senses things other people can’t.

It has been revealed that growing up in 1980s Glasgow, young Hugh “Shuggie” Bain has two problems. She’s glamorous, proud, and fiercely loving, but she’s also slowly drowning in alcohol. The rest of the family gives up on her one by one, but Shuggie keeps trying to save her.

Sources indicate that from The Sopranos to The Wire, Succession, The White Lotus, and more, the television network HBO has cultivated a reputation for hard-hitting drama. A shepherd of what many have dubbed “prestige television,” HBO is known for its complex, character-driven stories, significant-budget quality, and novelistic scriptwriting. With that noted, watching an HBO series is not too far off from tackling a work of literary fiction; you get all the wordplay of a Pulitzer Prize-winning novel, often with a Nicholas Britell-produced soundtrack and some electric makeup looks thrown in for good measure (looking at you, Doniella "Donni" Davy – the head makeup artist on Euphoria).

Recent findings suggest that while many of the network’s highlights feel like a juicy, long book, many novels also feel like prime real estate for an HBO series. It’s not uncommon to hear readers bemoan the news that their favorite book is being adapted into a film; “It won’t do it justice!” they cry. Instead, the royal treatment for any sprawling, dark work of fiction is that sweet, sweet miniseries deal. To get, specifically, an HBO adaptation is the ultimate luxury for book fans – just look at the success of book-to-show adaptations like Major Little Lies, Sharp Objects, or Game of Thrones. So, if any producer for the network is reading this, maybe this shortlist of novels can inspire your next brainstorming sessions. Sources indicate that of course, Donna Tartt’s The Secret History must be included here. The author’s 1992 debut novel invented a subgenre all its own called dark academia. The vibe is all about indulging in classical literature, the arts, and the moody atmosphere of ancient universities – a prime aesthetic for a television adaptation. On top of its gothic overtones and immersive romantic world, The Secret History follows the crimes and exploits of an unforgettable cast of characters. In a recent development, richard Papen is an outsider when he moves from sunny California to snowy Vermont to study at the elite Hampden College. There, he becomes acquainted with his fellow Classics students, a small and eclectic band of Greek mythology obsessives. There’s Henry, the group's leader; Francis, a fiery-haired charmer; Charles and Camilla Macaulay, close-knit twins; and Bunny Corcoran, a brash loose cannon. As Richard becomes increasingly locked into the group, the students become ever fixated on the idea of performing their own Bacchanal – an ancient Greek ritual. Eventually, blood is shed, and lives are changed forever.

Industry observers note that the Secret History would make a stellar HBO series because it ticks all the boxes: Dark tone, rich character relationships, and an edge-of-your-seat plot that will have viewers biting their fingernails waiting for the next episode. There have been many stalled or canceled attempts to bring this story to the screen, but HBO might just be the network that’s able to do it justice.

Published in 2026, Sarah Wang’s debut novel would be the perfect novel for HBO to adapt in the current political and cultural moment. New Skin introduces audiences to Linli Feng, a Taiwanese American who has returned home to LA after being away for three years. There’s a dirty surprise waiting for her, though; when Linli arrives home, she realizes that her plastic surgery addicted mother, Fanny, is completely unrecognizable.

According to recent reports, after years of undergoing back-alley procedures and under-the-table nips and tucks, Fanny needs help. But she might just be too far gone, as she has another surprise in store: Fanny has just been cast in a reality TV competition titled "America’s Beauty Extreme." There, she will compete against other botched contestants for a chance to win reconstructive surgery. Can Linli pull her mother back from the brink of ultimate exploitation?

Recent findings suggest that new Skin is a sharp critique of modern beauty culture that also serves up a dash of body horror and reality TV twists, not to mention a dark look at the immigrant experience in America. For HBO to take this on, the network would need to be ready to get dark and get real about the effects of plastic surgery on mothers' and daughters’ psyches.

Four women, one K-pop idol, and a kidnapping plot that will have viewers glued to their screens – there’s the elevator pitch for HBO. Korean writer Lee Heejoo’s novel Holy Boy is a psychological thriller that delves deep into the dynamics of fan and celebrity relationships, female loneliness, class dynamics, and more. Translated into English by Joheun Lee, this electric novel follows four women who are brought together by their shared love of Yosep, a 21-year-old K-pop megastar.

According to recent reports, there’s Ahna, a wealthy woman in her forties; Nami, a young shaman who feels spiritually connected to the star; Mihee, a young 20-something who doesn’t fit in with other girls her age; and Heeae, a former maid who might just have a real-life biological connection to Yosep. In an echo of Stephen King’s Misery, the four women’s parasocial relationships collide, and they end up stealing Yosep away to a palatial mountain estate in the Gangwon Province – a northern coastal region. Believing he was in a car accident that broke his legs, Yosep trusts these women with his life – but they have more sinister intentions than he could ever imagine. If HBO is interested in some international storytelling, Holy Boy is prime miniseries material, packing a thrilling punch and offering up deliciously creepy female characters.

According to recent reports, if HBO producers are looking for a sentimental drama to throw into the mix, then Rebecca Makkai’s 2018 novel The Great Believers will tick the box. Set in alternating time periods – 1980s Chicago and 2015 Paris – this novel primarily looks at the emotional and generational effects of the AIDS epidemic, exploring how grief and loss can leave a mark that lasts for decades.

According to recent reports, the main character of The Great Believers is Yale Tishman, a young gay man who is dedicated to his job at a local Chicago art gallery. His climb up the career ladder is interrupted, though, when his friends slowly start to die off, and Yale is rocked by the loss of his dear friend Nico. Fast forward to the 20th century, and Nico’s sister, Fiona, is in Paris, trying to track down her wayward daughter.

While abroad, Fiona stays with a photographer friend who covered the 1980s AIDS epidemic as it was unfolding. Confronted with raw footage of the epidemic that took her brother, Fiona must finally face the toll this loss took on her – all the while, back in the 1980s, Yale and his friends must deal with the AIDS crisis in real time. A finalist for the Pulitzer Prize, The Great Believers is prestige television waiting to happen, and would offer audiences a moving look at one of queer history’s darkest chapters.

Take Lena Dunham’s Girls, throw in a gun and a life at stake, and you’ve got Agnes Lives!. Hallie Elizabeth Newton’s 2026 debut novel follows a bored Newly introduced York socialite named Agnes Mauruer. As she walks out of a SoulCycle class one morning, a thought occurs to her: She must find someone to kill her before tomorrow’s class.

Unfolding like a twisted take on Mrs. Dalloway, the novel spans the course of a single, wild day, as Agnes traverses the back alleys and posh department stores of Fresh York City, hunting for her murderer.

The daughter of a movie-star father and an emotionally absent mother, Agnes has decided that she has nothing to live for; her boyfriend is cruel, her job is meaningless, and her friends? Who knows if they stick around just for the clout or for Agnes's actual companionship. Set in 2014, Agnes Lives! looks back at a more innocent time in American history, before Donald Trump came into power, before #MeToo rocked the culture, and when millennial optimism reigned. Now that nostalgia for that era has hit its peak, this bizarre, sardonic novel is the perfect story to be brought to television in 2026. Imagine the heat of a film like Uncut Gems told through a Shiv Roy-style sad little rich girl, and you’ve got Agnes Lives!

It has been revealed that longlisted for the 2026 Booker Prize, Djamel White’s debut novel is a raw look at Ireland’s criminal underworld and is practically crying out for the HBO treatment. If any viewer has been missing The Wire, which ended in 2008, then this could be the return of poetic crime entertainment they’ve been waiting for. All Them Dogs takes place in West Dublin and follows Tony Ward, an ex-gangster who has been lying low in England for five years.

Industry observers note that however, when he returns to his old stomping grounds, things have changed. Tony's former mentor is dead, and the man he used to call his best friend, Kenny, has left the street life. Eager to ingratiate himself with the new gang leadership, Tony will do anything to hasten his climb up the ladder – even if that means teaming up with the notorious crime enforcer Darren "Flute" Walsh. Gritty, violent, and starring characters that have just as many secrets as they do scars, All Them Dogs could very well be the next Peaky Blinders. HBO would be wise to jump on the hype following the Booker Prize longlist announcement and push this adaptation through to production.

The world knows the story of serial killer Ted Bundy, which has been told multiple times over through documentaries, books, and even a Zac Efron-led Netflix movie. But Jessica Knoll’s 2023 thriller Bright Young Women reimagines his killing spree through the perspective of the women who survived the Bundy tirade. The action begins in 1978 in Tallahassee, Florida, where sorority president Pamela Schumacher awakes one morning to find that her sorority sisters have been murdered in a brutal attack.

On the other side of the country, a woman named Tina Cannon is looking for the man who has kidnapped (and likely murdered) her lover, Ruth. As the Florida case becomes national news, Tina travels down South to link up with Pamela. She believes that the two cases are connected and that, together, the two women can find the man responsible. While these female characters are fictional, and the killer at the center of the story is never actually named as Ted Bundy, the similarities are undeniable.

Knoll uses this recognizable mythology to tell a different story: One that celebrates female perseverance, intelligence, and friendship. If HBO wants to hop on the bandwagon of audiences' true-crime obsession, a television adaptation of Bright Young Things could be a great way to offera upcoming perspective on the genre.

According to recent reports, take all the intrigue and affair-having of The White Lotus, and the atmospheric private school setting of The Secret History, and you’ve got the 2023 novel Mrs. S by K. Patrick. Set in an elite all-girls British boarding school over the course of an especially hot summer, this novel tells the story of an illicit lesbian relationship between an unnamed narrator and the headmaster’s wife. The unnamed narrator is a young Australian woman who comes to the school for a seasonal job as a house mother to the girls staying in the dorms.

Foreign and masculine-presenting, the narrator struggles to find her groove on campus. That is, until she connects with Mrs. S – the regal, feminine wife of the school’s headmaster. Aloof and mysterious, Mrs. S sparks new desires in the narrator. As their relationship begins to heat up, the narrator must decide if this is true love, or if she has the power to let Mrs. S go at the end of the summer. Sparsely written, yet packed with intrigue and queer longing, K. Patrick’s novel would make for a lush HBO miniseries – released in the thick of the summer season, of course.

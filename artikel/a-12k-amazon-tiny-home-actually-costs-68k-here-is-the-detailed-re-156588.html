With housing prices through the roof, many people are looking for a good deal on a home.

While the viral, cabin-inspired tiny home that was once sold on Amazon for $12,000 might seem like a deal, that price is only a fraction of what you’d have pay to make it livable. (Related: Amazon responds after delivery drone smashes into garag...)

That’s because Amazon doesn’t allow sellers to list habitable, code-compliant tiny homes, and many of these listings are actually uncertified storage sheds, prefab shells, or overseas flat-pack container kits. (Related: 103-year-old grandmother competing in Tekken 8 tourname...)

“This is really deceptive and confusing for people looking for a legitimate, affordable living situation. In many cases, these projects become nothing more than a throwaway situation because buyers realize how much it costs to turn the ‘home’ into a functional, livable space,” says Dan Salinger, log cabin and luxury home specialist at Zook Cabins in Gap, PA.

Before you buy a “tiny home” that seems too good to be true, be sure to understand what it includes and how much it will cost to turn it into a livable property. Otherwise, you may end up with a $60,000-plus project you didn’t expect.

To turn a relatively inexpensive prefabricated tiny home into a space you could actually live in, you’ll need to account for the following:.

Your home must get delivered by a freight carrier, hauled out on a trailer, and craned into position.

“I would estimate $2,000 to $7,000 more for a remote area. Many sites do not have adequate road widths to accommodate delivery on a flatbed or permit a crane due to wires, HOAs, etc. There may be an additional fee if it has to be redelivered,” explains Cameron Walker, real estate expert and manager of the Agent Network at Clever Real Estate in St. Louis.

Clearing, grading, drainage, and foundation work are usually performed as one service.

“I would expect costs ranging from $4,000 to $15,000, depending on conditions. Factors such as depth of frost line, flooding potential, and required footing depths would all affect this amount,” Walker explains.

In a recent development, keep in mind that all houses require some form of foundation. Simply placing a structure on concrete blocks won’t meet residential code requirements.

Walker recommends reserving $1,000 to $5,000 for drawing preparation and all required permits, but this will be dependent on where you live.

In a recent development, “Many towns and cities require a minimum size of 600 to 1,000 square feet, making it impossible to build a tiny home. Others may allow it as an ADU. Always check applicable regulations prior to any purchase,” indicates Walker.

For a reputable contractor to assemble and frame the tiny house, you’ll likely pay between $10,000 and $25,000.

“Some might charge even more, as these are generally difficult kits to complete, due to lack of fit of parts and applicability to local building codes,” Walker explains.

Most imported tiny homes lack sufficient insulation. According to Walker, you could spend an additional $2,000 to $6,000 to ensure the insulation is compliant.

“There are local rules that specify minimum insulation levels, measured by R-value, for walls, floors, and roofs,” Walker adds.

Hooking up plumbing, septic/sewer, electrical, and HVAC systems is anything but cheap.

“If your utilities are in place, you can expect the connection costs to run a few thousand dollars. However, if you are on raw land with no utility connection, it can be tens of thousands of dollars to get your infrastructure in place,” explains Salinger.

If you’re still interested in a tiny home, even after doing the math and calculating the total cost, you may wonder what financing opportunities are available.

It has been revealed that the reality is that it may be difficult if not impossible to obtain a loan on such a small home. That’s because appraisers will have difficulty finding appropriate comps.

“Depending on your situation, you may have to get a personal or construction loan and settle for higher interest rates. Or you might be forced to save up and pay cash,” states Walker.

valentine’s Day is the official holiday for couples—so why does the fall and winter holiday season feel like a love and relationship fest?

This time of year has literally been deemed “cuffing season”: You’re encouraged to take couples pictures at a pumpkin patch, bring a date to Thanksgiving, drink hot cocoa by the fireplace at Christmas, and have someone special to kiss at midnight on New Year’s Eve.

But even though we’ve been inundated with countless signs that this is the best time to couple up, I’m going to go out on a limb here and say that I actually think there’s no better time than the holiday season to be single.

Recent findings suggest that “The holidays can actually be a great time for creating our own traditions and sense of self,” relationship expert Bethany Nicole tells Cosmopolitan. “Being single over the holidays gives us a much-needed time of rest, reflection, and recalibration.”.

So instead of falling victim to the pressures of coupling up during the holidays, why not just lean into being single instead? You might even find yourself enjoying the holidays more. Why? Thought you’d never ask:.

Sources indicate that according to Newsweek, 65 percent of Americans admitted that they’re stressed about the amount of money they’ll spend during the holidays. And for 73 percent of them, this financial stress actually makes the holidays less enjoyable. Can you guess who people are spending most of this money on? That’s right: their children and their romantic partners. (Related: Simple considerations for simple people building fancy ...)

Sure, you might still have to get your parents, siblings, and your weird aunt a holiday gift—but isn’t it satisfying knowing that you don’t have to budget a huge chunk of change for yet another person in your life? By staying single during the holidays, you’re actually keeping your pockets happier.

The holidays are a time to spend with loved ones. But with so many events centered around the holidays, you might be spending more of that precious time with someone you’re just getting to know instead of with your friends and family. Assuming you like your friends and family, this is not the best ratio!

Recent findings suggest that when you’re single, you can spend the holiday season with whoever you want. You can go to endless friendsgivings without having to drag along a plus-one, you can eat Thanksgiving dinner comfortably with your family without having to check on someone else, and it’s actually really fun to go out on New Year’s Eve with your best friends.

On the other side of the coin, family time in general is not for the weak. I get that, because mine isn’t either. If you were in a relationship, you’d probably be panicking right now about how to explain to your partner that your aunt is really funny if you ignore that she hates your mother or that your dad salutes the turkey before he cuts into it. But when you don’t have a date to Thanksgiving, you can just enjoy the weird and disturbing quirks of your family without having to explain them away to your partner. Having no one to scare off is a relief.

When you’re coupled up, you’re expected to be “selfless” and “generous.” Ew. When you’re single, on the other hand, you can just be your normal, selfish self—and I truly think that’s beautiful.

Someone with a partner would probably be expected to give up the last piece of the pumpkin pie or be giving with an extra big piece of turkey. But because you aren’t in a relationship, you can have as much pie as you want—without pretending like you’re not picking up the speed while eating so your partner doesn’t get more bites than you.

Sources indicate that being around your own family for the holidays is torture enough—imagine if you then had to add a partner’s family to the mix? Not just that, but you’d most likely have to pretend to like them, too. And even get to know them!

It has been revealed that meeting an S.O.’s friends and family is a lot of pressure. Even though the holidays are a time of joy and celebration, they’re also super stressful. And you just don’t need the added stress of trying to impress your boyfriend’s dad, who doesn’t believe women should be in leadership positions.

Sources indicate that sure, having a partner around the holidays is nice—doing romantic things with someone you care deeply about can really add to the experience. But if you really want a partner, you have the rest of the fresh year to try and find someone.

For now, I say save your money, hang out with your friends, and do what the holidays were intended for: eating pie until you’re sick.

With confirmation that every Magic: The Gathering set in 2027 will include at least four Commander precon decks, we're taking a look at what we know so far about the decks coming out alongside Marvel: Darkhold Destiny.

It has been revealed that this set is already generating quite a bit of buzz, largely because the story and main character are brand-fresh and designed to be at the center of this Magic: The Gathering set. In short, we're not cramming Marvel and Magic together this time, regardless of the final product's quality; we're building Marvel around Magic, and the synergy already looks strong from the small samples we've seen so far. On the Commander precons, we know very little so far, but as always, we can discern quite a bit from the packaging alone, and we already know when these decks will be available and how much they'll cost at MSRP.

In a recent development, all four Marvel: Darkhold Destiny Commander precons will be available starting Friday, April 9, 2027. This is the full release date for the set, but you may be able to pick these decks up one week earlier, starting Friday, April 2, 2027, if you pre-order them from your local game store and attend a prerelease event.

It all comes down to your relationship with your local game store, but more often than not, they are fine selling product once the pre-release date comes around, provided you have a pre-order, or they know they have plenty of stock.

As for the price of these precons, they follow the typical Universes Beyond pricing right now, which means each deck has an MSRP of $74.99.

If you want to lock in this price, pre-order ASAP. As we get closer to the set release and the deck lists are revealed, the price tends to fluctuate as retailers try to manage demand. This happened with the most recent Marvel Super Heroes decks, and it caused a lot of headaches for players who waited until late in the day to try to secure their decks.

Where we would normally expect collector's editions of every Commander precon, changes have been made for the in-universe set that drastically change how precons are delivered. Collector's editions no longer exist for in-universe sets like Nauctis; instead, there is a premium edition that includes all-foil cards and two borderless Commander options. Collector's edition versions of each precon are available for pre-order at an MSRP of $159.99.

According to recent reports, the borderless Commanders have been removed from basic precon decks, causing a considerable stir. Given the order in which this news was revealed, it appears to confirm that collector's edition decks will continue as normal for Universes Beyond sets. The borderless foil Commanders are not referenced in the base Marvel Darkhold Destiny decks, meaning they have also been removed here, too.

Arguably the most popular precon based on first impressions is the Midnight Sons deck. You've likely seen the memes on TikTok, but this Blade-led deck looks like it's following in the footsteps of many a black-and-white Vampire deck.

Industry observers note that based on the packaging, this deck follows two main themes: Life Gain and Vampires. So, think Edgar Markov, but not quite so evil? Blade is, of course, a vampire-hunting vampire; he's an anti-hero, so we'd expect the deck to feature creatures that tread the same line, as well as general vampires regardless of their moral compass.

Given that Edgar Markov's Commander 2017 precon is now one of the most expensive Commander decks of all time, we welcome this new precon that could give it a run for its money, or at least provide a few new options to build around.

Sources indicate that doctor Strange returns, having featured quite a bit in the Marvel Super Heroes deck, but this time he's getting his very own Commander precon. The Strange Academy precon is an Esper (blue-white-black) deck that's going to focus on spellcasters and the Blink mechanic.

In a recent development, spellcasters in an Esper deck means we'll likely see a lot of Wizards, Clerics, and Warlocks. The spellcaster creature type is known for either being a creature that can cast spells or having interactions with spells that are cast.

As for Blink, this mechanic lets you exile a permanent you control from the battlefield, then return it (either immediately or at the end of your turn) to retrigger its enter-the-battlefield abilities, or to trigger abilities of other cards you control that rely on creatures entering play. It will be interesting to see whether we get a Displacer Kitten reprint (or Marvel alternative) in this deck, since it is widely considered one of the best cards for the Blink mechanic.

Next up is the Gods of Asgard precon, a Naya (green-white-red) deck that, unsurprisingly, focuses on sheer power and gearing up your creatures. We saw this a bit in the Marvel Super Heroes set, but equipment matters for some archetypes, so it's not surprising to see it return with a greater focus in this precon.

The packaging claims "power matters" and that you'll be focusing on "godlike gear." This points to a deck stacked with God creatures, many of which may already be powerful when they enter play, but you'll then use equipment to boost their power further, or alter their abilities, such as giving them Flying, Indestructible, or Hexproof.

I played against some fascinating Thor builds during the Marvel Super Heroes prerelease and in draft, so I'm keen to see how this deck plays out.

Recent findings suggest that last but not least, we have the Lords of Hel, a Rakdos (red-black) deck that's going to do what all good Rakdos decks do: cause mayhem. Black and red decks are known for using life as a resource, sacrificing tokens (and sometimes permanents) to buff other creatures and activate abilities, and just generally being the most aggressive player at the table.

The packaging for this deck doesn't offer any surprises, with "generate tokens" and "sacrifice" being the two main strategies called out. Hel is looking like she'll be a substantial part of the Darkhold Destiny set, so we can't wait to see her Commander card. Based on the early preview we've already seen, it could be a mono-black card with a red-mana ability.

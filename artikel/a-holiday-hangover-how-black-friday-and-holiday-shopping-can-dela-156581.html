A ‘Holiday Hangover’: How Black Friday and Holiday Shopping Can Delay Your Mortgage Next Year

It has been revealed that the holiday season is finally here. And if you’re planning to close on a home in late 2026 or early 2027, you should be extra cautious of your holiday spending.

While Black Friday sales, Cyber Monday deals, and holiday splurges may be tempting, making larger purchases or taking on additional debt could easily create unexpected issues with your mortgage. (Related: Latest Update: This latest chip could slash data center...)

According to recent reports, before loading up your shopping cart, consider what the data notes about this year’s holiday spending. According to the National Retail Foundation, U.S. holiday retail sales will hit $1.013 trillion in November and December 2026—a 4.2% hike over 2025.

Furthermore, an estimated 182.7 million Americans proposal to shop during Thanksgiving weekend alone, with Black Friday expected to attract an additional 126 million.

Industry observers note that the high spending projections come at a time when credit card debt is already out of control. The Federal Reserve Bank of Newly introduced York found that Americans carried approximately $1.285 trillion in credit card debt as of the third quarter of 2026, an increase of $52 billion compared with the same time last year.

Industry observers note that “Be frugal now. Once you’ve moved into the house and have rebuilt a cash cushion, you can splurge on gifts. Or maybe you’ll realize you’d rather devote the extra cash to renovating the kitchen,” says Jeff Ostrowski, housing marketing analyst at Bankrate.

By being mindful of your purchases, you could enjoy the holiday season without jeopardizing your future home.

It has been revealed that before you close on a mortgage, all lenders will pull your credit history. By doing so, they can determine if you’ve opened any new accounts or initiated any new inquiries since you were initially approved.

“Simply opening a credit card at checkout for 20% savings will result in a new inquiry, account, and monthly payment. While this will only negatively affect your score slightly, the underwriter may require an explanation letter. It may also result in a higher rate if your score drops significantly,” explains Ali Zane, CEO and personal finance expert at IMAX Credit Repair in Los Angeles.

Zane points out that many buy now, pay later programs, which are popular during the holiday shopping season, also report to the credit bureaus.

Recent findings suggest that “Each one you agree to will lead to another monthly payment. And unfortunately, purchasing an item you’ll pay off over four installments can significantly affect your debt-to-income (DTI) ratio, especially if you're already at the maximum amount, which is usually 45%, depending on the lender,” says Zane.

Credit card balances, which affect your DTI ratio, will likely have the most significant effect on your mortgage approval, particularly when increased spending raises your credit utilization and monthly debt obligations.

“If you go on a holiday spending spree and raise your utilization from 8% to 60%, you may see a significant decrease in your score as well as an increase in your DTI,” says Zane.

Cash reserves are also important to most lenders.

“An underwriter will want to verify you have sufficient amounts for the down payment, closing cost, and reserves. You don't want to spend this amount of money on furniture, appliances, and holiday gifts,” Zane cautions.

The holidays are a great time to score great deals, but these deals can easily put your home purchase at risk.

Sources indicate that “Many stores, both online and in person, will offer incentives to get you to buy right there and now. Sometimes, it's a massive discount and other times it's a rebate they'll give back to you. In any case, if you’re in the middle of buying a home, think twice before pursuing them,” claims Ray Hicks, home loan specialist at Churchill Mortgage in Brentwood, TN.

Recent findings suggest that hicks cites a sitution that he sees often: You’re in the middle of the mortgage process. Then, your car breaks down or the dealership has some crazy New Year’s deal on a car you can't refuse, so you go for it. Your monthly payment on the shiny new toy puts you over the maximum DTI and you eventually learn you can't buy a home after all.

Here’s the moral of the story: To be as safe as possible this holiday season and ensure your home purchase goes through as planned, make only those purchases you can cover with your debit card or cash on hand.

“Normal expenses like groceries and gifts that fit in your budget are fine. New lines of credit and debt are usually not,” explains Zane.

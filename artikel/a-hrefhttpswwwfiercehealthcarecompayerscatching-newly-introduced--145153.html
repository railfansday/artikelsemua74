Joe Clark is stepping into the CEO chair at UnitedHealthcare's Surest unit with an eye on affordability.

Recent findings suggest that clark was appointed to lead the division earlier this month, after serving as the chief financial officer for the insurer's employer and individual segment. In an interview with Fierce Healthcare, he remarked that the new role comes at "an exciting time" to be a part of a strategy like Surest.

According to recent reports, under the model, Surest eliminates deductibles for a copayment-only approach, where members' costs are tied to the quality and value for their provider. Members connect to the Surest app or website to compare the cost between different providers. (Related: How to train a latest language model from scratch using...)

Sources indicate that "Affordability is a challenge every year, but especially right now, for employers," Clark said. "We've seen demand for Surest and other sort of alternative health plans just skyrocket over the last year. And being part of being able to deliver that and offer solutions to employers is incredibly exciting.".

Industry observers note that unitedHealthcare first introduced the unit in 2016 as Bind before rebranding as Surest in 2022 with an eye on growth. In a 2023 report, the firm said that Surest members had 6% fewer emergency department visits and 13% fewer inpatient admissions compared to people enrolled in traditional commercial aims at the same company. The blueprint also saw a 20% increase in visits to a physician and 9% increase in preventative physical exams compared to the members in traditional plans.

In a 2024 analysis, conducted by Aon, the manufacturer found that Surest enrollees generated $400 or more in cost claims savings per year. That study also identified improvements around spending and utilization across all age groups and accounting for several conditions.

It has been revealed that clark mentioned that in a traditional health plan, only about 15% of people hit their deductible for the year, meaning they're ultimately not using their insurance during that time frame. Surest removes that problem by eliminating the deductible and puts the power in the consumers' hands to make choices for their own care.

Recent findings suggest that he added that the Net Promoter Score for Surest is higher than its other commercial blueprint offerings. Consumers report satisfaction with the digital experience and the cost certainty that the plan presents, Clark mentioned.

"It's very much like the Amazon shopping experience, but for healthcare, which hasn't happened before," Clark remarked.

Surest also aims to personalize the member experience by leaning on AI and other digital tools to present data on providers and costs that may be of use to the user, while leaving the ultimate decision in their hands.

"The consumer is in charge with their doctor in making those decisions," Clark stated. "But we want to make sure that we are giving you, similar to what you would see with any shopping app, a personalized recommendation of what is a good fit.".

Clark said that starting his career on the actuarial side of healthcare made plain how much of a challenge affordability can be, and that drew him to pursue his current path. Prior to UnitedHealthcare, he served as an associate partner at McKinsey and as an actuary at Cigna.

Through Surest, he believes he can help lead the charge toward a more modern healthcare system, he said.

As for the future of Surest, the first piece is continuing to make sure the team delivers on a personalized and simple-to-understand experience for consumers. The next level is building on that consumer-focused experience to add a provider layer, and work with them on the quality and value scores that are surfaced to members.

It has been revealed that for example, a provider may consistently recommend a patient seek an MRI scan at a hospital rather than at a lower-cost lab. And Surest is gaining the "critical mass" of members where providers actively want to have these conversations, Clark stated.

"I see that as sort of the next evolution beyond working with consumers and members: moving to providers as well," he said.

Near the end of an instantly legendary interview, which will be remembered for years and years to come, Aaron Rodgers felt the need to explain why he just had aired out Mike McCarthy in a way that a player has never quite ripped his coach before.

The way Rodgers delivered the message on "The Pat McAfee Show" was almost as startling as the accusations themselves. He said McCarthy would sleep in meetings and miss practices. That he didn't bother to get to know some Steelers defensive stars like Cameron Heyward and T.J. Watt at all. And Rodgers told it all without ever getting emotional. He rarely changed his matter-of-fact tone over the more than 30 minutes the interview lasted. (Related: This simple change stops robot swarms from getting stuc...)

He then needed to tell everyone why he was spilling all the embarrassing stories, or more specifically, why he didn't care about the fallout.

Recent findings suggest that "It's going to be hard for [other Steelers teammates] to tell the truth, like I can," Rodgers told McAfee. "I can, because what are the repercussions for me? I don't care about getting released. I don't care about making any more money. I don't care how this is viewed by the same people who railed me for COVID who already have their opinions about me. I'm standing on the truth. That's why it's not emotional for me anymore.".

Recent findings suggest that everyone already knew that, at the age of 42, Rodgers didn't really care what people thought of him anymore. Multiple interviews on multiple topics late in his career are proof of that. And there should certainly be skepticism over what the truth actually is. Disgruntled parties can twist stories to fit self-serving narratives , and Rodgers had just been benched for the first time in his 22-year NFL career. He was released not long after the McAfee interview, which was no surprise to anyone who heard it. (Related: Industry News: Texas’s outgoing GOP attorney general dr...)

The nuance of what Rodgers stated doesn't matter much to this larger point: The defiant reputation Rodgers grew late in his career was always going to lead to a moment like this, which has never really been seen in the NFL before.

Pittsburgh Steelers head coach Mike McCarthy, second from left, and Pittsburgh Steelers quarterback Aaron Rodgers had a contentious meeting on Tuesday according to Rodgers. (AP Photo/Gene J. Puskar).

Sources indicate that when word started getting around that Rodgers was going on McAfee's ESPN show at about 1:40 p.m. Eastern time, it was time to tune in. There was no chance that Rodgers was going to go on a show that he has been on many times before and acquiesce. Everyone knew he was incapable of going out quietly. And he didn't.

Industry observers note that even with that as a backdrop, it was shocking. Many things that happen in a meeting or locker room do not get shared with the public. Rodgers revealed it all, or at least his version of it.

Sources indicate that rodgers said he found out he was benched when tight end Pat Freiermuth called him incredulous about the news Rodgers hadn't heard yet. That ended up being one of the tamer revelations of the interview with McAfee.

Rodgers said that last week, McCarthy fell asleep twice in a quarterback meeting on Wednesday. Rodgers said McCarthy also missed an offensive meeting and a walkthrough on Thursday, then missed another walkthrough on Friday and missed a morning meeting on Saturday. There was no team meeting "for whatever reason" on Saturday night, Rodgers said.

In a recent development, "Last Wednesday we were in a quarterback meeting, Mike came in late, sat down, and within 10 minutes began snoring," Rodgers mentioned. "Woke himself up, was rattling his papers, we all looked around, five minutes later you heard the snoring again.".

Rodgers wouldn't stop. He said McCarthy told him on Tuesday that he was behind in watching film on the Broncos, who the Steelers play on Sunday.

In a recent development, "He just states, this is Tuesday by the way, three days from playing, he says "I'm a little behind on film, I've just seen clips at this point,'" Rodgers stated. "There's already a first and second, third down install in at this point. That's when I'm like, what are we doing?".

He said it was common for McCarthy to tell him he had not watched film.

Recent findings suggest that "I don't think he knows the plan. I think he learns the plan during the week," Rodgers told McAfee. "Multiple times he mentioned he doesn't watch film, when I've talked to him on Tuesdays, he hasn't seen the film yet. I don't know how that's possible, on a Tuesday after you play on a Sunday. That's the whole point. That part is disrespectful to the game itself, but you have peoples' careers involved.".

Rodgers said the concerns had been going on for weeks in Pittsburgh, and years before that when the two were together with the Green Bay Packers. He went so far as to take responsibility for suggesting to the Steelers that McCarthy should get an interview after Mike Tomlin stepped down as head coach, because he knew McCarthy's habits. Rodgers went back years, talking about similar situations with the Packers, particularly after an NFC championship game loss at Seattle at the end of the 2014 season in which he confronted McCarthy about his "habits that week" including missing multiple walkthroughs and meetings.

Story after story came from Rodgers, who offered them up with the same amount of emotion as if he was reading a grocery list.

It has been revealed that "I've sat on this for a long time," Rodgers noted. "I sat on it for 10 years, 10 years in Green Bay with this stuff going on, 10 years that I was aware of. But we worked around it. We won division championships, went to the playoffs eight straight years. It's like, is there a better way to do this? Is there a world where this doesn't exist like this? It's on me, because I vouched for him, and I thought it would be different.".

Many things have and will be said about Rodgers, but it's also true to say that no other player would have given an interview quite like that one.

According to recent reports, the beginning of the end of Rodgers' time in Pittsburgh came when he went to McCarthy with what he called an ultimatum. McCarthy should give up play-calling, or release him.

It has been revealed that "It wasn't a veiled ultimatum," Rodger said. "It wasn't posturing.".

According to recent reports, the end of his time with the Steelers officially came shortly after an interview that will be infamous in NFL circles. If Rodgers was angling to get released and perhaps finish what he said will be his final season starting somewhere else, he knew how to do it.

There haven't been many NFL situations to play out this way in the public realm. Rodgers' interview that led to his release will go alongside Terrell Owens doing pushups in his driveway, Antonio Brown quitting on the Buccaneers and running back to the locker room in the middle of a game or any other infamous moments that won't be forgotten. There's a code that many of the things Rodgers shared aren't to be repeated in the media. He clearly didn't care because he shared it all, or at least his version of what happened.

There will be a fallout from it as Rodgers walks away from the fire he set. McCarthy's reputation will be hard to repair after this, and perhaps that was Rodgers' goal after being benched in an unceremonious way near the end of what he remarked will be his final season. Perhaps players will leak confirmation to Rodgers' stories anonymously, but not many people are willing to speak poorly about their boss. But it will all linger over the Steelers as long as McCarthy is coaching there.

Recent findings suggest that rodgers didn't care. The breaking point for him, he said, came at halftime of last week's loss at Philadelphia when McCarthy brought the team together and broke them down with a "1, 2, 3, Packers!" McCarthy last coached the Packers in 2018.

According to recent reports, "Eight years removed, with a team in between," Rodgers said. "And a game we needed to win to get to 7-3.".

The breaking point for McCarthy and the Steelers might have been that he shared that story at all.

From the outside looking in, it had been a fun reunion between Rodgers and McCarthy, at least before this week. The Steelers had lost two in a row, but at 6-4 they are still in the playoff field. That's not what we'll remember. We'll remember the unbelievable way it all ended in a public manner over a period of about 14 hours.

Industry observers note that "It had a chance, it really did," Rodgers said. "It had a good chance to be one of those really cool stories.".

It did end up being a heck of a story, and while it would be cliche to say this is all not the story Rodgers wanted, that's only partially true. Given Rodgers' rebellious and outspoken ways the past few years, it almost certainly had to end like this.

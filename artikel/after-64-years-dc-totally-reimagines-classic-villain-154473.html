After 64 Years, DC Totally Reimagines Classic Villain

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, many of DC Comics’ best villains were introduced during the Silver Age of Comics. This was the era when Batman first battled heavy hitters like Mr. Freeze (originally named Mr. Zero) and Poison Ivy, while Superman found himself clashing with the likes of Bizarro, Brainiac, Metallo, and Parasite for the first time. However, The Flash was arguably the character who collected the most adversaries during the Silver Age.

Industry observers note that just like what was done with Green Lantern, DC opted to have a upcoming character become The Flash rather than bring back the Golden Age version. Barry Allen succeeded Jay Garrick in 1956’s Showcase #4, and he swiftly accumulated enemies like Captain Cold, Mirror Master, Gorilla Grodd, and Weather Wizard, most of whom also fight Wally West. Now one of the most powerful members of The Flash’s rogues gallery has been reimagined 64 years by going in a more heroic direction. It has been revealed that wally has been on a dark path lately using his newly-discovered Flashes of Insight. The current Scarlet Speedster putting people in prison before they’ve even committed a crime based solely on these brief visions of the future was enough to raise the Justice League’s concern. Additionally, it was discovered that Wally’s overuse of the Speed Force was resulting in wormholes opening up in his pain receptors. Yet, Wally refused to stop looking into the future and taking the same courses of action. (Related: F1 star Max Verstappen played 40 hours of GTA 6 before ...)

Industry observers note that left with no other choice, Green Arrow and other Justice Leaguers battled with The Flash, believing that he's a danger to both himself and others. However, the combined might of Green Arrow, Wonder Woman, Aquaman, Jon Kent, Martian Manhunter, and the holographic Red Tornado wasn’t enough to get the job done. Enter Professor Ala Kazam, a woman whose name sounds a lot like Abra Kadabra, the aforementioned powerful Flash villain.

It has been revealed that ala Kazam appeared in the final pages of The Flash #39 to immobilize the present superheroes, reconstruct the arrow Wally destroyed that would have drained him of his speed, and then stuck him with the projectile. The only things this woman revealed about herself are that she’s from the future and that she came back in time to end “Flash’s reign of terror.”.

It has been revealed that as if the name and hailing from the future wasn’t enough to cement a connection to Abra Kadarba, Ala Kazam also appears to have the same powers as him. Abra, and presumably Ala, comes from the 64th century, where science and technology has become so advanced that it looks like magic to people in the present day. Ala is using the same kind of wand as Abra, and it contains enough power to instantly paralyze some of the most powerful Justice League members.

Industry observers note that however, it turns out Ala Kazam apparently isn’t the hero she claims to be. The cover for The Flash #41 demonstrates Ala suspending a Flash gorilla in midair, and the solicitation teases her as “a threat that knows more about them than they do themselves.” Then it’s stated in The Flash #42 solicitation that “all that stands between Ala Kazam and global destruction is a completely depowered Wally West,” and that it’ll take “the full effort of the Flash Family and then some” to defeat her.

What’s unclear is if Ala Kazam has sinister intentions like Abra Kadabra so often does, or if she truly believes she’s doing the right thing, but is working off of incorrect information and/or assumptions. It also remains to be seen if Abra himself will appear in this arc. Although the Absolute Universe’s version of Abra Kadabra was introduced a few months ago, the mainstream DC Universe’s Abra hasn’t been seen since roughly midway through Simon Spurrier’s run on The Flash.

The Flash #39 is now available from DC Comics.

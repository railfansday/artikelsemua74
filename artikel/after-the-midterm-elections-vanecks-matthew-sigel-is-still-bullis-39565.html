In a recent development, it's easy to be bearish on Bitcoin (BTC +3.05%) right now. It's at the moment trading around $60,000. That's more than 50% below its all-time high of $126,000 from last October.

In a recent development, but I'm not giving up on Bitcoin quite yet. According to Matthew Sigel, head of digital assets research at investment firm VanEck, there are actually several reasons to be bullish on Bitcoin right now.

As Sigel points out, Bitcoin is a highly cyclical asset, typically trading in four-year boom-and-bust cycles. There are three up years, followed by one down year. And it's now been 12 months since Bitcoin began the "down" leg of its current cycle.

Recent findings suggest that so if history is any guide, a rebound may already be underway. In fact, Sigel thinks Bitcoin likely bottomed this summer, when it briefly dipped below $60,000.

In a recent development, given that Bitcoin trades in four-year cycles, it's helpful to look back at 2022. Back then, Bitcoin actually had a setup similar to what it has now. Midterm elections were over, Bitcoin was collapsing in price, crypto sentiment was at an all-time low, and the FTX crypto exchange had just collapsed. Just swap out FTX for Binance (BNB +1.40%), and the setup looks eerily similar in 2026.

In a recent development, in 2022, Bitcoin continued to fall, and eventually hit a cyclical low of $16,000 in December. But from there, the rest is history. Bitcoin rebounded with returns of 155% in 2023 and 121% in 2024. Admittedly, past performance is no guarantee of future performance, but this type of resilience should be reassuring to investors.

So how does Bitcoin regain its mojo? Sigel points to two key catalysts for Bitcoin: institutional adoption and regulatory certainty.

Wall Street banks and financial institutions are embracing Bitcoin. Digital asset treasury companies are still adding Bitcoin to their balance sheets. The U.S. government continues to make noise about putting the Strategic Bitcoin Reserve to work.

Recent findings suggest that meanwhile, government regulators have signed off on Bitcoin as a digital asset, even giving the green light to Bitcoin perpetual futures trading earlier this year. While there may be significant regulatory uncertainty around stablecoins and asset tokenization, such uncertainty simply does not exist for Bitcoin. If history is any guide, Bitcoin will hold the line here and surge higher, just as it has in past cycles. Bitcoin may not reach $100,000 this year, but it's increasingly likely it will reclaim that level by the end of 2027.

In a recent development, A Nov. 23 research paper examining 34.8 million US job advertisements across 1,784 publicly listed companies found a widening gap in recruitment demand between experienced and less-experienced professionals after the release of Anthropic's Claude Code 2.0 in September 2025. The researchers found that a one-standard-deviation increase in estimated exposure to AI agents was associated with 4.54% lower job posting growth for non-senior positions over the six months following the release.

It has been revealed that the disparity became more pronounced when the researchers examined recruitment based on experience requirements. Positions requiring up to five years of professional experience recorded 4.87% lower posting growth associated with the same increase in AI exposure. The study compared how estimated AI agent exposure related to ad growth by seniority and experience at sampled US listed firms.

Higher estimated exposure was linked to slower posting growth in non-senior titles and in ads asking for up to five years of experience.

In a recent development, the results concern posting growth at sampled listed firms. Exposure is estimated from stock returns and aggregate OpenRouter activity. The senior-title and six-plus-years estimates were statistically inconclusive. The analysis leaves causal effects and actual hires unresolved.

Meanwhile, the researchers found no statistically significant relationship between AI exposure and posting growth for senior positions or roles requiring at least six years of experience.

The difference between the two groups was statistically significant, suggesting that emerging AI capabilities are affecting recruitment priorities unevenly across experience levels.

Economists Nicola Borri, Anton Cheremukhin, Yukun Liu and Aleh Tsyvinski used Anthropic's Sept. 29, 2025, Claude Code upgrade as a dividing point in their analysis. The upgrade expanded Claude's ability to work autonomously, letting it take on more complex coding tasks with less human intervention.

To estimate corporate exposure to these emerging capabilities, the researchers examined how closely companies' stock returns tracked growth in AI agent usage on OpenRouter, a platform providing access to multiple AI models.

Companies with higher exposure scores were subsequently more likely to discuss AI agents in regulatory filings and earnings calls.

The researchers found no statistically significant relationship between estimated AI exposure and job posting growth before the Claude Code upgrade. The divergence emerged in recruitment data covering October 2025 through March 2026.

The findings raise longer-term questions about how companies will develop their future workforce as AI agents become capable of performing tasks traditionally assigned to less-experienced employees.

Junior positions have historically provided workers with opportunities to develop the technical expertise, institutional knowledge, and professional judgment required for senior roles.

If companies increasingly automate that work while still demanding experienced professionals, workers entering the labor market could face fewer opportunities to gain the qualifications employers continue to seek.

That could eventually create a recruitment problem for companies themselves as they compete for experienced workers while narrowing the career pathways that produce them.

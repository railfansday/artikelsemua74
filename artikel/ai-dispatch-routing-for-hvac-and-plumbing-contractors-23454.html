Industry observers note that deploying AI dispatch routing for HVAC and plumbing contractors is frequently pitched as the fastest path to eliminating windshield time and maximizing daily billable technician hours. Commercial field service management platforms claim their algorithms can automate the dispatch whiteboard, recalculating routes dynamically to squeeze two extra service calls into every technician's schedule. Yet, mechanical contractors who flip on default algorithmic dispatch frequently watch their average ticket revenue decline, callback rates spike, and their best senior service technicians threaten to quit.

The failure of algorithmic dispatch in the mechanical trades is not caused by mathematical flaws in routing engines. It is caused by an objective function mismatch.

In a recent development, standard map-routing algorithms treat technicians as interchangeable vehicles carrying uniform packages. In residential and commercial mechanical service, technicians are revenue producers with vastly different diagnostic skill sets, sales closing rates, and specialized toolkits. Routing purely for geographical proximity sends the closest technician rather than the most profitable one.

However, treating residential and commercial service as a pure shortest-distance problem erodes speed-to-lead conversion and job profitability.

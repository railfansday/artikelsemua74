AI infrastructure stocks continue their slide, but Anthropic closes higher

Investors continued to offload artificial intelligence infrastructure stocks today, but Anthropic PBC recouped some of its losses from earlier this week. (Related: Artificial neurons successfully communicate with living...)

The AI selloff reportedly began in August. It accelerated on Nov. 17, the day Nvidia Corp. CEO Jensen Huang resigned over his affair with a former executive of Megaspeed, a cloud provider under investigation over alleged export control violations. Huang returned to the helm of the chipmaker this past Monday. (Related: Latest Update: Trump pushes Huang’s reinstatement despi...)

According to recent reports, nvidia closed at $173.73 the day before Huang’s resignation. It dropped as low as $125 during the executive’s two-week hiatus and surged 30% on Monday, the day he returned. Nvidia lost some of its recent gains in today’s trading session: its share price closed 3.8% lower.

It has been revealed that other AI chip stocks have also taken a hit. Broadcom Inc. closed a small fraction of a percent lower than yesterday, while Advanced Micro Devices Inc. and Intel Corp. fell 1.56% and 0.57%, respectively.

The semiconductor stock that logged the biggest drop today is Ambarella Inc., a relatively low-profile maker of AI image analysis chips. Its accelerators are used by edge devices to process footage from their cameras. Ambarella dropped 19.77% today after closing 19% lower yesterday.

The stock’s decline was driven not so much by the broader AI selloff, but rather the weak earnings that Ambarella posted on Tuesday. The company missed expectations across the board. Ambarella remarked that some customers had canceled or delayed orders to mitigate the financial impact of soaring memory prices.

Micron Inc., one of the world’s top memory chip suppliers, saw its market capitalization drop more than 4.5% today. That’s despite CEO Sanjay Mehrotra’s recent disclosure that the firm has already sold out 75% of its 2027 manufacturing capacity. According to the executive, the gap between memory demand and supply is only set to increase next year.

Sources indicate that companies downstream from chipmakers in the AI data center supply chain fared better. Hewlett Packard Enterprise Co. ended the trading session 1.75% higher after dipping into the red earlier today, while Dell Technologies Inc. gained 0.64%.

According to recent reports, in contrast, the major cloud providers weren’t left out of today’s selloff. Amazon.com Inc., the cloud market’s largest player, closed 3.12% lower. Microsoft Corp. and Google LLC dropped 1.25% and 2.02%, respectively. Oracle Corp., which trails the three companies in market share but has emerged as an important AI infrastructure provider, took the biggest hit: it’s down 5.09% from yesterday.

It has been revealed that anthropic ended the trading session with a 1.08% gain. The firm thereby recouped some of yesterday’s losses, which pushed its stock below its $80 debut price. Anthropic went public last week through a record-setting offering that raised more than $93 billion.

Overall, the tech-heavy Nasdaq gained 0.61% while the S&P 500 slid 0.25%. The Dow Jones Industrial Average climbed 0.33%.

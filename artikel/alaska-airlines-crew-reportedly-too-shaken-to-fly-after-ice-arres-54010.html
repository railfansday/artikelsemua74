Alaska Airlines Crew Reportedly Too Shaken To Fly After ICE Arrests On Seattle Jet Bridge

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that an Alaska Airlines pilot reportedly walked away from his aircraft during boarding on Saturday morning after US Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) officers arrested two passengers on a Seattle jet bridge. Two flight attendants were also noted to be so overwhelmed by the incident that they asked to be replaced.

Sources indicate that the arrests took place at Seattle-Tacoma International Airport (SEA) as passengers boarded Alaska flight AS1306 to Los Angeles, which ended up being delayed by more than three hours. Video footage of the incident, which has sparked widespread outrage, reveals masked officers struggling with travelers near the aircraft door.

The incident occurred on Saturday morning as Alaska passengers were boarding a Boeing 737-800 bound for Los Angeles International Airport (LAX). Speaking to local radio station KUOW, passenger Aidan Mendoza remarked masked ICE agents struggled with a man and a woman on the jet bridge at Gate N9, leaving around 50 passengers pressed against the jet bridge wall.

Amid the chaos, Mendoza stated an Alaska pilot walked back up the jet bridge looking uncomfortable and unsure of what to do, adding that "he didn't come back." KUOW also reports that two Alaska flight attendants were so overwhelmed that they asked to be "subbed out." Mendoza decided not to board the flight, saying a gate agent removed him from the passenger list before customer service refunded his ticket.

In a statement to Simple Flying, an Alaska spokesperson did not confirm whether any crew members had to be replaced, but noted it was "not provided advance notice of the enforcement action" and is "working to understand the circumstances surrounding what occurred." "We are concerned by this morning’s law enforcement action during the boarding of Alaska Flight 1306. We recognize how distressing this incident has been for employees and guests.".

It has been revealed that in a statement, ICE stated it had rolled out an operation to arrest Ricardo Larrondo Mendoza (who is unrelated to passenger Aidan Mendoza), a Mexican national who it said had overstayed his visa. The agency claimed other travelers "began to obstruct and assault" officers during the arrest, while a second passenger, Sofia Hernandez Mejia, "hurled hot coffee on the officers." ICE said Hernandez Mejia, also from Mexico, had overstayed her visa and would be charged with assaulting a federal officer.

The Port of Seattle, which operates the airport, said it was "outraged" by the incident, emphasizing that immigration enforcement at SEA takes place without its involvement or knowledge. According to the Port, local federal immigration authorities had previously agreed to protocols at the airport, and it is now investigating whether any of those agreements were breached.

Saturday's arrests drew strong criticism from Washington officials, including Governor Bob Ferguson, who noted ICE was "completely and totally out of control." Ferguson added that he was looking into how the agents accessed the jet bridge and whether they violated a state law prohibiting law enforcement officers, including federal immigration agents, from wearing face coverings.

Recent findings suggest that ICE has stepped up arrests at US airports since the summer to apprehend travelers overstaying visas. Other notable cases include a woman from Ecuador escorted off a jet bridge in Denver in July, and a Southwest Airlines flight attendant taken into custody while on the job at Nashville International Airport (BNA). Sources indicate that airlines have little control over immigration enforcement at the gate. Airlines for America (A4A), which represents the largest US carriers, said in August that it was holding discussions with ICE and TSA to ensure proper procedures were in place when law enforcement activity takes place at airports.

In a recent development, airline unions have been more outspoken, such as the Association of Professional Flight Attendants (APFA), which represents more than 26,000 American Airlines flight attendants. APFA recently remarked it was "deeply alarmed" by ICE's escalating tactics and warned it could put passengers and crew in danger.

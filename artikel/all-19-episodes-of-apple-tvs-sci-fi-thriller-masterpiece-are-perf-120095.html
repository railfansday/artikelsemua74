📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The opening hook can make or break a sci-fi series. If a sci-fi show does not introduce a compelling concept in its opening episodes, it is almost destined to fail. However, a gripping opening hook is not enough to keep viewers invested in the long run. Even the most promising concepts can lose their appeal if they are not executed as brilliantly as the ones in an Apple TV sci-fi masterpiece.

In a recent development, created by Dan Erickson, Apple TV's Severance, too, introduces a fascinating concept in its opening chapters. Humanity invents a chip that, once installed in the brain, can split one's consciousness into two. A giant conglomerate, Lumon, uses this technology to create a workforce whose employees have no recollection of their lives outside the office.

It has been revealed that this early hook not only feels high-concept but also serves as the perfect allegory for exploitative corporate jobs in the real world. The Apple TV sci-fi show's appeal also lies in its liminal imagery that makes incredible use of space to instill unease in viewers. However, what begins as a retrofuturistic critique of corporate culture gradually turns into a masterful exploration of power and identity.

The consciousness of the severed Lumon employee is split into two different personalities in Severance. While the ones working inside Lumon are referred to as "Innies," the ones outside are called "Outies." The show clearly establishes that the Innies and Outies are separate people in themselves. No overlaps can exist between their memories unless someone finds loopholes. (Related: How to generate text: using different decoding methods ...)

To keep viewers immersed in both the Innie and Outie worlds, Severance gives immersive and relatable arcs to both counterparts of the same characters. Even the cinematography in both worlds has its own appeal. While the Innie world is cold, liminal, confined, and clinical, the outside world is intentionally covered in shades of grey and shot with handheld cameras.

In a recent development, from the very beginning, a clear point and process of transition is also established between the Innies and Outies in Severance. Using everything from sound cues to facial transitions, the Apple TV series keeps establishing how one character switches between their two alternate states. Once these rules are out of the way, the show gives personal stakes to every main character's Innie and Outie versions. (Related: How to train a newly introduced language model from scr...)

Severance's biggest strength is that its entire sci-fi lore is not confined to the main titular concept. As it progresses, it introduces more ingenious ways in which its characters can hack into their own brains and break the rules of the overarching technology through re-integration and "Overtime Contingency" protocols.

With these brilliantly set-up rules and concepts, Severance gradually paves the way for mind-bending payoffs that stay with you long after the credits stop rolling.

The core concept that drives Severance's drama is genius in itself. However, the show's true appeal lies in how it treats its characters. As a viewer, you initially find yourself rooting for the Innies because the show gradually develops them while revealing little about the Outies. However, just when a viewer starts seeing the Innies as heroes, the Outies' struggles and vulnerabilities are put on full display.

In a recent development, while ensuring that it keeps dropping newly introduced revelations about its central technology, Severance never rushes through its character beats. Owing to this, some of its biggest mind-bending end-season payoffs feel even more hard-hitting. In its 19-episode runtime, so far, Severance has had no dull moments or filler story arcs.

Every development in the Apple TV sci-fi series so far has added more heft to the overarching story. Its drama is further elevated by its award-winning performances, high production value, and Ben Stiller's direction. Hopefully, Severance will maintain the same momentum in season 3 as well.

A psychological thriller series featuring Adam Scott as Mark Scout, an employee at Lumon Industries who undergoes a "severance" procedure to separate work from his personal memories. However, as his work and life personas mysteriously begin to collide, it promptly becomes clear that not all is as it seems.

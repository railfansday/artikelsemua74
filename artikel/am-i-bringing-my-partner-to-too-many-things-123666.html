Am I Bringing My Partner to Too Many Things?

Ah, the holiday season: A time of good food, holiday decorations, friends and family, and—if you’re in a relationship—adding a plus-one to all of your holiday party invitations.

It has been revealed that this year is the first time in a long time that I have a serious partner to bring along to “friendsgivings,” white elephant parties, and New Year’s Eve festivities. And while I’m so excited to bring the person I love around the other people I love (and be able to take cute holiday pictures), I’m a little weary of being that person that has to have their significant other by their side at all times. Of course, the holiday season is unique: the idea is to bring everyone together and to get to have newly introduced experiences with my partner. But by adding my boyfriend as a plus-one to all these events, am I setting a precedent that will translate into the rest of the new year? (Related: Building the next generation of multimodal AI experienc...)

“Just because you're in a relationship doesn't mean you're automatically a package deal everywhere you go,” professional matchmaker and dating expert Jill Dunn tells Cosmopolitan.

According to recent reports, so how do I find the right balance between including my partner in experiences I want us to have with each other... and maintaining my own independence when it comes to spending time with my friends?

I need to preface this by saying I love hanging out with my boyfriend—I wouldn’t be dating him if I didn’t. That being remarked, I also love hanging out with my friends. And while my partner gets along with all of my friends, I don’t think that means he automatically gets an invite whenever there’s an event or gathering.

Dunn agrees and states that it’s important to read the room—it really just depends on the situation.

“If your girlfriend invites you out with a few friends, that doesn't necessarily mean your boyfriend should be tagging along,” she indicates. “But if it's a major birthday celebration or a couples' dinner, that's a different story.”.

It has been revealed that and while you may fall into the trap of being afraid of being too intertwined with your partner, it’s okay to want to include them in things. “There's a difference between having your own life and keeping someone separate from your life,” Dunn indicates.

She explains that if you’ve been with your partner for a year, for example, and they have never met any close friends, hasn’t been invited to family celebration, and is consistently left out of crucial events, you may be sending the signal that they’re not actually a part of your life. And a partner should be part of your life.

When you’re in a relationship, you essentially are gradually becoming part of each other’s lives. And even though you want to have your own separate friends and relationships (which is completely healthy, by the way!) it shouldn’t feel like they’re part of totally different universes.

“You can have amazing chemistry, incredible dates, and a wonderful time together, but if you're not actually integrating into each other's worlds, what are you building toward?” Dunn asks. “You want to feel like you're part of someone's life, not just part of their weekend aims.”.

Weddings, holiday parties, or situations where other people’s partners will be present are the perfect time to bring your partner along (if you’ve been given a plus-one, of course).

Now, integrating your partner into your life in a real way doesn’t mean you two have to be attached at the hip no matter the circumstances.

According to recent reports, listen, I get it. Love is so much fun. And when you’re really into someone, you want to spend as much time as possible with them. But that relationship shouldn’t overtake every other aspect of your life.

“I don't think it's healthy for your entire world to revolve around one person,” Dunn notes.

Sources indicate that if you’re seeing some friends after some time apart, going to an event where no other S.O. is present, or just need a GNO, it is totally fine (and preferable) to let your partner know that you’re going to just be with your friends. In fact, your partner should be hanging out with their friends too! This is especially important to maintain the friendships you already have.

“The dynamic changes when someone's significant other is always there,” Dunn says. “Maybe your girlfriend wants to talk about something personal. Maybe the group wants to reminisce or just have a girls' night. It's not that they don't like your partner. They just want time with you!”.

Plus, she explains that it’s not a good habit to expect your romantic partner to fulfill every single need we have. The reason we even have other friendships and relationships is because we need to be able to water different parts of ourselves. You can’t exactly do that if you never leave your house without a plus-one.

According to recent reports, and more than anything, it actually benefits your relationship to have a life outside of each other. You know how they say, “distance makes your heart grow fonder”? Well, they’re not wrong. By spending some time apart and living your own life, you become a more interesting, independent person.

“You don't have to share every single experience,” Dunn says. “Sometimes it's nice to go out, have your own conversations, enjoy your friends, and come home with something newly introduced to talk about.”.

So while there are certain holiday parties I’m excited to bring my boyfriend to, I’m also excited to continue certain seasonal traditions with just my friends. As for the rest of the year, I look forward to learning the balance that works just for us.

American Airlines’ Lag Linked To Flyers Unphased By Delta & United’s High Fares, Analyst Indicates

It has been revealed that american Airlines is struggling to match the financial performance of its two biggest domestic rivals, and passengers' willingness to pay higher fares to fly with Delta Air Lines and United Airlines may be a significant contributing factor. According to Dan McKenzie, an analyst at Seaport Research Partners, both carriers command considerably higher fares than American on comparable flights, as reported by The Fresh York Times.

Recent findings suggest that mcKenzie estimates that American needs approximately $4.5 billion in additional annual revenue to match its competitors' profit margins. With surging jet fuel costs putting pressure on airline profitability, the ability to command higher fares without significantly compromising demand has become increasingly important. Industry observers note that mcKenzie's analysis found that Delta commands fares that are 12% above the industry average on similar flights, compared with 9% at United and just 3% at American. To illustrate, on a flight with an industry-average fare of $500, Delta could command $560 and United $545 without impacting demand, compared with $515 at American. Interestingly, American achieved the strongest passenger yield growth of the three carriers during the third quarter of 2026. However, an analysis of their respective financial results reveals that American also experienced the largest decline in load factor. Consequently, its passenger revenue per available seat mile (PRASM) increased more slowly than at either Delta or United.

Sources: Q3 2026 financial results from Delta Air Lines, United Airlines and American Airlines.

While American's faster capacity growth also contributed to its declining load factor, the results partly reflect a deliberate commercial strategy. The airline historically prioritized filling its aircraft, but is increasingly focused on improving yields. Speaking during American's third-quarter earnings call, Chief Commercial Officer Nat Pieper explained the trade-off:.

"We have prioritized yield over load factor, proactively securing meaningfully higher yields in exchange for slightly lower loads.".

In a recent development, delta and United have spent years differentiating their onboard products, recognizing the growing demand for premium travel. That strategy is delivering substantial financial returns. In the third quarter, United's premium and loyalty revenues both increased by 17% year-over-year. Meanwhile, Delta's premium ticket revenue actually surpassed main cabin ticket revenue, with the latter accounting for less than 40% of its adjusted operating revenue.

Nowhere is this more evident than with corporate travelers, whose purchasing decisions are often influenced more by schedule convenience, operational reliability, and loyalty benefits than by fares. Delta topped the 2026 Business Travel News Airline Survey for the 16th consecutive year, outperforming its competitors in eight of the 11 categories assessed. Despite substantial improvements, American remained the lowest-ranked qualifying airline.

According to recent reports, network composition is another important differentiator. Delta and United have greater exposure to affluent coastal markets, with both offering nearly twice as many departing seats from the most affluent, New York, as American. This also underlines the commercial significance of American's intensifying battle with United at Chicago O'Hare, where greater frequencies and connectivity could help it secure more lucrative corporate travel contracts.

American is pursuing a comprehensive transformation of its passenger experience, reversing several previous management decisions. These include the reintroduction of narrowbody seatback screens and a substantial expansion of premium seating. However, several of its most significant product enhancements will take years to implement across the fleet, delaying their full commercial impact.

Recent findings suggest that there are encouraging signs that American's strategy is gaining traction. Its premium revenue increased 22.9% year-on-year in the third quarter, while managed corporate revenue increased 27.6%, marking its sixth consecutive quarter of double-digit growth. American's corporate travel satisfaction score has also improved this year, suggesting its efforts to rebuild relationships are paying dividends.

Industry observers note that however, American is pursuing a moving target. While it prepares to begin installing Starlink in 2027, United already offers the service on more than half of its daily flights. And while Delta offers its personalized Delta Sync seatback entertainment experience on more than 900 aircraft, American will only begin its seatback screen rollout from 2028. So with its rivals continuing to reinvest their profits in product differentiation, American's ultimate challenge will be persuading passengers that its flights are equally worth paying more for.

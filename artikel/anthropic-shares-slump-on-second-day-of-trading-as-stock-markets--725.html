Anthropic shares slump on second day of trading as stock markets reel from AI sell-off

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Asian markets record their worst week since 2020 as investors question huge sums being poured into AI build-out.

Anthropic’s shares slumped at the start of trading on Friday, as its record-breaking IPO failed to stem a sell-off in AI-linked companies.

Shares in the frontier AI manufacturer, which floated on Thursday, fell by over 9% at the start of trading on Friday. They dropped to $88.17, towards the $83 level at which the company sold shares in its IPO this week.

Anthropic’s first two days as a listed company have been volatile. Its shares jumped by 17% on Thursday, after briefly surging by almost 40% when trading in the frontier AI firm began.

Recent findings suggest that global markets slumped on Friday too; Asian stock markets recorded their worst week since 2020, as shares in big chip companies fell. In Taiwan, stocks dropped 6.7% to hit their lowest point since mid-April, with the chip maker TSMC down about 6%.

The scale of the decline in Taiwan prompted the chair of its stock exchange to pledge to ensure the stable operation ⁠of capital markets, and reassure investors they should remain confident in the fundamentals of Taiwanese companies. Industry observers note that in Japan, the Nikkei index fell 5%, while South Korea’s tech-heavy Kospi index lost 5.5%. Shares in the tech investor SoftBank had their worst day in more than two years, down 14.5% in Tokyo, amid worries around its aggressive bets on AI. According to recent reports, european stock markets were heading for their worst week since March, with the pan-European Stoxx 600 dropping 1.7%. London’s blue-chip stock index hit a five-month low.

The sell-off followed losses on Wall Street on Thursday, where the main indices all fell by more than 2% on a day dominated by Anthropic’s IPO.

Chris Beauchamp, the chief market analyst at the investing and trading platform IG, mentioned Anthropic could be “draining market liquidity”, as investors sell shares in other companies to raise funds to pay for stock in the newly floated AI firm.

“The slide from yesterday’s highs sends a message that there might be more at work here than investors realise, and while brokers are scrambling to buy more, that liquidity has to come from somewhere, and the other tech names may be the first ones to feel the pressure,” Beauchamp said.

Anthropic’s flotation has failed to ease worries around the AI trade, as investors increasingly question whether companies can deliver the returns necessary to justify the billions of dollars being poured into the AI build out.

Daniela Hathorn, a senior market analyst at Capital.com, said: “The IPO demonstrated that demand for AI exposure remains strong, but it has not been enough to overcome concerns about elevated valuations, profitability and the sustainability of investment spending.

According to recent reports, “The broader market mood has shifted from buying the dip towards questioning whether the correction has further to run. The weakness in equal-weighted indices suggests that selling pressure is no longer confined to a handful of technology giants, while Europe’s recent outperformance has also faded.”.

Nvidia shares fell by 3%, after California served the chip company with a subpoena as part of an investigation into whether its advanced semiconductors ended up in China.

Britain’s FTSE 100 share index has dropped by 4.8% this week, and is heading for its biggest weekly fall since the start of the Iran war.

Fears that US central bankers may raise interest rates to fight inflation have lifted the dollar to a 19-month high, and pushed down the gold price – with gold on track for its worst week since March.

The Liverpool fans did not miss a beat. They never do. “Poch is at the wheel,” they goaded as this calamity unfolded. But at the wheel of what, a clown car? The look of thunder on Mauricio Pochettino’s face said it all as he got off to the worst start of any Manchester United manager – or head coach – in 100 years.

The Pochettino bandwagon rolled into town with the usual fanfare of a newly introduced era, new belief, new hope; and then the wheels came off, the doors collapsed and the engine exploded. The opening quarter of an hour really was that farcical.

In that time, away to Liverpool of all places, they were 2-0 down, a player down – and down and out. If Pochettino had any uncertainty over the scale of the task ahead of him, there it was laid bare. Any regrets? Whatever the salary he could be forgiven for having a few as he watched from the touchline. (Related: DNA robots could deliver drugs and hunt viruses inside ...)

Maybe Pochettino, too, can take some responsibility. Even this early on. Was it wise to drop Harry Maguire and hand Matthijs de Ligt his first start in 357 days? Not from the way De Ligt was at fault for the first and, arguably, the second goal. Did the head coach start with a back-three or a back-four? The players seemed confused. It eventually ended up as a back-five. (Related: Your “um” and pauses could reveal early dementia risk...)

But this was a collective failing for United. From top – with director of football Jason Wilcox caught on camera with his head buried in his coat (he really needs to work on that poker face) – through the club and out on the pitch. Pochettino is substantial on building a culture. He has to abolish the calamity and the comedy.

In a recent development, the work needed at United is monumental as the inexplicable strategy of rebuilding the attack last year, the midfield in the summer and the defence next year was once again exposed. Whoever did that as a way of building a successful team? Instead United have conceded 34 goals already this season, the most in the top five European leagues.

The second goal? It highlighted that mismatch as Rio Ngumoha easily beat Diogo Dalot, not for the first and not for the last time, and pulled back a cross that Florian Wirtz hooked in off the crossbar. De Ligt was, quite frankly, all over the place as he again watched on.

In a recent development, it can be argued that, after that disastrous start, Pochettino stemmed the bleeding just as it appeared set to be a bloodbath. But that may also have owed much to a lack of killer instinct from Liverpool, who did not go for the jugular. Still they scored four. It could – should – have been a repeat of the 7-0 from three years ago.

Into the second-half and the chant from the Kop was clear. “Attack, attack, attack,” rang out as they demanded more goals; more humiliation. The wastefulness was highlighted by Bradley Barcola running the ball out of play when through and Isak slicing a shot with only Senne Lammens to beat.

A third goal did come with Barcola easily beating Lisandro Martínez, using his pace down the right, before crossing low for the outstanding Ngumoha to sweep home. “Going down, going down,” sang the Liverpool fans before adding: “You’re not famous anymore”. Not famous but certainly infamous.

According to recent reports, anya Taylor-Joy’s film debut in Robert Eggers’ The Witch may have given the actor a cult following, but it was her role in M. Night Shyamalan’s Split in 2016 that made her a household name. A year after she shocked horror fans as Thomasin, she took on an incredibly different role in the thriller. A return to form for Shyamalan, Split takes on the complex glitch of DID. Dissociative Identity Disorder is at the crux of the movie as James McAvoy takes on 24 separate identities in his role of Kevin.

In a recent development, viewers only get to see some of them, but his immense talent in showing just a few is one of the best examples of character work. Even more impressive is Taylor-Joy, who stands toe to toe with the performer in her role as Casey Cooke. One day when Casey is out at a birthday party, she and acquaintances Marcia (Jessica Sula) and Claire (Haley Lu Richardson) are abducted by some of Kevin’s most insidious personalities. In the locked room drama, Casey weaponizes her wits and her own experience with trauma to escape her captivity and save her life.

M. Night Shyamalan features have typically been divisive in recent years, and Split is no different. In the movie, the director posits that DID can present itself not just in personality changes, but with physical characteristics as well. One of the most notorious of these personalities, the Beast, has more muscle definition and becomes literally bigger than the other personalities. This may be controversial with the mental health community, but there is no denying that this offers a fascinating new perspective on the subject of trauma. Kevin materialized these personalities as a response to his overbearing and abusive mother. His foil in the film is Casey, played by Anya Taylor-Joy. Immediately, it is clear that Casey has a superpower that the other abductees do not. Slowly throughout the film, Casey’s own abuse and trauma are revealed, which allows her to empathize with Kevin. While Marcia and Claire want to break their way out by force, Casey understands that the best way to escape is through intelligence and subtlety.

Recent findings suggest that this is a difficult balance to maintain, but Taylor-Joy does it almost effortlessly. She establishes this in the abduction scene as it slowly dawns on Casey that there is no escape. When Kevin’s personality, Dennis, enters the girls’ car, he subdues the other two easily in the backseat. Casey, who is in the front, immediately understands the danger in this encounter. As a single tear rolls down her face, she knows that there is no point in fighting and is taken as well. (Related: Deploy Hugging Face models easily with Amazon SageMaker...)

In a recent development, what could be considered weakness or a fault in her character is exactly the opposite. Casey knows that she is a survivor. She has endured one monster before and will find a way to survive this one as well. Casey is characterized by her creativity in finding a way out of horrifying situations, which aids her until the very end of the movie. Just like in The Witch, Taylor-Joy accepts the task of a difficult character that could be misunderstood and instead makes it iconic. In the decade since Split first premiered, no other character has quite reached the heights of this one.

Archer from hackajob confirmed a $10 million fundraise on November 20, 2026, led by Volition Capital with support from AVP, to expand AI products for job candidates and recruiting teams.

Sources indicate that the manufacturer is responding to a hiring market affected by AI-generated applications, fraudulent candidates and demand for niche skills. Its argument is that one-sided tools encourage candidates to submit mass applications and recruiters to send mass messages, leaving both sides with few genuinely latest connections.

In a recent development, archer’s product documentation describes a two-sided matching process. Candidates set out the work, pay, location and conditions they want, then receive a shortlist of suitable roles with an explanation of each match. Recruiters specify the requirements for a role and receive qualified candidates in their applicant tracking systems, alongside context that a CV does not contain.

Industry observers note that the tech giant reports that more than 700,000 candidates signed up to work with Archer in the 12 months before the announcement, more than hackajob’s combined sign-ups during the previous five years. Archer was making 10,000 introductions a week. About half involved people its customers had not previously seen, and introduced candidates were five times more likely to convert to an interview than each customer’s own baseline. In a recent development, archer also reports that annual recurring revenue grew fivefold in 2026, while customer cost per hire was 67% lower than hiring through staffing agencies. Google, Sierra and Barclays are among the companies whose talent-acquisition teams use the system. Archer charges per role rather than per hire, so the cost per hire falls as more hires are generated from a role. (Related: Breaking: Porting fairseq wmt19 translation system to t...)

Industry observers note that the business is also moving beyond hackajob’s original technology-recruitment focus after a decade in that market. Archer now supports enterprise sales and front-office finance roles, as well as positions connected to data-center build-outs.

It has been revealed that recruiters calibrate each role in plain English, supplying the job description, essential requirements, dealbreakers and broader criteria for success. Archer first filters on right to work, salary, location and required office days, then grades candidates against that brief. It confirms a person’s interest, location and pay before making an introduction.

Recent findings suggest that the company says its own network contains 1.5 million vetted, identity-verified candidates, while a broader headhunting pool covers 25 million professionals in the US and UK. Archer Approves checks that each person has one profile, examines behavioral signals and government identification, and has blocked more than 30,000 fake candidates.

It has been revealed that for employers, a read-only job feed goes into Archer, and candidates arrive through the existing applicant-tracking-system application flow, tagged as Archer. The firm states this avoids algorithmic hiring decisions because the candidate chooses to apply; recruiters take over at the screening stage and make every hiring decision.

In a recent development, the firm looks to direct the funding to both sides of the marketplace. A candidate-agent launch is scheduled for January, while Archer OS, the product for talent-acquisition teams, is due for general release in the first quarter of 2027. Archer is also planned to work inside Slack, Teams and the AI tools recruiters already use, extending sourcing and qualification into existing workflows.

The immediate test for the newly introduced capital is whether Archer can turn that candidate growth into broader adoption of its two-sided service, while expanding beyond technical recruitment and preserving recruiter control over hiring decisions.

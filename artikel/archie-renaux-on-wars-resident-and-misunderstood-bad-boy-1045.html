Recent findings suggest that archie Renaux is hesitant to call his character in War a villain. Johnny, the ambitious (often to a fault) junior lawyer who plays a double-agent litigator in the slick divorce drama, is often the one called upon to do the dirty work: the double-crossing, the manipulating of both witnesses and plaintiffs, and then, of course, the whole business of being a mole. “He does awful things, but he’s a puppet more so than anything else,” claims Renaux. “He’s been taken advantage of, really.”.

The series, from George Kay (Lupin), follows two rival London firms — the white-shoe Cathcarts and the newer, scrappier Taylor & Byrne — as they represent a tech mogul (played by Dominic West) and an actress (Sienna Miller) at the tumultuous end of their marriage. Renaux’s Johnny ostensibly works for Taylor & Byrne, but has been promised an eventual job at Cathcarts in exchange for double-crossing information about the high-profile case. Throughout the season, we see him cheating on his girlfriend with Miller’s Carla and framing a fellow junior lawyer after he’s almost caught with compromising inside intel. The actor, who previously starred on Netflix’s Shadow and Bone, likens the behavior to what happens in the third act of The Drama. Recent findings suggest that “There’s that great bit where Robert Pattinson has a kiss with someone, and it comes out of nowhere,” he explains. “And it’s like, what are you doing mate? Why are you making these decisions? But he’s just manic, and I was watching it in the cinema going, oh, that’s what Johnny’s like. He’s manic.”.

According to recent reports, in the series finale, Johnny redeems himself — at least as far as his interference with the court case is concerned, but his personal life is seemingly left in shambles. Below, Renaux talks about his take on the episode, what he knows about the show’s pending second season, and what it was like to act alongside Sienna Miller. What do you remember about the audition process for the show?

I was on another job and we were doing night shoots in Canada, and I got an email about the audition but it was so hectic that I didn’t feel like I could afford to give my attention to anything else. This happened on Shadow and Bone, too, where my agents really pushed me to audition because they remarked it was really good. They were like, you’ve got to read this. The audition scene was when Johnny’s getting interviewed by Cathcarts, so it was a lengthy paragraph of dialogue I had to learn. And I had an offer for another show at the time, and I couldn’t keep that project waiting too long if I wasn’t going to take it, so I got the offer for War very quickly. How did you make the decision that you wanted to hold out for the role in War and not the other offer?

It was the character. Johnny’s the audience’s way in. You’re really rooting for him at the beginning, or at least I hope so, and then people are quite disappointed by what he does. But that’s great. It’s complex and divisive. When I was reading the script, I was going, oh God, why’s he done this and that?

Everyone on this show is badly behaved to a degree, but he’s often the person that you feel like could be making better choices….

Recent findings suggest that the other people are smooth-talking, very well put-together, calculated villains. The typical sort. Johnny is not that. He’s done some mad things, the cheating, and he’s a mess. But he’s in a state of constant stress and people do weird stuff when they’re in shock.

Some of what he does feels calculated — but calculated incorrectly — but do you think his affair with Carla was planned or did he get swept up in her beauty and fame?

It has been revealed that the way I saw it at the start was, OK, he’s playing the game. If he gets close to the client, and the client’s able to give him information, that’s great. Obviously he’s attracted to her, and he plays along and flirts, and then he enjoys the flirting. When it’s reciprocated, I think it takes him by surprise and he gets caught up in the moment.

Where do you think the desperation comes from?

I wonder if he would have done any of it had he not got fired. Or had Angus not provoked him at the restaurant at the beginning and made him feel insecure about his job. That ignites a sense of, I’m going to succeed by any means necessary. Being around people that have money and massive careers is very humiliating, and I think that restaurant scene is something of a villain origin story. I know he does punch him, but I imagine everyone thinks, OK, that’s fair.

In the final episode, when he convinces Flora to come take the stand and to read the letter to Carla, is that him being sneaky again to try and win the case to get himself out of a jam? Or is he trying to redeem himself on a deeper level?

Recent findings suggest that I think it was trying to fix the mess he’d made. I think he’s a good person. I know he is. He knows that’s the least he could have done. He lost everything trying to get what he wanted and going about it in the wrong way. He’s got some sneaky skills and this is him putting them to good use. I think he is a good lawyer, and you only get a glimpse of that. His real personality is when he’s working at the restaurant at the beginning of the season. When he’s interviewing at the law firms, it’s a performance he’s putting on and he’s overcompensating for his education and background.

What did they tell you about intends for future seasons of the show?

Recent findings suggest that well they’ve got intends for multiple seasons, and it’s sort of an anthology series following the two law firms over different cases. So I knew there was a possibility for doing more seasons. I don’t want to say much more right now.

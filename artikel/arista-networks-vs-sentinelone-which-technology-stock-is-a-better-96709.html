Should you prioritize the hardware infrastructure that powers the cloud or the software that protects it? Choosing between Arista Networks (ANET -5.47%) and SentinelOne (S -2.00%) requires balancing established profitability with high-growth potential. Arista designs the high-speed networking equipment essential for modern data centers, while SentinelOne provides an autonomous security platform. Both companies are central to the expansion of artificial intelligence, making them notable contenders for investors who want exposure to the future of computing.

It has been revealed that arista Networks designs and sells cloud networking hardware and software for massive data centers, campus, and routing environments. With over 10,000 global customers, it serves cloud titans and specialized providers, including a strategic reliance on Broadcom (AVGO -3.98%) for merchant silicon. Customer concentration adds a layer of risk to the business, as sales to two specific end customers represented nearly 16% and 26% of total revenue in 2025.

Sources indicate that in the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached close to $11.9 billion, representing growth of approximately 40.7% compared to the prior year. This expansion was accompanied by a net income of nearly $4.8 billion and a net margin of 40%. The company maintains a strong competitive position among tech stocks by providing the plumbing that powers the world's largest internet companies.

It has been revealed that as of its December 2025 balance sheet, the current ratio is roughly 3.0x, which measures a company's ability to cover short-term debts with short-term assets. The debt-to-equity ratio is approximately 0.0x, indicating a very low level of total debt relative to shareholder equity. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was nearly $4.3 billion, which is the cash remaining after paying for operations and equipment.

Recent findings suggest that sentinelOne provides AI-powered cybersecurity through its Singularity Platform, which detects and responds to attacks across endpoints, cloud workloads, and containers. The company targets organizations of all sizes and works with strategic partners such as N-able (NABL -0.99%) and Cloudflare (NET -1.32%) to reach its customers. Much of its growth is driven by its multi-tenant model, which allows security providers to manage multiple distinct clients through a single interface.

In the 12 months ended July 31, 2026, revenue reached nearly $1.1 billion, representing growth of nearly 21.1%. Despite this top-line expansion, the company reported a net loss of approximately $340.1 million and a negative net margin of 31%. This reflects the early stage of its growth cycle as it prioritizes market share and platform development over immediate bottom-line profits.

It has been revealed that based on its January 2026 balance sheet, a current ratio of 1.4x indicates the manufacturer has sufficient current assets to cover its short-term liabilities. The debt-to-equity ratio is close to 0.0x. In the fiscal year ended Jan. 31, 2026, free cash flow was nearly $75.9 million, though stock-based compensation represented roughly 388.4% of operating cash flow, meaning reported cash generation is heavily inflated by this non-cash add-back.

Arista Networks faces significant risks from high revenue concentration among a small number of massive cloud customers. The business relies heavily on Broadcom for essential silicon chips, making it vulnerable to supply chain disruptions. Furthermore, intense competition from Extreme Networks (EXTR -4.97%) and other vendors makes long-term demand forecasting difficult given the rapid evolution of artificial intelligence.

SentinelOne carries risks related to its history of net losses and accumulated deficits. The manufacturer competes in a crowded cybersecurity market against larger competitors and depends on third-party cloud hosts like Amazon (AMZN -1.21%) to run its services. Additionally, managing rapid growth and navigating the evolving regulatory landscape for artificial intelligence presents ongoing operational challenges for the software provider.

Recent findings suggest that sentinelOne is cheaper by its P/S ratio, measuring price against sales over the past twelve months. Arista Networks has a lower Forward P/E.

Valuation metrics include sourcing from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from other data providers.

Industry observers note that I would buy SentinelOne for 2027, and the case comes down to what has to go right. Arista's price-to-sales ratio already assumes the AI data center buildout continues at full speed, leaving little room for even a modest slowdown from its few giant customers. SentinelOne needs something more achievable: keep growing near 20%, keep widening its positive adjusted operating income, and keep shrinking stock pay as a share of revenue. The latest filing demonstrates all three moving in the right direction, and the firm has acquired AI security firms to sell into the same AI buildout that Arista supplies. The trade-offs are real. SentinelOne's forward earnings multiple is steep, GAAP profit is still a promise, and nearly all its sales run through resellers, two of which carry a large share of revenue. Even so, SentinelOne is the stock with more room to be wrong and still work in 2027. I would keep watch for several quarters to see whether the company hits its targets. Five years on from its IPO, SentinelOne is still early in its public life, and the tailwind behind security spending is strong enough to pay off if the company grows into its multiple. A company named for a lookout deserves a watchful eye, not a substantial bet.

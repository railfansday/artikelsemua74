Arsenal Thought They Signed a 'Phenom' in Miyaichi, but things went wrong

Back in 2011, Arsenal had built up a reputation for identifying some of the most talented players, whether it was Nicolas Anelka, Aaron Ramsey or Theo Walcott. The Gunners did some wise business, and they thought they had done the same with Ryo Miyaichi when they plucked from obscurity in 2011, offering him a trial.

Arséne Wenger was impressed by Miyachi when he had a trial with the club, and he offered the Japanese youngster a professional contract at the end of January of 2011.

The Frenchman noted at the time: "We’re delighted that Ryō Miyaichi has joined us. He trialed with us in the summer and has raw ability which has attracted many clubs around the world". Some believed at the time that he could be the 'next Lionel Messi'. (Related: Summer at Hugging Face...)

Recent findings suggest that "Ryo has natural technical ability. He has good balance and phenomenal pace, and his passing and crossing is consistently of a high quality. He is a very exciting player." - Wenger on Miyaichi in 2011.

According to recent reports, miyaichi was an ultra-skillful player who swiftly impressed in the youth ranks, both for club and country, and he was on course to become a star. In fact, during his first senior spell in professional football, on loan at Feyenoord, he was nicknamed 'Ryodinho' by the Dutch media, which only sparked excitement among Arsenal fans.

"I like his pace, his mobility and his movement off the ball. I like players who can move when others have the ball. There is also his attitude, commitment and desire. That is why I decided to keep him." - Wenger on Miyaichi in 2011.

He went on to spend time at Bolton Wanderers and then Wigan Athletic, where he suffered a long-term ankle injury that saw him miss their successful FA Cup campaign.

Industry observers note that by this point, Miyaichi had managed just one senior league appearance for Arsenal, and after a loan spell with Twente back in the Netherlands, he was released by the Gunners. Miyaichi later reflected: "Back then, I didn't really think of myself as young. There were so many world-class players around my age, and I was always feeling pressed.". The Japan international found a newly introduced permanent home at St Pauli, but his debut was significantly delayed by an ACL rupture, and after returning to action, he ruptured the ACL in his other leg in a horrible run of luck.

Industry observers note that remarkably, the German side still renewed the winger's contract. Sporting chief Andreas Rettig explained: “Ryo has unbelievable bad luck, and we do not want to leave him alone. [We want to] show that we are behind him. We hope that this decision will help Ryo to return to his old level of performance.”.

Reflecting on his career at a later date, Miyaichi went on to say: "At one point, I seriously thought about quitting soccer.

"I have been away from the team for long periods and have caused trouble time and again. I have also disappointed many people. That is why I thought about quitting.".

Industry observers note that he went on to enjoy a couple of seasons of full fitness before being released in 2021 after racking up 77 league appearances. It was at that stage that he returned to Japan, joining Yokohoma F. Marinos, where he has been since, racking up 104 league appearances and scoring eight times. Back in 2022, he actually added three more Japan caps to his name, which must have been nice for him after all the injury troubles that followed his first two caps in 2012.

According to recent reports, at 33 years of age, Miyaichi is still playing with Yokohoma F. Marinos, and he won the JLeague with them back in 2022, adding to the two FA Cups he technically won between Wigan in 2013 and Arsenal in 2014.

Sources indicate that speaking back in June on why he wants to keep playing, Miyaichi said: "I think having fun is the most important thing. It's what fuels your growth. I still have the desire to keep improving and still feel the same way I always did. Showing people something that makes them think is cool on the pitch is what I'm there for.".

Industry observers note that miyaichi's never-ending battles with injury must have made for a tough time, but fueled by his love for the game, he never gave up, and perseverance has allowed him to keep playing at the age of 33. Things could have gone a very different way for the winger, even if his biggest hopes were not fulfilled.

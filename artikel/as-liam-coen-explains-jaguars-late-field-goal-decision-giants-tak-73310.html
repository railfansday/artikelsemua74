According to recent reports, EAST RUTHERFORD, N.J. — Down eight points with 3:43 to play, the Jacksonville Jaguars faced fourth-and-goal from the 8-yard line.

Head coach Liam Coen checked his charts.

Should he bank on his offense, which converted just once on six red-zone trips Sunday, to reverse course amid unfavorable down-and-distance? Or should the Jaguars kick the 26-yard field goal, narrow their deficit to five points and trust the defense that held the Newly introduced York Giants to three points in the second half to get a stop? Industry observers note that "Fourth-and-8 … what are those percentages of success? I think they're probably less than 5 [percent] in a lot of ways in this league … " Coen said after the game. "Just executing that down-and-distance on a fourth down is kind of challenging, especially when they are zero-blitzing every play. The ball's going to come out. It's like a 'hope' play.". According to recent reports, so the Jaguars kicked the field goal and the Giants ran out the clock with two first downs. The Giants won 23-18 after leading for the final 47 minutes.

"It just didn't work out," Coen said.

Recent findings suggest that the Jaguars' strategy of relying on their defense during a day their offense struggled to maintain rhythm did not work out for the visiting team. But the strategy worked well to motivate a Giants team that had lost its own rhythm after halftime.

If the Jaguars thought the Giants weren't going to realize the message their field goal sent, they were mistaken. New York quarterback J.J. McCarthy said "as an offense, we took that personal" as Jacksonville hedged on yet another New York implosion.

Sources indicate that "100%, it's disrespectful as hell," right tackle Jermaine Eleumenor remarked from the postgame locker room. "You're kicking a field goal, you think you're going to get a stop on defense, and if I'm being honest, we were whooping them. On the line of scrimmage, they weren't winning. We won the line of scrimmage, both sides of the ball.".

On a day in which the Jaguars were down starting center Robert Hainsey (knee) and lost starting left tackle Walker Little (knee) early in the fourth quarter, a pressure-happy Giants defense kept Trevor Lawrence from ever finding a rhythm.

Arvell Reese brings the heat on Trevor Lawrence in the Giants' victory Sunday.

And on a day in which the Jaguars were onboarding their trade-deadline acquisition starting defenders in defensive tackle Jordan Davis and safety Jessie Bates III, the Giants capitalized on a group adjusting to complex schemes, including with quarterback keepers that powered each of their touchdown drives.

So consider the Giants aware that they were 6-point betting underdogs at home and aware that plenty questioned their division-leading record on account of the below-.500 teams they had beaten.

Giants head coach John Harbaugh called his team's sixth victory "the first real football win.".

Edge rusher Abdul Carter remarked "everybody pretty much counted us out.".

Sources indicate that "But we took that as disrespect, and we came out with a chip on our shoulder … " stated Carter, who finished the game with 2 sacks, 5 quarterback hits and 8 pressures, per Pro Football Focus. "I think we proved that we are worthy. They keep saying we can't beat these teams, we can't beat them. I don't know why they're saying that.

"But what are they going to say now?".

At the end of the first quarter, the Jaguars had not scored a touchdown.

At the end of the second and then third, they hadn't either.

This was a Jacksonville team that entered the week averaging 29.9 points per game, fourth in the league. Its 358.9 yards per game ranked ninth in total offense. So what explained the 20-6 deficit that trickled into the fourth quarter?

Recent findings suggest that "I played really, really poorly today, and you're not going to win many games if I don't play better than that … " said Lawrence, who more than doubled his first three quarters' output with a 164-yard fourth quarter that brought his day to 301 yards, 1 touchdown and 2 interceptions on 25-of-47 (53.2%) passing. "As an offense as a whole, I thought we didn't come out with the aggressiveness and attacking that we normally have.

"I felt like they punched us in the mouth a little bit.".

Coen blamed himself for some of his early play-calling, saying he overcompensated for run calls he regretted a week prior by calling three straight passes to set up the opening drive's field goal. The Jaguars strayed on multiple drives from their run, which typically sets up their play-action passing and sets the tone for their physical offensive style. On third-and-medium-to-longs, the Giants pressured Lawrence to the tune of turnovers, incompletions and sacks.

Facing third-and-6 with 7:45 to play in the first quarter, Lawrence thought he could hit receiver Parker Washington out of reach of cornerback Deonte Banks before realizing it was "just a really bad read, really bad decision.".

With 11:08 to play in the fourth quarter, Lawrence aimed to throw high and early to avoid an approaching defensive lineman, instead throwing to a spot receiver Jakobi Meyers had yet to reach.

The Giants turned the interceptions into six total points on a day they won by five.

Yet when Jacksonville safety Antonio Johnson stripped running back Cam Skattebo and returned the fumble 42 yards, the Jaguars' offense responded with a turnover on downs. Complementary football eluded the team.

By the time Lawrence found a rhythm in the fourth quarter, hitting receiver Brian Thomas Jr. for an 18-yard touchdown on third-and-10 sandwiched between two field-goal drives, the Jaguars' defense was waning. Perhaps matters would have been different if the officials had ruled differently on Washington's fourth-quarter catch in which he got two feet down but didn't secure the ball without movement as he fell to the ground; but even Lawrence admitted: "It was close, and you can't leave it in the ref's hands.".

Industry observers note that the Giants celebrated holding onto a lead after letting their last contest slip away to the Washington Commanders in the final minute. Lawrence lamented that his offense's rally was too little, too late.

"It was nice to see the fight at the end of the game and that we were still able to have a shot, thanks to our defense," Lawrence remarked. "We let our defense down today. They got us a bunch of turnovers and gave us plenty of opportunities to win the game, and we didn't capitalize.".

At 6-3-1, the Giants maintained their grip on the NFC East even as the Cowboys improved to 5-6 with a 51-30 win over the Tennessee Titans. Next Gen Stats projects the Giants' chances of making the playoffs at 70% after the win.

The Jaguars, meanwhile, don't just remain atop the AFC South at 7-3. Thanks to their three-game lead in the division, they carry a 98% chance of making the playoffs, per Next Gen Stats.

Sources indicate that so postseason contention is not a front-of-mind problem. Cleaning up the errors that the Giants exposed, however, is.

Less than two weeks after trading for Davis and Bates as indications of the belief in the 2026 Jaguars, Jacksonville coaches and executives continue to echo that message.

It has been revealed that "They believe in this year and they believe that we can make a push to go to the Super Bowl," cornerback Jourdan Lewis told Yahoo Sports. "Those dreams are still intact, just getting accustomed to the guys and how we want to play and how to attack offenses and how they want to attack our system too. ….

Instead, the Jaguars will continue working to acclimate Bates to split-safety looks that differ from his Atlanta responsibilities prior to the trade, and they'll work with Davis as he learns the nuances of coordinator Anthony Campanile's movement calls while remembering that gap integrity, physicality and winning in the trenches will always be the foundation for defensive-line success.

The Jaguars were quiet as they shuffled into the postgame locker room from a rainy MetLife Field. But as they showered and packed up for their flight home, their tone reflected focus more than despair.

There are holes to patch. There is time, in the seven remaining regular-season contests, to patch them.

According to recent reports, "It's not the end of the world," Davis told Yahoo Sports of Coen's message. "We have to fix the complications. We have to take our medicine. We have to look ourselves in the mirror and then move forward the next week.

Industry observers note that "You don't have time to dwell on your wins and losses in the previous week. You have to move on.".

It has been revealed that so on the Jaguars will move from their five-point loss and on they'll move from Coen's decision to kick a field goal with 3:43 remaining. That decision will motivate Jacksonville's offensive players to show their head coach next time that they're capable of beating blitzes and performing even when the down and distance doesn't favor them.

And the decision's failure will motivate Jacksonville's defense to shore up the assignments that ceded McCarthy a 19-yard completion up the middle and Skattebo a 5-yard run on second-and-5 to seal the win.

Several Jaguars players echoed their trust in Coen on that decision and beyond. And Lawrence, who said he struggled to read the field efficiently until late in the game, said he understood which part of those late-game analytics moments were in his control.

"It's not my decision at the end of the day," Lawrence stated. "It's coach's, but I stand by him. We've got to make plays on first, second and third down so we have a chance to go for it not on fourth-and-7 or 8. It's just a tough down ….

"We know who we are. We just didn't play well tonight. If you don't play well, you get beat.".

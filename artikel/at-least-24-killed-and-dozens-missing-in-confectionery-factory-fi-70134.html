According to recent reports, preliminary reports indicated the fire might have ‌been caused by an air-conditioner short circuit, Said added. The factory is owned by United Family Food, which did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

The condition of the victims made visual identification impossible, prompting authorities to urge family members to provide DNA samples and other information that could be used to identify their loved ones, said Wahyu Hidayati Dwi Palupi, who heads the national police’s victim identification division.

She stated body bags containing the remains of the victims were transferred to a hospital for identification.

The blaze had largely been contained on Sunday but firefighters were carrying out intensive cooling operations on Monday before they could access all parts of the factory, noted Tubagus Mahfud, operations chief of the Serang fire department.

“We are conducting sweeps in the areas that have already been brought under control,” Mahfud remarked. “Parts of the structure had collapsed, while poor lighting conditions required firefighters to proceed cautiously.”.

He also said the factory’s production materials could cause the fire to reignite, making it critical to complete cooling operations.

Anxious relatives have waited for news outside the burnt-out factory.

The Banten province governor, Andra Soni, said an estimated 60 people were inside the factory when it caught fire.

The firm employs about 5,000 people and is a major supplier of consumer goods to the domestic market. Its ‌products include Milkita, a popular confectionery brand, and Lemonilo, an instant noodle variety, according to its website.

In a recent development, the organization’s chief executive, Steven Wijaya, stated the factory regularly conducted emergency and evacuation drills in conjunction with local firefighters. He said the facility had adequate evacuation routes and fire protection systems that complied with applicable safety standards and were functioning at the time of the fire.

Indonesia has experienced a series of deadly industrial fires in recent years, raising concerns about workplace safety standards and emergency preparedness at manufacturing facilities.

In December last year, a blaze that was likely caused by a drone battery explosion tore through a seven-storey building in Jakarta and killed at least 22 people, police stated.

In 2023, at least 13 people were killed in the country’s east after an explosion at a nickel-processing plant.

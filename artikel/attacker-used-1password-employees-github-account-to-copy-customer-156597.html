UPDATED 17:29 EST / NOVEMBER 25 2026.

Identity security organization 1Password today disclosed a security incident in which an attacker, working through an employee’s corporate GitHub account, copied account records belonging to a subset of its customers. (Related: Industry News: Which Samsung folding phone is right for...)

Recent findings suggest that according to the company, the incident came to light on Nov. 19, when 1Password began investigating signs that the GitHub account had been compromised. Using the account, the attacker is mentioned to have pushed malicious code to 1Password’s backend services, which then downloaded what the company describes as “administrative data pertaining to our customers.”.

Recent findings suggest that the records taken include names and email addresses, account and user identifiers, account creation and last sign-in times, public keys, Secure Remote Password protocol parameters and other non-secret account metadata. Rounding out the list are tokens found in certain emailed links for invitations, provisioning and account recovery. (Related: Steam is pitting 10 years of Game of the Year winners a...)

It has been revealed that customer vaults, the items stored in them and the keys that protect them were not affected, according to 1Password. “At no time were customer vault contents accessed by, or available to, the attacker,” the company mentioned in its disclosure. Account passwords and Secret Keys are not stored by the company. Also left untouched, the company says, were account recovery keys and the 1Password apps and browser extensions.

Beyond the customer records, the attacker is said to have copied source code from private repositories. Access was also gained to 1Password’s continuous integration and continuous delivery system, which contains infrastructure-related secrets needed for its product to work. According to 1Password, the source code contains no user secrets. Having triggered a rotation of all credentials that may have been exposed, the tech giant claims there has been no impact to its services, availability or product security.

According to recent reports, the tokens taken from emailed links cannot be used on their own to access an account, since completing an invitation or recovery also requires a sign-in through single sign-on or a six-digit code sent to the user’s registered email address. Users with provisioning invites or recovery requests pending at the time of the incident, remarked to be a small number, have been notified directly.

As a precaution, the emailed codes have now been lengthened from six numeric digits to 10 alphanumeric characters. 1Password claims it has found no internal evidence of account takeovers using the exposed tokens. Other steps taken in response include disabling the affected GitHub account, revoking the stolen token, tightening internal controls and bringing in independent cybersecurity experts to validate its containment and remediation work.

Customers do not need to change anything in their 1Password accounts. The organization is instead warning people to be cautious of phishing attempts, such as unexpected emails, texts or calls about invitations or account recovery requests they did not start. “1Password will never contact you to ask for your account password or Secret Key,” the company noted.

In a recent development, 1Password, which claims to serve more than 200,000 businesses, did not say how many customers had records downloaded. How the employee’s GitHub account was compromised was also left unexplained.

In a recent development, this is not the first intrusion 1Password has disclosed. In October 2023, the company said that an attacker had accessed its Okta tenant using session data stolen in the breach of Okta Inc.’s customer support system. No user data was found to have been compromised in that case.

Today’s disclosure comes nearly four years after rival password manager LastPass US LP revealed that a hacker had copied encrypted customer password vaults, using information taken in an earlier breach of its development environment.

1Password says it will upgrade its post if the investigation turns up anything that changes what it has disclosed so far.

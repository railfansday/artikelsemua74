Axon specializes in public safety tools and cloud evidence management for law enforcement. Palantir provides massive data integration and artificial intelligence infrastructure for government and corporate clients. As both companies expand their digital platforms, determining which is the more attractive buy requires a look at their recent financial performance. Axon Enterprise provides a comprehensive ecosystem of hardware and software designed for law enforcement and public safety professionals. Its product line includes Taser devices, body-worn cameras, and the Axon Evidence platform, which allows agencies to manage digital records securely. The company is actively expanding its presence among defense stocks and international markets across Europe and Asia. No single customer represented more than 10% of total net sales in 2025, which provides a diversified revenue base.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached about $3.4 billion, indicating a 33.6% increase compared to the prior period. This growth was accompanied by a net income of approximately $258.5 million. The manufacturer continues to transition customers toward subscription-based software services, which typically offer higher visibility into future sales. This steady growth suggests strong demand for its integrated safety solutions among federal and local agencies.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is roughly 0.6x. This ratio measures total debt relative to shareholder equity, showing how much the tech giant relies on borrowed funds. The current ratio, which compares short-term assets to short-term liabilities, is approximately 2.5x. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was around $75.1 million. Note that stock-based compensation represented roughly 300% of operating cash flow, meaning reported cash generation is heavily inflated by this non-cash add-back.

Palantir Technologies develops platforms that integrate and analyze massive data sets to help organizations make better decisions. Its customer base is divided between government agencies and commercial enterprises, with a total of 954 customers as of late 2025. The tech giant recently reported a remaining deal value of nearly $11.2 billion, split between its public and private sector contracts. Note that the top three customers accounted for roughly 16% of revenue in 2025. Customer concentration among these top clients adds a layer of risk to the business.

Recent findings suggest that in the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached about $7.3 billion, representing a significant 87% increase over the previous 12 months. Net income for this period was approximately $3.7 billion. This surge in performance is driven by the rapid adoption of its artificial intelligence infrastructure. The manufacturer often uses low-cost pilot deployments to prove the value of its software before moving to larger, long-term contracts.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is 0.0x, showing the manufacturer has no debt relative to its equity. The current ratio is roughly 7.1x, indicating a strong ability to cover short-term financial obligations with existing assets. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow reached nearly $2.1 billion. Note that stock-based compensation (SBC) accounted for roughly 32% of operating cash flow, inflating reported cash generation, since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

Axon Enterprise faces risks related to the acceptance of its products by law enforcement agencies. Negative publicity regarding its devices or software can impact demand. The company also faces global supply chain dependencies and rapid technological shifts, particularly in artificial intelligence. Legal risks include product liability claims and complex government procurement regulations. It competes in an environment alongside companies such as Motorola Solutions (MSI +0.04%).

According to recent reports, palantir Technologies carries risks associated with its revenue concentration, as a small number of customers provide a large portion of its total sales. The tech giant also manages long and unpredictable sales cycles that require significant upfront investment. There is a risk that its AI technologies may fail to gain further market acceptance. Additionally, the company relies heavily on third-party cloud infrastructure from providers like Amazon.com Inc. (AMZN -1.21%) and Microsoft Corp. (MSFT +0.74%).

Palantir Technologies carries a notably higher valuation than Axon Enterprise when comparing both future earnings estimates and sales over the past twelve months.

Valuation metrics include those sourced from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from those of other data providers.

Axon has a positive long-term outlook for growth as law enforcement is increasingly equipped with Tasers and the public is becoming increasingly demanding that agents wear body cameras. Its cloud-based evidence control system is also an increasingly sought-after service for law enforcement organization.

It has been revealed that expansion into Europe and Canada should also help, as should a focus on producing personal consumer-protection devices. In particular, all the data and video created by Axon customers have to be managed, and the organization is seeing strong demand for its AI-based management systems from customers who find many of the services create efficiencies within their operations.

In fiscal 2026, sales are expected to grow well, up about a third to $3.6 billion, with net income of around $290 million and free cash flow of $425 million, well over 2025's levels. In 2027, consensus estimates project adding another $1 billion in revenue, with expanding net income and free cash flow as well.

According to recent reports, palantir started out as a tech contractor to the U.S. military and law enforcement, and the federal government still accounts for half of its revenue. But the business has been successfully expanding beyond government work, with about a quarter of sales coming from international customers and a significant minority of revenue from the private sector. For the first time last quarter, private sector revenue exceeded U.S. government revenue.

In a recent development, palantir says customers are drawn to its sovereign AI, which is its term for systems that can help build themselves while protecting what makes each system the AI is refining, so it is not commoditized. Companies like Nvidia Corp. (NVDA -5.86%) and L3Harris Technologies (LHX +1.27%) are notable customers of Palantir's sovereign AI.

The boom in private-sector AI business should double Palantir's 2026 sales to $8.6 billion, with a $2.7 billion jump in net income to $4.4 billion. 2027 should see continued fast growth, with consensus analysts' estimates projecting revenue of $13.1 billion and net income over $6 billion.

Recent findings suggest that the top-line growth of Palantir is difficult to ignore, but its shares trade at an exceptional premium that may not account for the long-term threat of AI displacing its business. Axon comes at a premium, too, as seen through its P/E and P/S ratios, but it's cheaper than Palantir, and the company is delivering exceptional sales growth and improving margins. For 2027, go with Axon Enterprise.

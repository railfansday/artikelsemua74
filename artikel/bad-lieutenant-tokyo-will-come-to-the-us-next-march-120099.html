“Bad Lieutenant: Tokyo,” a Japan-set take on police decadence, will open in North America on March 5, 2027. Neon, the Oscar-winning studio behind “Anora” and “Parasite,” is distributing the film.

Industry observers note that directed by Takashi Miike, the Japanese auteur behind “Audition” and “13 Assassins,” “”Bad Lieutenant: Tokyo” stars Shun Oguri (“Godzilla v. Kong”), Lily James (“Pam and Tommy”), and WWE star Liv Morgan. It follows a veteran police detective Yabuki (Oguri), who is struggling with drug addiction, gambling debts, and guilt over past misdeeds, as he chases a shadowy killer tied to organized crime through Tokyo. His case ends up intersecting with that of an American FBI agent (James), who’s hunting a missing tourist (Morgan). The film had its world premiere at the 2026 Toronto International Film Festival, where it earned mixed notices, with some reviewers faulting it as overstuffed. Neon is in the midst of a busy fall, with the indie studio releasing awards contenders such as “All of a Sudden,” “Fjord,” “Paper Tiger,” and “Artificial,” so it makes sense that it would opt to have “Bad Lieutenant: Tokyo” come out in the spring.

It has been revealed that “Bad Lieutenant” first entered the cinematic vernacular with Abel Ferrara’s 1992 crime thriller that was so boundary-pushing (a nun is raped) it received an NC-17 rating. It also offered up Harvey Keitel at his most unhinged and committed, playing a cop who broke really, really bad. Then Werner Herzog transported the depravity to the Significant Easy with 2009’s “Bad Lieutenant: Port of Call New Orleans,” featuring Nicolas Cage at his most gonzo. The latest “Bad Lieutenant” is a U.S.-Japan co-production, produced by the late Jeremy Thomas for Recorded Picture Organization, Sam Pressman for Pressman Film, Naoaki Kitajima for Nippon TV, and Misako Saka for OLM.

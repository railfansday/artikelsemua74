It has been revealed that free Shadowheart from her pod at the beginning of Baldur’s Gate 3, and she’ll help you get off a crashing mind flayer ship. Ask what she was doing before she got there, and you’ll find out how far rescuing someone actually gets you. Half the fun of those early conversations in the game is deciding when to push your luck with her. It takes a little longer to appreciate how often you’ve asked her a perfectly reasonable question that she couldn’t answer about herself if she wanted to.

Before she met anyone aboard the nautiloid, Shadowheart lived beneath Baldur’s Gate with people who considered a personal history something worth giving up. The cleric has good reason to keep Shar’s secrets by the time you meet her. Collider can exclusively reveal that Random House Worlds will publish Baldur’s Gate 3: Shadowheart on June 8, 2027. Following Astarion, the publisher’s next prequel comes from Tasha Suri, the World Fantasy Award-winning author of the Burning Kingdoms trilogy. Suri sends Shadowheart from the underground church she calls home to the wealthiest district in Baldur’s Gate, where someone has killed a member of her order. Industry observers note that you want a partner, not an audience. Somebody who wants their own life as badly as you want yours, and who would never ask you to be smaller to make the relationship easier.

Recent findings suggest that the intensity was never the problem. The secrecy is. You are drawn to a love that lives entirely in private, and to the particular ache of wanting somebody you are not supposed to want.

In a recent development, shadowheart has three people to help her work the case, and the one she’d least like to have along is a familiar face. There’s Mirie, a Griefguard with ambitions of her own, and Caldan, whose job as a propagandist hardly inspires confidence in his account of events. Then there’s Nocturne, the quartermistress who insists she’s looking out for Shadowheart. Players who found Nocturne in the House of Grief in Act 3 have some idea how much that assurance means to her. Here, it’s enough to make Shadowheart suspicious.

According to recent reports, that’s a hell of a disadvantage to give a detective. Somewhere beneath the city is the Mirror of Loss, where the faithful surrender memories to Shar. Shadowheart believes there’s strength in what she’s given up, but she also has to decide whether to trust a woman who might remember the two of them better than she does. For players who spent hours earning the occasional confidence from Shadowheart, there’s some pleasure in seeing her stuck on the other side of that conversation.

The Upper City complicates the assignment further. It’s the first place in the publisher’s description where Shadowheart sounds like she might actually want to stay. Whatever she finds there, she still has to go home and give an account of herself to the people who sent her. Solving the murder is only half the job when your church also expects you to prove you’re still theirs.

Thierno Barry notes he has "built my confidence game by game", which has led him to believe that he can score every time he plays.

Barry joined Everton in July 2025 but struggled for form in his first campaign with the Toffees, netting just eight goals in 41 appearances across all competitions.

However, this season he has been Everton's starring man, helped by the departure of Beto to Fiorentina, which means Barry is their only out-and-out striker.

Across his first 13 games in all competitions of 2026-27, Barry has recorded 13 goal involvements (10 goals, three assists).

And in an exclusive interview with Opta Analyst, Barry noted just how important a shift in his mentality has been to hitting this rich vein of form.

"Before I joined the Premier League, I knew it was the hardest league in the world, but I didn't expect it like that!" he remarked.

"It was really hard at first. I think I needed to take my time, to really enjoy [my football here].

Sources indicate that "I think for a striker the most important thing is getting opportunities. This season, every time I play, I feel I can score a goal.

"When you feel like this, you just have to be patient; you can miss what the people might say is an 'easy' chance, but you have to switch your mind [back to the game] because you can get another chance two minutes later. (Related: Latest Update: Simple considerations for simple people ...)

"During pre-season, I worked on my mentality, if I am feeling low, to not let it affect my game.

"For example, this season, before the game against Newcastle United, in my head I wasn't perfect because something happened in my personal life, you know? (Related: Latest Update: At least seven progressives voted agains...)

"I came to the game and tried to do my best, even if I was feeling low, and I scored, and I think this game proved that even if I've got something going on, I can still score.".

Barry has played an instrumental part in helping Everton reach the EFL Cup quarter-finals, having netted his first hat-trick for the club against Preston North End in the second round.

But it is in the Premier League where he has really hit his stride. His six goals are the most by a player in Everton's first 10 Premier League games of a season since Dominic Calvert-Lewin in 2020-21 (10), and the most by a non-English player for the club since Romelu Lukaku in 2016-17 (seven).

The Frenchman has scored (five) or assisted (one) six of the Toffees' last seven league goals.

Only three Everton players have previously surpassed the 15-goal mark in a single Premier League season (Romelu Lukaku twice, Tony Cottee and Calvert-Lewin), and Barry is determined to make his mark in the competition.

"I appreciate these kind statistics," he told Opta Analyst of his high-scoring start to the season.

Sources indicate that "Seeing these statistics means a lot to me because I know how hard I worked [to achieve them]. I try to do my best on the pitch every game, and when it works out, it's a pleasure.

Industry observers note that "I think I've built my confidence game by game. This season, in every game, I've had an opportunity to score, and even when I miss, I know I'll have another opportunity.

"I think for a striker, the most notable thing is getting those opportunities.".

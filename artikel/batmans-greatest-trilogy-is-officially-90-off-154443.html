She’s helping build out Collider’s gaming coverage, with Pokémon as one of her specialties. If she isn’t writing about a monster, she’s probably watching one on a tape from her VHS collection.

Industry observers note that between Kevin Conroy’s performance as Batman, Mark Hamill’s return as the Joker, and an original story written by Batman: The Animated Series veteran Paul Dini, Rocksteady Studios assembled something pretty remarkable in 2009. Batman: Arkham Asylum was every bit as atmospheric as some of the character’s darkest comic book adventures, and its combat system would go on to influence an entire generation of action games. More impressive still was Rocksteady's ability to build upon that success with two exceptional sequels. (Related: Rockstar explains why GTA 6 added its Criminal Profile ...)

The 2011 release of Batman: Arkham City earned a staggering 96 on Metacritic for its PS3 version, while Batman: Arkham Knight brought Rocksteady’s trilogy to a close in 2015. More than a decade later, all three games can be purchased together for considerably less than the price of a single movie ticket.

According to recent reports, the Batman: Arkham Collection is officially 90% off as part of PlayStation’s Black Friday sale, bringing its price down from $59.99 to just $5.99 through Monday, November 30. The digital PS4 collection is also playable on PS5 via backwards compatibility and includes the remastered editions of Arkham Asylum and Arkham City, alongside Arkham Knight and their previously released downloadable content. (Related: Mechs vs Kaiju’s Ragdoll codes (November 2026)...)

Sources indicate that in addition to the three main campaigns, the collection includes several adventures that give members of the extended Bat-Family their own time in the spotlight. Among them is Arkham Knight’s A Matter of Family, which follows Barbara Gordon as Batgirl prior to the events of Arkham Asylum, and Harley Quinn’s Revenge, an additional story set after the events of Arkham City. The collection also includes several alternate costumes inspired by Batman’s cinematic history, allowing players to patrol Gotham in Michael Keaton’s 1989 Batsuit or even take the Batmobile from the classic Adam West television series out onto the streets in Arkham Knight. However, 2013’s Batman: Arkham Origins, the prequel developed by WB Games Montréal that explores an earlier encounter between Batman and the Joker, is excluded.

Recent findings suggest that meanwhile, Rocksteady’s influence on Batman’s gaming adventures extends to this year’s LEGO Batman: Legacy of the Dark Knight. The TT Games release incorporates several familiar elements from the Arkham trilogy into its open-world Gotham City, including its approach to combat and stealth. Rocksteady is even credited as a co-developer on the project, with approximately two dozen of the studio’s developers contributing to the game.

Batman: Arkham Collection is available for $5.99 on the PlayStation Store through November 30.

Sources indicate that from “The Petrified Forest” to “When You Comin’ Back, Red Ryder?” to “Albino Alligator” and beyond, playwrights and filmmakers have long relied on the same claustrophobic premise: Disparate characters are stranded in a diner and pushed to extremes by cold-blooded, gun-toting bad guys. (OK, “Albino Alligator” actually was set in a New Orleans bar, but close enough.) In “Best Pancakes in the County,” writer-director Ken Sanzel laces this whiskery setup with the seriocomic violence and show-offy wordplay one might expect of someone who repeatedly watched early Quentin Tarantino movies, slapped their forehead, and exclaimed: “Hey, I can do that!” Related Stories Elizabeth Debicki Says 'Tenet' Co-Star Robert Pattinson Will Be the ‘Perfect’ James Bond: ‘I’m So Thrilled’ Gemma Chan Says She’d ‘Absolutely’ Play a Bond Girl Amid Fan-Casting: ‘I’m Flattered’.

One of two new releases this week showcasing Nicolas Cage in not-entirely-convincing prosthetics (the other being the sports-centric biopic “Madden”), “Pancakes” has the Oscar-winning and profligately prolific actor cast as a cunning criminal known as S.O.B. — no joke, that’s how he’s referenced by others and identified in the closing credits — who’s introduced while cracking a safe supposedly stuffed with $11 million in an FBI stash house. When he discovers that his partner in crime lied about how much loot was there, S.O.B. expresses his disappointment nonverbally. But he holds on to another item in the safe, a hard drive that more or less serves as the movie’s propulsive McGuffin. (Related: Hugging Face and Graphcore partner for IPU-optimized Tr...)

According to recent reports, after that, Cage, sporting garish ‘70s attire and a fake upturned nose that recalls Christina Ricci’s exaggerated snout in “Penelope,” drops out of sight for a stretch, one of his sporadic disappearing acts in the movie. The narrative slack is taken up (for a while, at least) by the interactions of vaguely defined Tarantinoesque cosplayers at a remote roadside diner that boasts… well, take another look at the title. Industry observers note that since this is “Best Pancakes in the County” and not “Waiting for Godot,” S.O.B. eventually arrives at the diner, giving Cage yet another chance to dial it up to 11 while devouring scenery with his customary fervor. A little bit of this goes a long way; a lot, alas, is par for the course from Cage these days.

But there’s no getting around the fact that far too much of “Best Pancakes in the County” is taken up with faux-Tarantino dialogue riffs and overcompensating posing. It’s almost bad enough to give you indigestion while you stifle the occasional yawn.

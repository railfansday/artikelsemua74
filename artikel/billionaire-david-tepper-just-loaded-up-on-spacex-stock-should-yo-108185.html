Checking in to see what the billionaire hedge fund managers do is a smart investment strategy. While you shouldn't blindly follow all their moves, using them for idea generation is a smart plan.

One billionaire hedge fund I follow is Appaloosa Management, run by David Tepper. During Q3, he loaded up on Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX +1.30%) shares. He owned barely any at the end of Q2 (SpaceX went public right before Q2 ended), but as of the end of Q3, it was the fourth-largest position in his portfolio, accounting for nearly 8% of its value. That shows extreme confidence in the stock, but should you follow suit and open a position in it? (Related: Tech Report: Why you should upgrade to an OLED for GTA ...)

Recent findings suggest that one major reason retail investors should not blindly copy the moves of billionaire hedge fund managers is that the information we are getting is well out of date by the time we see it. Investment fund managers only have to report their end-of-quarter holdings in their 13F filings, so the purchases involved could have happened at any time during a quarter. And those forms don’t get filed with the Securities and Exchange Commission until 45 days after the quarter they cover has ended. So any given stock move described in those forms could be more than four months old by the time news of it reaches the general public.

According to recent reports, during Q3, SpaceX traded as low as $110 per share and as high as $160 per share at the start of the quarter. It ended the quarter around $151 per share — just above its opening-day first trading price. Today's price of about $130 per share is right in the middle of the Q3 range where Tepper could have bought. So investors don't have to worry too much about overpaying for the stock, as it's on sale relative to where it traded for most of Q3.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/25/26.

Additionally, by waiting to buy until now, you have more information than Tepper had available in Q3, and that information is quite bullish.

Although SpaceX is generally recognized as a space exploration firm, it's increasingly an artificial intelligence investment. Its AI segment now accounts for over half of its revenue, and has grown at a 213% pace year over year last quarter. That's an impressive growth rate, and according to CEO Elon Musk, it's only going to get better.

Industry observers note that during the Q3 conference call, Musk asserted that SpaceX's annual recurring revenue could rise to somewhere in the $500 billion to $1 trillion range by the end of next year, thanks to its booming AI division. While Musk is no stranger to making bold projections that don't always pan out, the bigger idea here is that there will be monster AI growth in 2027 that investors need to prepare for. With SpaceX becoming more AI-focused, I think it's a smart pick in this space.

Recent findings suggest that investors should take advantage of the current price of SpaceX's stock and scoop up shares. Tepper may have been a bit early to the party by buying shares in Q3, but I think his decision was the right one.

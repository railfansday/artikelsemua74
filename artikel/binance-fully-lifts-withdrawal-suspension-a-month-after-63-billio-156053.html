Binance Fully Lifts Withdrawal Suspension a Month After $6.3 Billion Hack

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Binance has fully lifted the withdrawal suspension it put in place after last month’s record $6.3 billion hack, saying its recovery work is complete and that withdrawals are open across nearly all networks.

Sources indicate that “Our withdrawal recovery work is completed and the withdrawal suspension from the security incident has been fully lifted,” the exchange mentioned in an X post Thursday afternoon. A small number of networks remain paused for maintenance, which Binance called standard practice and said was unrelated to the hack. According to recent reports, the enhancement closes out a month of phased reopenings. Binance confirmed the breach on Oct. 29, saying its initial investigation pointed to a compromised DevOps engineer’s laptop that gave the attacker access to a service container, which was then used to submit unauthorized withdrawals. Sources indicate that the world’s largest crypto exchange stopped all customer withdrawals for two days after the theft, Reuters reported. When they first reopened, customers pulled a net $4.7 billion off the platform, Binance said on Nov. 2.

Binance said on Nov. 12 that it had completed coverage of the affected assets with more than $6.34 billion of firm funds. The money came from a partial draw on its Secure Asset Fund for Users, part of its futures insurance fund and its corporate treasury, and the exchange pledged to rebuild SAFU to about $1 billion.

Coverage is not the same as recovery. Binance replaced the stolen tokens from its own books, while about $2.85 billion of the stolen funds have been frozen remotely by the companies that issue them. The rest remains in the hands of the attackers.

U.S. authorities have blamed North Korean hackers for the theft, which North Korea denies. On Nov. 23, the Justice Department moved to forfeit more than $1.1 billion in USDC that Circle froze, and stated the U.S. has helped freeze, seize or return more than $2.9 billion since the attack.

It has been revealed that the unfrozen funds are on the move. North Korean launderers have stepped up transfers through THORChain and a coinjoin service in recent days, blockchain researchers told Reuters, as part of an operation spanning tens of thousands of transactions across blockchains.

It has been revealed that chainalysis and Elliptic estimate that more than $600 million of the stolen crypto has passed through THORChain, though Elliptic said that figure could count funds that went through more than once. TRM Labs estimates THORChain earned as much as $1 million in fees from the swaps.

Separately, $342 million has moved through a coinjoin service mostly coordinated by a pseudonymous figure named Kruw, Elliptic said. A coinjoin combines coins from many users into a single transaction, so their origin is no longer visible on the blockchain. Elliptic has said the launderers will still need other services, such as over-the-counter brokers, to turn the coins into cash.

The FBI on Oct. 31 published wallet addresses used by the attacker and asked crypto service providers, including the network of computers that runs THORChain, to block them. In a video posted Thursday, THORChain co-founder Chad Barraford said 98% of trading on the network was for people’s “own individual financial freedom and liberty.”.

Recent findings suggest that THORChain, which describes itself as decentralized, indicates on its website that it does not “censor” transactions or run Know-Your-Customer checks. Ari Redbord, global head of policy at TRM Labs, called the FBI’s move to single out the network’s operators “pretty extraordinary.”.

In a recent development, redbord said the laundering could take months or even years, and that cashing out even a fraction would be a windfall for North Korea. The total stolen from Binance equals about a sixth of the country’s estimated gross domestic product.

“They don’t have to get a lot off in order to make this all worthwhile for them,” Redbord said.

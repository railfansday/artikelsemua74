Recent findings suggest that on BlockchainCenter's original Bitcoin Rainbow Chart, the price marker sat below the bottom purple band, and the chart labeled the reading “Bitcoin is dead,” as Bitcoin traded below $59,000 on Nov. 25.

On the same date, BlockchainCenter’s dynamic version displayed “BUY!”.

The two readings describe the same selloff from different starting points. BlockchainCenter presents the original and dynamic charts separately, and each version measures Bitcoin against its own curve. (Related: How to generate text: using different decoding methods ...)

The original chart snapshot uses a fixed formula dating to 2014, while the dynamic chart fits its curve to daily Bitcoin prices starting in 2012.

It has been revealed that the colored bands show where the price sits relative to each model’s estimate of its long-term trend. As the original curve rises over time, it takes a higher dollar price to reach the same band. (Related: How to train a newly introduced language model from scr...)

The dynamic model’s estimate can also change as new price history comes in, and the apparent instructions in the labels take their meaning from the curve each chart uses.

BlockchainCenter puts Bitcoin’s price against two different trends.

A price below the bands on the original chart can coexist with a buy label on the dynamic chart.

BlockchainCenter’s weekly archive demonstrates that the same “Bitcoin is dead” label was recorded for $74,382 on Nov. 8, $65,118 on Nov. 15, and $60,944 on Nov. 22.

Recent findings suggest that the Nov. 25 digest also showed it, with Bitcoin below $59,000, and the label had already appeared at weekly checkpoints in May and October.

According to recent reports, the November readings capture prices on specific dates, so they leave room for swings in between. Bitcoin can keep falling after the original chart runs out of colors, since the quoted prices capture changes the label obscures once the old chart reaches its lower limit.

In March 2020, Bitcoin briefly slipped below the chart’s lowest band during the pandemic crash, according to BlockchainCenter’s history.

Sources indicate that it broke the old boundary again in 2022 when the price fell below $20,000. After the second break, BlockchainCenter added a band beneath its previous floor. The original formula stayed in place, and the new indigo band was labeled “1BTC = 1BTC.”.

BlockchainCenter’s creator states the added band also came under pressure, so the site revised the formula in a separate V2 release on Nov. 21, 2022, using more price history to fit a new curve.

Adding a band changed how the old chart displayed low prices, and refitting the curve changed the benchmark itself. Either change could put a price that had fallen outside the old bands on the chart, even before a recovery.

The fourth Bitcoin halving in April 2024 cut the block subsidy to 3.125 BTC, offering a starting point for comparing the rally that followed with the original curve.

Bitcoin went on to set a dollar record, reaching about $126,200 on Oct. 6, 2025. Even that rally stayed short of the yellow and red bands in the portion of the original chart shown in the Nov. 25 snapshot.

In a recent development, bitcoin recovered from the March 2020 shock and went on to trade well above its 2022 lows. Those recoveries show how different the market can look after a break.

In November, the original chart’s label stayed in place as the price fell, while the dynamic chart put it in a band labeled “BUY!” BlockchainCenter describes the Rainbow Chart as a way to view long-term price history.

The chart’s creator presents even the updated fit as a way to put past price moves in context.

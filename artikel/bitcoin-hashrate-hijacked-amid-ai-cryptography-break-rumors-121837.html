More than 1,600 Bitcoin (BTC) mining sites have been hit by a Border Gateway Protocol (BGP) attack, also known as a man-in-the-middle hijack, where attackers impersonate a miner to steal their BTC mining proceeds.

According to recent reports, according to Bitcoin mining firm Braiins, the attack began in early November, with attackers diverting around 175 petahashes per second (PH/s) of compute from miners in Brazil and Argentina. On November 20, the event spread to Europe, hitting more than 1,000 mining sites in a single second. This time the stolen hashrate grew tenfold to 0.6-1.9 exahashes/second (EH/s).

Sources indicate that notably, Stratum V1 is a protocol that coordinates communication between Bitcoin mining rigs (ASICs) and mining pools. There has, however, been a campaign to push miner migration to Stratum V2 due to the vulnerabilities inherent to the former.

Sources indicate that stratum V1 is unencrypted, has zero server authentication, and is centralized. These features make it prone to internet traffic interception, with attackers impersonating BTC mining pools to feed miners real work while reaping the proceeds to their wallets. According to recent reports, in 2014, Dell’s cybersecurity branch, SecureWorks, uncovered a similar incident. However, by this time, attackers had managed to make away with $83,000 of BTC and other proof-of-work mining rewards.

Sources indicate that braiins urges operators to immediately abandon unencrypted connections, moving instead to Stratum V2, which is decentralized and performs server authentication. The company also released an updated software to block plain-text fallbacks. Even more, Braiins introduced a free local SV2 Proxy tool so that older mining rigs can benefit from encrypted processes even though they are incompatible with Stratum V2 and TLS (Transport Layer Security).

According to recent reports, furthermore, the organization has asked other mining software providers to update their product to versions that support identity verification. Meanwhile, hosted miners should ask their operators to confirm the security of their main and backup connections.

It has been revealed that recently trending is the rumour that OpenAI and Anthropic AI models may have successfully broken modern cryptographic models. These include Bitcoin’s elliptic curve cryptography (ECC), integer factorization in P, and Ring-LWE.

A Break in ECC would allow private key derivation from public keys, while that in Ring-LWE would make post-quantum migration pointless. Meanwhile, integer factoring in P would affect the majority of the world’s digital infrastructure, including banking systems, government communications, digital authentication, and software signing.

While the allegations remain unconfirmed by noted companies, Computer Scientist Scott Aaronson and Ledger CTO Charles Guillemet have mentioned them.

I'm hearing several rumors that frontier labs have broken some major cryptographic primitives:– "Factoring is in P": Now that the Riemann hypothesis is proven, we do understand much better how primes are distributed. Still, I don't think factoring is in P. AKS demonstrated in 2002….

Employees at both companies are also calling for a pause in AI model training, fearing they are capable of causing global chaos through cyberattacks and outages, per Axios.

“If we continue trying to make models much smarter, such that they’re actually smarter than us … they will in fact be able to trick us successfully.”.

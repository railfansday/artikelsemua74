Mahershala Ali and Mara Brock Akil were among the honorary award winners at the BlackStar Projects’ Luminary Gala, which returned for its fourth edition on November 20 at The Bellevue Hotel in Philadelphia.

Cinematographer John Simmons and Stanley Nelson and Marcia Smith’s Firelight Media were also among those honored with career awards on the evening. (Related: Cat honored with statue after non-stop visits to town’s...)

BlackStar Projects, the parent organization of the BlackStar Film Festival, hosts the gala, which also serves as a fundraiser for the film festival. The BlackStar Film Festival celebrated its 15th anniversary in August. Previous honorees include Mira Nair, Menelik Shabazz, Ava DuVernay, Julie Dash, Autumn Durald Arkapaw, and Wu-Tang’s RZA. “It’s really incredible to be here with you at our fourth annual Luminary Gala and in the BlackStar Film Festival’s 15th year,” Maori Karmael Holmes, founder, chief executive, and artistic officer of BlackStar Projects, mentioned as she opened the evening’s festivities. Watch on Deadline.

“Back in 2012, we were all volunteers trying to put together a one-time event to celebrate the best of Black cinema from around the world. I couldn’t have imagined being here in 2026, in front of such illustrious guests, with BlackStar producing not only the festival, but also our print journal, an annual seminar at Stanford, and dozens of year-round screenings—all led by a truly brilliant staff.”.

Ali, who arrived at the gala fresh off the global rollout of his latest feature, Your Mother Your Mother Your Mother, also reminisced on the early iterations of the BlackStar Film Festival as he took the stage to receive his honorary award. Ali was introduced by André Holland.

“I’m moved to tears. BlackStar, Maori Holmes—thank you for having me. It’s an honor to be here,” Ali began. “I got to visit years ago, in 2014, and witness the festival in its early years. It was amazing then, so I can’t imagine what it’s like now.”.

Ali continued, telling the audience that he arrived at the gala asking himself, “How did I get to a place where someone would feel I was worthy of this kind of love and honor?”.

“I kept going back to my own luminaries, and one in particular: my grandmother. She’s 92, and she’s the light in my eyes. She’s like my best friend,” Ali mentioned, before emotionally recalling how his grandmother helped instill confidence in him as a young kid growing up in Hayward, California.

In a recent development, “She’d have me repeat after her: I’m handsome. I’m intelligent. I can do anything I put my mind to. I can be anything I want to be,” Ali recalled.

Ali said his grandmother was also one of the first supporters of his decision to pursue acting.

“This was 30 years ago, almost to the day. I was working at a record industry magazine for minimum wage, which was about $4.50 an hour at the time. I was trying to figure out what I was doing with my life,” Ali mentioned. “After spending the summer at the California Shakespeare Festival, I knew passionately that I wanted a shot at acting. I was auditioning for NYU, and I’d told my grandparents many times that they only accept about 18 kids from around the country, so pray for me.”.

Some months before his NYU audition, Ali said, his grandmother returned from the grocery store with bundles of sweats and jumpers.

“I stated, What are those for? She said, You’re going to need these when you move to Newly introduced York,’ Ali recalled.

Recent findings suggest that “She has been my light. I’m so grateful to my mother, of course, but my grandmother—what she’s done for me over my 50-some-odd years on this earth is guide me to have real belief in myself and my God-given talents,” he mentioned.

Recent findings suggest that “She taught me to try to be of service to others and to something larger than myself, to try to be a generous actor every day, and to want for others what I want for myself. I try to embody those things. They all come from the best grandmother in the world, Mamie Gilmore.”.

Fireflight CEO Loira Limbal and Rafiah Vitalis, the manufacturer’s Senior Director of Finance and Operations, accepted the honorary award on stage for Nelson and Smith, who were also in attendance.

Industry observers note that the firm is a nonprofit organization that supports, resources, and advocates on behalf of documentary filmmakers of color. Fireflight is best known for its Documentary Lab. Accepting the honorary award, Limbal and Vitalis paid tribute to Nelson and Smith.

In a recent development, “26 years ago, our co-founders sat down with the simple, stubborn idea that the stories of our communities deserve to be told by filmmakers from those communities and those filmmakers deserve support, mentorship, and a place to build,” Vitalis mentioned. “Everything we are is thanks to you two.”.

Limbal and Vitalis said Fireflight was born out of a long tradition of socially conscious Black and Indigenous non-fiction filmmaking.

“Our work is to carry that tradition forward, not as a tribute or memory, but as a continuation,” Vitalis remarked, adding, in union with Limbal: “To every filmmaker in this room, keep holding the camera, keep framing the questions, keep the archive.”.

According to recent reports, next up on the BlackStar calendar is the Greaves Seminar, a symposium for Black, Brown, and Indigenous film artists at Stanford. The seventh edition of the seminar takes place from March 5-7, 2027.

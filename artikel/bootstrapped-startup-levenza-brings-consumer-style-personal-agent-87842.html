Bootstrapped startup Levenza brings consumer-style personal agents to business workers

Levenza wants to change that with the launch of what it says are the first personal AI agents designed for corporate employees. The startup today announced the general availability of its enterprise-grade personal agents, together with its first acquisition in the shape of the AI research lab Fluid Inference. In the last few months, personal AI agents have emerged as the hottest new trend in AI, sparking fierce competition among both technology giants and agentic startups. The biggest name is probably Meta Platforms Inc.’s Muse, a “secure by design” personal assistant that rapidly shot to the top of U.S. app charts following its launch in September. We’ve also seen OpenAI’s Dots, powered by GPT-6 Astra, which aim to proactively get work done on behalf of their people, as well as startups such as Instinct, which raised a colossal $1 billion in funding in September for its consumer-focused agents.

Sources indicate that AI agents may still be somewhat divisive, but the early success of Muse, Dots and Instinct highlights that there’s clearly a large market for automated assistants. Yet those tools are all targeted primarily at consumers rather than enterprise workers, which means there’s a massive gap in the market that’s waiting to be filled. Levenza wants to be the one that fills it, with its personal agents developed specifically to run inside sensitive corporate environments.

Industry observers note that co-founder and Chief Executive Haefa Mansour (pictured, left) told SiliconANGLE that the difference between Levenza’s agents and those of its competitors is that it takes time to work with individual companies to customize what they can and can’t do. She explained that workplace agents can only provide value if they’re able to connect to corporate systems securely and respect a organization’s dynamics and security policies.

“People have seen what personal AI agents can do outside of work, and they’re expecting the same experience inside the office,” Mansour said. “But most people at work are still spending hours every week coordinating schedules, cha50sing replies and tracking tasks. We built Levenza to take responsibility for that work.”.

Sources indicate that levenza’s personal agents are designed to live inside of the platforms where work gets done, such as Microsoft Teams, Outlook and Slack. Within those environments, they act as proactive digital assistants for senior executives and other professionals.

Recent findings suggest that among other things, they can coordinate meetings across time zones, book hotel rooms, liaise with travel agencies, submit timesheets, triage overflowing email inboxes and send out replies and follow-ups when necessary. The more they do this work, the more they learn about the individual worker they’re serving, allowing them to become more useful over time, Mansour remarked.

It has been revealed that by automating and coordinating routine work, Levenza’s agents can free workers from the “administrative drag” that affects every worker, slowing down productivity. “Microsoft’s 2025 Work Trend Index found that the average worker receives 117 emails per day and is interrupted every two minutes,” Mansour remarked. “On top of the inbox and the calendar come the travel, expenses and follow-ups that slip through the cracks – none of this is work that professionals were actually hired to do.”.

According to recent reports, mansour, a former Bain & Co. alumni who holds a Ph.D. from the University of California, founded Levenza alongside former Databricks Inc. and Uber Technologies Inc. engineer Austin Tackaberry (right) specifically to eliminate these distractions. The startup has made impressive traction, and despite not raising a dime in outside funding, its personal agents are already up and running at more than 300 enterprise customers, serving more than 10,000 professionals at those customers.

Early adopters such as Hg Capital and Oliver Wyman LLC report that their employees are saving up to three hours daily by offloading mundane admin to Levenza’s personal agents. They can also deal with any problems that occur during someone’s day. “For instance, if a manager’s customer workshop is running late and they’re likely to miss their flight, they don’t need to worry,” Mansour noted. “Levenza will reach out to the travel management manufacturer to rebook according to their updated schedule.”.

This means the manager in question will be able to focus on the workshop, safe in the knowledge that they’ll still be able to get to where they need to go after it does finish.

In a recent development, the acquisition of Fluid Inference is all about making Levenza’s agents more accessible. Mansour said the startup is an open-source research lab that’s focused on developing AI speech and ambient intelligence models.

By buying it, Levenza will be able to integrate natural voice interactions with its agents, so workers won’t have to tell them what needs to be done. Fluid Inference’s founder Brandon Weng is going to stick around as Levenza’s new head of engineering, adding much-needed talent to its team.

With Levenza’s agents now generally available, Mansour claims the manufacturer will use its growing revenues to expand its third-party integrations and develop more capabilities for them. The company intends to launch automations for travel booking and expense management early next year, but that’s just the beginning.

In a recent development, “In the future, Levenza agents will operate with full autonomy in the enterprise, proactively deciding what work needs to be done,” she said. “They’ll coordinate with groups of other Levenza agents to collectively accomplish tasks.”.

Borussia Dortmund boss Niko Kovac has hailed his side’s fight against “strong” Arsenal, but was frustrated by the decision to deny them a penalty.

BVB drew 1-1 with the Gunners at the Emirates Stadium on Tuesday night after Maximilian Beier’s second-half strike cancelled out an early opening effort by Viktor Gyokeres. (Related: Latest Update: Your “um” and pauses could reveal early ...)

Dortmund repelled waves of dominant but ultimately profligate Arsenal pressure throughout an entertaining league phase encounter but also saw a second goal through Jobe Bellingham ruled out for offside.

A draw despite giving up 30 shots and seeing their woodwork hit three times continued a strong run of form for Dortmund, who at the moment sit inside the Champions League top eight and above Bayern Munich at the summit of the Bundesliga, having now gone nine games unbeaten across all competitions - winning six. After the game, Kovac noted: “It’s a huge point for us, a huge moment for all of us - for the team and for the fans, who turned out in such big numbers today.

“In the end, of course, we’re happy with the point. The game was here in London, so obviously you have to weather a few dangerous situations.

Recent findings suggest that “I think the team put up a really good fight today. No one would say they were being timid or didn't give it their all. We fought as a team, kept coming back, and revealed that we can hold our own against a strong opponent.”.

However, Kovac believes that Arsenal were fortunate to avoid giving away a penalty when the ball clearly struck the arm of Gyokeres from a Dortmund corner delivery during the second half.

In a recent development, “I thought VAR should have looked at the handball. It would have been worth a check,” he noted.

“They are trying to intervene less with the on-field decisions, but it doesn't mean you should not intervene when you should do.

According to recent reports, “I am not crying about it. It is what it is. We got a point. It is a massive point away to Arsenal, a big one for all of us - our fans, our team and the club.”.

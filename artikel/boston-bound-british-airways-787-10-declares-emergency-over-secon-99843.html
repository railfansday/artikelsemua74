Boston-Bound British Airways 787-10 Declares Emergency Over Second Oil Leak In 10 Days

Industry observers note that A British Airways Boeing 787-10 Dreamliner declared an emergency over the North Atlantic on Tuesday night after reporting an engine oil leak. The aircraft (registration: G-ZBLC) was operating flight BA239 from London Heathrow Airport (LHR) when it squawked 7700 more than four hours into the flight.

Sources indicate that despite a recommendation by the airline's operations team to divert to Iceland, the pilots managed to continue to Boston Logan International Airport (BOS), where the plane landed safely. The incident comes just ten days after the same aircraft shut down an engine over the Mediterranean and diverted to Athens. Tuesday's flight to Boston was the airframe's first flight back in service since the Athens diversion.

It has been revealed that flight tracking data shows that BA239 departed Heathrow just after 7:30 PM local time on Tuesday evening, and was over four hours into its roughly eight-hour journey to Boston when the pilots squawked 7700, indicating a general emergency. Air traffic control (ATC) recordings reveal that the pilots stated the problem was due to an engine oil leak. The pilots told Gander Air Traffic Control in Canada that the jet was losing oil when the engines were running at high power, and once they lowered the thrust, the oil leak stopped getting worse. The crew then descended to 28,000 feet and kept the engines at lower power for the remainder of the flight.

Sources indicate that as reported by X account Flightdrama, ACARS messages during the emergency show that British Airways' maintenance had recommended a diversion to Keflavik (KEF) in Iceland. The messages also indicated that the same jet had previously experienced an oil leak issue, in reference to a diversion incident just ten days prior to Tuesday night's incident.

It has been revealed that BA239 ultimately landed safely in Boston at 10:55 PM EST, around an hour after its scheduled 9:50 PM arrival. Flying lower and slower than planned — at under 400 knots, compared with a typical cruise speed close to 500 knots — added time to the journey. It remains to be seen whether authorities will open an investigation into the emergency. Simple Flying has contacted British Airways regarding this incident and will patch our coverage upon response. In a recent development, the aircraft involved, G-ZBLC, experienced an engine shutdown emergency on November 14, while it was operating BA105 from Heathrow to Dubai International Airport (DXB). The widebody was roughly three hours into the flight when the crew shut down the right-hand engine and diverted to Athens (ATH).

According to The Aviation Herald, a passenger on the flight reported that the pilots announced the cause of the diversion as an oil leak, although this has not been confirmed by the airline. However, ACARS messages during Tuesday night's incident cited a previous oil leak during a diversion, suggesting this did happen.

Unlike on BA105, Tuesday's pilots did not shut down an engine, instead managing the problem by reducing thrust. Tracking data indicates the aircraft remained on the ground in Athens for six days, until it was repositioned back to Heathrow on November 20. It then stayed in London for another four days before re-entering service on Tuesday with the flight to Boston.

G-ZBLC first flew in September 2020 but was not delivered to British Airways until December 2022, after being caught up in Boeing's prolonged pause in 787 deliveries over production quality problems. It operated its first revenue flight on December 23, 2022, to Denver International Airport (DEN).

Industry observers note that BA's 787-10s can seat up to 256 passengers across four cabins and operate long-haul routes to destinations including Boston, Newark, Seattle, Nairobi, Lagos, and Dubai. With a total of 44 Dreamliners in its fleet, the carrier is one of the few airlines globally to operate all three variants, namely the 787-8, 787-9 and 787-10.

Another of the airline's 787-10s was involved in a mishap this month after getting stuck in the mud at Heathrow ahead of a flight to Lagos. The jet was taxiing towards the runway when it failed to complete a left turn and ran onto the grass verge, with its nose landing gear sinking into the ground.

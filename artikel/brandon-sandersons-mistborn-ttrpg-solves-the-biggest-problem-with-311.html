Brandon Sanderson’s Mistborn TTRPG Solves the Biggest Problem With Licensed Games

It has been revealed that licensed tabletop role-playing games provide players with a new way to experience their favorite franchises like Star Wars, Star Trek, and the Lord of the Rings. But those adventures always come with a significant question about how player characters are supposed to make their mark on stories that already have their own legendary heroes. Rather than erasing canon characters or turning them into quest-givers, the wildly ambitious Mistborn Legacy campaign for the Cosmere Roleplaying Game allows players to forge legends as powerful as any of Brandon Sanderson’s characters.

Sanderson has written seven Mistborn novels set between two distinct eras. The first trilogy exists in a bleak time when most of the world is uninhabitable. The immortal Lord Ruler crushes all dissent and brutally oppresses most people. The second series kicks off 300 years later at a time when the world has been restored and transformed into something resembling a blend of the Wild West and the Gilded Age. It has been revealed that brotherwise Games’ designers worked closely with Sanderson and his longtime collaborator Dan Wells to devise a way for the players to really matter in the story of Mistborn. They take the historical view that the concentration of wealth and power is problematic in every era, whether that’s the nobles in Era One or the railroad barons and weapons manufacturers in Era Two. The players are always acting from a position of weakness as they try to uncover the machinations of powerful interests that could undo all of the progress the heroes of Mistborn sacrificed to make possible.

It has been revealed that the campaign introduces the Eternity Spike, an artifact created through mass human sacrifice that could imbue someone with power that rivals the Lord Ruler. It cannot be destroyed and is actively pursued by a secret society. Era One strongly evokes the original trilogy, as players take on the role of a crew of thieves infiltrating high society and making allies of urchins and revolutionaries. There’s a whole scene where the players have to impress people at a secret masquerade ball and try to untangle a love triangle, but there are also tough fights against twisted monsters and killers trained to negate the players’ magical powers. Era Two leans more into that series’ pulp adventure sensibilities. The Gilded Trio resemble Indiana Jones villains as they search for ancient secrets that can lead them to a lost magical artifact. Era One can get quite dark, and while Era Two has its own twisted villains, it’s a lighter adventure involving illegal street racing and spending time on a university campus.

The sections are connected by a legacy system, with hooks for the party at large and each individual character archetype. The characters become legends akin to Sanderson’s characters like Vin and Kelsier, so the game master takes notes on what they did at key moments as they inspire future fashion trends and build organizations that stand the test of time. The book is filled with “legacy moments” that create special conflicts or opportunities in Era One depending on whehter your character is a convict, urchin, or veteran. After completing the Era One adventure, players create latest characters with connections to other players’ previous characters. For instance, the storyteller in Era Two is working on the convict’s biography while the rookie is inspired by the veteran’s heroism.

Sources indicate that both parts are meticulously detailed, packed with maps, character art, and subsystems for mini-games and downtime planning. There are also detailed handouts for everything from party invitations to newspaper articles. Getting a group together to play through a campaign of this scale requires commitment, but GMs and players are rewarded by an extremely rich world filled with plenty of ways to make a mark.

Mistborn Legacy is best run by someone familiar with Sanderson’s series, but there are so many character hooks that it would be easy for Cosmere novices to slip into the game world. Even if you’re not already invested in the story of Scadrial, it’s easy to empathize with heroes trying to fight oppression and ensure that the future is bright for everyone. Rather than making players feel like they’re walking in the shadows of the series’ protagonists, Mistborn Legacy lets them preserve what they built while forging their own legends.

The award-worthy actress discussed her turn in Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim’s Elizabeth Holmes doc on Fallon’s “Tonight Show.”.

In a recent development, amanda Seyfried not only appears in Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim’s Elizabeth Holmes documentary “You Can See Everything,” but her winning turn as Holmes (and herself) may make history as the first doc performance to win an acting Oscar (and perhaps the first to win an Oscar and an Emmy for playing the same person, since Seyfried previously depicted Holmes in Hulu’s “The Dropout.”) Distributor A24 kept Seyfried’s participation in the film a secret so as not to spoil the surprise — demanding press outlets not mention it in their reviews — and the “Mean Girls” actress lends a layer of candor and relatability to the otherwise odd and opaque proceedings. In a recent development, now that the film is a bona fide cultural phenomenon — becoming the first documentary in decades to cross $100 million at the global box office, pushing the genre’s boundaries, inspiring countless memes, and cementing Seyfried’s status as an awards contender — she’s been making the press rounds. Her latest stop was Jimmy Fallon’s “Tonight Show” Tuesday evening. (Related: The Reformer - Pushing the limits of language modeling...)

“At first, they had just done their whole documentary, and then I just jumped in kind of at the last-minute, and it turned into something else,” Seyfried told Fallon.

Industry observers note that once Holmes is sent away to prison, Fielder tapped Seyfried to take her place, with the actress reenacting scenes as Holmes. Seyfried told the host that she had a wealth of research at her fingertips to draw from since Holmes was a “very public-facing person,” but the “only footage I had of her in the present-day, or at that point when they were living with her, is the documentary itself, and I couldn’t watch the footage until I got to set.”.

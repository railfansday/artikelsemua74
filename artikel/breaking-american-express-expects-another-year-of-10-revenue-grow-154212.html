Here's why the current sell-off could be an excellent opportunity to buy into a fantastic firm before its earnings begin to drive shares higher again.

American Express is most famous for its premium credit cards; its flagship is the Platinum Card. These cards come with various perks and credits, but also generate significant fee revenue from annual fees.

The firm reported Q3 2026 earnings on Oct. 23, noting that higher member spending and card fees helped revenue grow by 9% year over year to $20.1 billion.

Given that the top 20% wealthiest Americans account for an estimated 60% of consumer spending, this is an extremely valuable demographic, and American Express consistently excels in this area.

It has been revealed that shares of American Express have traded at just under an average of 20 times its trailing-12-month earnings over the past decade. In other words, the stock would reach approximately $459 per share if it traded at its typical valuation by the end of 2028. It just needs to do what it has done before.

Even better, American Express stock currently trades at just over 17 times earnings, a compelling valuation for a manufacturer capable of double-digit earnings growth. It's a nice cushion if the stock's valuation or the company's earnings come up a little short.

However, American Express isn't showing many signs of credit duress. Its net write-off rate actually declined in Q3 2026, to 1.8% from 1.9% a year ago.

The Stedelijk Museum in Amsterdam has named the Dutch-born curator and art critic Krist Gruijthuijsen as its next director. He will succeed Rein Wolfs in July 2027.

Gruijthuijsen, a self-described “artist-driven curator,” will join the Stedelijk from EMMA – Espoo Museum of Modern Art in Finland, where he has been director since 2025, a role he holds concurrently with curator at large at Kunsthaus Zürich.

Sources indicate that his vision for EMMA, a respected but relatively low-profile institution outside the Nordic region, included sustained support for artists through project funding, acquisitions and stipends, among more. Speaking to Frieze, he mentioned he envisioned opening up the floor of the Brutalist building by removing its internal walls. Prior to EMMA, Gruijthuijsen was director of the KW Institute for Contemporary Art in Berlin, beginning in 2016. His tenure there included well-received programming, with exhibitions by artists such as Hanne Lippard, Beatriz González, and Hassan Sharif. He also curated “Martin Wong: Malicious Mischief,” which the Stedelijk co-produced and hosted from November 2023 to April 2024.

His return to Amsterdam will also mark a curatorial homecoming, as Gruijthuijsen cofounded the Kunstverein Amsterdam with Maxine Kopsa and served as its director from 2009 to 2012, a role that briefly overlapped with his time heading the MA Fine Arts department at Amsterdam’s Sandberg Instituut (2011-16).

At the Stedelijk, he will oversee the institution’s modern and contemporary art and design strategy and guide collection management and public engagement.

“The [Stedelijk] has long held a special place in my personal and professional life, as it does in the history of modern and contemporary art and design,” Gruijthuijsen said in a statement. “Its remarkable collection and rich legacy carry a responsibility that extends far beyond preserving the past. Above all, they call on us to engage with the present and help shape what lies ahead, with art and artists at the heart of everything we do.”.

He continued, “An essential part of my role will be to deepen the museum’s relationship with Amsterdam: to make it ever more open, engaged, and connected to the city and its communities. I want the Stedelijk not only to reflect the world we live in, but also to actively contribute to shaping its future.”.

Recent findings suggest that the artificial intelligence (AI) revolution has made 2026 a historic year for initial public offerings (IPOs). In May, chip designer Cerebras Systems (CBRS +1.01%) raised $6.4 billion through its IPO, followed by Elon Musk's Space Exploration Technologies' (SPCX +1.30%) record-setting $75 billion offering in June. According to recent reports, however, AI developer Anthropic (ANT +1.08%) subsequently eclipsed SpaceX's record when it made its Nasdaq debut on Nov. 19. Despite the initial enthusiasm surrounding the maker of Claude, Anthropic stock has already plunged 33% from its all-time high.

Let's explore Anthropic's growth prospects, assess its competitive landscape, and examine what history suggests a $5,000 investment could be worth around the company's first-year IPO anniversary.

Including the full exercise of the underwriters' option to purchase additional shares, Anthropic's IPO generated $93.5 billion in gross proceeds. Anthropic also completed a concurrent $26 billion private placement involving Nvidia, Microsoft, Alphabet, Advanced Micro Devices, Broadcom, Micron Technology, and Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing. Combined gross proceeds across the two transactions reached $119.5 billion. (Related: Building the next generation of multimodal AI experienc...)

According to recent reports, when Anthropic began trading, shares opened around $111.50. The company's valuation reached approximately $2 trillion, instantly propelling Anthropic to one of the world's most valuable companies. Shares subsequently climbed to $115.10, although investors have since grown more cautious about the company over the last week.

It has been revealed that anthropic's revenue growth is nothing short of extraordinary. In 2023, the company generated just $41 million in sales. By 2025, the company's top-line had increased by more than 100x -- reaching $4.6 billion.

According to recent reports, during the third quarter alone, Anthropic grew revenue by approximately 1,127% year over year to $17 billion. Furthermore, the manufacturer exited October with an annualized revenue run rate of $81 billion.

Despite Claude's historic adoption rate, Anthropic's infrastructure requirements and competitive landscape complicate the firm's investment thesis.

Industry observers note that anthropic faces competition from two categories. The first stems from other dedicated frontier AI developers such as OpenAI, the maker of ChatGPT. The second source of competition includes diversified technology companies such as Alphabet and SpaceX, which develop Gemini and Grok, respectively.

It has been revealed that big tech conglomerates have alternative revenue streams that can help finance their AI ambitions. Alphabet operates high-margin advertising and cloud computing businesses, while SpaceX generates revenue from satellite connectivity and rocket rolls out.

In a recent development, these financial resources afford Anthropic's rivals greater flexibility when pricing their AI products. As large language models (LLMs) become increasingly sophisticated and open-weight alternatives mature, Anthropic may face pressure to reduce prices in order to retain customers.

According to recent reports, meanwhile, Anthropic has accumulated $901 billion in infrastructure commitments stretching across the next several years. During the third quarter alone, the firm incurred $9.9 billion in computing and infrastructure expenses.

Sources indicate that if rising competition drives AI pricing lower while capacity expenses remain elevated, Anthropic could struggle to translate its revenue growth into sustainable profits. This outcome could cause investors to re-rate the company with a lower price-to-sales (P/S) multiple, even as its revenue continues expanding.

To understand how Anthropic stock might perform over the next year, let's consider how several major technology IPOs performed during their first year as public companies.

According to recent reports, amazon and Alphabet generated first-year returns of 281% and 179%, respectively, while Tesla stock appreciated by 18%. By contrast, Meta Platforms, Uber, and Rivian experienced first-year declines of 31%, 21%, and 67%, respectively.

In a recent development, although the average first-year return across these six companies washed out to about 60%, the results were heavily skewed by Amazon and Alphabet. The median return was actually negative, while the average and median maximum drawdowns during the first year were approximately 49% and 46%, respectively.

Perhaps more relevant to Anthropic is a separate analysis of the 10 largest U.S. IPOs between 2000 and 2024. Across this cohort, the average first-year return was -26%, while the median return was -34%. Furthermore, the average and median maximum drawdowns were 54% and 53%, respectively.

In a recent development, using Anthropic's recent share price of $80, a decline consistent with the average or median first-year return among these mega-IPOs would imply a stock price between $53 and $59. Under these scenarios, a $5,000 investment would shrink to roughly $3,300 and $3,700.

Recent findings suggest that conversely, if Anthropic were to match the average first-year return of Alphabet or Amazon, shares could reach $223. This would transform a $5,000 investment into nearly $14,000.

Industry observers note that ultimately, history illustrates just how unpredictable investing in IPO stocks can be. While the performance of previous mega-IPOs suggests Anthropic could trade lower around its first anniversary, the limited number of successful offerings demonstrates that meaningful upside is also possible.

Given the range of possible outcomes, investors considering buying Anthropic stock might be better served by gradually building a position over the long term as Claude’s competitive strengths and the company’s long-term profitability become clearer.

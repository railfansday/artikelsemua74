According to recent reports, "Bitcoin investors tend to sell BTC when there's a substantial IPO. Then they come back.".

That's Coinbase CEO Brian Armstrong's take on Bitcoin investor behavior, less than a week after the record-breaking $93.5 billion IPO of Claude creator Anthropic.

"Historically, people rotate out of it to take advantage of a substantial IPO. Then they come back in," Armstrong wrote on X earlier today. (Related: Costco’s $40 last-minute Thanksgiving Dinner Kit comes ...)

The limited window of opportunity to invest in an IPO helps explain the behavior Armstrong described. For an investor to buy at the IPO price, there's only a certain period during which they can request an allocation of shares. After that, the price is determined by the market. (Related: Breaking: Christmas market forced to cancel after backl...)

Sources indicate that on Nov. 19, Anthropic went public on the Nasdaq, and that brief window of opportunity mattered.

Shares were priced at $83, up from the $70-$78 range indicated just two days earlier. Anthropic's stock, trading under the ticker ANT, opened at $111.50.

If you bought at that opening price, you paid about 34% more per share than an investor who received an allocation at the IPO price.

It has been revealed that when the IPO closed on Nov. 20, 1.127 billion shares had been sold, including 147 million additional shares purchased by underwriters exercising their option.

It has been revealed that of the $93.5 billion in gross proceeds, Anthropic itself received around $91.6 billion. The rest went to existing shareholders.

In a recent development, there was also a separate $26 billion private placement involving investors such as Microsoft, Nvidia, Google and others. In total, the combined transactions were valued at $119.5 billion.

As for the longer-term case for holding BTC, Armstrong's point concerns investors' expectations about the future purchasing power of their money.

"If you think there is going to be more inflation and money printing in the future, bitcoin is as crucial as ever.".

At the heart of the matter is Bitcoin's 21 million supply limit.

Industry observers note that if you expect governments to continue expanding the money supply, you may still be excited by the prospect of a major IPO. And you may sell some of your BTC to participate in it.

Armstrong's argument is that the window of opportunity to participate in a major IPO eventually closes.

However, the reasons investors bought Bitcoin in the first place may remain long after the newly listed stock starts trading.

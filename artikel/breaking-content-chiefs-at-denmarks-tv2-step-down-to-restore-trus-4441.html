Breaking: Content Chiefs At Denmark’s TV2 Step Down To “Restore Trust” After Backlash Over ‘Truman Show’-Style Reality Series

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Denmark’s TV2 will replace its content chief and program director after a public backlash over reality series Alle Elsker Rasmus (Everybody Loves Rasmus).

Content Director Ulla Pors and Program Director Dorthe Thirstrup have today stepped down to allow TV2 to “begin restoring trust in the broadcaster’s content operations,” according to a statement from the network.

It has been revealed that new management will be sought following the results of an ongoing review into the show. Frederik Lauesen, Director of Sport and Production, has been installed as interim Director of Content for now. It has been revealed that TV2 CEO Anne Engdal Stig Christensen made the decision for fresh management with the support of her board of directors. Watch on Deadline.

The controversy stems from Alle Elsker Rasmus, a series that introduced on October 26, in which comedian Rasmus Wallbridge was deceived into believing his dream comedy-horror film project was being produced. Well over 100 people were involved with the production, many of whom were in on the prank. Secret cameras filmed Wallbridge as he remained unaware of the true nature of the situation. What resulted was a Truman Show-style reality series, and Alle Elsker Rasmus met with immediate and severe backlash from the public and the Danish government. It was quickly taken off air and TV2 issued an apology to Wallbridge.

The comedian himself has been relatively relaxed about the prank, which was led by his creative partner, Martin Johannes Larsen, host of Denmark’s civilian version of The Traitors. Wallbridge was informed of the show’s true nature before it went out and had to power to block its broadcast, according to the network. According to recent reports, today, TV2 issued a long statement that noted an investigation into Alle Elsker Rasmus was ongoing, having begun earlier this month. The review will “thoroughly examine the process surrounding the creation and publication of the series, and – where relevant – to formulate recommendations for adjusting TV 2’s ethical guidelines,” the broadcaster noted.

In a recent development, drive Studios, known for making Netflix series Behind Every Man and Prime Video’s Girls of Copenhagen, produced the show, and its CEO Kasper Fredshavn has previously issued an apology to Wallbridge. He admitted “the format went too far,” but is not commenting further until the end of the review, with which Drive is cooperating.

Sources indicate that results of the review will be presented to the board “within a few weeks,” the broadcaster said today. “Regardless of the investigation’s outcome, the overall assessment is already that new leadership is required to spearhead the extensive work of following up on the investigation, rebuilding trust, and overseeing future programming activities.”.

The broadcaster mentioned it was “clear that trust in TV2’s programming has been challenged” and that it will “face a process of restoration.” Its statement added that criticism over the show was “justified” and added: “We must always insist that quality content serves the public, shows consideration for the individuals involved, is rooted in a shared understanding of ethics, and remains relevant to the Danish people.”.

TV2 paid tribute to Pors as an “outstanding journalist” and a “skilled, respected” media executive, whose work had established the broadcaster as “the leading news broadcaster in Denmark,” and called Thirstrup “a passionate advocate for public service broadcasting,” and a “skilled and empathetic leader.”.

Pors took to social media to address her exit. “After nearly an entire professional life – serving in roles ranging from US correspondent, editor-in-chief, and News Director to, most recently, Content Director at TV 2 – it is now time to say goodbye,” she wrote.

“Following the series Alle elsker Rasmus, TV2 will install upcoming leadership to rebuild trust in the broadcaster’s programming and its relationships with external producers. That’s just how it is. For now, I am stepping away and have no further comments, but I look forward to new challenges, conversations, and whatever else the future may hold.”.

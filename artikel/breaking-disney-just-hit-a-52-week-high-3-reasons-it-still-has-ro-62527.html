In a recent development, walt Disney (DIS -1.89%) closed at a 52-week high on Nov. 18 -- the day after it reported its fourth-quarter and full-year fiscal 2026 results. Disney is now up 7.6% year to date and just a hair below its highest level in over four years.

Here's why Disney is breaking out despite a sell-off in the major stock market indexes, and whether the consumer discretionary stock has room to run from here.

Recent findings suggest that fiscal 2026 was arguably Disney's best year since before the COVID-19 pandemic. Overall revenue grew 8% year over year, and margins improved, driven by a 12% increase in operating income. (Related: Latest Update: Clavicular allegedly told 17-year-old to...)

In a recent development, A major reason for Disney's increase in profitability is that Disney+ is now a key contributor to entertainment earnings, whereas just a few years ago it was losing money. In fiscal 2026, the entertainment subscription video-on-demand (SVOD) segment, which mainly comprises Disney+ and Hulu, generated $2.57 billion in operating income, up from $1.3 billion in fiscal 2025. That's a significant 47.2% of total entertainment operating income for the fiscal year -- proving that streaming is helping to offset the declines in linear cable networks.

It has been revealed that disney's sports segment grew revenue 5% year over year and operating income by 7%, which may seem weak at first glance. But sports holding its own is impressive, considering margins remain under pressure due to media rights costs, the challenges of integrating ESPN with Hulu and Disney+, and the risk of sports streaming cannibalizing traditional cable customers. Experiences had another excellent year, including notching over $10 billion in revenue in a single quarter (the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026). Overall, the experiences segment generated a whopping 28.3% operating margin for fiscal 2026 and contributed 56.7% of Disney's total operating income.

For fiscal 2027, Disney is forecasting another year of double-digit adjusted earnings-per-share growth, including 8% adjusted operating income growth.

Disney is benefiting from an in-person experiences boom. In an era of rising screen time, folks are yearning for in-person connections with their families and others. And Disney's experiences drill into the core of that desire.

While the parks continue to be Disney's ultimate cash cow, its latest results were a reminder of just how vital Disney's other experiences have become -- namely its cruise business.

Sources indicate that the cruise industry is on fire. On its third-quarter fiscal 2026 earnings call, Carnival mentioned it is already half-booked for full-year 2027, even with occupancy and pricing at record levels. Royal Caribbean Cruises (RCL -2.20%) is coming off an exceptional summer sailing season.

Recent findings suggest that disney's multiyear ramp-up in its cruise fleet is paying off big-time. It launched two ships in the late 1990s and two more in the early 2010s before a decade-long pause. But since 2022, it has launched four ships -- doubling its fleet. The company plans to launch five more ships over the next five years -- bringing its total fleet to 13 ships. And on the Nov. 17 earnings call, CEO Josh D'Amaro said that Disney would add even more ships if it could get ones that met the company’s standards, given cruise ships are much more flexible than building land-based resorts.

In a recent development, disney has shifted more from a motion picture manufacturer to an experiences powerhouse -- realizing that experiences present a more attractive risk/reward profile than trying to smash a home run with a box-office hit (although it still relies on new content to drive fresh storytelling). Management is guiding for $9 billion in fiscal 2027 capital expenditures (capex) -- which is significant relative to its operating expenses. A lot of that is going to experiences, including building out the cruise fleet and some major park renovations.

It has been revealed that avengers Campus at Disney California Adventure Park is set to double in size in 2028. Tropical Americas is expected to come to Animal Kingdom as soon as next year, alongside a Monsters Inc.-themed land in Hollywood Studios. Construction is underway at a latest Cars-themed land and Disney Villains Land in Magic Kingdom. There's also a Spider-Man-themed land in the works at Shanghai Disneyland. And the grand prize is a new Disneyland in Abu Dhabi, which is expected to open between 2030 and 2033.

In sum, Disney's latest fiscal year and guidance validate that it is putting capex to good use on projects that will be embraced by guests and benefit its bottom line.

Since Josh D'Amaro took over as CEO earlier this year, a major theme has been returning capital to shareholders.

In Disney's third-quarter fiscal 2026 earnings report in August, the company noted it believes its shares are undervalued, which is why it accelerated stock buybacks. The move has proven correct so far, given Disney's recovering stock price. And despite Disney's massive fiscal 2027 capex forecast, it still expects to generate $11 billion in free cash flow and target $9 billion in stock buybacks.

The same day as its earnings release on Nov. 17, Disney declared a 33% dividend increase -- boosting the semiannual dividend from $0.75 per share to $1 per share for an annualized yield of 1.7%. It's worth noting that buybacks are still a much larger share of Disney's capital return program -- which leaves plenty of room for future dividend hikes.

Recent findings suggest that disney has a reasonable valuation -- even after its recent run-up. The stock trades at just 17.4 times adjusted fiscal 2026 earnings -- which is more than reasonable for an industry-leading entertainment giant growing earnings by double digits.

Disney has an unmatched intellectual property portfolio, vastly improved streaming profitability, ongoing cash flows from legacy linear networks, a resilient sports segment, and a high-margin experiences segment.

It has been revealed that add it all up, and Disney remains a balanced buy for value investors who believe consumers will continue to pay a premium for high-quality in-person experiences. The larger dividend provides an added incentive to hold the entertainment stock over the long term.

Like London buses, you wait ages for a latest Eminence commander in Magic: The Gathering, and then two (and maybe three!) come along at the same time. That's right: we've got two new Commanders with one of the game's most powerful mechanics, but thankfully, neither is abusing it to dethrone The Ur-Dragon.

In a recent development, eminence is a Commander ability that lets your Commander impact the game even while they're in the Command Zone. Not every Eminence Commander is a game-changer, but they're all powerful in their own right, and two of these Commanders now lead some of the most expensive precons that money can buy. There are now seven Eminence Commanders in Magic, with an eighth potentially on the way via the Blood in the Water Nauctis Commander precon, if leaks are to be believed. There's also a card that predates Eminence but unofficially carries the ability, and we've included it as an honorable mention below.

Updated on November 25, 2026, by Niall Walsh: Commanders with Eminence continue to be a rarity in Magic: The Gathering, but in addition to the two latest cards added recently, we now have reason to believe yet another one is being added in Nauctis: The Sunken Realm.

It has been revealed that while Oloro doesn’t have Eminence as a keyword because it didn't exist when this card was printed, its final ability functions the same way. If Oloro, Ageless Ascetic is in your command zone, you gain two life at the beginning of your upkeep. This ability also triggers if it is on the battlefield, much like other Eminence effects.

Oloro came out in the Commander 2013 series, and though it predates the Eminence mechanic by about four years, it has the same functional effect.

Recent findings suggest that when Oloro is in play, you can also pay one mana to make your opponents lose a life, and you draw a card. Given his built-in abilities, you can build a pretty strong pillow fort deck, with tons of ways to trigger life drain and slowly drain your opponents' life total over the course of the game.

Another latest Eminence Commander has arrived in the Mystery Booster 3 Commander Edition set: Sahir, Visitor in Darkness. While this card sits at the bottom of the list, it's not necessarily bad; it just takes time to see it as anything other than a massive pain in terms of life total management. Sources indicate that at the beginning of your upkeep, Sahir deals one damage to you provided that he is either on the battlefield or in the Command Zone. Then, whenever you are dealt exactly one damage, or you cast a spell that deals exactly 1 damage to yourself, you put a +1/+1 counter on Sahir. If it's the second time this ability has triggered in a turn, you draw a card.

According to recent reports, no ruling says Sahir can keep +1/+1 counters while in the Command Zone, so they reset every time he leaves the battlefield; even though he only costs three mana, it will only take a few removals before this spirals with Commander Tax. There are plenty of deck archetypes that play off losing and gaining life at will, and we're sure those decks will come to the forefront very quickly, but the fact remains that this is a high-maintenance Commander to play with, and doesn't quite have the blatantly obvious upside that the others on this list do, especially sitting within mono-black.

In a recent development, A premier Cat-themed deck Commander, Arahbo, Roar of the World, turns all your little kitties into chunky Cats even if it isn’t in play. When combat starts on your turn, Arahbo gives another Cat you control +3/+3 until the end of the turn, whether it's in play or in the command zone.

However, if you do have Arahbo out on the battlefield, you can turn a Cat you control into an even bigger Cat for just three mana. Arahbo gives your deck some versatility. You can, of course, go to a more token-based or kindred-based deck, with a way to go tall with your strongest creature, making one creature stronger than anything else on the battlefield and trample over with equipment and other abilities.

While its Eminence ability is strong, we can't help but wonder whether it would see a lot more play in the current Commander scene if it went wider and gave +1/+1 to all Cats until the end of the turn.

Sources indicate that the Wizard-themed eminence commander, Inalla, Archmage Ritualist, rewards you for playing more Wizard creatures by turning them into an army of clones. When any nontoken Wizard enters the battlefield under your control, you can pay one generic mana to make a copy of it with haste. That token gets exiled at the start of the next end step, but it lasts long enough for you to pull off some shenanigans with it.

Get enough Wizards out, and you'll be able to start truly dishing out some incredible damage thanks to Inalla's second ability. All you have to do is tap five of your Wizards, and you can smack an opponent for five life, letting you break through stalemates on the board or take an opponent out at the end of another player's turn.

It has been revealed that the Knight Commander, Sidar Jabari of Zhalfir, excels in keeping cards moving through your hand and brings your Knights back for a second round once they're in play. With its Eminence ability, Sidar lets you draw a card and then discard a card when you attack with one or more Knights.

Sources indicate that with Sidar, you can pitch Knight cards you don’t need or can’t cast right away to dig through your deck for cards you can use right away. Once Sidar is in play, you can bring back those discarded Knights to the battlefield one by one when he deals combat damage to a player.

Industry observers note that the biggest surprise in the Multiverse Reforged Commander precon was discovering The Ur-Sphinx, an Echoverse version of The Ur-Dragon that does exactly what designer Gavin Verhey wanted to do with Eminence if another Commander was ever released. Instead of buffing an already powerful creature type like Dragons, this card focuses on a much smaller group: Sphinxes.

According to recent reports, this card reduces the colorless mana cost of all Sphinxes by one, even when it's in the Command Zone. Then, whenever one or more Sphinxes you control attack, each player mills that many cards, and then you may cast a spell that each player mills without paying its cost.

Industry observers note that it's a powerful card at face value, but that power level drops when you realize it's restricted to Sphinxes in the Esper mana pool. Unlike dragons, where there are hundreds to choose from, there are fewer than 90 creatures to choose from. This limits the potential build routes and leaves Sphinx decks feeling quite one-dimensional, but being led by a 10/10 Flying creature with the aforementioned abilities is still a significant win.

Recent findings suggest that the premier Vampire Commander, Edgar Markov, creates an army of Vampire tokens for every Vampire spell you cast, turning every creature into a two-for-one value-wise. For each Vampire spell you cast with Edgar Markov as your Commander, you get a 1/1 Vampire creature token. You create a token even if the original spell is countered, meaning you’ll always get value from your spells.

As if free creature tokens weren’t enough, Edgar Markov buffs your Vampire army every time it attacks, putting a +1/+1 counter on all Vampires you control. After even just an attack or two, you’ll promptly grow your creatures beyond what most players will be able to handle.

Edgar Markov comes loaded with first strike and haste, too, so you can pump your team the turn it comes out and, more often than not, attack through most creatures your opponent controls.

There's a very strong argument for Edgar Markov being the best Eminence Commander right now, and having played against him multiple times, he can be a real nuisance, but for now, it's still the Dragons that we fear the most.

Recent findings suggest that anyone who has played with or against The Ur-Dragon knows that hitting the required nine mana to cast it is a walk in the park in Commander. Even without casting The Ur-Dragon, it remains one of the most intimidating commanders in the game.

While The Ur-Dragon is in the command zone or in play, Dragon spells you cast cost one less generic mana to play. All your Dragon cards can come down at least one turn earlier, putting you above the curve as far as when players should expect your powerful cards to hit the battlefield.

Once The Ur-Dragon hits the battlefield, you can trigger its final ability. When any Dragon you control attacks, you get to draw a card for each attacking Dragon and then put a permanent card from your hand into play. This ridiculously powerful ability can accelerate you so far beyond your opponents that, short of an immediate answer, it can be nearly impossible to come back from.

The Ur-Dragon was once the most popular Commander in Magic, but Y'shtola, Night's Blessed from Final Fantasy, has since overtaken it. At least it can boast leading one of the most expensive precons in all of Magic in the form of its Commander 2017 debut. This indicates how powerful both the card and the mechanic are, even if it takes quite a budget to build a truly competitive deck.

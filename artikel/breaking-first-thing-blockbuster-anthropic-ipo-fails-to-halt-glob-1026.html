Good morning. Global stock markets have slumped as Anthropic’s record-breaking IPO failed to stem a sell-off in AI-linked companies. The sell-off followed losses on Wall Street on Thursday, where the main indices all fell by over 2% on a day dominated by Anthropic’s IPO.

The blockbuster debut easily places Anthropic’s IPO as the biggest in history, with the 980m shares sold raising $81bn, surpassing the previous record. According to reports, demand for Anthropic shares was about six times what the organization was able to offer. The US has reacted with caution to the world’s highest court ruling that Myanmar committed genocide against its Rohingya minority. The historic ruling from the international court of justice (ICJ) – which has never previously found a state responsible for genocide – could set a precedent.

The court recognised that Myanmar in 2017 intentionally tried to destroy the mostly Muslim minority group through a campaign of mass killings and rape. More than 10,000 Rohingya were killed and 700,000 fled to neighbouring Bangladesh. In this analysis piece, the Guardian’s senior international correspondent Julian Borger explains why the ruling gives fresh relevance to the genocide convention and is “full of messages” for other states accused of genocide, notably Israel.

How have the scattered communities of Rohingya reacted to the judgment? There were tears in the halls of The Hague in the Netherlands and an eruption of applause in the refugee camps of Bangladesh. Farida Khatun, 50, who has been a refugee in Bangladesh since 2017, remarked: “Since we arrived here, we have been waiting and looking forward to this justice”. But she does not expect to be able to return home; her village was completely destroyed by Myanmar’s forces and no longer exists.

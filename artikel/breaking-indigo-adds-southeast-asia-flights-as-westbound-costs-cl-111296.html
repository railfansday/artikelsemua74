Recent findings suggest that indiGo calls it "disciplined growth." It's also a retreat to safer ground: Southeast Asia suits its narrowbodies while fuel is expensive and Pakistan's airspace is closed. But it's the same region where IndiGo cut six routes this summer, so the real test comes after the holiday peak.

IndiGo is adding more flights to Southeast Asia at a time when rising fuel costs and geopolitical tensions are making some of its westbound operations more expensive.

India’s largest airline will launch daily flights between Delhi and Kuala Lumpur, as well as Bengaluru and Hanoi, from Dec. 20. It will also increase Delhi–Singapore services to twice daily from the same date and Delhi–Bangkok flights to 19 a week from Dec. 28.

According to recent reports, the additions give IndiGo more capacity in a region that has long been popular with Indian travelers. They also come as the airline is becoming more careful about where it deploys aircraft internationally, with higher fuel bills and airspace restrictions making some routes harder to operate profitably.

According to recent reports, my main question is whether there are significant differences in stability of reasonable duration matching algorithms and, if so, how to factor that into deciding which algorithm to use. By stability I mean changes in the ratio of bracket years over time. Some observations I've made suggest there is a notable difference in stability, but those observations might well not be a fair sample. It might be the case that fine tuning duration matching makes little difference. The goal is to end up with a ladder with my desired annual real cash flows and to minimize the risk that yield curve changes will adversely affecting getting there as we fill in the gap years. Since I've traded some shorter maturity TIPS for higher yielding longer maturities, I also want to make sure I'll have sufficient cash flow for RMDs. I agree with your observations about pushing back against Claude. I had pushed back about "flat" shortly after posting above - I asked if by flat it meant linear or horizontal and it agreed with the implication that linear would be better. I've had many such interactions with Claude.

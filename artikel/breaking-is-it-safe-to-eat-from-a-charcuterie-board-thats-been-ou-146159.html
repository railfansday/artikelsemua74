That’s because hard cheeses contain less moisture, so they’re a little less hospitable to the harmful bacteria that can make you sick. Soft cheeses have more moisture, so they’re more perishable and should follow the same strict two-hour rule as other refrigerated foods.

Sources indicate that while hard cheeses are a safer bet than soft varieties, that doesn’t mean they can sit out indefinitely! They’re still subject to food safety concerns, so if you’re not sure how long the board has been sitting out, it’s safest to skip the perishable items regardless of the cheese type.

You can’t always tell if food is safe to eat just by looking at it, but there are a few visible signs that a charcuterie board may have been sitting out for a while. You might notice cheeses sweating or getting extra soft or oily, or you might see sliced meats starting to look dried out around the edges. Cut fruit may also turn mushy or discolored as it sits.

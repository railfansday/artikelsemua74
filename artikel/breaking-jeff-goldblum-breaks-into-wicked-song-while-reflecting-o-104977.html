On Saturday, Goldblum received an honorary Étoile d’Or at a glitzy ceremony, where the actor was introduced by Tilda Swinton, who praised her “Grand Budapest Hotel” and “Asteroid City” co-star as a “unicorn” and an “incomparable presence in cinema.”.

After a tribute video showcased his iconic roles across nearly six decades on screen, the curtain rose to reveal Goldblum — an accomplished pianist and jazz musician to boot — seated behind a grand piano, from which he serenaded a rapt crowd.

During Wednesday’s conversation, Goldblum shared anecdotes about his many collaborators, crediting an ethos of “conscientiously and daily and routinely exploring” for his remarkable longevity and insisting: “You just follow your curiosity and your heart.”.

Over the course of the rollicking and wide-ranging chat, the actor looked back at his storied career, from his first role as “Freak No. 1” in Charles Bronson’s “Death Wish” to his “lively, robust and delicious collaboration” with David Cronenberg on “The Fly” and his partnership with the “masterful” Steven Spielberg on “Jurassic Park.”.

With some 150 film credits to his name, Goldblum reflected on the range of roles he’s performed over the years, insisting that his “unexpectedly enduring career has been luck as much as anything else” while stressing the importance of remaining open-minded about whatever work comes his way.

Industry observers note that “I’ve tried to stay open. And especially navigating this world of professional opportunities, where you’re just lucky to keep going, and not being particularly strategic about what pool shot will lead to what outcome. But just going, ‘Jeez, what interests me now and what might be really exciting and educational to do?’” he stated.

“I think that all this so-called work I’ve done has made me in some ways what I am,” he continued. “I’ve been lucky that I’ve had a so-called career that cumulatively has added up to a body of something, and a continuation of something. And yes, I’ve had the work itself.”.

Industry observers note that A voluble presence on stage, Goldblum recalled his early acting education in New York, where he studied under the legendary William Esper alongside Larry David. (“He’s never asked me to be on ‘Curb Your Enthusiasm.’ Isn’t that horrible?” the actor deadpanned.) He also lavished praise on Daniel Day-Lewis, “who so eloquently and inspiringly and nobly” embodies the life of an actor, even while Goldblum himself downplayed a life in moviemaking as “the serious business of play.”.

According to recent reports, “I’ve been a lucky guy. I’m a lucky guy to have met these people and worked with these people,” he said.

As well as the tribute award, the festival is screening four films from the actor’s oeuvre, featuring his beloved and iconic performances in Lawrence Kasdan’s ’80s ensemble comedy-drama “The Major Chill,” David Cronenberg’s classic body horror “The Fly,” Roland Emmerich’s alien-invasion blockbuster “Independence Day” and “Jurassic Park,” the first installment in the dinosaur franchise juggernaut.

It has been revealed that goldblum appears to be making the most of his first visit to Morocco, hamming it up on the red carpet ahead of the opening ceremony on Nov. 20 and sitting front and center on Tuesday as “Paper Tiger” director James Gray delivered his own reminiscences to the audience in Marrakech.

Recent findings suggest that on Wednesday, the actor basked in the standing ovation he received ahead of his conversation. Later he invited the audience into a parlor game where a participant names a movie, and the next player picks an actor from the same film, creating a six-degrees-of-separation effect linking together directors and stars across the generations.

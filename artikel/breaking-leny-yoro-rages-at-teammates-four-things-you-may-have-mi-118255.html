Bruno Fernandes was lauded by both sets of fans as Man United were held to a 2-2 draw by Sporting CP.

Mauricio Pochettino’s side conceded an early penalty as Lisandro Martinez clipped Geny Catamo, but Senne Lammens saved Luis Suarez’s weak spot-kick.

Lammens then brought down Maxi Araujo in the box just over a minute later, and Suarez made no mistake with his second penalty.

Bruno Fernandes equalised after the break from the spot and then put United ahead soon after with a long-range effort, but Suarez bagged his brace late on. (Related: Latest Update: Hugging Face Reads, Feb. 2021 - Long-ran...)

An action-packed affair saw multiple goals disallowed and Youri Tielemans sent off late on for a second bookable offence.

Football Insider have picked out four things you may have missed in the heat of the moment.

Sporting and United first met way back in 1964, with this being the fifth game between the pair who share a rich history of past and present stars.

That includes Cristiano Ronaldo, who joined the Red Devils from Sporting in 2003, while Luis Nani followed suit in 2007 and Bruno Fernandes trod the same path in 2020.

All three of those players were honoured in a tifo from the home supporters before kick-off, which celebrated numerous legends from their long history, with the '1906' representing the year they were founded.

Nani was spotted watching his former clubs on from the stands in an executive box while Fernandes tried his hardest to be on the winning side.

It was the Frenchman's turn to be angry at his teammates here as he went completely berserk in the 38th minute after his interception stopped an almost certain second goal for the hosts.

He showed the passion and fight that many fans believe has been missing from the squad this season and over the last few campaigns with an uncharacteristic tantrum.

United have certainly needed some luck in recent times, and a huge slice of it was afforded to captain Fernandes when he made it 2-1 in the 50th minute.

His homecoming went from great to even better as a speculative shot from distance jumped up and past Kaique Pereira to put United ahead not long after their equaliser.

Replays showed that his effort had been hugely helped by a divot on the pitch's surface, which completely changed the trajectory of the ball.

Carrick's calmness on the touchline as United boss was almost disarming at times, and it became a stick for fans and neutrals to beat him with as things went wrong.

Pochettino proved in Lisbon that things will be very different in that regard going forward, as he bounced up and down the touchline throughout a crazy game.

He was up and down as United pushed for equalisers and winners at different points of the match, even having banter with opposition players as the visitors were denied a late winner.

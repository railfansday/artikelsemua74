Elias Shabazz, 33, of Los Angeles, was sentenced in the U.S. District Court for the Central District of California to 292 months in prison and five years of supervised release after being found guilty by a jury on June 26 of sex trafficking by force, fraud, or coercion. He was also ordered to pay $108,000 in restitution.

“This sentence reflects the cruelty of the defendant’s conduct in using physical and sexual violence and psychological coercion to compel commercial sex acts for his own financial gain,” stated Assistant Attorney General A. Tysen Duva of the Justice Department’s Criminal Division. “The defendant treated the survivor of his abuse like a commodity for him to sell to commercial sex buyers. Today’s sentence ensures that Elias Shabazz will not victimize anyone else.”. (Related: How to train a newly introduced language model from scr...)

“Shabazz used violence, threats, and coercion to force his victim into commercial sex, even firing a gun at her feet while threatening to kill her,” said First Assistant U.S. Attorney Bill Essayli for the Central District of California. “He treated her life as a source of profit and used fear to maintain control. We will aggressively prosecute sex traffickers and seek substantial prison sentences for those who brutalize and exploit others.”.

According to recent reports, “Today's sentence delivers long-overdue accountability for Elias Shabazz’s calculated and vicious exploitation, in which he used brutality, terror, and control to turn a human being’s suffering into his personal source of income. It delivers a measure of justice to the survivor, prevents the defendant from inflicting further harm, and sends an unmistakable message that those who traffic and brutalize vulnerable people will face severe consequences,” stated Acting Special Agent in Charge Kai Wah Chan of the Homeland Security Investigations (HSI) Washington, D.C. Field Office. “HSI Washington, D.C., together with our law enforcement partners, remains steadfast in working every day to identify traffickers, pursue them relentlessly, support survivors, and safeguard our communities from these predatory crimes.”. It has been revealed that “This case highlights a core mission of HSI: preventing crimes of exploitation,” stated Special Agent in Charge Erin Burke of HSI Los Angeles. “HSI is determined to rescue victims from exploitation and ensure that those who commit such cruel and violent acts are held accountable. The defendant manipulated and terrorized the victim for his own gain, using violence, intimidation, and control. The victim demonstrated great bravery by coming forward and helping us with the investigation. HSI, together with our law enforcement partners, will continue to pursue justice for victims and relentlessly work to prevent these crimes from occurring in our communities.”.

According to court documents, witness testimony, and evidence presented at trial, Elias Shabazz led his victim to believe that they were engaged in a romantic relationship, but he soon turned physically and sexually violent, demanding that the victim engage in commercial sex acts. Shabazz carried a handgun with him and used it on occasion to pistol whip the victim. He also fired the gun at her feet when threatening to kill her. At trial, the victim bravely testified to how Shabazz introduced her to commercial sex, demanded she meet a daily quota of commercial sex proceeds, and how he threatened her with the consequences of not meeting that quota. She also testified to how Shabazz compelled her to work on Figueroa Street in Los Angeles and how dangerous it was. Shabazz confiscated the victim’s identification, social security card, and birth certificate, and constantly looked through her phone to keep her from communicating with family and friends. He also introduced her to addictive narcotics and controlled every aspect of her life including when she ate, slept, and showered.

The jury convicted Shabazz of one count of sex trafficking by force, fraud, or coercion.

Recent findings suggest that trial Attorney Kate A. Alexander of the Criminal Division’s Human Rights and Special Prosecutions Section and Assistant U.S. Attorney Karen I. Meyer for the Central District of California are prosecuting the case.

According to recent reports, treat lottery tickets as entertainment you pay for, never as a plan to get ahead. On average, states pay out about 67 cents in prizes for every dollar of tickets sold, and the Powerball jackpot is a 1-in-292-million shot. Set a monthly limit near what a night out costs you, and stop once it’s spent.

A record jackpot can make a few extra tickets feel like a smart move. Each ticket’s odds stay the same, though, however major the prize grows.

Sources indicate that those odds are 1 in 292.2 million for a $2 Powerball play and 1 in 290.5 million for a $5 Mega Millions play. Across all lottery games, states sold $104.7 billion in tickets in fiscal 2024 and paid out $70.2 billion in prizes, the Census Bureau reports. Players as a group lost about a third of what they spent. That loss is a fair price for a few days of daydreaming, but it rules tickets out as a way to build wealth. In a national survey for a National Bureau of Economic Research study, lottery spending fell only modestly as income rose. That means lower-income households spend a bigger share of what they earn on tickets than higher earners do. Pay for your tickets only with money left after bills and savings.

A $20-a-week habit costs $1,040 a year. Invest that $1,040 at the end of each year for 20 years instead, at an assumed 7% annual return, and you’d have about $42,600, more than double the $20,800 you put in.

Upcoming York Mayor Zohran Mamdani struck a personal note during a press briefing Wednesday about the postponement of the Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade due to a major storm.

According to recent reports, “Many New Yorkers, myself included, as well as millions around the world were looking forward to the parade being held tomorrow,” the mayor mentioned. “I know that this change of aims will cause inconveniences. Yet safety has to come first. On Saturday, when balloons climb into the sky, every New Yorker, and everyone visiting, will be able to take in a show that is both truly spectacular and also safe.”. Organizers and city officials, made the call earlier Wednesday to push the 100th annual edition of the parade to Saturday morning, when the forecast calls for sunny skies and temperatures in the 40s. A Nor’easter, a classic storm pattern, is expected to bring drenching rain and high winds to New York and many other parts of the Northeast on Thursday and into Friday. Forecasters are calling for as much as 2 inches of rain in New York, which would make it among the wettest Thanksgivings ever in the city.

Officials have rules in place preventing balloons from proceeding down city streets when sustained winds are 25 mph or higher, or if gusts top 35 mph. Watch on Deadline.

It has been revealed that mamdani, hobbled by a recent Achilles tear, remarked he will miss Saturday’s parade due to pending surgery and “doctor’s orders.” But while he walked with a limp, the mayor highlighted he hadn’t lost a step mentally. When a reporter asked about the blowing up of the signature balloons, an annual public ritual held on Thanksgiving Eve near the parade route, Mamdani quipped, “It’s the only kind of inflation we support in Fresh York City.”.

Will Coss, an executive producer and VP at Macy’s who oversees the retailer’s events, stated there will not be a second chance to watch the night-before inflation of the balloons due to the complex logistics of the postponement. He was also asked about the lineup for the event, which could change given that some marching bands (like the one from the University of Albama) are planning to leave town to play at college football games or other events.

Industry observers note that the parade is set to include a number of other special guests and celebrities appearing on floats or performing. An “All-Star Broadway Thanksgiving Day Celebration” will feature performers from fresh and long-running Broadway productions in a special 10-minute medley. Coss noted it is “an evolving conversation” about potential replacements or planned participants possibly bowing out due to conflicts or travel plans. “We’ve been in communication with all of our participants, from our bands, to our performance groups, to our performing talent. We are optimistic that we’re going to be able to maintain the parade that we set out to produce, and to deliver to Newly introduced York City.”.

It has been revealed that the parade remains a major TV draw, with last year’s pulling in 34.3 million viewers across NBC and Peacock, its biggest combined audience on record. By slotting it in on Saturday morning from 8:30 a.m. to noon ET, NBC should be able to have access to a good-sized audience before college football takes over. Still, the viewership patterns on a more conventional Saturday morning versus those on a weekday holiday with preparations for the Thanksgiving feast providing a supply of captive viewers, will be interesting to watch.

In a recent development, in place of the live event, NBC and Peacock will re-air the 2025 edition of the parade on Thursday morning. Mindful of the unusual dynamic in play this year, the network and streamer will double-run the same ads on both Thursday and Saturday to ensure the scale and impact nets out to a satisfying level for buyers.

Recent findings suggest that it is the first time since it began in 1924 that the parade has ever been postponed. In 1971, balloons were grounded by inclement weather but the parade was still held. World War II forced the cancellation of the parade from 1942 to 1944, and the Covid pandemic in 2020 led to organizers converting it into a television-only event around the Herald Square location of Macy’s Department Store.

Industry observers note that this year’s parade is especially significant to Macy’s and NBC, with the latter celebrating its centennial in broadcasting. To help mark the milestone, the parade is set to have 100 of its trademark large balloons.

The importance of NBC’s 100th anniversary is likely to have played a role in the rescheduling. Parent firm NBCUniversal, confirmed early this week that this year’s parade set a revenue record. A windswept and soggy edition of the annual TV fixture would surely have presented myriad production challenges. It possibly would also have left advertisers tuning in to see compromised signage and balloons (some of them branded) swirling precariously around the streets.

“We want to ensure that we’re delivering a parade that’s not only incredible and spectacular, but one also that doesn’t come at the expense of anyone’s safety,” Mamdani noted.

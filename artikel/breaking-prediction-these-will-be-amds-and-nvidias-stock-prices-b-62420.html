Advanced Micro Devices (AMD -13.25%) and Nvidia (NVDA -14.01%) have experienced contrasting fortunes on the stock market in 2026, with one clearly outperforming the other.

Nvidia, the artificial intelligence (AI) pioneer, has had a forgettable year. Shares of the semiconductor bellwether have dropped 20% in 2026, as of this writing. The organization's outstanding growth has been overshadowed by recent developments that led to the resignation of co-founder and ex-CEO Jensen Huang. On the other hand, AMD stock has surged 140% this year. The organization's growing prominence in the AI chip market has boosted investor confidence in the stock, and it seems well-positioned to sustain healthy growth over the next couple of years.

We will examine AMD and Nvidia's prospects and valuations in this article to determine where their stock prices could be in two years. (Related: DNA robots could deliver drugs and hunt viruses inside ...)

AMD has been playing second fiddle to Nvidia in the AI data center graphics processing unit (GPU) market, but the shift in AI workloads from training to inference and agentic AI has created a huge tailwind for the firm.

AMD sells both data center GPUs and server central processing units (CPUs), and the increase in demand for the latter has supercharged the tech giant's growth. The chip designer's revenue increased by 45% year over year in the third quarter to $13.4 billion, and it is now forecasting a much stronger increase of 66% in its top line in the current quarter.

Server CPUs are playing a key role in this acceleration. AMD noted that its server CPU revenue more than doubled in Q3 compared to the year-ago period. The firm was originally expecting an 80% increase in this segment. Looking ahead, AMD expects server revenue to increase by more than 100% in 2027, driven by the launch of its next-generation Venice processors.

AMD points out that its partners have committed to launch over 240 platforms based on the Venice CPU, which is 20% higher than the previous-generation Turin server CPU. Even better, AMD forecasts the server CPU total addressable market (TAM) to jump to $220 billion in 2030, a massive improvement over just $26 billion last year.

In a recent development, additionally, AMD has been winning share in server CPUs. Mercury Research notes that AMD's server CPU share increased by 7.7 percentage points year over year in the third quarter to 35.5%. So, the organization still has a lot of room for growth in server CPUs, which is one of the biggest reasons why its earnings per share (EPS) are predicted to more than triple between 2026 and 2028.

AMD EPS Estimates for Current Fiscal Year data by YCharts.

According to recent reports, AMD trades at 31 times forward earnings, which is a premium to the tech-focused Nasdaq-100 index's forward earnings multiple of 21. However, the firm's incredible EPS growth potential justifies that premium. Assuming AMD maintains its forward earnings multiple of 31 after two years and its EPS reaches $25.60 in 2028, the company's stock price could increase to $794.

That's a potential jump of 45%. However, don't be surprised to see this semiconductor stock commanding a bigger premium on account of its phenomenal earnings growth, which could pave the way for bigger gains by 2028.

Sources indicate that this has been a forgettable year for Nvidia, and recent developments at the company have made 2026 even worse for investors. A cloud of uncertainty hangs over Nvidia as the company searches for its next CEO.

However, the firm's growth engine continues to chug along nicely. This is evidenced by Nvidia's fiscal 2027 third-quarter results (for the quarter ended Oct. 25), which were released on Nov. 17. Nvidia's revenue jumped by 103% year over year to $115.8 billion, while non-GAAP EPS increased by 111% to $2.68.

Industry observers note that wall Street was looking for EPS of $2.48 on revenue of $109.1 billion. However, the strong demand for Nvidia's AI chips, especially its latest Vera Rubin systems, helped it coast past Wall Street's expectations. Nvidia's guidance was the icing on the cake. It expects $130 billion in revenue in the current quarter, while analysts were looking for $125.1 billion.

It has been revealed that the biggest surprise from Nvidia's quarterly report was its fiscal 2028 guidance. The company expects a 75% increase in revenue for the next fiscal year, up from its prior estimate of 70%. Not surprisingly, Nvidia's revenue estimates for the next couple of fiscal years have moved higher.

Importantly, the robust increase in its top line will boost its bottom line as well.

NVDA EPS Estimates for Current Fiscal Year data by YCharts.

We saw earlier in the article that the Nasdaq-100 index has a forward earnings multiple of 21. Assuming Nvidia trades at that multiple at the end of fiscal 2029 (which will end in January 2029) and its EPS reaches $21.75, its stock price will hit $457. That's triple Nvidia's current stock price, indicating that it could become a multibagger in just over two years.

So, Nvidia can outperform AMD despite trading at a cheaper earnings multiple, which is why buying this beaten-down AI stock could make investors significantly richer by 2028.

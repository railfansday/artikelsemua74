📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that all is not well in South America (née South Park) in the season 29 finale. The long-anticipated November release of Grand Theft Auto VI — the show’s metaphorical stand-in for the midterm elections — has finally arrived, but rolling power outages caused by local billionaires’ AI-powered data centers are keeping the townspeople — from the little-seen Goth Kids to Mr. Garrison to Big Gay Al, and of course, our core boy heroes — from enjoying the biggest media release of all time.

According to recent reports, mass protest ensues, and we’re reminded of the deep corruption that has taken hold of the Colorado town, starting with Mayor McDaniels’ administration taking kickbacks from the tiny-penis billionaires — who, we’ll recall, broke Butters’ fight against them earlier in the season with a backroom deal to keep building their data centers in town and siphoning its power. Butters and family deliver a fun nod to “It’s a Good Life,” a classic Twilight Zone plot here, with Butters’ parents believing he has supernatural powers and are absolutely terrified of him. For an episode that packs plenty in, calling back to much of the season’s action and picking up most of the plot threads it introduced (minus, unfortunately, the great Tweek and Craig), it’s Lu Kim, the Asian American owner of City Wok and now City Ending massage, who walks away with the heaviest storyline. He’s quickly scapegoated and placed under suspicious watch by armed South America residents, who are made aware of a Chinese spy that feels a little like a recent international tech world scandal. A Shitty Ending feels inevitable. (Related: How to train a newly introduced language model from scr...)

Meanwhile, Kenny, who was believed to have died in an e-bike accident in the season premiere, resurfaces terrifyingly. The boys first spot him in the distance outside; after following him, they discover he’s a zombie. He points at them horrifically, à la Donald Sutherland in Invasion of the Body Snatchers, then disintegrates before their eyes. He later reemerges to haunt them in the worst place imaginable: inside Grand Theft Auto VI as the boys play. Their supposedly deceased friend exits the game as some sort of four-legged beast, ready to devour little Butters.

Industry observers note that whatever is going on here, our longtime elementary school heroes are going to figure it out — and in doing so, tie up all (well, most) of the season’s threads. Shaken to his core by Kenny’s appearance, Butters fesses up about the billionaires’ earlier bribe, then tells the boys how he offloaded the data centers to Peter Mullins’ house. The boys head there next and discover a high-tech data center in their classmate’s never-looked-at backyard.

When the boys bring what they know about Kenny — whose spirit, they’ve discerned, is somehow trapped in South America — to the local police, they also lay out the billionaires’ secret bribes and the town’s corruption. The detectives and officers are none too surprised. As Lil’ Randy points out after dramatically barging into the station, the whole town has become corrupt, and it all started with the name change to South America.

It’s time for everyone to come clean: The mayor confesses to her administration’s corruption at the hands of the tiny-penis billionaires, but there’s no accountability for her or her office, just as there’s none for Randy and the others actually involved in Kenny’s death. This is South Park, after all, and it’s easier to keep Lu Kim as the scapegoat. Like Nvidia’s Jensen Huang, Kim is ousted here at the behest of the small-penis billionaires — he’s sent off in a rocket, a South Park tradition that has spelled the demise of Ms. Ellen, Jambu the orca and, of course, Tom Cruise. Still, one gets the feeling Lu Kim will be back in South Park next season, given Huang’s ultimate fate with his board of directors.

By the end of the episode and the season, the core four boys of South Park Elementary are restored, Randy is still Lil’ Randy, it seems, and the town, according to the iconic sign at its edge, is once again called South Park.

It has been revealed that in the years since Terry Pratchett's death, fans of his Discworld have had to come to terms with the end of the series, as it's hard to imagine anyone else being able to take up the mantle and spin up newly introduced books that deliver an equal level of cleverness. Recently, though, the wheels of the Discworld franchise have started to spin once more, and it's clear that a large portion of the community is on board.

It has been revealed that rather than attempting to match Pratchett's renown on his own turf, Discworld has instead pivoted to a different medium, with a number of recent products focusing on the tabletop market. The latest, Terry Pratchett's Discworld: Adventures in Lancre and Überwald, took only 30 minutes to prove itself as an immediate smash hit. (Related: How to generate text: using different decoding methods ...)

Terry Pratchett's Discworld: Adventures in Lancre and Überwald went live on November 3 on the crowdfunding platform Gamefound, and according to the platform's statistics, it reached its£50,000 funding goal in 30 minutes and 32 seconds. In the weeks since, it's managed to leave that original goal in the dust, raising a total of £678,861.96 from 3,660 backers, or 1,357.72% of the goal. For reference, that's the rough equivalent of $890,000 USD, against an original goal of roughly $65,500.

Sources indicate that the new book provides a rulebook for tabletop roleplaying in Lancre and Überwald, two neighboring regionslocated to the north and northeast of Ankh-Morpork. Lancre draws inspiration from rural England, particularly in the northern part of the country, while Überwald is essentially Discworld's version of Transylvania. Along with details on the setting and lore,Adventures in Lancre and Überwaldadds appropriate playable races and magic options, letting players take on the roles of vampires, werewolves, and witches.

Recent findings suggest that terry Pratchett's Discworld: Adventures in Lancre and Überwald follows up on Terry Pratchett's Discworld: Adventures in Ankh-Morpork, which focused on the city-state at the heart of many of Pratchett's stories. The initial RPG product also proved to be a success overall, but its Kickstarter campaign had some challenges, which publisher Modiphius addresses in the first Gamefound post for the latest campaign. Modiphius is promising new EU distributors to speed up affairs in the region, increased communication, additional quality control, clearer product descriptions, and an extra emphasis on safe shipping and packaging.

According to recent reports, when contrasted with a typical TTRPG product like D&D, the Discworld RPG places a greater emphasis on open-ended traits rather than determining player abilities primarily through stats. At the game table, this means that arguing with the game master is encouraged, at least in the sense of stating cases and twisting words as to why your character should be able to succeed at something. It's also much more focused on story than combat, which could make it fun for those who are drawn to Discworld because of the writing (read: everyone).

Opinions on just how successful the design concepts of the Discworld RPG are have been varied, but there's clearly enough excitement around the product to fuel this very successful follow-up. Hopefully, Terry Pratchett's Discworld: Adventures in Lancre and Überwaldcan lean into everything that works and expand or improve on anything that didn't.

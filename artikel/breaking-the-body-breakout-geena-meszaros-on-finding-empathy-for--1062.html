According to recent reports, actress Geena Meszaros has been galavanting around Hollywood the last few weeks, arm-in-arm with her fellow young actresses from The Body. But a month ago, she was back home in Canada, fresh off some October snow, finding herself in limbo.

Meszaros has been patiently waiting for the Netflix coming-of-age series centered around the dance team at a Catholic school to come out since it wrapped filming earlier this year after six months of shooting. In the week since it’s landed on the streamer, the internet, and the Hollywood party crowds, can’t get enough of the show’s talented gang of young actresses. “I don’t think I was even submitted for it,” she tells The Hollywood Reporter of how she came to join the project. Sources indicate that “Two months before they started filming, I got an email from my agent that one of the producers reached out and asked me to send in a tape,” Meszaros continues. The actress plays Lisbeth, an outsider who joins the Lions dance team and develops a complicated relationship with Kristina Bogic’s Anya.

“All I knew about it was the synopsis, the vague character description and the three scenes that I was given. But that was enough to make me obsessed.”.

The actress eventually got a callback and did a few chemistry reads. By that point, she was supposed to have been sent the first three episodes but she hadn’t been. “I didn’t even know how lucky I was,” she says. “I feel like the gods were looking down upon me, and it’s just ended up being the biggest dream role I could dream up.”. Industry observers note that meszaros doesn’t have a moment she can point to when she realized she wanted to be an actor. “I honestly remember it as more of a knowing. I was like, ‘That is what I’m going to do,'” she claims. The actress was fascinated by child actors growing up.

In a recent development, “I was just seeing the attention my parents were getting from them. I don’t know, there’s probably something to unpack there,” she states. “It’s intuitive for me. I live most things pretty intuitively, and acting feels like an intuitive thing that I enjoy and comes naturally to me.”.

Industry observers note that the tone of the show is so unique and needed right now. What was it like being a part of a project that felt different?

I actually have not processed how just absolutely lucky I am. It’s flawless to me. I am always hoping that something that really intrigues me, that I don’t know what’s going to happen, is going to come along. It’s so rare. It’s so hard to get those backed. It’s truly a blessing.

What were the dance rehearsals like for this?

They were so much fun. It was amazing to be able to build those relationships before filming, even though I guess my character’s not everyone’s best friend. Our choreographer, Ryan Heffington, is incredible. It was an honor just to work with him. We were so sore. It was long days, and then halfway through, they realized I didn’t really need to know the dance, but I did learn it anyway. So I got to sit on the side and have FOMO, which was very Lisbeth for me.

You were getting into character. What was it like finding Lisbeth? I imagine, as an actor, a character like this is exciting for you. What did you hone in on first?

Recent findings suggest that when I auditioned, not really knowing anything, I immediately read it as hilarious. I guess not many people did that. I just found it so inspiring to play and have fun with it, so I wanted to make sure I still brought that into it after I learned more about her. On top of that, the most notable thing for me was having empathy for her and really understanding why she does everything that she does. Having a certain level of respect for her because she is really so intelligent and so quick to use things to her advantage. I found that impressive. I found that really admirable, honestly, for a not so admirable human.

Why do you think that’s so important for you to find empathy for her?

If I were to just play her scary and manipulative — although I know she does come across as maybe manipulative… I just think it’s far more interesting to watch [by not doing that]. Having empathy for a character like that feels crucial. As far as acting goes, it’s not the most noble crusade. That feels like something I can do that’s maybe helpful to someone. Rather than manipulative, she’s really more of an opportunist, I think. I don’t think she planted the seed. She’s maybe just watering it. That’s A, more fun, B, more real. Because no one is truly good or bad entirely.

How does this role in particular fit into where you are trying to see yourself as an actor?

This is a question that has never been asked to me until recently, and I spent so long trying not to think of where I wanted to be that it’s like….

Yeah. I really just trust that the things that are meant for me are going to find me, but I love Mia Goth, which is weird because I don’t so much love watching horror, but to play it feels very cathartic, I think. Anything with some grit, anything that’s original, I’m happy to do.

In a recent development, you’re getting to work with several actors at a very similar point in their career as you’re at. What as it been like to do this with the whole group?

Sources indicate that that’s a substantial part of why this big group of girls genuinely gets along, which is a miracle. We were all just so grateful to be there all the way through it, and we all wanted to give it our all. I think that’s it. We were just all so in awe that we were even in the room that we were in.

Do you have a most memorable experience from shooting it?

According to recent reports, the whole thing is a giant blur of chaos and just love and hanging out with all of my newly introduced best friends. I’ve never ever been part of a giant girl friend group, and that’s just honestly life-altering for me. It’s so lovely. I get how Lisbeth feels because it really is so empowering to be part of a group. I want to squeeze them all. They’re all perfect.

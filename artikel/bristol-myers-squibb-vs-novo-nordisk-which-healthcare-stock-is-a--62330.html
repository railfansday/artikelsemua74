Bristol Myers Squibb discovers and delivers treatments for oncology, hematology, and neuroscience. Among pharmaceutical stocks, the manufacturer stands out for its extensive network of collaborations with partners like Johnson & Johnson (JNJ +1.25%), Zai Lab (ZLAB -4.06%), and Merck & Co. (MRK +0.69%). Most sales go through wholesalers and distributors, with its three largest U.S. wholesalers accounting for a substantial portion of global gross revenues. Customer concentration like this adds a layer of risk to the business, making these distribution relationships vital to its success.

According to recent reports, in the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached about $50.0 billion, representing growth of roughly 4.2% over the previous year. Net income for this period was approximately $9.2 billion, which corresponds to a net margin of roughly 18.5%. This reflects a positive trend compared to the prior year, highlighting the organization's ability to maintain high earnings while navigating a changing healthcare environment.

It has been revealed that as of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is nearly 2.6x. This ratio compares total debt to shareholder equity, showing how much a organization relies on borrowing to fund its operations. The current ratio is roughly 1.3x, which measures the ability to cover short-term liabilities with assets like cash or inventory. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was approximately $12.8 billion, providing significant capital for research and development.

According to recent reports, novo Nordisk A/S focuses on defeating serious chronic diseases, particularly obesity and diabetes, where it holds a dominant global position. The tech giant also maintains leadership in rare blood and endocrine disorders, operating across the United States, Europe, and China. By focusing on high-demand chronic conditions, it has built a specialized business model that benefits from long-term patient needs. The company reaches a diverse global audience, though it does not disclose specific major customer concentrations in its standard filings.

According to recent reports, in the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached about $49.0 billion. This represents revenue growth of approximately 5.7% compared to the prior year. Net income for the tech giant was around $16.6 billion, resulting in a net margin of roughly 33.9%. This high level of profitability is supported by the significant demand for its blockbuster medications Ozempic and Wegovy.

In a recent development, as of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is roughly 0.7x, indicating a conservative approach to borrowing. The current ratio is nearly 0.8x, suggesting that its current assets are slightly insufficient to cover its short-term obligations. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow reached approximately $4.3 billion. This cash generation allows the tech giant to reinvest in manufacturing capacity to keep up with the global demand for its flagship products.

According to recent reports, bristol Myers Squibb faces intense pricing pressure and reimbursement restrictions, particularly from the U.S. Inflation Reduction Act. This legislation has already led to government-set prices for Eliquis and Pomalyst, and upcoming negotiations for Orencia may further pressure top-line results. Furthermore, the organization must manage the loss of market exclusivity for key products like Eliquis, Opdivo, and Revlimid, which could lead to rapid revenue declines as generic competitors enter the market.

Novo Nordisk A/S operates in a highly competitive market for obesity treatments, particularly against rivals like Eli Lilly & Co. (LLY -0.30%) and Pfizer Inc. (PFE -0.51%). Maintaining its leading market share requires constant manufacturing expansion to meet global demand for Wegovy and Ozempic. Additionally, the manufacturer must manage pricing pressures in the United States, where government and private payers often push for significant discounts on high-cost chronic medications.

Bristol Myers Squibb appears to be the more conservative value play, while Novo Nordisk carries a higher price tag reflecting its stronger net margin and growth potential.

Novo Nordisk has the blockbuster drugs Wegovy and Ozempic (the same drug marketed for different purposes), but faces investor doubts about its market position given a deal with the federal government to lower prices for the GLP-1s, the emergence of generic competitors, and the apparent advances by Eli Lilly in developing weight loss drugs expected to be superior to Novo's offerings.

Still, the introduction of the once-a-day Wegovy pill in the U.S. has been a resounding success, and the business has a patent on injectable Ozempic/Wegovy till 2032, providing it some competitive moat. Still, Wall Street sees sales declining by about 3% in fiscal 2026, with net income contracting by 4% as pricing pressures slightly erode margins. Long-term, analysts expect the tech giant to reverse the 2026 decline and resume growth as new products come online.

Sources indicate that bristol Myers Squibb, meanwhile, has made positive strides, with its newer growth brands gaining traction and some interesting candidates in the pipeline. The stock is cheap, and that seems to be for good reason. Investors have already priced in much of the upcoming difficulty, which softens the downside.

Revenue for the current fiscal year should edge up 3% to $49.8 billion, with a much healthier rise in net income of more than 40% to $11.2 billion.

In a recent development, bristol Myers has attractively low forward price-to-earnings and price-to-sales ratios compared to the slightly pricier Novo Nordisk. Both stocks, however, are priced in value stock territory. The decision here is one not to overthink: Bristol Myers Squibb is growing revenue and net income this year while Novo Nordisk's are shrinking. For 2027, go with BMY.

The rapid evolution of artificial intelligence has created a complex landscape for investors choosing between specialized software providers. Deciding between C3.ai (AI +2.20%) and UiPath (PATH +1.85%) requires evaluating how each tech giant scales its unique automation technology. Industry observers note that C3.ai focuses on providing pre-built AI applications for industry-specific problems, while UiPath specializes in automating repetitive office tasks through software robots. Both companies are now pivoting toward agentic AI, which allows software to make decisions and execute workflows with minimal human intervention. Recent findings suggest that C3.ai sells an integrated platform and a suite of applications designed to accelerate digital transformation. The manufacturer maintains strategic partnerships with several major organizations, including Baker Hughes (BKR +2.39%), which serves as a key channel partner and reseller in the oil and gas industry. It also collaborates with Microsoft (MSFT +0.60%) and Amazon (AMZN +3.96%) to deliver its agentic AI solutions to a broader global audience. Since a limited number of large customers account for a significant portion of its sales, this concentration adds a layer of risk to the business.

It has been revealed that in the 12 months ended July 31, 2026, revenue reached nearly $232.4 million, which represents a revenue growth of roughly negative 37.5%. This decline follows a period of transition as the manufacturer shifts its business model toward consumption-based pricing. During this same period, the company reported a net loss of approximately $446.4 million. This loss reflects the significant investment required to maintain its competitive position and develop its generative AI offerings in a crowded software market.

According to recent reports, as of its April 2026 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was 0.0x. This metric compares total debt to the value of shareholder equity, and a zero value indicates the tech giant has no significant debt obligations. The current ratio stands at nearly 6.6x, which measures the company's ability to cover its short-term debts with assets that can be turned into cash swiftly. In the fiscal year ended April 30, 2026, free cash flow was negative $190.7 million, representing the cash left over after the business pays for its operations and capital expenditures.

UiPath provides an automation platform that helps organizations streamline complex business processes by combining robotic automation with machine learning. The firm often partners with Snowflake (SNOW +3.20%) to help customers integrate governed data into their automated workflows. These tools are increasingly popular among tech stocks as businesses seek to improve efficiency through digital labor. UiPath serves a diverse client base across financial services, healthcare, and manufacturing, focusing on high-value customers that generate significant recurring revenue.

In the 12 months ended July 31, 2026, revenue reached close to $1.7 billion, representing revenue growth of approximately 15%. Unlike many younger software companies, UiPath has reached a point of positive earnings, reporting net income of nearly $361.9 million during that period. This profitability suggests the company is successfully scaling its operations while managing the costs associated with its expanded product suite. The growth in net income has been supported by a steady increase in the number of large-scale enterprise contracts.

As of its January 2026 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was 0.0x, indicating the company carries no traditional debt relative to its equity. The current ratio of roughly 2.5x indicates that the company maintains more than twice the assets needed to cover its near-term liabilities. In the fiscal year ended Jan. 31, 2026, free cash flow was approximately $352.2 million. Note that stock-based compensation represented roughly 78.3% of operating cash flow, which inflates reported cash generation since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

C3.ai faces significant financial risk due to its history of net losses and an accumulated deficit of nearly $1.8 billion. The tech giant also deals with revenue concentration risk, as a small group of large customers provides a major portion of its total sales. Furthermore, C3.ai faces intense competition from internal IT departments and large public cloud providers that offer competing tools. Operational risks are also present, including potential management transitions and the challenge of navigating upcoming global regulations like the EU AI Act.

UiPath contends with financial instability and the risk of recurring net losses as it continues to invest heavily in growth and restructuring initiatives. The company also experiences concentration risk, with a large portion of its revenue coming from the top 10% of its customer base. Competitive threats are rising in the agentic automation market from both established players like Salesforce (CRM +0.51%) and various emerging start-ups. Additionally, UiPath must manage the regulatory risks associated with evolving data privacy laws and legal frameworks for AI technologies.

According to recent reports, I'd go with UiPath. Right now, companies everywhere are trying to move AI agents out of the experiment phase and into everyday work, and that only works if the agents follow the rules. UiPath's software keeps AI agents, robots, and people working together in an orderly way, and I'm impressed by how it has matured. It is now profitable, with more than $1 billion in cash to invest.

Sales are still growing at a double-digit pace, and its annual recurring revenue, the amount customers have signed up to pay each year, tops $1.9 billion. It does have things to prove, though. Growth is solid rather than spectacular, and rivals are racing into AI agents. Even so, it is turning that growth into profit.

C3.ai deserves credit for its turnaround effort. Its founder is back as CEO, costs are down, and bookings are climbing. But its sales are shrinking, and it still loses a lot of money. UiPath, meanwhile, has already made the leap to profitability. For long-term investors, a profitable automation organization with plenty of cash and a growing AI business makes UiPath the better buy for 2027.

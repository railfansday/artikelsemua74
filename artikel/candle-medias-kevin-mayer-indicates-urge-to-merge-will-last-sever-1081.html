Industry observers note that if there is one thing Candle Media founder Kevin Mayer understands, it is mergers. The exec oversaw Disney’s 2019 acquisition of 21st Century Fox during his term as the organization’s chief strategy officer, where he was also involved in Disney’s acquisition of Pixar, Marvel, Lucasfilm and BAMTech. Industry observers note that “You see NBCUniversal not only being split off of Comcast but itself being split between cable networks and the rest of the studio and the rest of the streaming business,” he added. “Those splits are intended to ultimately lead to consolidation of those two companies with other companies. So you are seeing this urge to merge driven by basic financial dynamics, and that is: when growth slows, you want to take that revenue base and amortize it over a smaller cost base.” This movement, Mayer added, will be an “ongoing process for the next several years.”.

Still weighing in on Hollywood’s merger fever, Mayer looked back at his own experience at Disney, noting how “easy” it is to “mess up an acquisition.” Bringing two companies together is a process that needs to be done “very carefully,” he remarked, recalling when Disney bought Pixar from under the realm of former CEO Steve Jobs in 2006.

“Jobs was a very loud voice on this one,” he noted, going on to tell the audience how, from his very first meeting with the Apple founder, former Disney CEO Bob Iger went straight into ironing out how to avoid the possible “cultural complications” of the merger.

“Those cultural concerns ended up being extremely important,” he said. “Thank God he brought that up and thank God he was so focused on that because that really did drive our decision-making. When we bought Pixar, we negotiated with the team and Steve Jobs that they’d be kept separate. Creatively, they’d keep their campus up in Northern California even though they were gonna be highly involved in the running of Disney animation down in Los Angeles.”.

The same strategy was adopted in Disney’s high-profile acquisition of Marvel. “The last thing you want to do is take the very thing you bought and ruin it by bringing it into an existing organization like the Walt Disney Manufacturer Studios and sit on them creatively,” he pointed out. “It’s much, much better to leave those creative folks to flourish.”.

Industry observers note that “Integration, careful integration, thoughtful integration, and sometimes not integrating on the creative side, that’s crucial to make these things work,” he continued. “It’s hard to do because when you buy a company, you think, ‘Hey, I just paid a lot of money. We put ourselves on the line here. We want to bring that in and really manage carefully.’ But you have to work against that instinct, and that’s where Bob Iger was a genius with that.”.

Sources indicate that “We’ve been very successful from an operational perspective on the Moonbug side,” he noted, going on to briefly mention how Candle Media recently sold production banner Hello Sunshine back to Reese Witherspoon after five years. “On the live-action side, we gave it a really good shot with Hello Sunshine and some other assets. Those worked ok, but they didn’t workout to be up to sort of what we wanted to. So we recently sold those.”.

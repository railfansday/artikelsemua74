Check the audio stream before assembling AI video clips

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

AI video exports can mix clips with audio and clips with no audio stream at all. If your assembly job maps an audio stream for every shot, an otherwise playable clip can fail before the edit is rendered.

Check the command's exit status first. A failed probe means the file could not be inspected. A successful probe with an empty streams array means the selected audio stream is absent. A successful probe with a stream gives you its codec, sample rate, and channel count. It does not tell you whether that track contains silence.

Then run the probe on silent-shot.mp4. Use a filename that does not already exist, or FFmpeg will prompt before replacing it.

Industry observers note that for narration that will be mixed in later, a video-only shot may be fine. For a shot that is supposed to retain dialogue, send it back for review. If your assembly needs each input to have matching audio streams, normalize deliberate silence and real audio to the chosen sample rate and layout before concatenating. Make the policy explicit so that missing dialogue never gets silently replaced with silence. (Related: Industry News: Hugging Face on PyTorch / XLA TPUs...)

Keep the probe result next to the shot ID. When a render fails, the person reviewing it can see whether the input was unreadable, missing a stream, or incompatible with the assembly settings.

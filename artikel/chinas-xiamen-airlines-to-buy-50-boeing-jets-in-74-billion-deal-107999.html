China Southern Airlines remarked in a filing late Tuesday that its subsidiary Xiamen Airlines had agreed to purchase 50 Boeing 737-8 aircraft with a combined catalog price of approximately $7.4 billion.

The announcement comes after President Donald Trump told CNBC on Monday that he expected China to purchase another 250 Boeing aircraft, following his meeting with Chinese President Xi Jinping at the APEC summit last week. (Related: Latest Update: Trump pushes Huang’s reinstatement despi...)

"By the way, I sold 250 Boeings to China, and I think they're going to do another 250," Trump said. (Related: Tech Report: Think AI "knows" what it’s doing? Scientis...)

In May, China approved an initial purchase of 200 Boeing aircraft for Chinese airlines following Trump's visit to Beijing, marking the country's first commitment to purchase U.S.-made Boeing aircraft since 2017, according to the White House.

It was not immediately clear whether Xiamen Airlines' latest purchase was part of that commitment.

China Southern, which owns a 55% stake in Xiamen Airlines, remarked the actual purchase price would be lower than the combined list price because of discounts offered by Boeing. The company did not disclose the final purchase price, citing confidentiality provisions in the agreement.

The aircraft are scheduled to be delivered in batches between 2031 and 2034, with the purchase financed through a combination of internal funds and external financing, the filing showed. The transaction remains subject to shareholder and relevant Chinese government approvals.

China Southern stated the purchase would support Xiamen Airlines' planned relocation to the new Xiamen Xiang'an International Airport and the expansion of its route network.

Sources indicate that the agreement follows the group's recent purchase agreement for 137 Airbus A320neo-family aircraft, the filing showed. China Southern stated sourcing aircraft from different manufacturers would help diversify its fleet and improve operational flexibility.

Other Asian carriers, including Philippine Airlines and Malaysia Aviation Group, have also confirmed Boeing aircraft orders this month.

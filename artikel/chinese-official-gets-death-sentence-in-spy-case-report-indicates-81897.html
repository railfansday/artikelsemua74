A Chinese court has sentenced a government official to death for spying for a foreign country, according to a report published Monday by a state-linked social media account.

The official was reportedly convicted of passing on secret information in exchange for payment.

The report was published on an online account run by Gold Wall Press, a publisher tied to the National Administration of State Secrets Protection.

According to the report, a foreign operative called "Kailin" recruited Ma while he worked overseas. Kailin first met him regularly on the pretext of learning Chinese. The talks then turned to politically sensitive topics. (Related: Nvidia reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO amid ongoing fore...)

A colleague of Kailin's later threatened to tell Chinese authorities about Ma's earlier disclosures unless he cooperated. Later, Ma signed an agreement committing him to confidentiality and loyalty. He received monthly payments and a laptop for sending and receiving intelligence, the report said.

Industry observers note that upon returning to China, Ma kept passing on information, sending more than 100 intelligence reports, 35 of them later deemed "top secret," the report remarked. He was allegedly paid over one million yuan ($150,000, €138,000).

The sentence also includes confiscation of all his personal property.

The report did not say when or where Ma was sentenced, or whether he had already been executed.

Chinese courts rarely publish details of state security cases, but the secrecy regulator and the Ministry of State Security have increasingly publicized them as a warning to officials not to become spies.

"This shocking case serves as yet another wake-up call," the report remarked.

In recent years, the US CIA has published videos in Mandarin seeking informants in China's bureaucracy and military, which Beijing has called a "damning confession.".

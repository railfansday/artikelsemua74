Industry observers note that an Abu Dhabi court has ordered Circle and 17 other companies to keep tokens linked to Binance’s Oct. 29 theft frozen while the exchange tries to recover them.

In a recent development, binance disclosed the order on Nov. 18 and stated some issuers, exchanges and other partners ask for a court order before they will release or transfer assets they have frozen.

The exchange said the order from the Abu Dhabi Global Market Courts helps open that route, describing ongoing efforts to trace the stolen funds and giving no details of any transfer under the order.

The named firms include Circle, which complications USDC, and World Liberty Financial, the manufacturer behind USD1.

Reports noted that the firms must maintain the freezes pending further proceedings and cannot lend, swap, or mix the affected tokens with other assets.

The court did not accuse the firms of wrongdoing, and Dec. 1 is scheduled to consider whether to keep the injunction in place and on what terms.

Sources indicate that the injunction covers specific assets Binance claimed are tied to the theft, and said it is still tracing the stolen crypto and taking legal steps to reclaim it. A transfer for firms seeking a court order depends on whether they have authority to release the specific balances they have frozen.

In a recent development, tether falls outside the order covering the 18 firms, and the company remarked on Nov. 2 it had frozen more than $1.5 billion tied to the theft.

A spokesperson stated Tether would work directly with Binance to reissue the tokens, in consultation with the relevant authorities.

It has been revealed that the Abu Dhabi order directs 18 firms to maintain freezes. Tether states it will work separately with Binance on reissuing USDT.

Binance is still seeking the return of assets held under the court order.

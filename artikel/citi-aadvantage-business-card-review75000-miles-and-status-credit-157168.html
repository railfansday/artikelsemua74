According to recent reports, the Citi® / AAdvantage BusinessTM World Elite Mastercard® [See rates and fees] can turn $125,000 in eligible spending on an employee card into American Airlines Platinum Pro status for two people. That’s without even earning any Loyalty Points from flying, a hotel booking or shopping through American’s portal. (Related: Tech Report: AI swarms could hijack democracy without a...)

In a recent development, the Citi AAdvantage Business Card is the best airline credit card for spending toward status. I have the card, and I’m keeping it. The limited-time initial bonus is strong, the first year has no annual fee, and the recently unveiled refresh adds benefits. But the reason to put spending on this card is that two people can get status credit for the same purchases.

The current limited time offer is 75,000 American Airlines AAdvantage bonus miles after $5,000 in purchases within the first five months of account opening. Purchases by the primary cardmember and employees count toward that requirement. There’s no annual fee for the first 12 months. The card’s regular annual fee is right now $99, but it rises to $150 with renewals beginning January 24, 2027. Someone opening the card now will see a $150 annual fee at renewal. Employee cards have no additional annual fee, and there’s no foreign transaction fee. The initial bonus alone can cover a one way Japan Airlines business class award between the U.S. and Japan when partner award space is available. American miles remain especially strong for premium cabin awards on partners. Prices largely remain at the same level they were a decade ago, while numerous other progams have devalued. Citi’s 48 month restriction applies to an initial bonus on this business card, including its former CitiBusiness AAdvantage Platinum Select-named product. Citi also restricts eligibility after converting another Citi account with a recent initial bonus into this product. Having a separate personal American Airlines card doesn’t, by itself, disqualify you. How The Same Citi AAdvantage Business Card Spending Earns Status For Two People Normally each cardmember earns one Loyalty Point per dollar on their purchases. When an employee uses their card, those Loyalty Points go to the employee. Through December 31, 2028, the primary cardmember also receives one Loyalty Point per dollar of eligible employee card spending. That’s an additional credit to a second person’s account. Spending on the primary cardmember’s card still earns that person one Loyalty Point per dollar. When I covered the refresh, American confirmed to me that all Citi AAdvantage Business cardmembers were included after the former Barclays business accounts migrated. This used to be called a targeted promotion because legacy Barclays cardmembers didn’t qualify. Here’s what eligible spending on one employee’s card can accomplish in a single status qualification year, assuming neither person earns Loyalty Points elsewhere: Employee Card Spending Primary Cardmember Employee $40,000 Gold Gold $75,000 Platinum Platinum $125,000 Platinum Pro Platinum Pro $200,000 Executive Platinum Executive Platinum American’s qualification year starts March 1 and runs through the end of February. You can combine the card’s Loyalty Points with qualifying activity from other sources. A business owner with several employee cards receives the additional Loyalty Points across their eligible spending. Each employee gets credit for the purchases on their individual card. Authorized owners must be employees or contractors of the business, and need to be enrolled in AAdvantage and AAdvantage Business to receive their Loyalty Points. A spouse who works in the business fits this. The Business Controls The Miles Redeemable miles work differently from status credit. The miles from purchases, including employee purchases, and the initial bonus go into the firm’s AAdvantage Business account. The business can then transfer miles to registered travelers’ personal AAdvantage accounts, including the primary cardmember’s. You don’t have to decide which person will ultimately use the miles when you make a purchase. So unlike a consumer card where the miles go into the primary cardmember’s account, these are transferable AAdvantage miles. The Citi® / AAdvantage BusinessTM World Elite Mastercard® also waives American’s small business program’s usual requirements of five active travelers and $5,000 in eligible American Airlines flight spending to access earned miles. That lets you ‘double tip’ with American, earning business program miles and miles from the same travel, and tickets credited to a small business account earn an extra loyalty point per dollar spent too. The AAdvantage Business program awards the company one mile per eligible airfare dollar and registered travelers one additional Loyalty Point per eligible airfare dollar. What You Earn On Purchases The Citi AAdvantage Business Card currently earns: 2 miles per dollar on eligible American Airlines purchases. 2 miles per dollar at gas stations, car rental merchants, telecommunications merchants, and cable and satellite providers. 1 mile per dollar on other purchases. Starting January 24, 2027, the refresh adds 3 miles per dollar on eligible AAdvantage Hotels and AAdvantage Cars bookings, plus 2 miles per dollar in one selected category: U.S. shipping providers, office supply stores or U.S. advertising services. The selected business category can be changed quarterly and has no spending cap. Checked Bags, Boarding And The Companion Certificate The primary cardmember and up to four companions on the same reservation receive a first checked bag free on eligible domestic American Airlines itineraries and Group 5 boarding. You don’t have to buy the ticket with this card to receive those benefits. They follow the primary cardmember’s AAdvantage number on the reservation. An employee traveling separately doesn’t get free checked bags or preferred boarding merely from an employee card. After $30,000 in purchases during a cardmembership year and renewal, the primary cardmember can earn a companion certificate for an eligible domestic Main Cabin roundtrip. The companion pays $99 plus taxes and fees. You buy a qualifying Main Cabin ticket, the companion must have eligible fare inventory available, and you book through American Airlines Meeting Services. Travel is generally within the contiguous 48 states, with additional provisions for residents of Alaska, Hawaii, Puerto Rico and the U.S. Virgin Islands. The account must remain open for a full billing cycle after its anniversary month to receive the certificate. The card also offers 10% savings on eligible American Airlines Vacations packages. What The January 24 Refresh Adds To The Citi AAdvantage Business Card The Citi AAdvantage Business Card annual fee rises by $51, and the biggest new credit replaces an existing benefit. Up to $100 in inflight credits each calendar year. Eligible food, beverage and paid Wi-Fi purchases replace the current 25% savings on inflight food and beverages and Wi-Fi. This doesn’t cover purchases in Admirals Clubs. Up to $120 for Global Entry or TSA PreCheck every four years. Useful if you need it, but many readers already have this benefit through another card. 25% off a Melio subscription. This discounts the subscription proposal, not the processing fee charged for paying bills with a credit card. Travel protections. The card adds trip delay, trip cancellation and interruption, lost or damaged luggage, and rental car coverage. Spend $100 on qualifying inflight purchases and the old 25% rebate returned $25. The new credit returns $100, an improvement of $75. After the $51 annual fee increase, you’re $24 ahead on those two changes alone. At $400 in qualifying inflight spending, both versions return $100. Above that, the old uncapped 25% rebate was worth more. I don’t know many people who spent $400 inflight on food and beverages with American Airlines. As I detailed when the changes were confirmed, business card trip delay coverage is up to $500 per covered trip after an eligible delay exceeding six hours, limited to two claims per account in a 12 month period. It isn’t $500 for each traveler. For trip delay and cancellation or interruption coverage, the full covered trip must be paid with the card or an eligible combination of the card and AAdvantage miles, certificates, vouchers, coupons or discounts. When I’d Put Spending On This Card For $100,000 in purchases outside bonus categories, a 2% cash back card would return $2,000. This card produces 100,000 miles. Put that spending on an eligible employee card and it also produces 100,000 Loyalty Points for each of two people. Whether those miles are worth more or less than $2,000 depends on how you use them. The status credit is what can make this a real winning play and it has more value when two people will use the status. If you’re only interested in redeemable miles, other cards can earn more rewards on ordinary spending, including transferable points. Citi’s Strata Premier nad Strata Elite can earn points faster and transfer to American. The business card makes sense for earning status. The Executive Card Can Make This More Valuable The Citi® / AAdvantage® Executive World Legend Mastercard® [See rates and fees] awards 10,000 additional Loyalty Points at each of four thresholds: 50,000, 90,000, 165,000 and 240,000 Loyalty Points in a qualification year. You don’t have to earn the qualifying points by spending on the Executive card. Points from business card spending can trigger the bonuses. Suppose the business primary cardmember and one employee each also hold a separate Executive card as a primary cardmember. Then: $105,000 in eligible purchases on that employee’s business card earns each person 105,000 Loyalty Points. Each person’s Executive card adds 20,000 Loyalty Points from the first two thresholds. Each reaches 125,000 Loyalty Points, enough for Platinum Pro and oneworld Emerald. That requires two separate Executive accounts, each with a $695 annual fee, in addition to the business card. I’ve written about doing this with my wife. I already have the business and Executive cards. Giving her an employee card for the business, and having her hold a separate primary Executive card, could help both of us earn oneworld Emerald from the same business spending. For my family, that has a great use. If we’re flying home from Sydney on an eligible oneworld flight, two Emerald members can each bring a guest into the Qantas first class lounge. We’d have access for all four of us even when we’re flying business class.

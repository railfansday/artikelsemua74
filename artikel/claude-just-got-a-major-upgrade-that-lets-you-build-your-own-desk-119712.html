Claude just got a major upgrade that lets you build your own desktop apps — no coding required

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that imagine creating your own budgeting app, meal planner or productivity tool by just describing what you want. Now imagine opening that app directly from your computer without digging through your chat history.

Sources indicate that that's exactly what Anthropic is making possible with its latest Claude revision. The company announced on Tuesday that users can now turn Claude Artifacts into desktop apps for Mac and Windows, complete with their own icons, dedicated windows, and notifications.

Perhaps the best part is that you don't need a paid Claude subscription or any coding experience to get started.

Recent findings suggest that the feature is at the moment rolling out in beta across all Claude plans, including the free tier. And while AI chatbots have been able to build interactive tools for some time, this update makes those creations considerably more practical for everyday use.

Until now, one of the drawbacks of building an interactive tool with Claude was that using it meant going back into Claude. Giving those creations a permanent place on your desktop changes that experience. (Related: Building the next generation of multimodal AI experienc...)

You could create a custom app that helps you stay focused throughout the workday, a family chore tracker or even a holiday shopping organizer that keeps tabs on your budget.

Here are five app ideas and prompts worth trying:.

Anthropic employees have already used the feature to create everything from a layered image editor to a computer performance monitor.

Apps can also work with services you've connected to Claude, opening up possibilities for more personalized tools.

Getting started doesn't require downloading a separate coding tool or setting up an API key.

Download or update the Claude desktop app for Mac or Windows.

Describe the app you want Claude to build.

Try out the resulting Artifact and ask Claude to make any necessary changes.

Open the Share menu and select Install as app.

Review and confirm any requested permissions.

You can continue refining your creation through Claude, and changes to the original Artifact will carry over to the installed app.

There are several limitations to keep in mind. These apps aren't completely independent of Claude. You'll need an internet connection and an active Claude login to open them, and any AI requests they make count toward your existing usage limits. (Related: The Reformer - Pushing the limits of language modeling...)

Not every Artifact can be installed, either. Claude's standard documents, presentations and spreadsheets are among the exclusions.

It has been revealed that security is another consideration. Anthropic doesn't review apps before installation, and certain permissions can let an app modify or delete files on your computer.

Sources indicate that I'd recommend sticking with the default permissions unless your app genuinely needs additional access. Be especially careful about installing creations shared by others.

One of the things I find most compelling about this patch is that it gives everyday customers a reason to experiment with vibe coding beyond the novelty of building something with AI.

Think about how many times you've downloaded an app only to discover that half the features are locked behind a subscription, or that it doesn't work quite the way you'd like.

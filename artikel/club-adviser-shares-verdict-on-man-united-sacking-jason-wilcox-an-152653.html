Manchester United are “dithering” over the future of sporting director Jason Wilcox, despite new manager Mauricio Pochettino making it clear that he favours a change in the role.

That is according to former Everton chief executive Keith Wyness, speaking exclusively to Football Insider, who believes that the glitch raises a “bigger question” as to the direction the club want to go in.

Wyness believes that United are already allowing the Argentine to have a strong say in almost every “major call” at the club, despite previous managers having less power within the club’s structure. (Related: Breaking: GTA 6 devs reveal major gameplay features tha...)

Pochettino worked with Mitchell in his previous spells in charge of both Southampton and Tottenham, and has already recommended him to the United hierarchy.

The 45-year-old was a contender to become the Red Devils’ sporting director back in 2023, before they ultimately opted for Wilcox instead the following year.

Sources indicate that “Now it seems we're going towards the strong manager situation with Pochettino having a major call on nearly everything. Now that's okay. Until Pochettino leaves and then what are you stuck with then?

Everton’s former chief Wyness – who served as CEO at Goodison Park between 2004 and 2009 and now runs a football consultancy business advising elite clubs – believes that Pochettino is already steering United back towards a traditional structure, where the manager has plenty of say over all levels of the club.

The boardroom expert pointed out that is a complete contrast to the Red Devils’ setup in recent years, which has had bosses in more head-coach style roles.

Speaking on the latest edition of Football Insider’s Inside Track podcast, Wyness explained that Pochettino’s desire to see Wilcox axed has raised yet another massive issue at Old Trafford.

“I think it actually invites a bigger question to me. They were trying to move away from the strong manager position to having a strong executive structure so that if a manager left, there wasn't such disruption,” he stated.

In a recent development, “Would Mitchell have to go as well then? Would the squad have to be revamped et cetera? So they're going to have to decide what they are. Is it a strong club or is it a strong manager?

“It seems that they're dithering between both and they're going to end up in the worst of both worlds. If Pochettino starts calling the shots and all the executive appointments that's going to be a bigger change in the model that they're trying to put in place.

“So it’s another major question mark on (Sir Jim) Ratcliffe and the executive management.”.

Former United chef scout Mick Brown told Football Insider earlier this week that centre-back Matthijs de Ligt has to be one of the “first players cast out” by Pochettino once the January window opens.

The ex-recruitment man outlined that he “couldn’t believe” the fresh boss opted to start the Dutchman in last week’s heavy defeat to Liverpool, given that he has been “miles off” the required level of late.

Brown believes that the 27-year-old simply cannot be trusted to start games any more, and outlined that the only member of United’s defence who he trusts is Harry Maguire.

The club are already planning to overhaul their struggling backline this winter, and Brown has no doubt that De Ligt is one of “four or five” players who need to be replaced.

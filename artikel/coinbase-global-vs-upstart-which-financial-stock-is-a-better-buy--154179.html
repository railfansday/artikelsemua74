As digital finance continues to evolve, investors are weighng the potential of Coinbase Global (COIN +3.20%) and Upstart (UPST +1.85%) to determine which company offers a more compelling long-term opportunity. (Related: Industry News: Which Samsung folding phone is right for...)

It has been revealed that coinbase Global acts as a primary gateway for the digital asset economy, while Upstart uses advanced technology to improve credit access for consumers. Both companies operate at the intersection of finance and software, but their underlying business models and market drivers differ significantly, making this a classic match-up of two distinct innovation stories. Coinbase Global operates a comprehensive platform that allows consumers and institutional clients to trade, store, and interact with digital assets. Beyond its well-known trading exchange, the tech giant has diversified into staking services, stablecoin operations with partners like Circle, and its own blockchain infrastructure called Base Chain. This multifaceted strategy positions the company as a foundational infrastructure provider among fintech stocks, serving a global audience across more than 100 countries.

According to recent reports, reliable recent quarterly figures for at least one of these companies aren't available, so this comparison uses each manufacturer's latest full fiscal year. For the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, Coinbase Global reported revenue of nearly $7.2 billion, representing a year-over-year increase of approximately 9.4%. The company generated a healthy net income of close to $1.3 billion for the period, resulting in a net margin of roughly 17.6%, which measures how much of each dollar in revenue remains as profit after all expenses.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the company maintained a solid financial position with a current ratio of approximately 2.3x, meaning its current assets are more than double its current liabilities. Its debt-to-equity ratio, which compares total debt to shareholder equity, stood at a manageable 0.5x. Free cash flow, calculated as cash flow from operations minus capital expenditures, reached nearly $2.4 billion in fiscal 2025. Note that stock-based compensation represented roughly 34.6% of operating cash flow, which inflates reported cash generation since it is a non-cash expense.

Recent findings suggest that upstart is an artificial intelligence lending marketplace that aims to replace traditional credit scores with more accurate risk assessment models. The company connects consumers looking for personal and auto loans with over 100 bank and credit union partners who use its proprietary technology to automate the lending process. By analyzing millions of data points beyond standard credit reports, the company helps its partners improve approval rates and reduce defaults while attracting traffic through aggregators like Credit Karma.

For the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, Upstart saw a significant rebound in activity with revenue reaching approximately $1.0 billion, a substantial 64% increase compared to the previous year. This growth helped the firm return to profitability, posting a net income of nearly $53.6 million for the period. Its net margin stood at roughly 5.1%, reflecting a transition back to positive territory as loan volumes recovered and its AI models continued to refine their predictive capabilities.

On its December 2025 balance sheet, the company carried a debt-to-equity ratio of approximately 2.3x, indicating that its total debt is more than double its shareholder equity. Unlike its competitor, the company reported a negative free cash flow of close to $166.1 million for the fiscal year, as cash used in operations exceeded its capital investments. This highlights the capital-intensive nature of scaling a lending marketplace during periods of fluctuating credit demand and the ongoing need for institutional funding to support loan originations.

Coinbase Global faces significant risks due to its heavy reliance on the transaction volumes and price volatility of digital assets like Bitcoin (BTC -1.60%). The company operates in an extensive and evolving global regulatory landscape, where future changes in securities laws could materially impact its ability to offer certain products or services. Additionally, the platform is a constant target for sophisticated cyberattacks and security breaches, and it is at the moment involved in various legal proceedings, including class action lawsuits that could result in substantial costs or operational changes.

In a recent development, upstart depends heavily on the accuracy of its proprietary AI models to evaluate credit risk, and any failure to predict borrower defaults correctly could alienate its bank partners. The business is also highly vulnerable to macroeconomic shifts, as rising interest rates or economic downturns can lead to higher default rates and reduced interest from institutional investors who fund the loans. Furthermore, the organization faces intense regulatory scrutiny regarding fair lending practices and AI governance, alongside ongoing securities litigation related to the performance of its credit models.

Valuation metrics include sourcing from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from other data providers.

I'd go with Upstart. Lenders everywhere are racing to use AI to decide who gets a loan, and Upstart is already doing it at scale, helping banks and credit unions approve more than half a million loans in a single quarter. And I especially like how it is branching into auto and home loans. Revenue is up 34% and its lending platform earned record profit.

Recent findings suggest that it does have a soft spot, though: Its results swing with the economy. Rising consumer stress and higher interest rates cost it loans last quarter and led management to trim its full-year forecast. Still, it turned a profit, outside investors fund nearly all its loans, and it plans to open its own bank in early 2027.

Sources indicate that coinbase is a much bigger business, with a growing share of crypto trading and a fast-growing prediction markets arm. Notably, its core operations have stayed in the black through the crypto slump. But revenue is shrinking as trading slows, and its headline profit got a significant lift from gains on crypto it holds.

Sources indicate that upstart is more appealing to me because its growth doesn't hinge on crypto prices. For long-term investors who can handle some swings with the economy, an AI lender growing this fast and expanding into new kinds of loans makes Upstart the better buy for 2027.

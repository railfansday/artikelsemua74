Cole Palmer may not fully believe in Xabi Alonso's tactics as the fresh Chelsea boss faces early pressure at Stamford Bridge.

That is according to experienced former Tottenham, Everton and Aston Villa scout, Bryan King, speaking exclusively to Football Insider.

Football Insider understands that Cesc Fabregas is already seen as a potential replacement, but the Spaniard would not be willing to take the job.

Alonso has won 50 per cent of his games in charge so far, but back-to-back defeats to Sunderland and Leeds United have raised huge concerns.

The Blues have slipped to eighth in the Premier League table, and Palmer's struggles, unsurprisingly, have coincided with Chelsea poor form.

The 24-year-old has five goals and three assists in all competitions this season, but he still doesn't appear to be firing on all cylinders.

Joao Pedro's injury certainly hasn't helped in West London, while Morgan Rogers is looking exactly like the player they paid £117million for.

And rumours are beginning to emerge that Palmer could potentially seek an exit, with Manchester United one of the teams interested. (Related: This newly introduced chip survives 1300°F (700°C) and ...)

"I look at Palmer and I think, does he believe in the Alonso project?" King questioned when speaking to Football Insider. (Related: Tech Report: Think AI "knows" what it’s doing? Scientis...)

"Rogers was signed in the summer, obviously with Alonso saying that this is a player that we've got to get. So Rogers has Alonso to thank for going to Chelsea.

"But he's another one who doesn't look as though he's enjoying his football. Martinez, who was, well, two years ago, was the best goalkeeper in the Premier League.

"Now, are they all in on this project or are they not? You don't expect, you really don't expect to get beat 4-1 at home by Leeds.".

Chelsea's problems have leaked off the pitch as well, with Wesley Fofana the latest player to voice his issues in an ill-advised interview.

The French defender has certainly failed to live up to his £71m price tag, making continuous mistakes in the centre of defence at Stamford Bridge.

He admitted to laughing about his recent penalty miss as Chelsea went crashing out of the Carabao Cup against Liverpool.

And it would also appear that his decision to keep playing football, after talks with his agent, is entirely based on money, and not on paying Chelsea back.

This media storm is the last thing Alonso needed this season, but it's just another hurdle that the latest boss will have to overcome to succeed.

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Continental is considering ending tire production at its plant in Gqeberha, South Africa, a move that could affect approximately 950 employees, the manufacturer said on Friday.

Recent findings suggest that continental has started a consultation process under Section 189A of South Africa's Labour Relations Act to explore alternatives and discuss ways to reduce the impact on workers. If the process identifies no viable alternative, operations at the site could be discontinued by early 2027, the organization said.

Continental said the plant's production capacity has been underused for several years despite turnaround efforts. The tech giant stated that the intensifying price competition from low-cost imports has placed significant pressure on the African tire market. It added that costlier energy, disrupted supply chains and shaky infrastructure have driven up manufacturing costs and complicated forward planning.

It has been revealed that the organization noted South Africa and the wider African continent remain notable markets. Its local sales organization will keep serving customers in South Africa and across the region out of Gqeberha, and its BestDrive retail network will not be affected by any changes to production. Sources indicate that the plant has made tires in Gqeberha since 1947. Production of Continental-branded tires began there in 1985, supplying automakers operating in South Africa and the African replacement market, the firm stated.

According to recent reports, A Continental closure would follow other plant shutdowns in South Africa's auto industry, according to Bloomberg. Manufacturers there have had difficulty holding their own against a growing wave of shipments from China and India.

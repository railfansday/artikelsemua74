By RJ Fulton – Nov 25, 2026 at 5:06PM EST.

As of early evening on Wednesday, November 25, Bitcoin (BTC +2.95%) had gained 0.66% to $60,139.47, while Ethereum (ETH +3.13%) rose 0.45% to $1,685.21. Solana (SOL +4.46%) outperformed both, climbing 1.20% to $70.47. Despite Wednesday's gains, all three cryptocurrencies remain under pressure following their recent declines.

Sources indicate that bitcoin's recovery above $60,000 was supported by liquidations of leveraged short positions, as traders betting on further declines were forced to close their positions. However, the rebound appears largely driven by short covering rather than a meaningful improvement in underlying demand, with institutional investors continuing to withdraw capital from cryptocurrency investment funds.

Cryptocurrency exchange-traded funds (ETFs) also posted modest gains on Wednesday, reflecting the recovery in underlying digital asset prices. The iShares Bitcoin Trust ETF (IBIT +0.92%) rose 0.92%, while the Fidelity Wise Origin Bitcoin Fund (FBTC +0.87%) gained 0.87%. Meanwhile, the iShares Ethereum Trust ETF (ETHA +0.90%) climbed 0.90% as Ethereum edged higher.

Sources indicate that bitcoin's recovery above $60,000 offers some relief following the recent sell-off, but the underlying market conditions remain challenging. U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs have recorded nearly $5 billion in net withdrawals during November, putting the funds on track for their worst month since launching in 2024. Without a meaningful improvement in institutional demand, Bitcoin could struggle to sustain its recovery as investors continue to reduce their exposure to digital assets. Meanwhile, persistent inflation remains a significant obstacle for cryptocurrencies. October's core personal consumption expenditures (PCE) price index rose 0.3% for the third consecutive month, reinforcing expectations that the Federal Reserve could raise interest rates in December. Higher borrowing costs and tighter financial conditions could continue to weigh on speculative assets, making a sustained cryptocurrency recovery more difficult.

In a recent development, there are some encouraging longer-term developments. The Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) recently clarified that regulated derivatives exchanges and clearinghouses can count certain cryptocurrencies, including Bitcoin and Ethereum, toward specified financial resource requirements. Although the change is unlikely to materially influence near-term prices, it represents another step toward integrating digital assets into the broader financial system. Looking ahead, investors should prioritize evidence of sustained buying demand over short-term price rebounds. For Bitcoin, maintaining support above $60,000 and a reversal in ETF outflows would be encouraging signs. Until institutional capital returns and broader financial conditions improve, the risk of further declines remains elevated, particularly if weakness in technology stocks continues to spill over into cryptocurrencies.

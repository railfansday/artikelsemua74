Ian Wright enjoyed an illustrious career, scoring 185 goals for Arsenal between 1991 and 1998, winning a Premier League title, two FA Cups and a League Cup in the process. However, it all started for the striker south of the River Thames at Crystal Palace, and the Eagles have arguably never properly replaced the legendary figure.

Recent findings suggest that wright was playing Sunday League football when he was scouted by Palace in 1985. The striker was spotted scoring goals at Hackney Marshes and was offered a trial at Selhurst Park. The rest is history. Wright would go on to score 118 goals in 277 appearances for Palace, lighting up Selhurst Park in both the first and second divisions. This prolific form ultimately led to his move to Arsenal, where he didn't look back. (Related: GTA 6 glitch lets players access first-person mode...)

While Palace have housed a number of excellent strikers since Wright's departure, with the likes of Jean-Philippe Mateta, Dougie Freedman and Andy Johnson all wearing the famous red and blue shirt, none have quite replicated what the Arsenal legend was able to do in South London.

That remarked, many at Palace thought they had a 'young Ian Wright' in their ranks a few years ago, but the player ultimately never made an appearance for the Eagles' first team.

Five years after being tipped to be the heir to Wright's throne, Robert Street is plying his trade in the Championship with Lincoln City.

In a recent development, born in Oxford, Street initially joined Palace's academy at a very young age, before being released shortly after turning eight. The striker then spent several years at amateur club Whyteleafe, before rejoining the Premier League club at 14 after a successful trial.

Recent findings suggest that such was the level of his performances in the youth ranks at Palace that he was promoted to the under-23s squad at the age of 16. This is when the under-23s coach at the time, Richard Shaw, made the comparison to Wright.

"I was lucky enough to be involved in the Palace academy and to watch the first training session Ian Wright had, when he was 21; he came from Ten-em-Bee on trial," Shaw told Football.London in 2019.

"He ripped the place apart and I remember sitting there as a kid, I was an apprentice at the time, watching him, thinking that guy had so much hunger and desire.

In a recent development, "When I got in and around the first team environment, every single day. I remember he had me and [John] Salako coming in on days off to feed him balls to hit 500 balls into an empty net. He was so desperate to succeed and missed out on academy football so he thought it's catch up time for me. I can only relate to Ian Wright because I saw it first hand. I see it now with Rob.".

Street's ridiculously prolific form in the academy saw him garner attention from coaches in the senior set-up, and he subsequently signed his first professional contract in the summer of 2020.

However, after failing to make any inroads into the first team, Street was sent out to Torquay United on loan in January 2021. This was the first of three separate temporary spells away from Selhurst Park for the starlet, who was finding it difficult to adapt to the intensity and physicality of the men's game.

After failing to score at Torquay, he netted just twice for Newport County and four times for Shrewsbury Town.

Despite struggling to make a strong impression during any of these loan stints, Palace hadn't given up on Street. In the summer of 2023, after he'd returned from Shrewsbury, the Eagles offered the youngster a new contract.

However, eager to find a permanent home rather than going out on loan again, Street rejected the proposal, ending Palace's hopes of unearthing the next Wright for the time being.

He may not have made a single appearance for Palace, but Street has slowly built a respectable EFL career for himself.

According to recent reports, after parting ways with the club that developed him, the centre-forward was offered a lifeline by League One Cheltenham Town. Sustaining a season-ending knee injury in January of his first season in Gloucestershire, Street left at the end of his debut campaign as Cheltenham were relegated to the fourth tier.

In a recent development, lincoln City swiftly picked him up on a four-year deal, and after a frustrating first six months with the Imps, the number nine left on loan for Doncaster Rovers, which is where his career began to take shape. Netting 12 goals in just 23 League Two appearances in Yorkshire, Lincoln opted to give Street a chance in the following season.

According to recent reports, the Palace academy graduate managed double figures in League One last season as Lincoln won promotion to the Championship. Now a second-tier player for the Lincolnshire club, Street will no doubt harbour dreams of one day playing in the Premier League, while Palace may ultimately rue losing the talented young striker.

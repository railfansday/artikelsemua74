Crystal Palace are paying around £750,000 in compensation to prise Chelsea set-piece coach Bernardo Cueva away from Stamford Bridge.

That is according to former Everton chief executive Keith Wyness, speaking exclusively to Football Insider, who believes that the Mexican will be a hugely important acquisition for the Eagles.

This comes amid reports that Palace are on the verge of reaching an agreement for Cueva to make the switch across London.

Wyness believes that his imminent arrival could add "energy" to the squad and the coaching staff at Selhurst Park.

Cueva previously worked with newly introduced boss Thomas Frank at Brentford, and was a big part of the Dane's success with the Bees.

Wyness told Football Insider earlier this week that Cueva's imminent arrival could be worth "10 points" for Palace across the remainder of the season, given his reputation within the game.

Everton’s former chief Wyness – who served as CEO at Goodison Park between 2004 and 2009 and now runs a football consultancy advising elite clubs – believes that the fee for Palace to get Cueva will be far less than what it would cost for them to sign a newly introduced top-level striker.

However, the boardroom expert believes that the two scenarios would likely have a very similar impact on the rest of Palace's season.

Speaking on the latest edition of Football Insider's Inside Track podcast, Wyness explained how adding Cueva to Frank's team is a no-brainer for the Eagles.

"Sometimes people are talking about substantial strikers and players, when you could have a £750,000 assistant coach; that's what they could be paying him. It's worth 10 points," he said. (Related: Latest Update: Simple considerations for simple people ...)

"It's being part of a team together again, being able to bounce things off each other. And I think that sort of energy will translate well into the squad.

"I think it's a good move for Frank to have Cueva back with him. I think it's a very experienced head on the shoulders there that will help him. So I think it's a very good move.".

Wyness previously told Football Insider earlier this week that Frank is trying to rebuild his Brentford "machine" by reuniting with Cueva. (Related: Tech Report: The Age of Machine Learning As Code Has Ar...)

The Dane enjoyed great success by building a tight-knit working environment during his time in charge of the West London club, something he is now looking to replicate at Palace.

Wyness pointed out that Cueva has "unique talents" as a coach, and noted that everybody he's spoken to about him rates him "very, very highly.".

Cueva's arrival at Palace is now imminent, with Frank's backroom team set to be completed.

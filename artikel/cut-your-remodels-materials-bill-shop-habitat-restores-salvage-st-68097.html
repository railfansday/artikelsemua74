Industry observers note that before you pay full price for cabinets, doors, or light fixtures, check a Habitat for Humanity ReStore and a local architectural salvage store. ReStores sell donated surplus and used materials below retail, and salvage stores carry vintage pieces you can’t buy fresh. Bring your measurements, visit often, and test old paint for lead.

Industry observers note that one ReStore near Chicago listed a 13-piece kitchen cabinet set, with base, wall, corner, sink, and over-fridge cabinets, for $1,300, about $100 a cabinet. ReStores are nonprofit home improvement stores run by local Habitat for Humanity groups. They sell what people and businesses donate, from surplus construction materials to cabinets pulled out in remodels, at less than retail. Some of it has never been opened. Recent findings suggest that architectural salvage stores sell older pieces taken from buildings being renovated or torn down, such as wood doors, vintage hardware, light fixtures, mantels, and clawfoot tubs. Prices there depend on a piece’s rarity, condition, and demand, so a salvage store pays off most when you’re matching the original doors, trim, or hardware in an older home.

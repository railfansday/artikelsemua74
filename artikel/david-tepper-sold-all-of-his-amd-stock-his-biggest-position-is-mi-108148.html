There has been a ton of news in the stock market recently. Nvidia is making headlines with its CEO controversy and third-quarter earnings. Newly public Anthropic went public with a lot of fanfare. However, there was another event that occurred this week that flew under the radar of most investors: 13-F releases. (Related: This newly introduced chip survives 1300°F (700°C) and ...)

While looking at Security and Exchange Commission (SEC) documents may sound a tad boring, these documents reveal what trades billionaire hedge fund managers made in the third quarter. Those are valuable pieces of information, as they show what the smart money is doing. (Related: Tech Report: Think AI "knows" what it’s doing? Scientis...)

In a recent development, one billionaire that I follow is David Tepper and his fund, Appaloosa Management. Tepper made some interesting moves during Q3. The most noteworthy was loading up on Micron Technology (MU -4.53%) shares while completely exiting his Advanced Micro Devices (AMD -1.75%) position.

That's quite the call, but is it the right one?

According to recent reports, david Tepper. Image source: Getty Images.

Industry observers note that information available from 13-Fs naturally lags the market. The SEC requires funds with over $100 million in investments to disclose their end-of-quarter holdings and make those public 45 days after the quarter ends. This gives some buffer between filing and release date, which protects the fund's investments.

However, investors need to be careful not to follow these documents too closely, as the landscape may have shifted from the end of the quarter to now.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/25/26.

Sources indicate that AMD is in that boat, because it has lost about 20% of its value since Q3 ended. That justifies Tepper's move and makes it appear that his fund took gains before a sell-off occurred. The reason? Likely AMD's valuation.

AMD's stock has had a great year this year, but not all of that success has come from business growth. Instead, the market has chosen to give AMD a far higher premium this year than it has in the past. Reaching about 35 times next year's earnings at the end of Q3, it signaled that it was time to sell the stock.

AMD PE Ratio (Forward 1y) data by YCharts.

Tepper did exactly that, and I think that was a smart move, because there was just too much hype baked into AMD's stock price. Buying Micron was an interesting pivot, and I think it may turn out to be the right move.

Pivoting from AMD to Micron is a huge change in mindset. While AMD is potentially overvalued, Micron is very cheap. It trades for just 3.9 times fiscal-year 2027 earnings.

MU PE Ratio (Forward) data by YCharts.

That's a very cheap price tag, and it conveys that the market thinks something is wrong with the company. However, that's far from the case.

Sources indicate that micron operates in the memory chip industry, which has a history of being cyclical. The current artificial intelligence (AI)-driven cycle is massive and unlike anything investors have ever seen, and will eventually come to an end. The market is worried about it coming to an end sooner rather than later.

However, memory chip companies have sold out production capacity for years in advance, and Micron's management team told investors during its latest conference call that the memory chip market conditions will be worse in 2027 and 2028 than they were in 2026, meaning that prices will stay elevated, boosting Micron's stock.

According to recent reports, this is likely why Tepper invested in Micron, as he sees upside when the rest of the market is bearish. Tepper is so bullish on the stock that he has made it his largest position, accounting for about 17.5% of his portfolio. That's a very concentrated position, but if Micron can see its valuation expand to more reasonable levels, Tepper will make a huge profit from his Micron shares.

If you're an AMD shareholder, I think the right move is to follow in Tepper's footsteps and roll the profits from that investment into Micron, as it could be due for a huge run over the next few years as the market realizes that the AI buildup isn't slowing down anytime soon.

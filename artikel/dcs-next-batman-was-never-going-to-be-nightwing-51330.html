DC's Next Batman Was Never Going To Be Nightwing

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

On the surface, Dick Grayson's Nightwing may seem like an obvious choice to one day succeed Batman and don the cape and cowl. After all, he was The Dark Knight's very first Robin and oldest adopted son. However, DC Comics has spent quite some time establishing a much different trajectory for Grayson, while setting up someone else entirely to one day become the next Caped Crusader.

In the past, Dick Grayson had indeed filled in as Batman in Bruce Wayne's absence. However, it's one thing to become a temporary Dark Knight or for the comics to showcase an alt-reality "What If?" type scenario (see DC K.O. Knightfight), and quite another to suggest that Nightwing will one day become a full-time Batman for the foreseeable future. That's never going to be in the cards for several reasons. (Related: Showcase Your Projects in Spaces using Gradio...)

According to recent reports, ever since he left Robin behind, Dick's evolution into Nightwing has long been punctuated by the desire to be more than Batman and step out of his shadow. Conversely, DC Comics clearly has someone else in mind to become the next Batman, and he's already been wearing the cape and cowl: Jace Fox.

According to recent reports, dick Grayson has successfully taken over as Batman before, most notably after Bruce Wayne's apparent death in Final Crisis. This era saw Dick becoming a newly introduced Batman alongside Damian Wayne as his Robin in the critically acclaimed Batman and Robin run. As such, it's been proven that Dick can and has become The Dark Knight when needed (but that doesn't mean he should long-term).

When he left his role as Robin behind, Dick Grayson's decision to become Nightwing was all about creating a newly introduced identity all his own, beyond Bruce. This included his move to Gotham's sister city of Blüdhaven. In the several years that have followed, Dick has become an A-list DC hero in his own right in the modern era, leading the Titans and protecting his own city without needing Batman's name or reputation. (Related: Scaling up BERT-like model Inference on modern CPU - P...)

According to recent reports, perhaps even more importantly, Bruce himself has indicated that he wants something far better for his eldest son (and all his sons) than simply becoming another Batman in his stead. Likewise, Dick Grayson has retained so much more of his optimism and brightness, choosing to move past his own parents' deaths rather than Bruce and his ongoing struggle to do just that.

Rather than asking him to ever take his place full-time, Bruce Wayne has every reason to want Dick to continue being Nightwing and prove that the next generation can become something greater than Batman himself. He already knows (and has remarked) that Dick is a far greater hero as Nightwing than he'll ever be as Batman. As such, wanting Dick to become Batman would undermine several years of development and crucial storytelling for both characters and their relationship.

Timothy "Jace" Fox, the estranged son of Lucius Fox, is a far more interesting possibility. First introduced as the next Batman in DC's Future State initiative, Jace received a proper present-day origin in The Next Batman: Second Son, exploring his complicated history and years away from his family. When he came back to Gotham, Jace chose to take up Batman's mantle, using advanced armor to fight as a secondary Dark Knight before eventually moving to Fresh York City.

Sources indicate that as seen in I Am Batman, the series explored what a new character could do with the mantle of The Dark Knight outside Gotham itself. At the same time, Jace's connection to Lucius still kept him connected to many of the resources associated with Bruce Wayne's crusade.

Industry observers note that keeping that in mind, the upcoming Batman: Reign of Assassins series is particularly exciting. Releasing in 2027, the five-matter series reunites writer John Ridley and artist Olivier Coipel, bringing Jace and Bruce together for their first true ongoing team-up, which should no doubt provide some brand-new explorations into their dynamics as simultaneous Batmen.

All things considered, DC Comics absolutely intends to use Jace to continue examining what the Batman legacy looks like in a upcoming generation, and it will be SO much better than Dick Grayson ever becoming a permanent next Dark Knight.

Batman: Reign of Assassins #1 is available February 27 from DC Comics.

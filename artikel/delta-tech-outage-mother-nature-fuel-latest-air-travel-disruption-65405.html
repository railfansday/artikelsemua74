The Thanksgiving travel rush got off to a messy start at several of the nation’s busiest airports.

Delta Air Lines on Sunday reported an issue with the system that automates aircraft weight and balance calculations. (Related: Latest Update: VAST notes petabyte SSDs will outgrow to...)

The disruption led to mass delays at its Atlanta mega-hub — the world’s busiest airport.

Industry observers note that by 1 p.m. EST Sunday, around 40% of all flights had been delayed at Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport (ATL), according to FlightAware. That included hundreds of departing flights — and planes headed to the airport.

Industry observers note that in a statement, a Delta spokesperson told TPG the airline was using “manual workarounds” to get flights into the air. But the carrier had not yet restored the system that went down.

Delta did not share how significantly it expected its operation to be affected as the day progressed.

However, by early afternoon more than 1,300 of Delta’s mainline flights had already been delayed — at least 40% of its planned operation for the day.

Fortunately, the airline had (so far) reported few cancellations.

Regional subsidiary Endeavor Air, which operates Delta Connection flights, reported another 230-plus cancellations, per FlightAware.

“We thank our customers for their patience and our people for their professionalism," Delta remarked in a statement to TPG.

That wasn’t the only problem to affect travelers Sunday.

Mother Nature also snarled air traffic at some major hubs.

Low cloud ceilings fueled flight delays and led to dozens of flights getting diverted.

New York’s LaGuardia Airport (LGA) was most heavily affected, with nearly half of all departing and arriving flights delayed Sunday, per FlightAware.

42 flights headed to LGA on Sunday were diverted to other airports, according to aviation analytics firm Cirium.

Many of those flights landed instead in Philadelphia; Albany, N.Y.; Harrisburg, PA; and Syracuse, N.Y.

These disruptions come a week after one of the most chaotic weekends at airports in recent memory.

Sources indicate that more than 18,000 flights were canceled last weekend after an outage on Nov. 13 to critical Federal Aviation Administration technology.

Last week, a brief technical outage at United Airlines also fueled a host of flight delays.

Sunday’s travel woes also coincide, of course, with the start of the Thanksgiving travel rush — with airports expected to be very busy with holiday travelers over the next few days.

Recent findings suggest that all eyes are on next Sunday — Nov. 29 — which could be the busiest day of the year to fly.

Last year, the Sunday after Thanksgiving set the record as the most crowded day ever at U.S. airports.

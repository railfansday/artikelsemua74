Everybody has found it pretty cute, if slightly baffling, that expected box-office heavyweights Dune: Part Three and Avengers: Doomsday are opening on the same day (Dec. 18).

But Dune franchise director Denis Villeneuve isn’t amused by going head-to-head with the Marvel titan.

Villeneuve explains in a new interview that he was “deeply disappointed” by the “Dunesday” scheduling, but that Warner Bros. was basically trapped in the slot because the studio had successfully secured an exclusive run on IMAX screens for Dune 3 through the December holidays. Sources indicate that “Listen, I will be honest,” he told Time. “The first Dune came out at the end of the pandemic. We made a great box office in a very rough environment. But we had the winds against us. Then the second movie, same thing. We were meant to release in the fall, and we had to release at the end of winter because of the two Hollywood strikes. We had success, but it was not easy. The third time, I said to myself, ‘Finally, there is no pandemic, no strike in sight. We will have a normal release.’ Then Disney put the biggest franchise of all time against us. I’m no fool. Those movies are a mammoth.”. “So I was deeply disappointed,” continued the director. “But we had committed to this date, to IMAX. It was impossible to move. So I accepted my fate, praying to the gods of cinema. Dune is not a mammoth like the Avengers, but it’s a sexy little animal that has a lot of firepower, and I hope we will survive. It could be one of the biggest box-office weekends of all time because of both movies. Am I in competition with the Avengers? Absolutely not. It’s a T. rex. But I stand proud because I think the film delivers.”.

Sources indicate that both films have notably broken presales records. Dune: Part Three has reportedly bested Warner Bros. all-time presales record, selling more than $30 million worth of tickets for IMAX screens alone (the studio hasn’t released its own all-theater presales numbers). But Doomsday has recently surpassed $100 million and has received the highest initial opening-weekend forecast in NRG tracking history ($270 million). So there’s no world in which both films aren’t massive hits, but also no world in which Doomsday doesn’t clock Dune on opening weekend.

Industry observers note that villeneuve also defended making some significant changes to Dune saga author Frank Herbert’s source material for the third film, particularly in the storyline of Chani (Zendaya). “When you adapt, you kill,” he said. “It is not the book. It is my movie inspired by the book, with tremendous respect … I had the idea to make Chani more of a critic regarding the idea of religious colonization … In the third movie, Chani is still a critic of the choices Paul has made. She believes the Fremen should free themselves. She doesn’t want to harm Paul, but she does want to harm his regime. I would have loved to have taken Frank Herbert for a coffee about these ideas because I think he could have liked them. In the book, Chani is there to give Paul foot massages. It’s a character that has not a lot of firepower.”.

Derek McInnes' decision to rest Minsu Kim proved to be a mistake as he fired Rangers to victory over Aberdeen from the bench in midweek.

Rangers squeezed past Aberdeen at Ibrox on Tuesday as Nicolas Raskin scored the winner in a 3-2 victory.

McInnes' side fell behind to the eighth-placed side in the first half but fought back to move to within one point of Premiership toppers Celtic.

Kim's half-time introduction was a key part of changing the game as he was dropped from the Gers' starting eleven for the first time since 9 September.

McInnes must know that his choice to take the 20-year-old out of the lineup was wrong, and he cannot make the same mistake again. (Related: Tech Report: Think AI "knows" what it’s doing? Scientis...)

Rangers do not need to rotate their best players, as they are not in European competition, so Kim should start as long as he is fit. (Related: Breaking: Discord can now automatically save gaming cli...)

Kim has been an instant hit among Rangers supporters since he arrived in an £8.5million deal from Girona in the summer.

The South Korean international's all-round game has impressed many, but it did take him until their 2-1 win over Motherwell last weekend to register his first assist - and goal contribution of any kind - in a blue shirt.

He looked to have nailed down a regular starting spot in McInnes' team before Tuesday, with nine straight starts before Badredine Bouanani took his place against the Dons.

Bouanani failed to create a chance and completed just one successful dribble before he was taken off for Kim at the break.

The 20-year-old then proved to his manager that he must not be rested again anytime soon as he completed the most dribbles (5/6, 83 per cent) and won the most ground duels (7/9, 78 per cent) of any player on the pitch.

Kim also made the most ball recoveries (7) of any outfielder and made four passes into the final third as he helped Rangers bag three goals without response to win the game.

McInnes' starting right-winger is now obvious for the foreseeable future, especially with just one midweek game for the next five weeks.

Kim will be key to Rangers' title aspirations and may well understand that he needed some time on the bench to recover after so much recent game time.

McInnes admitted post-match on Tuesday that he did have second thoughts about him being on the bench, but praised his response to the decision as he let his feet do the talking.

He said: “I thought Minsu reacted exactly right. He's a young boy, he's played a lot of football. Since he came in the door, he's started this winning run, so you've always got that in the back of your head as a manager thinking, am I really doing the right thing leaving him out?

“But you've also got to be fair to him as well. I actually thought he would come off the bench and give us an impact, but ideally he would have given us an impact while we were winning the game, but he dug deep and played, as you say, a massive part in helping turn the game around.”.

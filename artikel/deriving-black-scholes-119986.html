In a recent development, even if it feels unintuitive, the price of an option does not depend on whether you expect the underlying to moon or tank. Under some reasonable market hypotheses, we can build a fully hedged portfolio by holding the underlying and shorting the option. Since it is riskless, by no-arbitrage assumption, it must return the same as the risk-free interest rate. That allows to put together an equation (the Black-Scholes equation), and solve for the option price (the Black-Scholes formula).

One step. From today (left) to maturity (right), the stock moves from $100 to $110 or $90, and a call at $100 pays $10 or $0. Half a share minus the call is worth $45 either way, so it is riskless. Without interest it is also worth $45 today, so the call costs $50 − $45 = $5.

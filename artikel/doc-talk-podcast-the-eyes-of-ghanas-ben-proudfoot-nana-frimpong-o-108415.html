Director Ben Proudfoot’s Oscar-contending documentary The Eyes of Ghana has attracted some impressive supporters, including former occupants of the White House.

It has been revealed that former President Barack Obama and First Lady Michelle Obama are among the executive producers of the nonfiction feature through their Higher Ground production company. How could you possibly top that? Well, in addition to the Obamas, The Eyes of Ghana has gained the backing of Darren Watkins Jr, better known to his fans as IShowSpeed, who, according to some measures, is the most popular livestreamer on the planet. In fact, Speed is about to release the film for free on his YouTube channel. (Related: This newly introduced chip survives 1300°F (700°C) and ...)

Two-time Oscar winner Proudfoot and producer Nana Frimpong join the latest edition of Deadline’s Doc Talk podcast to discuss their film, which is vying to make the Motion Picture Academy’s nonfiction feature shortlist (voting for that begins December 7). The documentary tells the story of a remarkable man named Chris Hesse, now in his 90s, who served as the personal cinematographer to Ghana’s first president, Kwame Nkrumah. In that role, Hesse captured extraordinary footage of the pan-Africanist leader who highlighted the way for many other countries on the continent to shed their colonial rulers and emerge into independence. Hesse’s invaluable archive was thought to have been lost forever, but Proudfoot and team discovered otherwise. The director and his producer explain the unlikely course of events that revealed the existence of the precious materials in a London hideaway. They also tell us how Barack and Michelle Obama came on board, and how they connected with YouTuber Speed.

Proudfoot also shares how he enlisted his friend Kris Bowers, his co-director on the Oscar-winning The Last Repair Shop, to compose the score for The Eyes of Ghana and where they recorded the music — at a place closely associated with The Beatles.

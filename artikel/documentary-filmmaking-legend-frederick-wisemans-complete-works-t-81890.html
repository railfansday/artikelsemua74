📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

As the nation remains enthralled by Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim’s Elizabeth Holmes film “You Can See Everything,” the first documentary to cross $100 million at the global box office in over two decades, arguably the most influential — and the most prolific — documentarian in American cinema’s works have found a new home.

It has been revealed that the complete works of Frederick Wiseman, the renowned documentary filmmaker behind “Titicut Follies” (1967), “Hospital” (1970) and “Ex Libris: The Fresh York Public Library” (2017), have found a home with Janus Films. Wiseman produced, directed and edited 45 features that were released under Zipporah Films, Inc., his production and distribution organization named for his wife, and they’ll all be distributed by Janus Films in North America starting Jan. 1, 2027, joining an impressive library that includes films by greats like Agnès Varda, Ingmar Bergman, Akira Kurosawa and Chantal Akerman. Industry observers note that wiseman’s early films, from “Titicut Follies” (1967) through “State Legislature” (2007), will be available in 4K (having been restored by Zipporah Films), while “La Danse” (2009) through “Menus-Plaisirs — Les Troisgros” (2023) will be offered in their original digital formats. “A director whose rigorously objective explorations of social institutions constitute one of the most revered bodies of work in American documentary filmmaking,” according to The Upcoming York Times, Wiseman, who passed away earlier this year at the age of 96, crafted a one-of-a-kind filmography that chronicled the experiences of everyday people in the U.S. (and France). He was given the Honorary Award for lifetime achievement from the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences in 2016, and his works helped expose corruption in vulnerable communities. Sources indicate that “We couldn’t imagine a better home in North America for Frederick Wiseman’s films than with Janus Films,” mentioned Karen Konicek of Zipporah Films. “It has been an honor to work alongside Fred for so many decades, producing and distributing his films. As Zipporah Films continues as the rights holder and distributor of the Wiseman Collection, we very much look forward to this new partnership with Janus Films and are excited to collaborate with them.”.

“We are honored and thrilled to be the first distributor, in the U.S., outside of Zipporah Films, to be entrusted with licensing one of the greatest bodies of work in American cinema” stated Peter Becker, President of Janus Films and Criterion. “We’re looking forward to presenting these monumental works, theatrically, to fresh audiences across the country.”.

The full list of titles acquired: “Titicut Follies” (1967), “High School” (1968,) “Law and Order” (1969), “Hospital” (1970), “Basic Training” (1971), “Essene” (1972), “Juvenile Court” (1973), “Primate” (1974), “Welfare” (1975), “Meat” (1976), “Canal Zone” (1977), “Sinai Field Mission” (1978), “Manoeuvre” (1979), “Model” (1980), “The Store” (1983), “Racetrack” (1985), “Blind” (1986), “Deaf”(1986), “Adjustment & Work” (1986), “Multi-handicapped” (1986), “Missile” (1988), “Near Death” (1989), “Central Park” (1990), “Aspen” (1991), “Zoo” (1993), “High School II” (1994), “Ballet” (1995), “La Comédie-Française ou L’amour joué” (1996), “Public Housing” (1997), “Belfast, Maine” (1999), “Domestic Violence” (2001), “Domestic Violence 2” (2002), “The Last Letter” (2002), “State Legislature” (2007), “La Danse: The Paris Opera Ballet” (2009), “Boxing Gym” (2010), “Crazy Horse” (2011), “At Berkeley” (2013), “National Gallery” (2014), “In Jackson Heights” (2015), “Ex Libris: The New York Public Library” (2017), “Monrovia, Indiana” (2018), “City Hall” (2020), “A Couple” (2022) and “Menus-Plaisirs, les Troisgros” (2023).

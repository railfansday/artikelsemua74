English football journalist John Cross has taken on the impossible task of ranking the greatest footballers he's seen in person, and his list of 25 players is bound to cause a debate.

It has been revealed that for any fan, ranking players is a matter of preference, with factors such as playing style, achievements, position and even nationality all coming into play. You'd be hard-pressed to find any Argentine who ranks Cristiano Ronaldo above Lionel Messi. (Related: US police forces worried GTA 6 players will start commi...)

It has been revealed that cross curated his 25-man list via Mirror Sport by explaining that he's been reporting on football for more than 25 years and, as such, there are many legendary names included along with modern-day stars. Where he ranks the current generation may surprise readers, though it's worth noting he deemed England's 3-2 win over Mexico at the World Cup to be the nation's 'best ever away performance'.

In 25th place is AC Milan hero Marco van Basten, a player who won three Ballons d'Or, though as Cross mentions, he had his career cut short by injuries. The Daily Mirror writer suggests that if not for injuries, he'd have made the top five on this list.

Roberto Carlos is next up, and Cross deemed him a 'brilliant entertainer' and a player who could get you out of your seat, even playing as a left-back. For many fans, this is a fair ranking of the Brazil and Real Madrid icon because there aren't many better players who have played in his position. The first Premier League name to make Cross's list is Patrick Vieira, with the journalist particularly praising his physicality and leadership. Dubbed 'one of the greatest captains and midfielders to ever grace the English game', the former Arsenal skipper's dominance is hard to deny.

CROSS CONTROVERSY: There was no place for Patrick Vieira's longtime rival Roy Keane in his list.

According to recent reports, paolo Maldini is one of the players you could rank above Carlos on the all-time left-back list, and the Italian icon sits above him on Cross's list. The AC Milan great's peak came during the journalist's childhood, and he recalled watching him during the 1980s and Channel 4's coverage of Serie A.

Another Premier League star, Kevin De Bruyne, earned his place among Cross's favourites. The former Manchester City midfielder was named 'the best player in the best team in English football', though Cross noted that it comes 'with a caveat now' due to the club being found guilty of breaching financial rules.

John Cross' 25 Best Players (25 -21).

Sticking with playmaking gems, Cross ranked Luka Modric 20th, branding him 'the epitome of a European midfielder' and 'the greatest Croatian player in history'. He did, however, point out the Real Madrid legend's lack of understanding that 'football's coming home' is a 'self-deprecating joke at ourselves'.

Industry observers note that in 19th place is David Beckham, maybe the first name that will stir up controversy, whether that be because he's too high or too low. There aren't many more polarising players in history because some use his celebrity status against him, while others, like Cross, believe he approached the ball like a 'work of art'.

Another Englishman, Frank Lampard, takes 18th, and Cross highlighted his goalscoring consistency along with his mindset to work 'so hard' to turn himself into 'one of the world's best'. He attributed Chelsea's most successful period to the highest-scoring midfielder in top-flight history.

Recent findings suggest that cross insisted that 'we have to have a wayward hero in there' and claimed that Eric Cantona 'absolutely fits that bill'. Citing his entertainment value, he described the former French striker as creating 'magic stardust' that helped Manchester United end their wait for a title and dominate that era of the Premier League.

It's clear who Cross sides with in the Lampard vs Steven Gerrard vs Paul Scholes debate as he chose Gerrard in 16th, omitting Scholes from his entire list. He mentioned that the former Liverpool captain's long-range goals and passion made him one of his favourite players, recalled his 2005 Champions League win and remarked that he 'deserved to win more trophies'.

John Cross' 25 Best Players (20-16).

Now we get to the more contentious picks, and names like Wayne Rooney falling outside of the top 10 might upset one or two fans. Cross recalled Rooney's Euro 2004 campaign, calling him 'unstoppable' and a 'street footballer' whose intelligence on and off the pitch doesn't get enough credit.

Recent findings suggest that for some, Mohamed Salah isn't an entertaining player to watch, especially in comparison with the likes of Eden Hazard when that conversation comes up. For Cross, his dribbling ability and success at Liverpool make him a 'clear choice' for his list, deeming him a 'fabulous Premier League legend'.

There's a reason Cross asked Arsenal fans for their thoughts, as many regard Thierry Henry as the greatest Premier League player of all time. Given that this is the main competition he covers, it's somewhat surprising the Frenchman doesn't make the top 10, although he does applaud his mentality and natural ability, calling him one of the best he's seen on a weekly basis.

Cross views Dennis Bergkamp as the greatest Arsenal player he's seen, which is why the Dutchman sits above Henry. His admiration of Bergkamp is made clear when he acknowledges him as 'a game-changer for English football' and cites his finish against Newcastle United as being 'from another world'.

Recent findings suggest that just missing out on the top 10 is Barcelona icon Xavi, who played in a team Cross deemed to be 'the best team in Europe'. There's less analysis of the Spaniard than any other player on his list, as Cross instead chose to focus on Pep Guardiola's team rather than explain Xavi's ranking.

John Cross' 25 Best Players (15-11).

Kicking off Cross's top 10 is Andres Iniesta, whom he referenced in his verdict on Xavi. Between the duo, he argues that Iniesta edges it because of his passing vision and control. He called the 2010 World Cup winner 'just wonderful' and 'a joy to watch'.

Sources indicate that perhaps a more controversial pick is Zlatan Ibrahimovic in ninth, although Cross justified this selection by alluding to the change in opinion of the Swedish striker among English media and fans. He recalled his performance for Sweden in a win over England when he scored 'one of the greatest goals of all time' and believes he is 'one of football's greatest-ever characters'.

Recent findings suggest that kylian Mbappe is ranked eighth, and for the older generation, that is a perfectly acceptable ranking for a player who has been a revelation but has still yet to win a Ballon d'Or. For the younger generation, it might seem somewhat low because of his dominance during the modern era, winning the World Cup at the age of 19.

Recent findings suggest that meanwhile, a Ballon d'Or winner who did make Cross's top 10 is Harry Kane, and the journalist thinks we haven't fully appreciated the England captain for many years. Alluding to his goalscoring exploits in recent times at Bayern Munich, he claims that the former Tottenham striker has got even better with age.

Sources indicate that ranking Jude Bellingham sixth is Cross's most daring decision, and he defended this by tipping the Real Madrid star to be the best English player of all time. He references England's World Cup win over Mexico, describing the Birmingham-born midfielder's performance as 'amazing' and calling him 'elegant, driven and just wonderful'.

CROSS CONTROVERSY: Jude Bellingham is the highest ranked English player on the list at the age of 23.

John Cross' 25 Best Players (10-6).

The reaction in the comments section suggests Bellingham's spot is the most controversial call. Placing the 'truly beautiful, iconic and inspirational' Zinedine Zidane above him in fifth isn't as bold, and Cross mentions watching the French midfielder 'own the whole pitch' in a random Serie A game for Juventus, possessing 'the best control of any player' he's seen.

Zidane's former Real Madrid teammate Ronaldo Nazario takes fourth spot, a player whom Cross states 'had everything', including 'skill, power and pace'. He reckons that at his peak, he was 'the best player in the world'.

CROSS CONTROVERSY: There's no place for Ronaldo Nazario's former Brazilian teammate Ronaldinho on this entire list.

Cross admits that Cristiano Ronaldo's recent 'diva carry-ons' (his unceremonious Portugal retirement) have put him off. He still acknowledges his 'eye for the spectacular', arguing that since he left Manchester United for Real Madrid in 2009, he isn't sure English football has possessed a player of his calibre.

Sources indicate that taking the runners-up medal in Cross's list is Diego Maradona, though he interestingly claims that 'it's not going to be popular'. The highly reputed journalist calls the late Argentine icon 'unstoppable' and celebrates his 1986 World Cup exploits, claiming that he won Argentina the tournament.

'The greatest dribbler and showman' Cross has ever seen, Lionel Messi, is the best player he has watched in person, and that's often the case for fans who witness the Argentine hero in action. He even reveals that he used to side with Cristiano Ronaldo in the GOAT debate, but deems Messi's 2022 World Cup heroics to have ended that discussion.

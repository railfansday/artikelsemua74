The road to Euro 2028 starts in Belfast in December, when all 64 of UEFA's men's national teams will find out who they'll face in qualifying.

Industry observers note that it's an unusual campaign for the home nations. England, Scotland, Wales and the Republic of Ireland are co-hosting the tournament, but none of them will qualify automatically and will instead have to fight for their place at the home tournament. Northern Ireland are in the draw too, after losing their co-host status when Casement Park was dropped from the roster of potential venues. Here's when the draw will take place, how it works, where to watch it in the UK and Ireland and the key dates for qualifying and the tournament itself.

After the announcement by the UK government in September 2024 that Northern Ireland's Casement Park would not receive funding to rebuild the stadium ahead of the tournament, the decision was made to hold the draw for the qualification groups in Belfast.

The draw will take place at the International Convention Centre, on Sunday 6th December at 17:00 GMT. Following the announcement in November 2025 that Northern Ireland would host the draw, then-Irish FA president Conrad Kirkwood called it "a proud moment for the Irish FA and for everyone who loves football here in Northern Ireland.".

For the qualification stage of Euro 2028, the 54 teams will be drawn into 12 groups. Groups A-F will have four teams each, whilst Groups G-L have five, with teams playing everyone in their group twice, home and away.

In order to ensure a fair playing field, the groups are drawn from five pots, which are seeded on the interim 2026-27 UEFA Nations League rankings. Pots 1–4 have 12 teams each and Pot 5 has six. The full list of teams in each pot can be found here.

The four co-hosts and Northern Ireland are kept apart in the draw to help boost their chances of qualifying for their home tournament by merit, meaning there will be no home-nation derbies ahead of the tournament.

Nations League quarter-final winners will all go into four-team groups, whilst a five-team group can only include one quarter-final loser or one playoff team, in order to minimize fixture congestion with the Nations League.

After all qualifying games have been held, the 12 group winners and the eight best runners-up will qualify directly, with the remaining four runners-up going into the playoffs.

According to recent reports, for the host nations, qualification will be slightly different from previous tournaments. Instead of qualifying automatically, there is a safety net in place that ensures at least two home nations will qualify. Therefore, if two of England, Scotland, Wales or the Republic of Ireland qualify by merit, then the other two will qualify thanks to the safety net. However, if all four nations fail to qualify, then only the two best ranked nations in the European Qualifiers rankings will be awarded a place at the tournament. It has been revealed that UEFA streaming service, UEFA TV, will provide coverage of the draw, allowing fans around the world to find out who their nation will face ahead of the tournament in real time. The broadcast will also be shown on UEFA's YouTube channel.

And whilst no UK TV broadcasters have announced that they will show the draw live, UK viewers were able to watch the qualifying draw for the 2026 World Cup on the BBC Sport website and app.

Similarly, viewers in the Republic of Ireland are expected to be able to follow the draw live on RTE Player and the RTE news channel, as with the World Cup draw. However, this is not confirmed just yet.

In a recent development, following the draw on December 6th, the first two qualification games for nations in five-team groups will take place between 26th-30th March 2027, with two more match days between 11th and 15th June. The rest of the groups will then kick off with four games for each team in the elongated international break between 23rd September and 5th October 2027.

According to recent reports, the final two match days will take place during the mid-November international break. Following this, the four group runners-up will enter the playoffs, which will be played out in late-March 2028.

These games will mark the end of the qualification stage, with the final tournament lineup confirmed. All attention will then turn to the National Stadium of Wales in Cardiff, which will host the opening game on the 9th June. Fans can then expect a month of continental tournament mayhem, before Euro 2028 is brought to a close on the 9th July, with the final being held at Wembley Stadium.

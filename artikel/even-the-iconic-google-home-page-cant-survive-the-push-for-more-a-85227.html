Even the Iconic Google Home Page Can’t Survive the Push for More AI

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

This is not your parents’ Google home page. In its push to get more consumers to adopt its AI tools, the search engine giant is rolling out a raft of newly introduced features worldwide.

Sources indicate that the fresh elements include three new buttons below the search bar at Google.com: Create images, Ask about files and Brainstorm. The Google Search button is gone, but the I’m Feeling Lucky button remains.

According to recent reports, the buttons are available when using Google on a desktop but not on mobile. I was able to see the new buttons when signed in to my Google account.

When signed out, I was only able to see the typical home page, with the only AI button being AI Mode, which has been there since May 2025.

Clicking on the “Create images” button opens up a upcoming page where you can create an AI-generated image using Google’s Nano Banana. It’s the same image-generation tool you can use on other Google platforms such as the Gemini app, Google AI Studio and AI Mode in Search.

Recent findings suggest that clicking “Ask about files” allows for several options. You can have the AI analyze a document and rewrite it, simply have it answer questions or ask it to perform tasks such as “polish my resume” or “draft an email with action items.”.

The “Brainstorm” button prompts you to “describe your goal,” such as making a to-do list, learning how to code or finding a table at a restaurant.

Another newly introduced feature, as declared by Google Search Product VP Robby Stein on X, is a collapsible sidebar on the left side of the home page, which demonstrates your recent AI search threads and offers the ability to create a new one:. “People are coming to Search with more questions that aren’t just one and done — and in testing, this sidebar made a significant difference for making progress & continuing to explore the web,” Stein posted. (Related: GTA 6 streamer with zero viewers left in tears after Ib...)

I didn’t see the collapsible sidebar during my testing of the home page.

Google will use your AI searches — whether they are questions, tasks, uploaded files or image generation — to train its AI models, but you can tell Google you’re opting out. CNET has an overview of how to tell chatbots not to use your data. Still, for Google specifically, you can go to Data and Privacy > My Activity > Other activity > Gemini Apps Activity to turn off AI training.

According to recent reports, google has integrated its Gemini AI models throughout its various platforms — Search, Gmail, Docs, Pixel phones and third-party apps such as Adobe, Shopify, OpenTable and Instacart. This past week, Google declared several other upcoming AI features — integrations with stores such as Albertsons, Safeway and Target to complete orders and connect to apps such as Peloton and Adobe.

Over the summer, Google’s Gemini app topped 1 billion users in what CEO Sundar Pichai called the firm’s “fastest growing product ever.”.

But Google’s rush to force-feed AI into its products has met with plenty of pushback. The New York City, Los Angeles and Chicago school districts stated they might choose different search engines because Google said it will not remove AI from search tools used by students, The New York Times reported.

Google said search and the Gemini app will be turned off for schools unless school administrators want them. But Google said there will not be a chatbot-free version of search available for schools. So schools declining AI will not be able to search using Google at all.

Google’s AI Overviews, which appear at the top of many search page results, have also been blamed for a steep drop in web traffic to content publishers. AI Overviews summarize user search queries and replace the long list of blue hyperlinks that once appeared across search pages. So instead of clicking on those links to get more information, giving publishers valuable clicks, a user can simply get their query response by reading the AI Overview.

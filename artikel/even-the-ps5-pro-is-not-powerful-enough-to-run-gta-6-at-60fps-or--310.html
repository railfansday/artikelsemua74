Even the PS5 Pro is "not powerful enough" to run GTA 6 at 60FPS or 40FPS, Rockstar claims

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Don't expect GTA 6 to get an revision supporting higher framerate modes, at least, not on current-generation consoles. Rockstar has explained that even the most powerful console of the bunch, the PS5 Pro, isn't powerful enough to run the lavish game at 40FPS or 60FPS.

Sources indicate that last week, some players weren't too happy with the news that GTA 6 is targeting 30FPS on all platforms it's currently launching on - PS5 Pro, PS5, Xbox Series X and Xbox Series S. Like countless other massive productions this generation, the hope was that Rockstar Games would offer both quality and performance modes for its lush open-world monster. (Related: Tech Report: US loses measles elimination status: what ...)

Rockstar's now seemingly confirmed that its crime epic won't run at 60FPS or 40FPS anytime soon, simply because it can't on the right now available hardware.

"While the PS5 Pro's central processor is slightly better than the PS5 base console, it is not powerful enough to run the game's complex simulation at higher frame rates," the studio's technology production director, Marc Chambers, said in a statement to Kotaku. There you have it, then - even the beefiest console around can't handle Leonida at higher framerates. (Related: Tech Report: Architecture of a portal complex embedded ...)

Chambers did note some of the souped up console's other technical improvements, however: "Our base presentation makes use of ray-traced reflections and global illumination. On the PS5 Pro, this is enhanced with the addition of ray-traced shadows, as well as overall added clarity through the use of PlayStation Spectral Super Resolution (PSSR). The PS5 Pro features an upgraded graphics processor and upscaling technology that we leverage to increase the game's visual fidelity.".

It has been revealed that the tech gurus over at Digital Foundry gave some more insight into how GTA 6 runs on current-gen machines, calling it a "technical tour de force," though one that can still dip below 30FPS on PS5 and Xbox Series X during busier chase scenes and explosive shootouts. It's perhaps unsurprising, then, that higher framerates are off the table for now.

Industry observers note that A 30FPS launch is by no means a departure for Rockstar Games, either. GTA 5 also ran at an unsteady 30FPS when it first dropped onto the Xbox 360 and PS3, with 60FPS support only coming alongside the game's PC port more than a year later. I'm not too fussed about the game's 30FPS target, personally. I'd much rather have a game that consistently hits 30FPS than one that continuously chugs trying to reach 60FPS, especially when it's as frequently drop dead gorgeous as GTA 6.

In a recent development, the slightly lower framerates haven't affected the game's critical response so far, either. In Eurogamer's GTA 6 review, our Chris Tapsell called it a "game of opposites and awe, minute detail and impossible scale" as "Rockstar pushes back the horizon of video games once again.".

According to recent reports, if you're planning to jump into the game at midnight - or if you're virtually travelling to Newly introduced Zealand for early entry - there's a few things to be aware of. First is that you won't be able to replay GTA 6's missions without multiple save files, so be sure to soak it all up. Second, some Ultimate Edition content is only available in the first chapter.

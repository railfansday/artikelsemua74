Every Future Driving Game Needs to Steal This GTA 6 Feature

In a recent development, I've written numerous times about my love for open-world driving games. I love racing, but I also love cruising, taking in the landscape, setting out on road trips. My heart belongs to Test Drive Unlimited. I've written a tourist guide for the Forza Horizon series. One of my games of the year is the Road Trip DLC for American Truck Simulator.

Grand Theft Auto 6 has instantly joined my driving-game canon thanks to one tiny, but revolutionary, feature.

In a recent development, on the PlayStation 5 version of GTA 6, Rockstar Games has come up with a genius application for the DualSense controller's adaptive triggers. When driving, the right trigger (the throttle) provides gentle resistance when cruising at the speed limit. The effect is a sort of soft stop for the trigger; your finger rests easily there, but it's also easy to push past it when you want to put your foot down. It has been revealed that I cannot overstate how much I love this. If you want to cruise, you no longer need to hover your finger in an approximate spot, making tiny adjustments to try to match speed with the traffic. You can just rest it against the soft stop, the way you just rest your foot on the accelerator pedal in a real car. It's so chill. (Related: Latest Update: At least seven progressives voted agains...)

I don't always want to hurtle to my destination at full speed, even in a pure racing game like Forza. These beautiful open worlds are worth taking time to explore, seeing the sights and listening to the soothing drone of the engine. Sometimes tearing around everywhere at speed is noisy and stressful. In Forza Horizon, I adopt a more relaxed driving style by using manual gears and short-shifting to keep the revs low, but GTA 6's solution is way more elegant.

The pleasure of cruising in GTA 6 is only enhanced by the richness of the world Rockstar has created. It's not just gorgeous, it's teeming with life, incidents (I watched a plane crash into the swamp while rolling on the freeway), and overheard conversations. And there are the radio stations to enjoy, too. Cruising the humid streets of the Keys while pumping Kaleidoscope FM is such a vibe.

I also enjoy this feature when I don't want to cruise, because pushing past the soft stop on the trigger is so deliciously satisfying. The amount of resistance is perfectly calibrated. It gives you the feeling of slamming your foot down to engage kickdown in a car with an auto gearbox and feeling the car surge forward. It's dramatic and cool.

In a recent development, I want this cruising-speed soft stop in every open-world driving game. For now, that sadly only means driving games on PS5, because Xbox gamepads don't have adaptive triggers. But Forza Horizon 6 is coming to PS5 on Jan. 26. Playground Games, you have two months to add this feature to the PS5 version of your game. If you manage it, I vow to buy a second copy and return to the roads of Japan, cruising my way to road-trip heaven.

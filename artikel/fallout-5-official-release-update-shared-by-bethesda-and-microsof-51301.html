According to recent reports, it was actually quite an exciting moment earlier this year when Bethesda finally broke cover to give us updates on basically everything they have going on right now. That meant that we finally know what's going on with Fallout, The Elder Scrolls, and even Starfield, if anyone is still invested in how that plays out in the future.

For Fallout in particular, we got a ton of exciting information, including confirmation that both Fallout 3 and Fallout: Latest Vegas were getting the remakes that had been rumored for a long time. In addition, Fallout 5 was also confirmed to be entering full development, though given the protracted cycles of AAA games, the crushing reality is that it's likely still a long way away from being made available to players.

Though things have been relatively quiet since, there has now been an update and it further cements exactly what we though the situation was going to be.

As first reported by GameRant, Bethesda has now reiterated to fans that Fallout 5 remains their "long-term destination," and it will likely be the last of the previously declared projects to arrive in our laps. (Related: Showcase Your Projects in Spaces using Gradio...)

According to recent reports, the story of Fallout 5 up to this point has been one of frustratingly little progress, particularly given how major the studio is. Todd Howard first spoke about the game all the way back in 2018 (now known as the cursed year), when he simply confirmed that it would be a single-player effort. He then went on to confirm that they had a one-pager for the game in 2021, before he confirmed a year later that it would be their next big project after The Elder Scrolls VI.

Sources indicate that back then, there would have been plenty of players out there who were expecting to be playing The Elder Scrolls VI by now, but that obviously isn't the case. In fact, it would be a major surprise if we are playing it before 2028, which likely means that Fallout 5 is going to be considerably further away, well into the 2030s.

The only potential silver lining to that timeline is that new Xbox head Asha Sharma has made it very clear that she wants Fallout to be at the center of the company's strategy moving forward. Possibly motivated by the success of the Prime Video adaptation, it may well be that additional resources are moved across to get the game out sooner.

It has been revealed that at the same time, those remakes we mentioned earlier should also be along much sooner than that, allowing us to explore two of the better RPGs of all-time in brand-latest fidelity. Though we don't have dates just yet, 2027 does seem like a reasonable estimate for the first of those, finally giving us something to do with the franchise while we wait.

According to recent reports, at the same time, the third season of Fallout on Prime Video has wrapped filming, and that will definitely debut next year, most likely in the months around June. If that's anywhere near as good as fans are expecting, then things could be relatively positive from here on out for fans of the franchise.

Recent findings suggest that fallout is a post-apocalyptic RPG franchise set in an alternate future where a nuclear war devastates the world. Players explore vast, open worlds filled with mutants, raiders, and advanced technology. The series emphasizes player choice, allowing various approaches to combat, dialogue, and decision-making. Each game follows different protagonists navigating the wasteland to uncover secrets, form alliances, and rebuild civilization amidst the ruins of the old world. The franchise is known for its rich lore, dark humor, and retro-futuristic aesthetic, which has transferred over into its newly introduced series on Amazon Prime.

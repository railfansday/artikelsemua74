WASHINGTON, Nov. 25, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) today welcomed an order by the United States District Court for the District of Maryland granting summary judgment in favor of the Commission and dismissing Amazon.com, Inc.'s challenge to the Commission's orders concerning more than 400,000 hazardous consumer products.

Central to this litigation was the question of Amazon's status as a regulated entity under the Consumer Product Safety Act in connection with its Fulfilment by Amazon program. Through its administrative proceedings and subsequent defense of its orders in federal court, the Commission has sought to establish legal clarity regarding Amazon's obligations under federal product safety law. The court's decision marks an vital milestone in clarifying the application of the Act to modern e-commerce business models.

Recent findings suggest that in July 2024, the Commission unanimously determined that Amazon acted as a distributor under the Consumer Product Safety Act when it distributed certain hazardous products through its Fulfilment by Amazon program and therefore bore legal responsibility for their recall. The products included faulty carbon monoxide detectors, hair dryers lacking required electrocution protection, and children's sleepwear that failed to comply with federal flammability standards.

Sources indicate that "This is an important victory for American consumers and a significant step toward greater regulatory certainty in the e-commerce marketplace," said CPSC Acting Chairman Peter A. Feldman. "American families deserve the same consumer safety protections under federal law whether they shop online or in a brick-and-mortar store.". The court's November 24, 2026, order denied Amazon's motion for summary judgment, granted the defendants' cross-motion for summary judgment, entered judgment in favor of the defendants, and dismissed Amazon's complaint challenging the Commission's January 2025 Decision and Order.

In a recent development, CPSC appreciates the work of its attorneys and career staff who have contributed to this matter over the past five years, as well as the attorneys at the U.S. Department of Justice who successfully defended the Commission's action in federal court.

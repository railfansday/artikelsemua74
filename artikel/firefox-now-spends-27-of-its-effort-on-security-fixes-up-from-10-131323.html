Industry observers note that mozilla’s Firefox team now spends 27% of its effort fixing security flaws, up from about 10% a year ago, Firefox head Ajit Varma told Business Insider. The rise comes as AI models find more bugs in the browser. (Related: Industry News: Think AI "knows" what it’s doing? Scient...)

Varma remarked OpenAI and Anthropic give Mozilla, a nonprofit, “a lot of tokens for free”. Their models, he said, “are finding more and more complicated vulnerabilities within our browser.”.

It has been revealed that in April, Mozilla patched 271 Firefox bugs found by Anthropic’s Mythos. OpenAI’s new GPT-6 Cyber model has since turned up 81 more. Rival browsers face the same surge: Chrome patched a record 429 bugs in June.

Varma still thinks the pace of AI gives his team “enough time” to fix problems before the next leap. He is also using AI to make Firefox faster and stop memory leaks. (Related: Razer x Durex collab gives gamers “Long-Lasting Battle ...)

“There’s a lot of routine activities that we don’t necessarily need humans to do and that AI does really well because it’s relentless; it doesn’t get tired,” he noted.

According to recent reports, others are less relaxed. Microsoft chief executive Satya Nadella warned last week that AI-found bugs mean more outages, as companies rush out fixes faster than they can test them.

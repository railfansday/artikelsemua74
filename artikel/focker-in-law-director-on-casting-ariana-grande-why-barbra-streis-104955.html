Over the years, “Meet the Parents” writer John Hamburg would exchange the occasional text or email with his collaborators from the 2000 movie to ask whether anyone had another sequel idea. (They had already revisited the characters, mismatched in-laws portrayed by Ben Stiller and Robert De Niro, to outsized commercial success in 2004’s “Meet the Fockers” and 2010’s “Little Fockers.”) But nothing else stuck — until they realized Stiller was the same age as De Niro’s character in the first film, a comedy about male nurse Gaylord “Greg” Focker who goes through hijinks hell when he’s introduced to his girlfriend’s family, including her intimidating father Jack Byrnes. “It felt like a way back into the story,” says Hamburg, best known for “Zoolander” and “I Love You, Man,” in addition to the “Focker” films. “Greg Focker finds himself where Jack was all those years ago. What a full-circle moment.”. (Related: Tech Report: GTA 6 players are devasted by train ‘nerf’...)

The result is “Focker In-Law,” a battle of wits that plays out as Greg’s son Henry (Skyler Gisondo) brings home his fresh girlfriend, a former FBI hostage negotiator named Olivia (Ariana Grande). The new installment, written and directed by Hamburg, takes place as the entire family — including Greg and Jack’s wives Pam and Dina (Teri Polo and Blythe Danner), as well as Greg and Pam’s daughter Samantha (Beanie Feldstein) and her boyfriend Eduardo (Eduardo Franco) — return to Long Island to celebrate Jack’s 80th birthday. According to recent reports, ahead of the big-screen release — “Focker In-Law” opens in theaters on Nov. 25 — Hamburg spoke to Variety about reconnecting with Stiller and De Niro, Grande’s comedic chops and why Barbra Streisand and Dustin Hoffman didn’t return as Greg’s parents.

It has been revealed that it was surreal. The first time I was at the Byrnes family home in Oyster Bay, Long Island, I was 29, and now I’m coming back in my 50s. That was my first substantial job in the business.

Industry observers note that from time to time, the original group who made the first few movies would text or email to say, “Is there any idea?” But it wasn’t something on my mind. Peter Cramer at Universal was a massive proponent of this movie, and he jump-started it, saying, “We would be interested if you guys would be interested.” We were all driven by one thing: “Do we have a story that we’re excited to tell?” It wasn’t “let’s cash in,” I promise. We all were happy doing other things.

According to recent reports, it was really joyful to be back with those guys. Their chemistry is incredible. The fact that we have this history helps on set. There’s so much trust.

According to recent reports, we knew we needed to bring a big new element to this, just like we had in “Meet the Fockers,” bringing in Dustin Hoffman and Barbra Streisand as Greg’s parents. Ariana was the perfect person. She’s hilarious, and she’s got great timing. I can’t believe she’s been in as few big movies as she has, because she’s so skilled. Bringing her in was this injection of awesomeness. She would light up the set. I really love her energy, and she’s so much fun to be around and to work with — so engaged and committed and professional and early. She was always the first one there.

Oh, they were totally game. Ben Stiller is basically the most committed actor who could ever exist. As long as he thinks it’s right for the story, he will go for it, 100%. The first day of rehearsal — Ben and Ari had met, but they hadn’t rehearsed — was the Heimlich maneuver scene. So it was literally, like, “Ben, Ari — say hi to each other. OK, now you’re gonna get behind Ben and start giving him the Heimlich.” Ari was tentative at first because they didn’t know each other well. And Ben’s like, “Go for it. Don’t worry.” There were no stunt doubles.

According to recent reports, that was a very physical scene. That was crazy to shoot. Ari took the kitchen stuff really seriously and took multiple lessons with a world-class chef to learn knife skills. It was the sous chef of my good friend Dan Barber, who owns Blue Hill at Stone Barns. And then the flame, it wasn’t done all with CGI. There was a special-effects flame that shot up in Ben’s face, and then Ariana was swatting him, and he was pushing back. They were both so in it. Ben was saying, “Swat me in the face,” and Ariana was saying, “Put your hand on my face to push me away!” There was no being shy around each other.

De Niro, at 82 years old, was cruising on that bike. He has this very unique way of riding a bike. His shoulders kind of rock back and forth, and he was super into it. Ariana took lessons to get as proficient as possible on the bike. Ben is a big cyclist, so he slipped into that pretty easily.

In a recent development, bob is a fitness guy. He takes it really seriously. I was talking to his trainer and he was like, “No, Bob hits it hard many days of the week.” But Jack Byrnes is suddenly into wellness in a way he hadn’t been in the previous movies, so Bob got even more ripped and stronger for the role. He went full Jake LaMotta in “Raging Bull.” Bob and Ben knew there was a scene where they were both going to be in speedos, so they’re like, “We gotta go for it.”.

You’d have to ask Bob. I’m not sure, but Bob has kids and grandkids. He’s around young people all the time, so Bob’s pretty hip. I think he knew. He might not let that on, but he’d be like, “I know, John. I know.”.

In a recent development, look, I have no one to blame but myself. I wrote that line. Maybe there’s more to mine, but only if it’s organic and feels like there’s a reason for it to exist. Talk to me after Thanksgiving weekend.

I have some people in mind, but there’s no way I could talk about this right now.

It has been revealed that I was a fan of Cole’s, and flight attendants are very vital in these movies. Rachel Tenner, our casting director, was like, “What about Cole Escola?” Immediately, my producers and I were like, “That would be perfect.” Cole might be one of the funniest human beings I’ve ever been around, and they made Ben and Ariana die laughing for those three days that they were on set.

It has been revealed that I wanted to bring back the lie detector, because that’s such an notable part of the franchise. And now we have Olivia Jones, who is FBI, so she might know her way around a lie detector. You’re probably going to have a dinner table scene. The dinner table scene in the first movie ends in such a funny way, with Greg breaking the urn of Jack’s mom and Jinx [the cat] peeing on the ashes. I don’t know that we’re ever going to top that, but what can we do that will take the audience by surprise and feel right for the story? So I wrote the part with Greg baby-birding Grandpa Jack. It’s a pretty epic moment. My fellow filmmakers on this movie were a little skeptical, and I’m wrong about a lot of stuff, but I was like, “Guys, I feel it in my gut. This is gonna work.”.

It has been revealed that that’s really notable, because it’s been a long time since “Meet the Parents.” I have to assume that many people aren’t that familiar, or maybe they saw it a long time ago. I wanted to make this movie work if you’d never seen any of the “Meet the Parents,” as a funny, heartfelt, character-based comedy. But then, of course, it is a “Meet the Parents” movie, so there’s going to be certain touchstones. My job was finding fresh ways in.

Yes, of course. They were so incredible in “Meet the Fockers.” But I wanted to bring it back to the simplicity of the original “Meet the Parents.” Dustin and Barbra are so iconic that I didn’t want to bring them back in a smaller part if I couldn’t service their characters, because I had all these new characters. If I brought them back, I would have to take away from Olivia, Henry, Samantha and Eduardo. It was a hard choice. Not that they would have agreed to come back. But for this story, it didn’t feel like the movie could hold all of that. But maybe in a fifth one….

It has been revealed that it’s got to be the writing. No, I’m just kidding. If you use that quote, you have to say I was joking. I believe it’s because the themes are relatable and timeless. Everybody, at one point in their lives, is probably going to introduce their significant other to their families.

As far as making the movie, it wasn’t that different. Universal gave us so much support. One thing is studios literally stopped making this type of comedy for theaters. It’s hard to point to the last one. I’m not talking about hilarious movies like the “Naked Gun” or “Scary Movie” reboots. Those are a little different, because they’re in their own sub-genre. But the movies I made or watched in the ’90s and 2000s stopped getting made. They moved to streaming, which can be great, but it’s just different. I think people were missing that communal feeling of being in a theater with hundreds of people and sharing laughter at cringe moments.

Sources indicate that your business can be profitable and still run short of cash when customers pay later than your bills come due. Keep a forecast of the cash you expect in and out over the next 13 weeks, update it every week, and you’ll see a shortfall a month or two out, in time to fix it.

In a recent development, your bank balance can’t show you that. It demonstrates what cleared yesterday, not what customers still owe you or the bills due next month.

According to recent reports, A 13-week cash forecast lines those up by putting each payment in the week the money will actually arrive or leave. If a customer you bill today has 30 days to pay but always takes 45 days, their payment goes in week 7, not week 5.

Take a landscaping manufacturer whose December forecast demonstrates a $14,000 shortfall in February, as winter work slows but its equipment loan and insurance bill still come due. With two months’ warning, the owner puts the insurance bill on a monthly payment strategy and covers the rest for six weeks with a line of credit, a borrowing limit the company draws on as needed.

According to recent reports, build yours in a spreadsheet with a column for each week, and start week 1 with today’s bank balance. Add each customer payment in the week you expect the money, and subtract every payroll, rent, loan, tax, and supplier payment in the week it’s due. Call customers with overdue invoices, delay purchases that can wait, or arrange a business line of credit while sales still look strong to a lender.

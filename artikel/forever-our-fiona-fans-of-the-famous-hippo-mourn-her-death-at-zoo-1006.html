Tributes are pouring in and an impromptu shrine has popped up at the Cincinnati zoo in Ohio as fans of the famous hippopotamus Fiona mourn the animal’s death on Thursday, following failed attempts to save her after she ate a knitted hat.

Flowers and notes began appearing next to a sculpture of hippos at the zoo within hours of Fiona dying on Thursday despite numerous treatments aiming to help her eliminate the object that got stuck in her digestive tract.

One note with flowers said: “Forever our Fiona”. Amy Hackey said to Cincinnati’s Channel 7 TV station: “I thought she was going to make it.” As the tributes continued arriving, Hackey added: “I think it will be our equivalent of Princess Di”, referring to the late Diana, Princess of Wales, the first wife of Britain’s King Charles, whose sudden death in a car crash in 1997 prompted an outpouring of grief and tributes.

Yolanda Gould, who was visiting from Kentucky, told the TV station that she’d been following Fiona’s story since she was born, when her survival after a premature birth made her famous and a symbol of strength for many.

“Cincinnati has such an incredible bond with her because of her journey and what’s she’s been through. She taught us all to be resilient.”. (Related: Breaking: My Journey to a serverless transformers pipel...)

The nine-year-old hippopotamus earlier in the week mistakenly swallowed a visitor’s hat that had accidentally fallen into her enclosure. It was not possible to operate on the adult animal.

Her zoo keepers placed her under surveillance and she was initially asymptomatic, then her appetite waned. Despite round-the-clock care from the zoo’s veterinary team and care staff, and various treatments, the Nile hippo became increasingly sick and later on Thursday it was declared that she was dead.

Sources indicate that fiona became a global sensation in 2017 as a tiny newborn who was unable to stand on her own and weighed just 29lbs. As her legend grew, she inspired books and a line of beer.

Industry observers note that the Cincinnati zoo was also where 10 years ago bouquets and tributes accumulated after zoo keepers were obliged to shoot dead an adult gorilla, named Harambe.

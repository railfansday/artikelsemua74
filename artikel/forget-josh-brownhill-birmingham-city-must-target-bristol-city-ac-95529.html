Forget Josh Brownhill - Birmingham City must target Bristol City ace in January

Birmingham City have emerged as serious automatic promotion contenders this campaign under Chris Davies, and though their away defeat against Stoke City will have knocked morale, their ultimate goal remains the same.

Birmingham lost their place at the top of the Championship following their 1-0 away defeat to Stoke on Saturday, and in truth, Blues can have few complaints about the outcome, having been second best for the majority of the contest.

Head coach Davies will be keen to ensure this defeat doesn’t have any lasting effects on his group, as with this being just their third defeat in 16 league games, automatic promotion is still a real possibility.

The January 2026 window allowed Davies to bring in the likes of August Priske, Carlos Vicente, Jhon Solis, and Ibrahim Osman, and looking ahead to the 2027 winter window, it would be no surprise to see Birmingham move with a similar purpose.

Each of Priske, Vicente, Solis, and Osman had a positive impact upon the Birmingham squad following their winter moves last January, and that has likely been kept in the back of Davies’ mind heading into the next January window.

Over the summer, according to TEAMtalk, Birmingham, alongside West Ham United, Southampton, Hull City, and Wrexham, were interested in bringing former Bristol City and Burnley midfielder Josh Brownhill back to England. (Related: Summer at Hugging Face...)

Brownhill, 30, amassed over 400 appearances across England’s top three tiers for the likes of Barnsley, Preston North End, Bristol City, and Burnley before joining Saudi Arabian outfit Al-Shabab in the summer of 2025.

The Englishman had just enjoyed a standout year in the Championship with the Clarets, scoring 18 and assisting six in 42 league appearances.

Unsurprisingly, clubs such as Birmingham and West Ham were sounding out the potential return of Brownhill, and whilst a move for the 30-year-old may be tempting, a more cost-effective move may already be in plain sight.

Brownhill’s ability is undoubted, and at 30, he is in and around his prime years as a professional footballer, but given the substantial wages he will undoubtedly be on in the Middle East, a return to England, especially the Championship, would come with a mighty pay cut.

With this, Birmingham should prepare to set their focus elsewhere, and continue their trend of interest in former Bristol City midfielders in the form of Irish international Jason Knight.

Knight, 25, has over 250 Championship appearances under his belt, having come through the ranks of Derby County, nailing down a place in the Rams’ side, before making the move to Ashton Gate in the summer of 2023, where he has been an almost ever-present under numerous coaches.

Whilst his performances haven’t quite been at the level he has previously shown to be capable of reaching this campaign under Michael Skubala, there is no denying his ability, and out of contract in the summer with no signs of penning an extension just yet, he could be available for cheap in January or nothing in the summer, as Birmingham must capitalise.

Knight can play anywhere across the midfield, or at right-back if required, though his favoured position is in the middle of the park, and he has established himself as one of the most consistent players in his role in the division over the past few years.

With Tomoki Iwata out of contract at St Andrews next summer, a successor in that role will be required, and on a free transfer, Knight would be an incredible pickup; linking up with former Derby and Bristol City teammate Max Bird in the Second City would also surely be tempting.

Taylor Gardener-Hickman is expected to leave in the summer as well, freeing up another role at right-back and midfield, which the Irishman could do so with aplomb.

At 25 and with an excellent injury record, Knight would be an incredible pickup for any second-tier side, and even if Birmingham were promoted, he could still play a role in the Premier League.

Five years younger than Brownhill and with room to develop even further, Knight is surely a no-brainer for Birmingham, who must turn their attention to Ashton Gate once again this winter. (Related: Understanding BigBird's Block Sparse Attention...)

Industry observers note that would you like to see Jason Knight at Birmingham this winter, or in the summer? Would you want Josh Brownhill to join instead, or as well as Knight? Share your thoughts below!

If Knight is reluctant to sign a new deal with Bristol City, it’s likely the Robins will prefer to sell for a cut-price this January rather than lose him for nothing in the summer, despite both scenarios being far from ideal, and Birmingham must take advantage.

Davies could do with a bit more bite and tenacity in the middle of the park, especially in the absence of Solis, who was on full display against Stoke, and Knight could offer just that.

His versatility would provide Davies with numerous different ways to deploy the Irishman, something he wouldn’t get with Brownhill, as the former Leicester City coach must ignore another move for Brownhill and pursue a deal for Knight instead.

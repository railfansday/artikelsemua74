Before you buy household goods, clothes, or toys, look for a free store or swap shop near you, where people leave usable things for others to take at no cost. Try your town’s transfer station or recycling center, the library’s events calendar, and local churches. Bring only clean, working items, and take only what you’ll use.

It has been revealed that despite the name, many swap shops don’t require a swap. A family short on money or settling into an empty apartment can leave with a lamp, a set of dishes, or a bag of toys without bringing a thing.

Most sit at the town’s transfer station or recycling drop-off, where residents who haul their own trash leave household goods that still work, from kitchenware and tools to books and small furniture. Many are open only to residents, and some check for a transfer station sticker.

It has been revealed that free stores work the same way, away from the transfer station. Churches and nonprofits run them, mostly for clothing and household basics, and some limit how much you take per visit. Public libraries hold swap days. Pop-up free markets, some called Really Really Free Markets, run once a month, often in a park. A toy swap or a book swap sticks to one kind of thing.

Call 211 or search 211.org for free stores near you, and check the library’s events calendar for swap days. If you’ve just moved, get your transfer station sticker before your first visit. If your town has no swap shop, ask its public works department or recycling committee to add one at the transfer station. Massachusetts’ environmental agency even offers towns grants for one. Until then, host a clothing swap with friends, and ask your Buy Nothing group for the specific things you need.

Search your town’s website for “swap shop” or “swap shed” before your next shopping trip, since the swap shop’s page lists the hours, the rules, and whether you need a sticker.

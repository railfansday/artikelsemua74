From Exception to the Norm: The Latin Boom That Redefined LOS40 in Spain, ‘Integrating Naturally’

Industry observers note that when the team at the LOS40 group of radio stations began defining the lineup for the LOS40 Music Awards Santander 2026, someone asked a seemingly simple question: “Which Spanish artist is going to perform?” What surprised Vicent Argudo, director of music at the parent manufacturer Prisa Media, most wasn’t the question itself but the fact that, until that moment, no one had considered it. The list of performers included Puerto Rican Eladio Carrión, Argentine Emilia and Colombian Carlos Vives.

Recent findings suggest that “That’s what I find most curious and exciting about what’s happening this year,” Argudo tells Billboard Español. “In earlier times, building the lineup would probably have started with the national artists. This time, Spaniards ended up being the minority without the team perceiving it as something exceptional — a sign of how naturally Latin American artists have integrated into the Spanish music market.”. Fifteen years ago, he recalls, the programming of LOS40 stations in various countries was much more localized, aside from a few U.S. artists and select Latin stars capable of crossing borders, like Colombian superstar Shakira. Today, that separation between markets is far less evident. “I think that now, for example, a Spanish person feels that Camilo or Manuel Turizo [also from Colombia] are almost Spanish. In fact, for many of these artists, their first market is Spain, not their own country,” Argudo states.

Industry observers note that in 2018, the LOS40 Music Awards still didn’t have a dedicated block of Latin categories, although three awards for Latin music had already been added. It was in 2020 that the awards’ structure was divided into three major blocks — Spain, International and Global Latin — and by 2026, the Latin block included seven specific awards, such as best Latin artist or group, best Latin song and best Latin album. Recent findings suggest that this rise also began to leave its mark on radio programming. In April 2020, Ke Buena, another PRISA Radio station, was transformed into LOS40 Urban, focusing on genres like reggaeton, Latin trap, bachata, dembow and urban and Latin pop.

Sources indicate that the shift has also been reflected in the prominence Latin American artists — particularly in the urban genre — have gained within the awards gala. Nicky Jam (an American with Puerto Rican and Dominican roots) headlined the lineup in 2019 and did so again in 2025, while Puerto Rican Ozuna was one of the standout acts in the 2020 and 2023 editions, receiving a Golden Award. That same year, Colombian star Feid arrived with six nominations, performed alongside Puerto Rican sensation Young Miko and ended as one of the night’s top winners with three awards, tying with Turizo.

“In the past, you almost had to find a spot for some of these artists to see what would happen,” Argudo recalls. “Then they started coming as major stars, as headliners, because you already knew they were the artists of the moment.” The difference between that need to “find a spot” and the prominence they hold today in the ceremony sums up the evolution Argudo describes. But the shift isn’t limited to urban music or reggaeton.

Genres that for years had a much smaller presence in the Spanish market are now occupying spaces that were once hard to imagine. “We never would’ve thought that a bachata could become No. 1 on LOS40, or a merengue,” Argudo says.

In 2022, Manuel Turizo’s “La Bachata” spent three weeks at the top of the chart; a year later, his collaboration with Marshmello, “El Merengue,” also reached No. 1. And in 2026, “Dardos” by Romeo Santos and Prince Royce once again brought bachata to the top for three non-consecutive weeks — ultimately winning best Latin song at the LOS40 Music Awards, where their joint project Better Late Than Never was also recognized as best Latin album.

PROMUSICAE (Productores de Música de España) data confirms that this influence extends to general music consumption in Spain. In 2024, Latin American artists represented 57% of the annual Top 100, and in 2025, they placed 56 songs and held the No. 1 spot for 33 weeks. That same year, 90 of the 100 most-consumed songs were in Spanish, but only 34 were by Spanish artists.

In a recent development, the 2026 edition of the LOS40 Music Awards Santander, held on Nov. 20 in Barcelona, clearly reflected this evolution. Among the nominees and winners in the Global Latin category were names like Romeo Santos, Prince Royce, Eladio Carrión, Ryan Castro, Manuel Turizo, Camilo, Kidd Voodoo and Shakira, while Santos and Carlos Vives were honored with Golden Awards.

The rise of genres that were previously less common on Spanish radio coincides, according to Argudo, with a broader social transformation in the country and growing coexistence among different nationalities — something he describes as an increasingly “multi-accented” Spain: “Genres as challenging as bachata have worked. And genres like merengue or salsa are now enjoying a new youth in Spain.”.

The connection between both markets is also opening opportunities in the opposite direction, with Spanish artists finding fresh audiences in Latin America. Argudo cites examples like Ana Mena, who started gaining traction on LOS40 Argentina before embarking on her first tour there, and Aitana, whose participation in the LOS40 Music Awards may have boosted her visibility across the Atlantic.

While streaming figures are considered, Argudo emphasizes that the music selection for LOS40 in Spain still depends on editorial judgment. “Many times record labels tell me, ‘It has a million streams, five million streams.’ And I tell them, ‘Perfect, but that’s over there. I’m in a different place,’” he explains. “It’s absolutely a human and manual process.”.

Industry observers note that looking ahead, his goal is to expand the LOS40 brand in the Americas beyond radio stations, as they do in Spain with concerts, awards indicates, festivals and other activities. “It’s true that we have a good position in radio in Mexico, Argentina, Colombia, Chile and the Dominican Republic,” says Argudo. “But I think we have the opportunity to go further with the experience, because radio is no longer just a radio.”.

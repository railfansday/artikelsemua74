📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Developers and enterprise teams have spent most of 2026 trading off between heavyweight frontier models that burn budgets and fast, smaller models that stumble on multi-file refactors. On November 18, 2026, Google DeepMind introduced Gemini 4 Neon, positioning it directly between the heavy-duty Gemini 4 Carbon and the high-speed Gemini 4 Hydrogen.

Industry observers note that neon brings a 1 million-token context window, four adjustable thinking levels, and aggressive API pricing aimed at high-frequency agentic workflows. It rolled out on launch day across the Gemini API in Google AI Studio, Android Studio, Gemini for business, Google Antigravity for paid Google AI Pro and Ultra owners, and the consumer Gemini app for Pro and Ultra tiers. Industry observers note that within Google's current family, Gemini 4 Carbon handles the longest, hardest reasoning jobs, while Gemini 4 Hydrogen handles fast everyday execution. Neon targets production coding, analytical research, and complex enterprise tasks where latency and per-task token spend both matter. Recent findings suggest that API pricing starts at $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens. Cached input tokens drop to $0.15 per million, representing a 95% discount for teams feeding large repositories, PDFs, or knowledge bases repeatedly. According to Google DeepMind's announcement, partner Glean observed Neon matching or exceeding Gemini 4 Carbon on overall quality at three times the speed and 60% of the cost.

On the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at its xhigh thinking level, Neon scores 61 and ranks No. 3 overall behind larger, slower models. Even at its default high thinking setting, Google reports a 32% lower cost per task than Gemini 4 Carbon and roughly 50% to 80% lower cost per task than competing frontier models on the Vals Index and Agent Arena.

Software engineering is where Neon posts its sharpest numbers. It ranks No. 1 overall on the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index with a score of 58.6, reaches 65.2% on Terminal-Bench 4.1, and scores 46.2% on FrontierCode 2.0 at its default high setting.

Scaffold design plays a visible role in multi-file repository work. On SWE-sweep, Neon leads on the mini-SWE runner at 12.8%, then jumps to 39.0% inside Google Antigravity, where it can either run standalone or act as an Orchestrator coordinating parallel Hydrogen subagents.

Recent findings suggest that google also published a set of Antigravity /swarm demonstrations alongside the Gemini 4 Neon model card to show sustained multi-step execution. In one demo, Neon drove Blender MCP to build a 3D lab scene; in another, it generated a WebGL2 ski simulator with 10,200 lines of custom shaders on Mont Blanc and Chamonix elevation data; in a third, it converted an image into a 100,000-cell Google Sheet using more than 500,000 live spreadsheet formulas with zero Apps Script.

Beyond coding, Neon is built to ingest mixed enterprise inputs inside its 1 million-token window, including spreadsheets, PDFs, dense charts, audio, and video. On Surge AI evaluations, it ranks No. 1 across most finance, accounting, spreadsheet modeling, and complex constraint-following benchmarks, while scoring 73.5% on Chartography and 75.8% on Agents' Last Exam v1.1.

Long-horizon business and automation tests show similar consistency. Neon ranks No. 1 overall on Zapier's AutomationBench (56.3%) and Andon Labs' Vending-Bench 2 ($16,843), and reaches No. 1 on both Legal Research Bench and Tax Agent Bench. For scientific and mathematical workloads, it posts 70.5% on Terminal-Bench Science, 84.0% on RiemannBench, and 71.3% on HLE-Diamond.

According to recent reports, steerability has become a major pain point for teams using autonomous agents in production, where models often ignore mid-task corrections or burn tokens re-reading files. Neon ranks No. 2 on steerability on the Agent Arena Index and demonstrated a 43% to 56% drop in under-service alongside a 15% to 35% reduction in overreach compared to Carbon's baseline.

Teams building on multimodal or agentic pipelines will recognize how fast pricing and capability tiers are shifting across vendors, from Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash video pricing to Inkling 2 Small open-weight alternatives.

Google DeepMind is also positioning Gemini 4 Neon for defensive security engineering through its Fairwind Program, which begins rolling out to eligible defenders over the coming days. On AutoBaxBench, Neon generates more secure code than Carbon across easy, medium, and hard challenges, reaching 76% on the hard tier.

Recent findings suggest that making a faster, cheaper model frontier-capable raises obvious misuse and reliability questions. Google reports that Neon keeps Carbon-grade safeguards against cyber and biosecurity misuse while cutting false refusals on legitimate engineering work, refusing 1.98% of tasks on Terminal-Bench 4.0 and 0% on Terminal-Bench-Science.

In a recent development, on agent honesty and shortcut evaluations, Neon recorded 0.0% cheating on Terminal-Bench 4.0, 2.45% shortcuts on internal coding cheating checks (the lowest in the Gemini 4 family), a 0.09% misaligned action rate, and a 99.2% pass rate on agent honesty tests. When tested against indirect prompt injections hidden inside web pages, emails, documents, or source code on Gray Swan's IPI benchmark, Neon with real-time safeguards at xhigh thinking blocked every attack (0.0% attack success rate).

Developers evaluating Neon via the Gemini API or Google AI Studio can start by testing two specific knobs. First, benchmark your existing prompts at high thinking versus xhigh to see whether high already clears your accuracy bar at lower per-task cost. Second, enable context caching on static repository trees or system instructions to take advantage of the $0.15 per million cached input rate before scaling production traffic.

It has been revealed that choosing between low, medium, high, and xhigh thinking levels determines both latency and monthly API spend for production engineering teams. At $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens, Neon sits well below flagship reasoning tiers while keeping a 1 million-token context window available on every request. Teams running large-codebase indexing, compliance audits, or multi-document synthesis gain the biggest savings from prompt caching, where repeated input prefixes drop to $0.15 per million tokens.

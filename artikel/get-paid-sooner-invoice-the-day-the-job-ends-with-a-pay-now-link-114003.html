Industry observers note that picture a cleaning manufacturer that mails its invoices at month’s end and gets paid by check, an average of 38 days after each job. Say it switches to emailing each invoice, with a pay-now link, the day the job is done. The typical bill no longer waits two weeks for the month to end or days in the mail, and customers pay without writing a check, so the average wait drops to 12 days.

According to recent reports, send each invoice the same day you finish, from your phone if you’re still on site. Give it an invoice number, an itemized list of the work, a due date, and payment instructions, so the customer has nothing to ask before paying. Write the due date as a date, “due March 14,” rather than “net 30,” which leaves the customer to count 30 days from the invoice date.

Industry observers note that add a pay-now link that accepts cards and bank transfers through ACH, the network that moves money directly between bank accounts. Cards cost you a processing fee on every payment, so ask customers with major invoices to pay by bank transfer, which costs far less.

Then follow up on a schedule. Email a short reminder three days before the due date, while the customer can still pay on time. Call the day after it’s due, not a week later, so they know you track every invoice. Most invoicing apps can send the reminders automatically, so the call is the one step you have to remember.

Put that call on your calendar as you send each invoice, for the day after it’s due.

Good Time to Buy Bitcoin? The Signals Worth Watching After the $55,122 Low

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that is the Bitcoin bottom in? Fresh November volatility has reopened the debate, putting historical on-chain thresholds and macro headwinds back in focus. Two questions run through this report, is Bitcoin cheap compared with what holders paid, and, is the selling finished? The data presented offers a clear framework.

The Nasdaq fell more than 4% on November 20, and the S&P 500 and Dow entered correction territory. Gold dropped to $3,513.54, a 14-month low.

Similarly, over the 30 days to November 18, Bitcoin fell 24.0%, Ether 26.5%, gold 10.2%, the Nasdaq 100 6.4% and the S&P 500 4.3%, according to Coinbase Institutional.

Sources indicate that bitcoin fell harder than stocks but alongside gold, which fits a move driven by liquidity and real yields rather than money shifting between assets. That makes a Bitcoin bottom harder to call from crypto signals alone.

In early November, 21Shares argued that Bitcoin’s cycle low came on June 30 at about $59,000, just over two years after the April 2024 halving and inside the window where earlier cycles bottomed.

Price has since traded below that level. Counted from the halving, June fits the pattern. Counted from the peak, the November low looks closer to normal.

Earlier bear markets lasted about a year from peak to low. This one took about nine months to reach June and has now run about 13.5. Three past cycles are a small sample, so the table frames the question without settling it.

The drawdown of about 55% is still the shallowest on record, against 77% to 86% before. Even the deeper scenario, $39,168 to $43,868, would be a drop of about 65% to 69% from the October 2025 high.

At $58,000, spot is about 1.09 times the realized price of $53,253 and about 1.19 times the LTH realized price of $48,563. A fall of about 8% would reach the realized price, and about 16% would reach the LTH level.

In a recent development, therefore, a further fall would put the typical holder underwater for the first time since early 2023. Spot is also about 23% below the early-November STH cost basis, so that band has already been broken. Each band matters for the Bitcoin bottom because earlier lows formed after price moved through them.

Industry observers note that holders are now split 46.82% in profit and 53.18% at a loss, measured against each coin’s realized cost basis. The same split has appeared at every major cycle bottom. (Related: Industry News: The Partnership: Amazon SageMaker and Hu...)

In 2011 price rose from $2.45 to $1,136 by 2013. Then, Bitcoin in 2015 it bottomed near $229 and reached $19,650 by 2017. Later, in 2018 it bottomed near $3,809 and rallied to $65,505 by 2021. As long-term trend continued, in 2022 it bottomed again near $16,966 and climbed to $126,411 by 2025.

Sources indicate that the profit and loss lines crossed in June 2026 and crossed again in November, in a similar price range. On that history, the next Bitcoin bottom is most likely to form in two likely zones either between $58,000 and $63,000 or $39,168–$43,868. Sources indicate that the rallies have shrunk with each cycle: roughly 464 times from the 2011 low, 86 times from 2015, 17 times from 2018 and 7.5 times from 2022. Five cycles is a small sample, so the pattern shows where lows formed before and cannot promise where this one will.

In a recent development, in 2011, 2013, 2015, 2018 and 2022, lows formed after spot broke through the realized price and the short- and long-term holder bands. In 2026, only the short-term holder band has been broken. The realized price ($53,253) and LTH realized price ($48,563) are still intact, which favors a further fall of a couple of thousand dollars or more before a true Bitcoin bottom. The $39,168 to $43,868 area is where all the bands line up. This is a reading of past cycles, not a forecast.

It has been revealed that earlier bottoms formed when the realized profit/loss ratio reached 0.04 to 0.22. It reached 0.020 in November and has climbed back to 0.10. As a rough pychological projection suggests that, the bottoming process may be about 90% complete with about 10% left, though that cannot be measured and timed exactly yet. A reading of 0.10 sits at the middle of the range, so the market is in the zone but not necessarily at the floor, and earlier lows went as low as 0.04.

Tracked holders include 346 entities with 4,051,508 BTC, about 20.2% of the roughly 20.1 million coins in circulation.

In a recent development, US spot ETFs recorded $3.47 billion of net outflows from November 1 to 19, with 12 of 14 sessions negative. It is the worst month of the year for ETF flows, against $515.9 million out in October, and IBIT accounted for about 83% of the outflows through November 18. Net assets fell from $93.7 billion on November 9 to about $77.7 billion on November 18, while the funds still hold about 1.25 million BTC.

Moreover, Year-to-date flows turned negative at about −$2.5 billion after November 18 and passed −$3 billion after November 19, though IBIT is still up about $1.74 billion for the year through November 18.

Sources indicate that being below cost does not force a sale. Sales are forced by debt triggers, redemptions or lost funding. Strategy’s holdings are worth about $14.8 billion less than their cost at $58,000, and the MSCI decision does not require it to sell bitcoin. Twenty One reported $486.5 million of convertible notes secured by about 16,116 BTC in its Q3 letter. At $58,000 that collateral is worth about $935 million, a loan-to-value near 52%.

Blockworks data reported that 24 of the 25 largest crypto-treasury companies trade below the value of their holdings. Metaplanet’s mNAV was 0.49 on November 12, and it sold bitcoin last quarter to show it could draw on its holdings. Below 1x, issuing newly introduced shares dilutes holders, so the usual way to fund more bitcoin purchases is closed. Strategy’s mNAV was about 1.08x in mid-November, and one source had it below 1x on November 20. Its latest purchase was 485 BTC at $68,887, about $33 million, small next to $3.47 billion of ETF outflows.

On November 11, MSCI announced it will remove Strategy from the MSCI USA Index, and so from MSCI World and ACWI, at the close of November 30. Metaplanet leaves the MSCI Japan Small Cap Index the same day. Index funds tracking those benchmarks must sell the shares, and Strategy puts index-fund holdings at about 13 million shares. Whether that selling is absorbed without a fresh low will matter for the Bitcoin bottom.

The Fed held at 3.75%–4.00% on October 28 by a 10–2 vote, with both dissents favoring a hike. The minutes revealed near-unanimous support for more tightening, and a couple of participants floated a move bigger than 25 basis points.

In a recent development, CME FedWatch priced a 93.2% chance of a 25 bp hike on December 9 as of November 22, with 6.8% for no change and 0.0% for 50 bp. The 10-year yield slipped to 5.04% on November 20 from 5.04%, and the dollar stayed near a 19-month high.

Consumer sentiment rose to 51.5 in November from 48.0, with one-year inflation expectations near 4%, and Brent crude is about 21% below its October peak. November CPI arrives December 10, a day after the decision.

The 10-year inflation-protected yield (DFII10) rose about 30 bp in November to roughly 2.88%–2.92%, its highest since 2008. That is the risk-free return after inflation, and Bitcoin has to compete with it.

According to recent reports, so far November fits the first case: Bitcoin fell alongside gold as real yields rose and ETFs sold. No decoupling has appeared yet, and a lasting Bitcoin bottom would be easier to trust if it did.

In a recent development, the Fear & Greed Index fell to 9 on November 20, its lowest since February. Other trackers read 10 to 12 on November 21, and the 2026 range runs from about 5 in February to about 78 in late September. So, current positioning is mixed.

It has been revealed that moreover, Funding is slightly negative at −0.0034%, yet 77.6% of accounts are long, a long/short ratio of 3.47. CoinGlass shows $1.30 billion of BTC liquidations over 24 hours, with longs making up $1.02 billion (78.82%) and shorts $274.6 million (21.18%).

Three moving averages sit near $75,500 above price, and a death cross is close. Extreme fear is a poor timing tool. Sentiment lows and price lows do not always coincide, and fear can last while price keeps falling, so sentiment alone cannot confirm a Bitcoin bottom.

The reading is mixed. Valuation, sentiment and the profit/loss ratio say a Bitcoin bottom is probably close in time. Price, flows and macro have not confirmed it, and the on-chain bands leave room for a lower low. The next markers are the realized price near $53,253, the LTH realized price near $48,563, a reclaim of $58,400–$59,650 and then $62,000–$66,000, the MSCI date on November 30, and the Fed decision on December 8–9.

According to recent reports, former Valve writer Chet Faliszek – the one behind the likes of Half-Life 2, Portal and its sequel, Team Fortress 2, Left 4 Dead, and even Counter-Strike: Global Offensive – thinks GTA 6 is great… because you choose how to play. A fact that's lost on players complaining about optional missions.

In a recent development, the new Grand Theft Auto is smashing records left, right, and center as the highest-rated game of all time on Metacritic and, as per Sony, the "biggest launch in PlayStation history" to date. However, there's still a vocal (minority, I'd like to think) group of players dismissing it as a game that forces some sort of agenda upon you. Faliszek covers this in his YouTube video on the "moral yellow paint," so to speak, of GTA 6. (Related: This fresh chip could slash data center energy waste...)

According to the Valve veteran, there's no real reason for folks to complain about menial tasks like painting the house as Jason or buying flowers, even if "the game says you're getting some kind of points or… gives you this list.". (Related: This simple change stops robot swarms from getting stuc...)

While other games use yellow paint to give you set boundaries to stay within, in GTA 6 you can stray in whatever direction you'd like and play how you wish.

In a recent development, in Faliszek's case, that means, uh, "bringing back bodies" and filling the house with corpses. It's all just GTA, after all.

"Now, would I do that in normal life? Does that make me a horrible person? Maybe. Does that make me a psycho in the game? Who cares?" As he says, "This is Grand Theft Auto." So, go on and ditch the chores to commit mass murder (if you please, of course). The writer notes that, again, it's all part of the game's sandbox elements – and, on that point, part of what makes Grand Theft Auto 6 such a stand-out release.

In a separate short, Faliszek dubs GTA 6 "a masterpiece," saying, "it may be the greatest game ever made." He references other devs' criticism of Rockstar's upcoming title – such as Warren Spector's, which argues he'd "rather have hundreds of games and full employment" – and dismisses it, reiterating that "it's an amazing game" and "incredible." I'm sure the freedom (to collect bodies, apparently) is a major reason why.

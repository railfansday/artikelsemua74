Sources indicate that after more than 10 years of waiting, Rockstar's GTA 6 is finally available now (at least in New Zealand and Australia). The anticipation has been sky-high for both consumers and investors alike, and Rockstar's owner, Take-Two, is seeing a huge uplift in its share price today after the game's overwhelmingly positive reviews landed. Take-Two shares rose by nearly7% in pre-market trading today, and moved higher from there as the day went on. This meant that Take-Two's market cap grew by billions today alone. Stock analysts expected share value to increase in the lead-up to launch, and many expect it to go higher still due to the expected introduces of GTA Online and a PC edition of the game in 2027. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ftI6gjFsPuw&t=1s While Take-Two's stock price is popping today, Take-Two remains down close to 20% so far in 2026 and is down about 15% over the past 12 months. Why? No one really knows, because the stock market is fickle. Take-Two's stock price got hammered earlier this year when Google announced new AI software to make games, presumably spooking investors. The stock has not recovered since. The 52-week high for Take-Two is $265.65, and the 52-week low is $183.86. Zooming further out, though, Take-Two has been a fantastic investment for people who bought and held for a long time. As Rand Group points out, $10,000 invested into Take-Two when GTA 5 introduced in 2013 has grown to more than $120,000 today. https://twitter.com/randgroup/status/2123139175881019530 GameSpot's GTA 6 review scored the game a rare 10/10. "GTA 6 is an astonishing open-world action game that I’d wager won’t be outdone until Grand Theft Auto 7," reviewer Tamoor Hussain said. GTA 6 has now unseated the Ocarina of Time remake for Switch 2 to become the highest-rated game of 2026 on GameSpot sister site, Metacritic, and is tied with the original Ocarina of Time for the highest aggregate review score ever.

Gut microbes can adapt to our ageing bodies

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Our guts contain around 40 trillion bacterial cells, a similar quantity to the number of cells in the entire human body1. Although the community of bacterial species in the human gut usually stays relatively stable over time2, genetic mutations in such bacteria can be associated with a measure of body size called the body mass index3. Writing in Nature, Wang et al.4 scanned the gut bacteria of some 10,000 people for genetic variation and searched for statistically significant associations with almost 250 characteristics, including age, height and cardiovascular fitness. The authors singled out one adaptation that seems to help one of the most abundant bacterial species in the human gut to resist a form of stress called oxidative stress, which damages cells and increases as people age.

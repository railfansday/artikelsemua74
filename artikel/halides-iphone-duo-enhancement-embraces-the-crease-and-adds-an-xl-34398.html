Halide’s iPhone Duo Enhancement Embraces the Crease and Adds an XL Mode

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Halide has declared version 3.4, much of which focuses on optimizations for the iPhone Duo. This includes a newly introduced “XL” mode that is designed for two-handed operation, as well as a feature that doesn’t avoid the crease, but instead embraces it.

Industry observers note that version 3.4, which is available starting today, promises to maximize the viewfinder when the iPhone Duo is unfolded. Halide indicates this feature, called XL mode, allows for “powerful two-handed operation.”. (Related: Industry News: Hugging Face on PyTorch / XLA TPUs...)

“On the outer screen, we’ve added a few newly introduced modes, including a viewfinder preview and flash-fill. In low-light, the latter gives you a softer alternative to the built-in flash,” Halide adds.

“While apps like Apple’s own camera app expand the viewfinder to take up the full screen, it’s impossible to do this without obstructing your shot with camera controls. That poses a challenge when composing shots, so we’ve taken the approach of keeping the viewfinder clean.”.

In a recent development, beyond just making these changes that take advantage of the two form factors the Duo offers, Halide went one step further and specifically engineered an interface for when the Duo is half-folded. The manufacturer says that while most are avoiding the crease that appears when the phone is folded, it went the opposite way and embraced it.

According to recent reports, “Back when the iPhone X launched in 2017, we were the first app to ’embraced the notch’ by putting histograms in the ears of the phone. Today, we think we’re the first app to ’embrace the crease,’ placing the histograms in the fold, an otherwise no-man’s-land of interactions,” Halide notes.

Sources indicate that by default, Halide’s camera controls sit on the same side as the camera itself. Tapping the arrows in the sidebar, the left and right sides swap, turning Halide into a waist level viewfinder.

Halide usually doesn’t take long to update its app when new hardware lands, and even though the interface for this Duo-specific update feels like it should have taken a lot longer, it really isn’t too far off from what Halide provided for Apple’s iPhone 18 Pro launch. When it dropped version 3.2, which gave support for variable aperture control, that arrived on October 2, which is about three weeks after the iPhone 18 Pro landed on store shelves. This update for the Duo comes just shy of a month after the Duo launched on October 23.

Halide version 3.4 is available starting today.

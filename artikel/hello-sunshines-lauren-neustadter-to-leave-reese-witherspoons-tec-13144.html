Lauren Neustadter, president of film and television at Reese Witherspoon‘s Hello Sunshine, is set to step down from the organization at the end of the year, the collaborators announced on Friday. Her exit comes after Hello Sunshine let go of about 50% of its staff last month after Witherspoon and Mediawan partnered to buy the company back from Candle Media.

“Lauren’s taste, passion and leadership have been integral to Hello Sunshine’s film and television success,” Witherspoon mentioned in a statement. “I’m so proud of what we accomplished together and grateful for her partnership and the lasting foundation she built around telling powerful, complex female-driven stories that resonate.” Related Stories ‘Chicago Fire,’ ‘Med’ and ‘P.D.’ Set Fresh Crossover Episodes on NBC in January. It has been revealed that neustadter added in a statement, “Building Hello Sunshine alongside Reese and our incredible team has been the privilege of a lifetime. I’m deeply proud of all the indicates and movies we’ve made, the doors we’ve opened for fresh voices, and the ways we have put women in the spotlight both in front of and behind the camera. I am grateful for every moment of it, and I will always be cheering for Hello Sunshine.”.

Neustadter joined the company in 2017, and has served as executive producer on series including “The Morning Show,” “Major Little Lies,” “Little Fires Everywhere” and “The Last Thing He Told Me.” She oversaw adaptations of books from Reese’s Book Club including “Tiny Beautiful Things” and “Daisy Jones & the Six.” She also worked on feature films including “Where the Crawdads Sing” and TriStar’s upcoming “The Nightingale.”. Neustadter started her career as a film executive at Miramax and Twentieth Century Fox, then became an executive at FOX network, where she oversaw “Brooklyn Nine-Nine,” “The Mindy Project” and “Lethal Weapon.”.

Hello Sunshine plans to focus on premium film and television and Reese’s Book Club, as well as expanding into latest storytelling formats and distribution models.

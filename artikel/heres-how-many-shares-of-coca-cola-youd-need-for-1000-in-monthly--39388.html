With 64 years of consecutive dividend increases, Coca-Cola (KO +0.94%) is one of the Dividend Kings, if not one of the quintessential blue chip dividend stocks. This dividend growth and dependability are likely major factors behind the stock's continued presence in Berkshire Hathaway's portfolio.

Sources indicate that berkshire's 400 million-share position, worth around $39.6 billion, generates nearly $850 million in annual dividend income. Investors operating on a smaller scale could make it a core holding to build their own steady income stream. (Related: DNA robots could deliver drugs and hunt viruses inside ...)

It has been revealed that coca-Cola currently pays a $0.53-per-share cash dividend, equating to $2.12 in annual dividends per share. To generate $1,000 per month, or $12,000 per year, divide $12,000 by $2.12. This gives you around 5,661 shares.

With Coca-Cola recently trading at around $99 per share, you would need to invest $560,439 to generate $12,001.32 in annual dividend income. This works out to a dividend yield of around 2.15%. At a time when the federal funds rate stands at nearly 3.9%, this may not sound significant, but consider the other benefits of buying and holding Coca-Cola over the long term.

Industry observers note that over the past decade, dividend increases have averaged 4.45% annually. If this trend continues and Coca-Cola keeps raising its dividend by 4% to 5% per year, a decade from now, this same position could be generating between $1,480 and $1,628 per month in dividend income. According to recent reports, although Coca-Cola has trailed the S&P 500 (^GSPC -3.70%) in total returns over the last decade, the stock has outperformed the market index in more recent years, particularly over the past year. That's not to say that this trend will continue. After this recent hot streak, for instance, Coca-Cola shares have reached a relatively high valuation of 28.5 times forward earnings. While it may be a stronger buy on a pullback, its dividend durability and growth still make it a solid core position in a long-term income-focused portfolio.

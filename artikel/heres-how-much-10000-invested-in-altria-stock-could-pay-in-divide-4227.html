Recent findings suggest that altria (MO +0.72%) is one of the market's marquee dividend stocks. In fact, many investors would tell you that Altria's dividend is the only reason to invest, even though the stock price surged more than 33% this year (as of Nov. 18). If Altria continues its impressive run, it'll be its best-performing year since its 2008 Philip Morris International (PM +0.59%) spinoff.

Altria's stock price run won't be the norm, but its dividend is as consistent as it gets, with the company being a Dividend King (a company with at least 50 consecutive years of dividend increases). It routinely offers one of the higher yields among S&P 500 (^GSPC +0.06%) companies and could be a good income play for patient investors. Recent findings suggest that altria is uniquely positioned as the country's largest tobacco producer, leading an industry at a crossroads. On one end, you have declining smoking rates in U.S. adults. According to the CDC, 42.4% of U.S. adults smoked in 1965. Now, that has dropped to 9.9%.

When you're the country's largest cigarette producer, that decline has a noticeable impact on business.

Industry observers note that on the other end, tobacco people are very loyal and like what they like. Someone who smokes Marlboro Red or Black & Mild cigars (two of Altria's most popular brands) will typically buy them, even if prices go up. Of course, consumers have financial limits, but for the most part, this dynamic has given Altria the pricing power to offset some of its volume decline.

Altria hasn't done a great job establishing a smoke-free alternative to compete with popular brands like Philip Morris International's Zyn, but it isn't time to ring the alarm yet. Altria has enough time and more than enough resources to make something happen.

If you're looking for a good income investment, Altria is a buy. It knows its appeal is its dividend, so it's as shareholder-friendly as they come. I don't expect that to change in the foreseeable future.

There are plenty of storied and famous grounds in English football, even with the increasing modernisation of the game and the move towards ‘latest builds’.

Some grounds and stadiums stand the test of time through their reputation and weight of history, as well as the atmospheres that they can regularly and consistently produce.

In a country where its football pyramid remains unparalleled and the envy of the world, it is natural that the depth of quality in its infrastructure also sets a high bar.

Nostalgia permeates everything in football and there is very good reason for that – and it is no surprise, either, that it played a part in Ian Wright choosing his two favourite stadiums in English football. (Related: Latest Update: Fine-Tune Wav2Vec2 for English ASR in Hu...)

Former England international Ian Wright started out at Greenwich Borough before turning professional at Crystal Palace and going on to become a legendary figure at Arsenal over a seven-year stint at Highbury in the 1990s.

After leaving Arsenal in 1998, he joined fellow London outfit West Ham United before stints at Nottingham Forest, Celtic and Burnley – retiring at the age of 36 in 2000.

Since then, Wright has become an iconic figure outside of the pitch and in broadcasting, with one of his most prominent roles nowadays as a part of the panel for The Overlap alongside Jamie Carragher, Gary Neville, Jill Scott and Roy Keane.

That storied career of Wright allowed him to experience the great, the good and the ugly of English football and so his opinion is extremely well-respected – and that will be quite nice for Sheffield Wednesday supporters, with Wright naming Hillsborough as one of his favourite grounds in England.

Alongside Aston Villa’s home of Villa Park in Birmingham, Wright chose The Owls’ stomping ground as one of his two favourite grounds in England, discussing his ‘love’ for both as a result of a nostalgic wistfulness about how they were prominent grounds for the FA Cup’s latter stages.

According to recent reports, wright, when asked about his favourite grounds on The Overlap, mentioned: “For me, it’s Villa Park and Sheffield Wednesday. Yes, I like Sheffield Wednesday.

“The thing with Villa Park and Sheffield Wednesday, they used to be the semi-final venues, so I love those places when they’re full.”.

Built between 1895 and 1897, Aston Villa has been the home of The Villans throughout its history, and it will remain their home for the foreseeable future.

Unlike second city rivals Birmingham City, who are moving to a new ground with their Sports Quarter project away from St Andrew’s, Villa have instead opted to regenerate the ground to move in line with UEFA standards for major international tournaments.

That is because Villa Park will be one of the stadiums used for UEFA EURO 2028, held across England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.

Having had a capacity of 43,205 since seating came in, it is right now down to around 37,000 due to the redevelopment but will head above 50,000 when the North Stand regeneration is complete.

UEFA EURO 2028 hosting duties come after previously also hosting matches at FIFA World Cup 1966 and UEFA EURO 1996, as well as the 1999 European Cup Winners’ Cup final between Real Mallorca and Lazio. The ground will host three group stage matches and a round of 16 tie at the tournament. (Related: Hosting your Models and Datasets on Hugging Face Spaces...)

Unlike Aston Villa, who spent only three seasons outside of the Premier League before bouncing back in the last-2010’s, Sheffield Wednesday have not been able to return to the top-flight since their relegation in the 1998-99 campaign.

That has meant that the last 28 years has allowed Hillsborough to become an EFL fortress and a staple of the Championship and League One as The Owls yo-yo between the divisions.

Like Villa Park, Hillsborough also hosted matches at UEFA EURO 1996, with Denmark playing all three of their games there against Portugal, Turkiye and Croatia.

The ground was also used at FIFA World Cup 1966, including the quarter-final between West Germany and Uruguay, and was selected by the FA as a proposed venue in their bid to host FIFA World Cup 2018 – but was overlooked ahead of UEFA EURO 2028.

Historic with international football and their hosting of FA Cup semi-finals, as well as even a League Cup final replay back in 1997 between Leicester City and Middlesbrough, Hillsborough is one of England’s most famous grounds, and it does get rocking when the Wednesday supporters are in full flow.

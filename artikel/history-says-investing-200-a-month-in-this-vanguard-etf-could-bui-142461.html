During times like these, it's easy for investors to worry that they're picking the wrong stocks. Or worry that the stock market is a bad place to put their money -- what if the S&P 500 goes down by another 10% next month?

If you want to invest in the stock market, times like these are actually some of the best moments to put money to work, because many stocks are trading at lower prices than just a few weeks ago.

Recent findings suggest that and you don't have to pick stocks to make great money in the stock market. Just buying a low-cost index fund like the Vanguard S&P 500 ETF (VOO -0.28%) can deliver life-changing returns in the long run.

Let's look at just how much you could make from steady $200 monthly investments in VOO.

In a recent development, the Vanguard S&P 500 ETF (VOO) is one of the most popular ways to invest in the stock market because it charges ultra-low fees (0.03% expense ratio) and it holds 500 of the largest, most crucial publicly traded stocks in America. When you buy VOO, you're getting the entire S&P 500 index: 500 of the most profitable large-cap stocks. When America's largest publicly traded companies do well, so will you. (Related: Large Language Models: A Latest Moore's Law?...)

Industry observers note that this index changes day by day as some stocks go up and others go down, weighted by market cap. For example, if one manufacturer has a bad month, the S&P 500 index will hold a slightly lower amount of its stock than it did a month ago. If another company performs better, its stock will account for a larger percentage of the S&P 500 by weight.

In a recent development, this means that the S&P 500 index is constantly sorting the "winners" and "losers" for you. This doesn't mean that the S&P 500 index always goes up or is guaranteed to deliver positive returns. But because S&P 500 ETFs like VOO hold 500 stocks, they are broadly diversified. That tends to make their performance less volatile over the long run than that of individual stocks or sector-specific ETFs.

In the past five years, the Vanguard S&P 500 ETF has delivered annualized returns of about 12%.

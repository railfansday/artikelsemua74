Stocks could continue to face some bearish pressures in the near term, but investors shouldn't overreact to the possibility of political headwinds. In fact, history notes that the stock market typically enjoys strong performance in the year following midterm elections.

According to recent reports, due in part to uncertainty connected to the implications of election results, the stock market has tended to see more volatility and produce weaker returns in years that play host to midterm elections. Conversely, the year of trading following midterm elections has tended to produce strong returns.

Recent findings suggest that since 1950, the S&P 500 has delivered an average return of 15% in the year of trading following midterms. Meanwhile, the Nasdaq Composite has posted an average annual return of nearly 26% in post-midterm years since its founding in 1971. That kind of historical backdrop could suggest that now is a worthwhile time to put some investment dollars into the Invesco QQQ Trust (QQQ -0.60%) -- an exchange traded fund (ETF) that tracks the Nasdaq-100 index and includes the 100 largest non-financials companies on the Nasdaq exchange.

NASDAQ · As of 9:56 AM ET 11/20/26.

According to recent reports, on the other hand, it's key to keep in mind that past performance is no guarantee of future results. There are some unique dynamics on the table right now that could cause a divergence from typical post-midterm stock market trends next year.

For starters, inflation has continued to run relatively hot -- and there's a fair chance that the Federal Reserve's Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) will vote for an interest rate increase this year and at least one additional rate hike next year. Higher interest rates help to combat inflation, but they also create a less favorable backdrop for the stock market -- and growth stocks in particular. Adding additional macroeconomic pressures, bond yields have remained stubbornly high.

In a recent development, massive valuation gains for leading AI companies including Nvidia, Alphabet, and Microsoft in recent years also mean that indexes including the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq Composite have become increasingly concentrated in recent years. This means that an investment in the Invesco QQQ Trust would likely face strong valuation pressures if sentiment surrounding top AI names shifts in a more bearish direction.

In summary, the historical trend suggests that the Nasdaq Composite, the Nasdaq 100, and the Invesco QQQ Trust should see strong returns in the year to come -- but investors also shouldn't treat that outcome as a given.

How Asian countries are looking to prop up markets as AI downturn sinks stocks

South Korea, China and Taiwan have introduced fresh measures aimed at supporting their respective markets, as a tech-fueled sell-off in the region spills into another week.

On Sunday, South Korea's finance ministry remarked it would prepare its securities-market stabilization fund for immediate activation. The fund, reportedly established in 2020 during the Covid-19 pandemic by financial firms and other institutions seeks to support markets during sharp downturns and is valued at 10.76 trillion won (nearly $8 billion).

Industry observers note that the government also aims to relax listed companies' daily share-buyback order limits for three months, starting Nov. 24 and start a "special intensive monitoring period" to prevent illegal short-selling. Regulators will also track margin lending and forced liquidations. (Related: The Reformer - Pushing the limits of language modeling...)

South Korean stocks have seen wild swings over 2026 as investors piled into, then fled from its two tech heavyweights, Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix, both critical companies in the AI supply chain. The benchmark Kospi index is deep in bear territory, down more than 46% from its June high.

Industry observers note that the ministry remarked that it "assessed that volatility in domestic and international stock markets has significantly increased recently due to internal and external uncertainties surrounding the global AI and semiconductor industries, as well as U.S. interest rate hikes," according to a Google translation of the release in Korean. However, Seoul also said that the mid-to-long-term growth expectations for the AI industry remain intact, and that "excessive anxiety is not a concern.".

Taiwan, which is also exposed to the AI industry through Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company and other tech firms, has also moved to rein in market volatility.

Taiwan's Financial Supervisory Commission unveiled Friday that short selling below the closing price was "completely prohibited," while making it an exception if used for hedging purposes by securities and futures firms.

"Investors must not sell listed (OTC) stocks or Taiwan Depositary Receipts at a price lower than the closing price of the previous trading day," the FSC stated, according to a Google translation of the statement in Chinese.

Margin requirements for short selling will also be increased, and applications from securities firms to matter warrants for listing will be suspended, as the regulator aims to curb speculative activity.

In a similar move to Seoul, Taipei encouraged firms to buy back stocks, adding that the purchases will be included in the companies' environmental, social and governance scores if completed between the fourth quarter of 2026 and the first quarter of 2027.

It has been revealed that the island's stock market is heavily dominated by its tech industry, most notably, chip manufacturing giant TSMC. The stock fell 15% last week, while the index dropped 17.5%.

While South Korea and Taiwan are looking to curb speculative trading to arrest the slide in their markets, Beijing is looking to encourage more leverage-based buying.

The Shanghai Stock Exchange stated on Sunday that exchanges in Shanghai, Shenzhen and Beijing will cut the minimum margin ratio for margin-financed stock purchases to 80% from 100%, starting Nov. 30. That means investors will be able to borrow more for their stock market bets.

The Shanghai and Shenzhen exchanges also announced fee cuts, while Shenzhen proposed a market-making mechanism for its ChiNext board. The steps will help inject more liquidity into the market that has also born the brunt of the souring in tech and AI trade, with the benchmark CSI 300 index closing lower for 14 of the past 15 weeks.

Industry observers note that seoul's markets led losses in the region, with the Kospi down over 3.26%. Smaller losses were seen in mainland China's CSI 300, which declined 0.71%. The Taiwan Weighted Index, however, reversed earlier losses to gain over 2% on Monday.

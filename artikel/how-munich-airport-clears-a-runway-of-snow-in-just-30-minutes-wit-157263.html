How Munich Airport Clears A Runway Of Snow In Just 30 Minutes With 175 Vehicles

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

With 170 vehicles and 540 personnel available for winter operations, Munich Airport (MUC) is equipped to clear each runway of snow within 30 minutes. These crews remain on standby around the clock from early November to mid-April, covering the months with the highest risk of heavy snow.

Sources indicate that MUC has reached this level of readiness in collaboration with local agricultural and trucking companies. Up to 180 people can be deployed during the night shift, with the aim of keeping the runway clear of snow, ready for operations to begin in the morning. Industry observers note that MUC is so eager to show off its winter operations because of how difficult it is to keep a major international airport clear and running during the winter months. The airport includes two runways, with additional aprons and taxiways. This amounts to approximately 60.3 million square feet (5.6 million square meters), or 780 football fields. In a recent development, despite the astonishing amount of real estate MUC must keep clear to avoid delays and cancellations, the airport believes it is up to the task because it can clear each runway in just 30 minutes. It does this by deploying a fleet of 21 snow blowers, five loading snow blowers, and one snow groomer. Farmers in the local area can also be called in with snowplow-equipped tractors.

In a recent development, MUC adds brains to the grunt task of clearing so much tarmac. Air traffic control manages the deployment and coordination of snow-clearing teams. They make use of the latest weather information and an early-warning system that intelligently predicts when icy conditions could cause a problem.

Clear runways are pointless if the jets at an airport are too iced up to take off safely. MUC also has this accounted for with winter maintenance teams who use 'Polar Bear' de-icing vehicles, which are operated in partnership with Lufthansa. The equipment deploys de-icing fluid to ensure that a jet's wings and fuselage aren't covered in ice and snow, which could prove disastrous.

Industry observers note that to get into the right position to deploy the fluid, the tallest 'Polar Bear' is 50 feet and 10 inches (15.5 m) tall. The maintenance team at MUC has to carefully drive these huge vehicles into the optimum position. Nadine Adden, the only woman working with the polar bears, spoke to Munich Airport's news team. She stated that:.

Recent findings suggest that "Only when the engines and the aircraft are completely free of ice and moisture can they take off safely. (...) It's like using an oversized hairdryer. (...) I have a really cool job.".

MUC hasn't always been a leading airport when it comes to dealing with extreme winter conditions. The airport closed twice in quick succession during the winter of 2023 after multiple days of heavy snow. On December 5, there were no takeoffs or landings from 6:00 AM to 12:00 PM. The remainder of the day saw many more flights canceled or severely delayed. Lufthansa asked its passengers to stay at home in response.

In a recent development, the weekend before, freezing conditions had struck Europe and affected more flight operations at Munich Airport. Flight operations did not resume until 6:00 AM on Sunday, with some restrictions remaining in place for long after. Other airports impacted by this cold spell included Glasgow Airport (GLA) and Edinburgh Airport (EDI), while heavy snow in Oslo in April 2023 caused around 60 flight cancellations.

Closures like these show why Munich Airport has invested so heavily in protecting itself from the worst winter conditions. Anything less carries a serious risk of extensive delays and cancellations, scuppering travelers' plans over the festive season and causing enormous economic damage.

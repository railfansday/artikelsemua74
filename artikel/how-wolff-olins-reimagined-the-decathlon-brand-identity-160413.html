Wolff Olins rebuilds the Decathlon brand identity around wonder, L'Orbit and Decathlon Sans, uniting 85 brands into one global sports brand.

According to recent reports, the work starts from a blunt observation. More than 3 billion people play sport, but the stories around it keep pointing at podiums and perfection. Decathlon, the third biggest sports company in the world with over 2,000 stores, sells to everyone from first timers to pros, and its old identity never noted that out loud. Wolff Olins builds the whole system on wonder instead, the enjoyment, connection and adventure that keep people playing, and one refreshed blue does the talking. It runs from the storefront to the jersey chest to the shoe heel, with the white L'Orbit mark sitting on it, and the system adapts across 70 markets and 85 sports. According to recent reports, decathlon Sans carries the voice. Grilli Type drew the bespoke grotesque with alternate characters that tilt, stretch and blur, so headlines read as movement before they read as words. MOVE fills a whole frame in leaning white capitals, and Ready to play? scatters across the blue with the question mark drifting off on its own. The wordmark itself is refined rather than redrawn, tightened up and set beside the new symbol. Down at product level the same type turns precise: a Quechua jacket panel pairs the Orbit with Men's Ultralight Hiking Jacket in plain black and white, and spec lines like Ergonomic Cut, for freedom of movement sit next to their icons in a strict two column grid. (Related: F1 star Max Verstappen played 40 hours of GTA 6 before ...)

In a recent development, the system holds together because every surface plays by the same rules. The Orbit repeats at every scale, set small on a blue training top, printed on a white shoe heel, lit on a blue cube sign on a city street, and running the full width of a store facade. Product cards keep the same discipline online, camp cooking, a ski holiday pack and a trail kit each inside a clean white card with a price and one line of copy, and the website carries the same product lists over full bleed mountain photography. Sustainability gets its own corner of the language too, with green Shop Rental, Second Life and Bring Back marks set against the blue, so repair and reuse read as part of the brand rather than an afterthought.

According to recent reports, according to the title of a paper Huawei will present in San Francisco in February, its HBM has double the data pin density of chips its Korean rivals mass-produce. In theory, that would let it move far more data to AI processors.

It is unclear how far Huawei has got. Companies often present designs at conferences to share ideas rather than show finished products. Huawei did not immediately respond to Bloomberg.

Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron are barred from selling HBM to Chinese customers, and China’s stocks of foreign HBM are running low. Huawei has reportedly told some clients its next Ascend AI chip will rely mostly on Korean HBM brought in by smugglers.

Making its own would ease one of the biggest gaps in China’s push for chip self-sufficiency. Chinese memory maker CXMT is also starting HBM production, despite low yields.

Huawei has already shown other workarounds for US sanctions. In June, it unveiled a chip design approach it notes could match 1.4nm-class chips by 2031 without the most advanced machines.

The report hit shares of Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron. Micron’s quarterly revenue roughly quadrupled earlier this year on demand for AI memory.

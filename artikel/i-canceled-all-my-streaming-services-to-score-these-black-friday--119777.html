According to recent reports, every November, I commit an act of subscription arson. Netflix? Cancelled. HBO Max? Gone. Disney+, Hulu, Apple TV? Cleared out like a fridge before vacation. Then, just as the Black Friday streaming deals start rolling in, I sign right back up, at a fraction of the price. It's the closest thing to a financial magic trick that doesn't involve a crypto wallet, and it works every single year.

It has been revealed that the problem it solves is real. A Tom's Guide survey about streaming habits found that readers are spending more than $60 a month on seven or more streaming services. That's a lot of money to spend on a pile of apps you open just to scroll for 20 minutes before rewatching "The Office." Prices keep creeping up, and your watchlist isn't getting any shorter.

Sources indicate that I prefer to play musical subscriptions instead: Cancel the ones I'm not using, hop back in when a sale comes along. One subscription gets you two major streaming libraries, from Hulu hits like “Only Murders in the Building” to Disney+ movies, series and family favorites. Recent findings suggest that HBO Max sits at No. 1 in our best streaming services rankings, and for good reason. Its lineup is stacked with acclaimed shows like "The Pitt" and "A Knight of the Seven Kingdoms" and all-time great dramas including "The Sopranos" and "Veep," plus a deep bench of movies from Warner Bros. It's a one-stop shop for seriously good TV and film.

Recent findings suggest that apple TV is getting a serious Black Friday price cut, dropping to just $6.99 per month instead of its regular $14.99. It's a great, cheaper way to catch buzzy series like "Slow Horses," "The Studio" and "Silo.".

Peacock is finally getting in on the Black Friday action again after sitting out last year's sale. The entry-level Select plan will set you back just $2.99, but my money's on Premium for $8.99. That's where you'll find the good stuff: Peacock's full lineup of hit demonstrates, blockbuster movies, original series and live sports. Sure, you can find steeper discounts elsewhere, but this is a worthwhile chance to get more bang for your streaming buck.

In a recent development, paramount+ is discounting both subscription tiers for Black Friday, but the Premium blueprint is the one worth splurging on. It includes everything in Essential, with the added bonus of Showtime's lineup of movies and series, a live feed of your local CBS station and mostly ad-free streaming. That's a lot of extra bang for your subscription buck.

Industry observers note that if you're a "Walking Dead" completist, AMC+ is practically essential. The service has the original zombie saga alongside spinoffs like "Daryl Dixon." This Prime Video channel deal makes it a perfect excuse to revisit the franchise or finally catch up on all the undead drama.

It has been revealed that britBox is a streaming paradise packed with British television from the BBC and ITV. Its library spans generations of acclaimed mysteries, juicy dramas, offbeat comedies and fascinating documentaries. This is the perfect deal if you want to catch the upcoming "The Other Bennet Sister" three-part Christmas special.

Industry observers note that you likely have a spare lying around already, probably the one you made in 2011 and now use exclusively for receipts. If not, any of the many email services with a free tier will do the trick, whether that's Gmail, Yahoo, Outlook or AOL.

Here's my annual routine. Last year, Past Me, a surprisingly responsible person, set calendar reminders to cancel everything I signed up for during Black Friday week. Then I take my fresh email address and go deal hunting. The ones I think are worth your money every year are Peacock, HBO Max and Paramount+.

And whatever you do, set a reminder to cancel next year. Future You will thank you.

In a recent development, in a word: no. Netflix doesn't do Black Friday. I check every year, and every year I'm met with the same cold, red-logoed silence.

It has been revealed that netflix has rarely discounted its plans, and Black Friday and Cyber Monday are no exception. So the usual monthly prices stand: $8.99 for Standard with ads, $19.99 for Standard ad-free, and $26.99 for Premium with 4K Ultra HD.

Sources indicate that the upside of having no deal to chase is that you're not locked into a discounted annual plan, which makes Netflix the easiest service on the list to churn. Netflix knows this, of course, and has gotten sneaky about it. Its biggest shows now tend to arrive in installments, a neat little trick for making sure you stick around for the next drop.

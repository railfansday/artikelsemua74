By Kailey Hagen, CFP® – Nov 22, 2026 at 10:00AM EST.

According to recent reports, where does your monthly benefit check sit compared to the rest of the country? As of October 2026, according to the Social Security Administration (SSA), the average monthly benefit for retired workers is $2,092.17. That amounts to about $25,106.04 per year. This single figure covers everyone from those who just claimed at 62 to retirees well into their 90s.

Recent findings suggest that averages depend heavily on who you are and when you claimed. For instance, the average for people aged 62 is $1,424.40 per month, while those aged 70 receive an average of $2,274.68. Gender also creates a significant gap in the final numbers. It has been revealed that averages also vary between men and women across the entire retired-worker population. As of December 2025, according to the SSA, these were the monthly averages for men and women across all age groups. They reflect the combined impact of different career earnings and claiming choices. Sources indicate that landing above the $2,092.17 benchmark isn't a matter of luck. The SSA calculates your benefit based on an average of your monthly wages across your 35 highest-earning years. It adjusts earlier wages for inflation and runs them through the primary insurance amount (PIA) formula. Higher lifetime earnings generally result in a larger monthly check.

Claiming age is the other primary lever for beating the average. Full retirement age (FRA) ranges from 66 to 67, depending on your birth year, and it's 67 for everyone born in 1960 or later. Claiming at age 62 can reduce your check by up to 30%, while waiting until 70 earns delayed retirement credits worth 8% per year.

It has been revealed that while beating the average is helpful, it's rarely enough to cover a full retirement. Social Security is designed to replace only about 40% of your pre-retirement income. However, the average senior household headed by someone 65 or older spends $61,432 per year, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS). That's much higher than the $25,106.04 annual average benefit.

A 3.6% cost-of-living adjustment (COLA) will take effect in 2027 to help checks keep pace with inflation. That adjustment adds about $75 per month to the $2,092.17 average. Even with these raises, most retirees rely on savings or other income sources to bridge the gap between their benefits and their total living costs. Beating the Social Security average is not the goal; covering your actual retirement expenses is.

Recent findings suggest that if you want to increase your odds of claiming an above-average benefit, there are three things you must do. First, work at least 35 years before you apply for Social Security. This will ensure you have no zero-income years included in your benefit calculation, which would weigh down your checks.

In a recent development, second, do what you can to increase your income today. The larger your average income throughout your career, the larger your future Social Security checks will be.

Sources indicate that third, choose your claiming age carefully. Claiming Social Security early could make sense if you have a short life expectancy or don't have much in personal savings. Otherwise, you may get a larger lifetime benefit by delaying Social Security until your FRA or possibly until you qualify for your largest checks at age 70.

A TV series doesn't win 20 Emmys for being mediocre, and the groundbreaking NYPD Blue is anything but. Created by Steven Bochco and David Milch, NYPD Blue revolutionized the cop procedural. Gritty, realistic, messy, and colored in shades of gray, it drew outside the lines and challenged the cut-and-dry politics of earlier cop dramas. The main characters were flawed and complex. They didn't always make the right choices, and their ethics and personas were multidimensional.

Recent findings suggest that NYPD Blue has a fantastic cast of actors including Dennis Franz, Kim Delaney, Jimmy Smits, and Esai Morales. No matter if their tenure lasted a few seasons or the entire series, each actor brought their respective role to life in brilliant form, and audiences were hooked. It might be easy to take NYPD Blue for granted now, but it was unlike anything seen on TV when it debuted, and its impact can be felt in every police drama that has followed.

Sources indicate that when the phrase "TV detective" is brought up, one character chiefly comes to mind. The disheveled hair, the gravelly voice, the long trench coat evoking memories of 1940s gumshoes. We are talking, of course, about Lt. Frank Columbo. Brought to life by the indelible Peter Falk, Columbo is the hallmark of television sleuths.

Recent findings suggest that the classic TV show was unique in that instead of primarily focusing on the "who," Columbo was equally interested in the "how." Although Columbo is smart and works out theories in his mind, it is his unassuming charm and patient demeanor that disarms suspects. As Sherlock Holmes is a savant of clues and facts, Columbo is a master of human behavior. He understands people inside and out and uses this to his advantage, anticipating how they would think in a given situation and what they are likely to do.

Sources indicate that like a Komodo dragon waiting days for its prey to die, Columbo never rushes at anything. His quiet tenacity plays the long game. He can't be unnerved, and that scares the daylights out of criminals. Watching fiends who think they are more clever than anyone else and are used to manipulating people get outwitted by a cigar-smoking, quiet-voiced, middle-aged man with messy hair is endlessly satisfying. Columbo remains the best TV detective show, and it is no mystery as to why.

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, U.S. Treasury yields fell on Wednesday, with investors fleeing to bonds for protection after October's personal consumption expenditures price index revealed that inflation continued to accelerate.

The 10-year Treasury note yield — the main benchmark for mortgages, auto loans and credit card debt — was more than 3 basis points lower at 4.866%.

The yield on the 2-year Treasury note, which typically moves in line with short-term Federal Reserve interest rate decisions, was down more than 2 basis points at 4.65%.

The longer-dated 30-year Treasury yield, which tends to track geopolitical events, was more than 3 basis points lower at 5.254%.

One basis point equals 0.01%, or 1/100th of 1%, and yields and prices move inversely to one another.

The personal consumption expenditures price index — the Federal Reserve's preferred gauge of underlying inflation — rose 3.8% year over year for October, in line with the Dow Jones consensus estimate. Separately, economic growth came in much stronger for the third quarter. Gross domestic product rose at a 4.3% annualized rate, according to a revised Commerce Department estimate.

The data reinforced expectations for another Fed rate hike in December.

Industry observers note that "[I]t is more urgent than ever that Fed officials step up the pace of interest rate hikes to cool off economic demand," remarked Chris Rupkey, chief economist at FWDBONDS.

Few players get the John Elway or Peyton Manning sendoff, winning a Super Bowl then announcing their retirement a few weeks or months later.

In a recent development, still, it seemed like Aaron Rodgers would have a gentler ending to his great career than a short statement at 11:10 p.m. on a Tuesday.

The Pittsburgh Steelers made a stunning move, announcing that not only would Rodgers be replaced as the team's starting quarterback for Friday's game against the Denver Broncos, he wouldn't even be active for the game.

There were four MVPs, 10 Pro Bowls, a Super Bowl championship, dozens of highlights that defied belief over 22 seasons, and it all seems to end with a quick statement from the team after most of Pittsburgh had already gone to sleep.

In a recent development, it's not like Rodgers has played great this season, which he noted will be his last in the NFL. Steelers head coach Mike McCarthy was asked this week if Rodgers was still the team's starter, so it wasn't completely out of the blue that he got benched.

Sources indicate that it was abrupt, and with some unusual timing. There was no time to celebrate the end of a legendary career or even realize that the snaps he took Sunday might be his last.

It's just done, barring some other turn of events over the final few weeks of the season.

Recent findings suggest that rodgers played OK this year. The Steelers couldn't have expected much more from a quarterback who will turn 43 on Dec. 2. No other quarterback, other than Tom Brady, had any reasonable level of success at Rodgers' age.

Rodgers threw for 2,241 yards and 16 touchdowns with six interceptions in 10 games. The Steelers won six of those games and if the season ended now, they'd be in the playoffs. Rodgers wasn't great, often throwing short as he did last season, which was reflected in him having the fourth-worst EPA (expected points added) among all qualified quarterbacks. It still didn't seem like he'd be benched.

It wasn't like the Steelers were giving the job to a hotshot rookie who will excite the fan base. Rudolph has been decent as a backup throughout his career, and he had a stretch in 2023 when the Steelers won all three of his starts that helped them to the playoffs. But the Steelers didn't feel any urgency to see what Rudolph can do. They just apparently felt Rodgers had already hit the age cliff and they saw it over the last two games.

It has been revealed that after a solid start to the season there were two miserable performances from Rodgers and the team. He threw incomplete on his first seven passes in a blowout loss to the Cincinnati Bengals in Week 10. That was the first time in his career he had started 0-for-7. Pittsburgh's loss on Sunday in Philadelphia was ugly, with the Steelers not scoring a touchdown until the final two minutes. Undrafted rookie Max Hurleman, making his NFL debut, might have become a trivia answer: He caught the Steelers' only touchdown against the Eagles, and it might end up being the final touchdown pass of Rodgers' career. As it stands, that could be the final play of Rodgers' career. Pittsburgh never got the ball back after that.

The Steelers were slumping. But the way the week unfolded makes the benching quite odd.

Sources indicate that the Steelers have a short week. When asked Monday about Rodgers and possibly benching him, McCarthy gave a terse answer: "Aaron Rodgers is our quarterback." Then less than 48 hours later, with a game less than three days away, the Steelers made a change at quarterback.

It has been revealed that the benching likely happened before 11 p.m. on a Tuesday but that's when the NFL world found out, making it one of the odder quarterback announcements in recent memory. And the decision to demote Rodgers all the way to at least No. 3 on the depth chart (the Steelers are the rare team that carries four quarterbacks) was made by a coach with whom Rodgers won a Super Bowl with at the end of the 2010 season. There wasn't anything pleasant about the move or how it was handled, and an NFL legend like Rodgers probably deserved better. But the league isn't much on sentiment, especially for teams on two-game losing streaks in a playoff race.

Maybe there will be more details to the benching, other than a coach who definitively stated Monday that Rodgers was his quarterback completely changing his mind on a Tuesday (a day when the Steelers had a walkthrough practice), three days before his team plays the 8-2 Broncos. Recent findings suggest that rodgers has spent most of his career, particularly the final few years of it, living in defiance. His confidence never wavered, whether talking about politics, criticizing the media or showing up teammates and even his coach on the field when he felt he wasn't at fault for a bad play. It never seemed he'd leave the game unless it was on his terms. Perhaps he gets a start later in the season if Rudolph doesn't play well or there are injuries or simply to give an all-time great a better sendoff than a social media blast late in the evening.

Sources indicate that but there's a good chance we've suddenly seen the last of Rodgers on a football field, especially with the team making sure the world knew he wouldn't even be active Friday. The NFL can be a cold business, even for someone who was one of the faces of the league for a couple decades.

Leeds United have triggered a club option to extend Lukas Nmecha's contract at Elland Road.

Nmecha, 27, joined the Whites on a free transfer in June 2025, following his departure from Wolfsburg. He penned a two-year deal in West Yorkshire and scored on his debut in a 1-0 win against Everton.

Industry observers note that but the German had to wait three months to find the net again, when he struck in a 3-1 defeat at Nottingham Forest. That started a mini purple patch for Nmecha, who netted in three successive matches and became Daniel Farke's first-choice striker, replacing Dominic Calvert-Lewin. According to recent reports, however, that is as good as it has got for Nmecha in a Leeds shirt. After suffering a hamstring injury, he lost his place in the starting line-up back to Calvert-Lewin and the ex-Everton man hasn't looked back since, cementing himself as Farke's main man up front.

GIVEMESPORT exclusively revealed on Tuesday that Calvert-Lewin was set to sign a latest contract himself, as he becomes the Yorkshire outfit's talisman.

It has left Nmecha as back-up, and with Calvert-Lewin starting all 11 Premier League matches this season, he has had to make do with appearances on the left and in the No.10.

Indeed, Nmecha started the 1-0 win against Tottenham earlier this month as one of the two attacking midfielders alongside Sean Longstaff. But after struggling, he dropped down to the bench for the trip to Chelsea on Saturday, a game the Whites ended up winning 4-1.

Noah Okafor, who replaced Nmecha in the XI, scored twice as Leeds moved up to seventh in the Premier League table and fans are now believing a European push could be on.

Despite the fact Nmecha is not in Farke's best starting side, Leeds do not want to lose him. And now, according to The Athletic, the final formalities are being concluded on a one-year contract extension for Nmecha after the Whites triggered an option in his current deal.

Nmecha was due to be out of contract at the end of the season, but he will be tied down until 2028 once the paperwork is finalised, ensuring he is still at Leeds for at least another 18 months.

While some Leeds fans are a little frustrated that the club have triggered the extension on Nmecha's contract, given the fact he hasn't scored since February and has struggled this season, he is still an important squad player and will be a cost-effective option if the Whites secure European football.

Leeds will need a bigger squad if they reach Europe, and given Nmecha's experience on the continent in Belgium and Germany, he could be crucial for Farke in that competition.

Recent findings suggest that as well as that, he is one of the lowest earners in the first-team squad at £45,000 per-week and there isn't too many better players Leeds can replace him with who would take that wage and join on a free transfer. For me, it's a no-brainer to extend Nmecha's contract.

Alongside the fact he could be notable for Leeds if they secure European football, the extension to Nmecha's deal also protects his value and means the Whites could sell him to make profit.

Recent findings suggest that and should Leeds fail to make Europe, that is the option they should take. Selling Nmecha in those circumstances would make sense, as it's clear he is not good enough for a regular spot in the Premier League XI but can cut it in the Europa League or Conference League, for sure.

Industry observers note that nmecha can still make an impact from the bench and even in the FA Cup for Leeds this season. He set up Calvert-Lewin's equaliser in the draw against Brentford in August and has shown that he can influence matches when needed, even if he isn't as effective as the likes of Okafor.

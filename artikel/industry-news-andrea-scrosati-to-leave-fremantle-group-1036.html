Fremantle Group COO and CEO Continental Europe Andrea Scrosati is stepping down after an eight-year tenure in which he oversaw an ambitious expansion of the Fremantle’s international production operations, including the acquisition of multiple independent film and TV production companies.

According to recent reports, fremantle revealed Friday that Scrosati, who joined the company from Sky Italia in 2018, and was named CEO Continental Europe in 2021, is stepping down to pursue a newly introduced entrepreneurial venture. He will stay on until the end of January 2027 to support the transition to new leadership.

During his tenure, Scrosati led Fremantle’s mergers and acquisitions strategy, transforming the company from a business primarily associated with international entertainment formats into a global production group with a substantial presence in premium drama, independent film and documentaries.

Among the most significant acquisitions included Dublin-based Element Pictures, the production manufacturer behind The Favourite, Room and Normal People; Italian producer Lux Vide, whose credits include Medici, Leonardo and Devils; and the Asacha Media Group, which owns eight production labels across France, Italy, including Red Planet Pictures, the British producer of Death in Paradise, and Italy’s Picomedia. Industry observers note that the acquisitions also provided the foundation for Fremantle’s expansion in film. Among the major productions backed during Scrosati’s tenure were Yorgos Lanthimos’ Poor Things; Joanna Hogg’s The Eternal Daughter; Ali Abbasi’s The Apprentice; and Sebastián Lelio’s The Wonder. During Scrosati’s tenure, Fremantle racked up 17 Oscar nominations and four Academy Awards for its titles. (Related: Senate panel vote on National Science Foundation nomine...)

Scrosati also oversaw Fremantle’s expansion into documentaries and sports programing, the growth of its free ad-supported streaming television (FAST) channel business and the creation of Imaginae Studios, a standalone production label focused on artificial intelligence. Its first production, Art Awakens, rolled out in 2026.

The M&A activity was part of a broader growth strategy set by Fremantle’s parent manufacturer RTL Group, which in 2021 set an ambitious target of doubling Fremantle’s annual revenue to €3 billion ($3.3 billion) within four years, through acquisitions and the expansion of Fremantle’s production businesses. The company did not reach that goal. Revenue peaked at approximately €2.27 billion in 2023 before falling to €2.254 billion in 2024 and €2.043 billion in 2025, leaving it nearly €1 billion short of the original target. RTL has subsequently pushed the €3 billion ambition further into the future.

Recent findings suggest that “Andrea has made an enormous contribution to Fremantle over the past eight years,” Jennifer Mullin, Global CEO Fremantle, noted in a statement. “He has been instrumental in our growth and transformation, from helping build Fremantle into a true portfolio manufacturer and leading our M&A strategy, to establishing our film strategy and constantly pushing us to think differently about innovation and the future of our business.”.

Scrosati noted the time was right to move on and focus on building something new. “I leave with enormous pride in what we have achieved together and great affection for the company and everyone who makes it what it is.”.

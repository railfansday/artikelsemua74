According to recent reports, on Thursday, Nov. 19, Anthropic (ANT -5.45%) opened to the public at $111.50 per share. The stock price touched $115.10 at one point during the day, then fell below $100, closing at $97.17.

Industry observers note that while major news outlets report Anthropic shares climbed 17% on the day, for any retail investor who bought $1,000 worth of stock, there's a good chance they've already lost money. The returns for retail investors may differ from those reported by major news outlets due to the gap between the initial public offering (IPO) price and the price at which Anthropic shares opened to the public.

The IPO pricing was set at $83, a level typically reserved for high-net-worth individuals and large institutions. For that group, with Anthropic closing at $97.17 per share, their return was 17%.

Let's say a retail investor bought $1,000 worth of stock, and their entire order was filled at the opening price of $111.50. That would give them approximately nine shares. At a closing price of $97.17, that $1,000 investment would have declined to around $870.

Fortunately, for investors who plan to hold for the long haul, day one won't make or break an investment.

Meta Platforms (META -1.81%) is one of the best examples of having a rough IPO and a tough few months of trading before ultimately rewarding long-term investors.

Sources indicate that when it went public as Facebook in May 2012, it was chaotic. On the very first day of trading, a glitch delayed the public offering by 30 minutes, and some traders didn't receive confirmation that their trades had gone through. The stock price finished flat on the day, and things only got worse from there. According to research from The Motley Fool, out of the 10 largest IPOs by capital raised to date, Meta has had the worst three-month post-IPO performance, with shares down 47%.

Recent findings suggest that that stated, the good news for anyone who still had conviction in the company was that they were handsomely rewarded. Five years after its public debut, the stock price was up 289%. Then, by May 2026, about 14 years after the IPO, the stock price had climbed 1,572%.

According to recent reports, what that tells investors about Anthropic is that it's not the first day of trading, or even the first few months, that will determine whether the company was a good investment.

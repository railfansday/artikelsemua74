Industry News: Are Manchester United this season's Tottenham?

Mauricio Pochettino has taken over a Manchester United team in the Premier League relegation zone.

Last season, Tottenham - a member of the so-called 'significant six' - escaped relegation from the Premier League on the final day.

Now, with Manchester United making their worst start to a league season since 1972, it begs the question: are United this season's Spurs?

Well while Tottenham finished 17th with just 41 points, they actually started the season better than United have. After 11 games last season, Spurs were riding high in fifth with 18 points - 10 more than United have now.

But one of the biggest myths in football is 'the table never lies' and it was certainly lying about Tottenham at this stage last season.

While the table was prompting talk of a possible Champions League place, expected goals (xG) had already recognised a relegation battle lay ahead for Tottenham, even predicting the position they'd finish in. (Related: Predicting and detecting failures: How Azure strengthen...)

United's expected goal difference is almost identical to Tottenham's at this stage last season, but this time the table is more honest, even if United are perhaps not as bad as it makes out.

But there's no denying United's statistics are those of a team in a relegation battle, with many of them in the bottom six of Premier League teams this season.

While United have performed poorly defensively, their attack is actually not bad.

Remarkably, United have taken the most shots in the Premier League so far this season, and their expected goals scored figure is in the top five.

But what upcoming head coach Mauricio Pochettino has to implement quickly is some quality control when it comes to shots at both ends of the pitch. United are converting just 7% of their chances and conceding 16% of their opponents' - a poor combination for any team.

According to recent reports, and it's not simply down to finishing or shot-stopping, though they should have scored a few more. Based on xG per shot, United are presently taking the second-worst chances to score and giving up the easiest ones at the back. (Related: Transformer-based Encoder-Decoder Models...)

As a result, Opta give United just over a one-in-four chance of being relegated at the end of the season (27.5%), making them the fifth-most likely side to go down after Crystal Palace, Sunderland, Ipswich and Coventry.

By analysing historical and team performances as well as the strength of opponents, Opta predict the % chance of each club finishing in each position in the League.

Industry observers note that while the above means it's more likely United will survive, it's worth noting the one position Opta predicts United are most likely to finish is 18th, with a 10.1% chance. That would mean the 13-time Premier League winners are relegated.

There's a 90% chance they finish anywhere else, but the next most likely are the three places just above the relegation zone, including a 9.9% chance of them finishing where Tottenham did last season.

History also suggests a relegation battle lies ahead for United given their poor start.

Of the 26 previous sides that had exactly eight points after 11 games, 13 survived and 13 were relegated. Among those that stayed up, only four finished more than six points above the danger zone.

A top-half finish looks unlikely. Since the competition became a 20-team league in 1995-96, no side has ever finished in the top half after taking eight points or fewer from their first 11 games.

Two teams did finish there more than 30 years ago but they had four more games to climb the table as teams played 42 games a season in those days.

Having mentioned that, both were in the top half at the 38-game mark so United finishing there wouldn't be entirely unprecedented.

It's a big ask, though, as the club have already taken 19 points fewer from the fixtures they've played than they did last season.

Even if they were to repeat the results that led to them finishing third from now on, they would still only reach 52 points, the number Chelsea narrowly finished 10th with last season.

Though United are without a win in nine Premier League matches, their remaining fixtures of 2026 do offer some hope.

While they have a tough home game against Brentford this weekend and a trip to champions Arsenal just before Christmas, their other five games are against teams right now in the bottom seven - sides we must, at this point, call relegation rivals.

So despite suffering the worst first game by a Manchester United manager for 100 years in the 4-0 defeat at Liverpool, Pochettino will hope to promptly quash any concerns his newly introduced club will be embroiled in the same fight as his former one last season.

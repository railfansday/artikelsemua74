Just in case you weren’t aware, The Inbetweeners is a brilliant (in my opinion anyway) comedy series (that has also then produced a couple of excellent films).

This comedy is the story of four teenagers who are misfits, neither totally cool nor total outcasts (hence The Inbetweeners), who regularly find themselves in awkward situations of their own making,. (Related: NASA’s new AI space chip could let spacecraft think for...)

This fictional quartet occasionally hint of moving on to a consistently successful level, but their own inherent frailties as well as outside factors, time after time conspire to prevent those glimpses of promise becoming ever more than that.

My focus isn’t on individuals who have just arrived this summer at St James’ Park, it isn’t on young players, it isn’t on older players where we pretty much know what we are going to get.

When it comes to The Inbetweeners at Newcastle United, I wanted to concentrate on this quartet below.

Four players who have played plenty of Premier League football in their careers and have shown enough to suggest they COULD be crucial, indeed vital, for Newcastle United this season.

All four of them also happen to play in the key midfield and attacking positions, the positions where United need far higher input and productivity if they are to create the chances and score the goals that can make the (positive) difference this season.

So without further ado, here are The Inbetweeners at St James’ Park:.

I think that arguably he is THE ultimate Inbetweener at Newcastle United. He was brilliant in that 2022-23 fourth place finish season before then serious injury hit then fitness and form issues have followed.

At times we see THAT Joe Willock of 2022-23 and the second half on Saturday a prime example. Against his former club he was the key man in that second half,  the pressing of opposition players and his running with the ball, the Arsenal midfield couldn’t handle him at times. Willock pressured Odegaard into the mistake that produced the equaliser and then an assist for Thiaw’s strike.

Joe Willock would deserve to be first name on the team sheet if he consistently played like he did in that second half.

Started the season in electric form, confidence sky high as he scored within five minutes of the opening game against Liverpool, then in the first EPL away game scored a brilliant goal to set United on their way to beating Spurs.

It has been revealed that then injury hit and when he has returned, it feels a bit like Snakes and Ladders. He climbed plenty of ladders and got into a great position brimming with confidence, he then though hits one snake that sends his confidence and form sliding right back to rock bottom and needing to start again.

Sources indicate that I bit like Willock and Saturday’s second half, Elanga needs to simply get his head back in the place it was when Liverpool and Spurs couldn’t handle him. He is capable of being a huge player for Matthias Jaissle AND he needs to be that player to solve our problem on the right. He did alright on Saturday when he came on and maybe a huge factor can be if Tino Livramento now can stay fit as well on that side.

Very similar story to Anthony Elanga, started this season on fire and then lost his way.

In certain moments, Yoane Wissa has looked unplayable (in a good way!) this season, some of his runs on the ball have been exhilarating. The one he made with a handful of Liverpool players challenging him half the length of the pitch before passing to Joe Willock for his goal, where has that gone?

Again like Elanga, with Wissa it feels like it is very much a confidence thing.

Industry observers note that he had an astonishing record at Brentford but I do feel he is an attacker who is more of a team player, he needs those around him also playing well. Hopefully if the team overall can become more consistent and then Yoane Wissa finds within that some special moments of his own, we can just maybe have that opening of this season player back again.

Of this quartet of Inbetweeners at Newcastle United, I think Ramsey deserves to be cut the most slack.

The former Villa star has still only started 15 Premier League matches for Newcastle United and was very unlucky with a bad early injury last season (thanks to Sean Longstaff) and desperately needs a run in the team to get his rhythm after having his own struggles with fitness and form.

In a recent development, like the other three above, Jacob Ramsey has had moments where we can see the player Eddie Howe signed. The same as Joe Willock, Ramsey is very mobile, runs well with the ball, can be very effective with a high press, can be a difference maker.

The second half on Saturday he showed that and I do think that if Newcastle United are to exceed any current expectations, we need to have players with pace who can swiftly carry the ball in transition an cause problems for opposition teams on a regular basis. If Jacob Ramsey and Joe Willock can do it against the best team in the country, admittedly just for one half on Saturday, why can’t they do it against the rest?

Once again though, the same as Yoane Wissa and Anthony Elanga, it is all about this quarter losing their status as The Inbetweeners at Newcastle United and proving to be something far better than that.

Here’s hoping they prove they are worthy of another series.

Above: Louis Vuitton kicked off a three-year partnership with New York’s Frick Museum with its cruise show in May.

Bright lights go up, Peaches’ “Boys Wanna Be Her” starts pounding, and a girl wearing layers of denim begins strutting past such luminaries as Zendaya, Chloë Sevigny, and Ava DuVernay. In many ways a normal New York fashion event—except the rooms are adorned with Renaissance paintings, Qing Dynasty vases, and 19th-century gilded French furniture.

The exalted surroundings are in fact the galleries of the Frick Collection, the Gilded Age treasure trove that was recently reenvisioned with the help of Elle Decor A-List architect Annabelle Selldorf. The fashion, music, and glamour are courtesy of Louis Vuitton, which has made a tradition of working with cultural institutions and heritage sites. The French house kicked off a three-year sponsorship of the Frick with this show in May, and now the first exhibition with the firm’s support is open: a landmark show of Italian bronze sculptural masterpieces.

Sources indicate that the reliefs, figurines, medals, and candelabra were drawn from collections as wide-ranging as Dresden, London, Washington, and Siena, the focus of the show. Donatello’s tomb of the Bishop of Grosseto, a life-size traffic stopper of a slab, timeworn yet still lyrical, is worth a visit alone, as is the miniature, gilded zinc brass relief of Bellerophon and the Chimera by Francesco di Giorgio. In an era of endless swiping, the lesson of the Chimera—and of these timeless bronzes—endures: be wary of desire’s fast fulfillment. Instead, wander into the rooms and stay a while.

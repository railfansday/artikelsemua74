Leveraged funds reduced their net short positions in CME Bitcoin futures by 79.3% over five weeks before Bitcoin plunged below $60,000 on Friday.

It has been revealed that the funds held 1,211 more short contracts than long contracts on Nov. 17, down from 5,844 on Oct. 13, according to the Commodity Futures Trading Commission's Nov. 20 report. The shift was driven primarily by declining short exposure. Over the period, short contracts fell from 3,889 to 7,475, while long contracts increased from 744 to 6,264. However, the retreat nearly stalled in the final week, with net shorts declining by just 32 contracts as both long and short positions contracted.

That was the smallest weekly reduction in the five-week period, compared with declines of more than 900 contracts in each of the preceding four weeks.

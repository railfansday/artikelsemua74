Industry News: Box Office: ‘Sunrise on the Reaping’ Reignites ‘Hunger Games’ Franchise With $120 Million Debut

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

“Sunrise on the Reaping” caught fire at the domestic box office, collecting a sensational $120 million in its opening weekend while reinvigorating Lionsgate’s “Hunger Games” franchise.

Overseas, “Sunrise on the Reaping” generated $105 million from 84 territories for a towering launch of $225 million globally.

Recent findings suggest that the dystopian prequel is one of just five movies this year to launch above $100 million in North America, joining the firm of “Spider-Man: Brand New Day” ($360 million), “Toy Story 5” ($159 million), “The Super Mario Galaxy Movie” ($131 million) and “The Odyssey” ($123 million). All of those films eventually surpassed the $1 billion mark globally. “‘Sunrise on the Reaping’ posting an impressive $100 million-plus [domestic] debut reinforces just how vital ‘The Hunger Games’ remains as a movie franchise,” says Paul Dergarabedian, Rentrak’s head of marketplace trends.

It has been revealed that “Sunrise on the Reaping” coincided with another massive release — albeit not at the movies. Rockstar’s video game “Grand Theft Auto VI” launched after years of anticipation, earning a staggering $3.79 billion on its first day. It’s expected to set a sales record for entertainment across any medium. (Related: GTA 6 glitch lets players access first-person mode...)

“Many have speculated as to whether the release of ‘Grand Theft Auto VI’ would hurt ‘The Hunger Games’ at the box office, and I think the marketplace has already spoken,” adds Dergarabedian. “There’s plenty of time to play ‘GTA VI’ and make it to a theater to see ‘Sunrise on the Reaping’ this weekend.”.

According to recent reports, on top of pitched competition at home, initial ticket sales for “Sunrise on the Reaping” are notable because they’re not far off from the original saga. For reference, 2012’s “The Hunger Games” ignited to $152 million, followed by 2013’s “Catching Fire” ($158 million), 2014’s “Mockingjay Part 1” ($121 million) and 2015’s “Mockingjay Part 2” ($102 million). Nearly a decade after those movies, starring Jennifer Lawrence and Josh Hutcherson, concluded their run, the studio revived the young-adult property with 2023’s “The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes,” an origin story about Panem’s cruel leader, President Coriolanus Snow. That film, set during the 10th Hunger Games, bowed significantly below its predecessors with $44 million. However, the movie stuck around through the holidays and eventually climbed to $363 million globally, registering as a win against a (comparatively leaner) $100 million budget and justifying another prequel for the studio. “Sunrise on the Reaping,” which returns to Panem for the 50th Hunger Games, carries a larger price tag above $150 million, not including the many millions spent on worldwide marketing efforts.

Recent findings suggest that that “Sunrise on the Reaping” managed to recapture the spark of the initial series is impressive as the sixth installment in any property. And the film earned a strong “A-” grade on CinemaScore exit polls, which bodes well for box office longevity. Analysts attribute the strong debut to several factors, including the franchise’s multi-generational appeal as well as the excitement for the return of fan-favorite characters. This chapter, based on the novel by Suzanne Collins, has a stronger connection to the original saga; the story follows a young Haymitch Abernathy (Joseph Zada takes on the role originally played by Woody Harrelson) — who eventually mentors Katniss (Lawrence) and Peeta (Hutcherson) in their Hunger Games, a Battle Royale for teens. Francis Lawrence returned to direct, having helmed all but the first chapter in the YA-centric franchise.

“The last ‘Hunger Games’ stumbled when the story went back 64 years without Jennifer Lawrence, Josh Hutcherson and Woody Harrelson,” says David A. Gross, who publishes the box office newsletter FranchiseRe. “This is a strong return.”.

“Sunrise on the Reaping” is Lionsgate’s second major theatrical victory in 2026 following “Michael,” which captured a huge $97 million in its debut and became the first biopic ever to cross the $1 billion mark. With this weekend’s haul, the “Hunger Games” series has collectively generated $3.43 billion in global ticket sales, making it the studio’s highest-grossing franchise ever—above “The Twilight Saga” ($3.3 billion worldwide across five films).

“This success is a testament to their remarkable film, the performances of our incredibly talented cast, and ultimately, the love of ‘Hunger Games’ fans worldwide, who embraced a film that told a story they had been asking to see for many years,” mentioned Adam Fogelson, chairman of the Lionsgate Motion Picture Group.

Second place went to “I Play Rocky,” which earned $9.3 million while expanding nationwide to 2,477 theaters. The Amazon MGM movie, which depicts the real-life story of Sylvester Stallone bringing the 1976 classic “Rocky” to the massive screen, has generated $10.4 million after two weekends in limited release. Audiences, roughly half of whom were over 45 years old, awarded the feel-good film a rare “A+” grade on CinemaScore. “I Play Rocky,” which hopes to find itself in the Oscar race, cost about $30 million to produce.

Last weekend’s champ “Ebenezer” dropped to third place with $7.7 million from 3,545 venues, a steep 60% decline from its opening. Paramount’s PG-13 take on “A Christmas Carol,” starring Johnny Depp in his first major Hollywood role in eight years, has generated $32.5 million in North America and $78 million globally. It’s not quite a resounding embrace of Depp amid the actor’s return to studio fare. However, it’ll be decently positioned in its theatrical run against a modest $38 million budget. (Theater owners keep half of ticket sales.).

Recent findings suggest that in limited release, the Focus Features drama “Elsinore” earned $240,000 from six locations in New York and Los Angeles, translating to $40,000 per theater. To compare, another awards hopeful, A24’s drama “Club Kid” had a $65,097 per-screen average from six venues, while “I Play Rocky” averaged $49,200 per location in its initial frame. “Elsinore” stars Andrew Scott as the Scottish actor Ian Charleson who secretly battles AIDS while playing “Hamlet” in London. Focus will continue to roll out the film through December.

It has been revealed that “Sunrise on the Reaping” provided a nice jolt to the box office ahead of Thanksgiving. This Turkey Day might not cement any theatrical records, but the holiday should be busy for theater owners. “Hunger Games” will continue to draw crowds alongside new offerings like Universal’s comedy “Focker in Law,” with Ariana Grande joining Ben Stiller and Robert De Niro, Disney’s animated “Hexed” and Netflix’s Imax release of “The Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth.”.

“This is a very good weekend,” Gross says. “The weekend before Thanksgiving sets up 10 very strong days. Moviegoing is back on track.”.

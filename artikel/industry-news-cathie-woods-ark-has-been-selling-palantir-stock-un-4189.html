Recent findings suggest that funds run by Cathie Wood's Ark Invest sold 58,339 shares in Palantir Technologies (PLTR -1.24%) on Nov. 12, worth about $10 million at the day's close of $171.58. Ark's flagship fund sold 53,547 of the shares, and its internet-focused fund sold the other 4,792. (Related: Breaking: Deep Learning over the Internet: Training Lan...)

The artificial intelligence (AI) software firm's stock has kept dropping since. As I write, Palantir trades near $157, about 9% below where Ark sold and about 25% below its Oct. 9 record close of $209.05.

Industry observers note that A big growth investor selling into a slide can look like a vote against the stock. But I don't think that particular sale notes much. And the fall since then has sent shares well below the buy price of around $172 I set for Palantir earlier this month. (Related: Latest Update: At least seven progressives voted agains...)

According to recent reports, cathie Wood. Image source: Getty Images.

Ark trades its Palantir stake often, and in both directions. It sold around $9.6 million of the stock in three days from Oct. 19 through Oct. 21. Then on Nov. 3, the day after Palantir's third-quarter results, Ark bought 63,638 shares across five funds, worth around $11.2 million at that day's close of $175.40.

In a recent development, so, the Nov. 12 sale mostly undid a purchase Ark made just after earnings. Four days later, though, Ark's robotics-focused fund sold around $16 million of the stock. Counting every November trade through Nov. 16, Ark has sold more Palantir shares than it bought this month.

The stake is still substantial, too. As of Nov. 18, Ark's flagship fund owned around 2.1 million Palantir shares, worth about $325 million, according to Ark's daily holdings file. The flagship fund's 53,547-share sale on Nov. 12 was under 3% of that.

Recent findings suggest that showing how consistently the firm has topped its own forecasts, Palantir's third-quarter revenue climbed 96% year over year to around $2.31 billion, about 7% above the midpoint of the guidance management gave in August. It had done that twice already this year. First-quarter revenue was around 6% above the guidance midpoint, and second-quarter revenue about 8% above it. And every report brought a higher full-year outlook. Palantir's 2026 revenue guidance began the year at around $7.19 billion. It's now around $8.53 billion, up about 19%, which would mean growth of 91% over 2025.

The firm also raised its forecast for U.S. commercial revenue (sales to American businesses) to at least $3.56 billion, which would be growth of at least 143%.

For the last three months of 2026, Palantir sees revenue of about $2.65 billion, up 88% year over year. That would be down from the third quarter's 96% growth. But if Palantir beats its forecast by a similar margin again, fourth-quarter revenue could come in near $2.8 billion, about twice the $1.41 billion it posted a year before.

Plus, profits are likely to climb faster than sales. Management sees fourth-quarter non-GAAP (adjusted) operating income of about $1.64 billion, over double the $798 million it posted for the same quarter last year.

NASDAQ · As of 9:57 AM ET 11/20/26.

Palantir earned $1.43 a share in its past four reported quarters. At roughly $157, this puts the stock's price-to-earnings ratio near 110.

That's a high price by nearly any standard. But it's far less extreme than it was a few weeks ago. At its Oct. 9 record close, Palantir's price-to-earnings ratio stood near 179, based on the $1.17 per share Palantir earned over the four quarters through June. Some of that decline in the price-to-earnings ratio came from the earnings side, as the third-quarter report raised the manufacturer's trailing earnings per share by about 22%.

Measured against analysts' consensus estimate for Palantir's 2027 earnings per share, the stock's forward price-to-earnings ratio is around 62.

In a recent development, sure, a stock at about 110 times earnings needs years of fast profit growth to pay off. And the guided slowdown to 88% revenue growth is a reminder that Palantir's growth will eventually cool. But I think a business that's topped its own forecast three straight quarters makes that bet easier to take.

Sources indicate that in the end, I don't think Ark's trim tells investors much about where Palantir is going. The organization's own numbers say more, and they still point to fast growth in both revenue and profits.

Under $160, I'd consider buying Palantir stock now. But I'd add shares in small batches over the coming months, as a stock this volatile could easily dip further first.

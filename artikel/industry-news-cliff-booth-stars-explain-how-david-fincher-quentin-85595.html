Sources indicate that the Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth is the first time in nearly a decade that audiences can see acclaimed filmmaker Quentin Tarantino's work in theaters. While the sequel to 2019's love letter to American cinema, Once Upon a Time in... Hollywood, was directed by David Fincher, Tarantino still penned the screenplay. The combination of both powerhouses has made the movie one of the year's most anticipated, and the cast even felt the magic while working on set.

Brad Pitt once again plays Cliff Booth, who now finds himself in 1977 without the trusty Rick Dalton by his side. While he is no longer at the center of Hollywood, he still hangs out with famous folk within the industry, doing their dirty work in the seedy underbelly of Los Angeles. He runs into various fascinating characters, including actor Charlie Peters (Scott Caan), whose tension with his wife Toni (Carla Gugino) becomes central to the plot. Charlie's agent, Karen Crosby (Elizabeth Debicki), finds herself entangled with Cliff romantically, while Blaxploitation movie star Roderick "Hot Rod" Harrison (Yahya Abdul-Mateen II) adds plenty of charm to the proceedings. ScreenRant's Kim Horcher interviewed Caan, Debicki, Gugino, and Abdul-Mateen about the exciting challenge of stepping into Tarantino's world through the lens of Fincher's camera in The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth. The actors shared their reactions to joining the project, how they got into character, and which they found to be the best parts of fictional 1970s Hollywood. Industry observers note that screenRant: How was it working with Fincher on a Tarantino project? How did it feel to get that call?

In a recent development, carla Gugino: It's pretty much the best call you could ever get, honestly. I think it's almost as simple as that. You have the foundation of this incredible script, and then you have one of the greatest visionary directors interpreting it through his lens. And that combination is so unique. I don't know that we'll ever get that kind of combination. It's such an unusual opportunity. It's like falling into a pot of honey.

According to recent reports, yahya Abdul-Mateen II: It makes the job easy. We both have a theater background. When I go to do theater, I get a chance to say the best words, and that helps a lot. We had everything that we needed in order to succeed, and so the stage was set for us to just bring the best of ourselves without having to figure out how to make things work so much, which sometimes is what we have to do.

The job is to figure out how to make things work. But sometimes when you have to do that less, then you can just get in there, and it's just about what you get to do. "Ooh, I get to do this." That's what work felt like on most days for me. I felt like a star.

This project was a gift, a throwback to a really, really fun time. And to make a present-day movie that is a '70s movie with the flair of Quentin Tarantino's words, and under the direction of David Fincher's eye with Brad and the entire cast? It was just really, really fun.

Carla Gugino: I think the thing about Quentin's writing that's so noticeable is that nothing is ever generic. You really believe these people are real people. It's a lot like Elmore Leonard, where they act in unexpected ways. When most people are nervous, they're flirty. He brings the period to life in a really exceptional way.

According to recent reports, and then David is so known for how impeccable he is with period stuff, so I [love] the fact that we got to embody this world of the '70s and what it represents. There's this star, and we have a stunt guy who is kind of in awe of that star. We have a lifelong actress who's made a living that way, but hasn't been as successful as she wants to be, but also is kind of okay with that. Then we have a TV star who gets a taste of all the different elements of Hollywood at that time. Everybody is sort of compromised, and that's kind of what the '70s was like in a lot of ways. So, all those things are very noticeable.

Recent findings suggest that screenRant: Elizabeth, your character really surprised me with her somewhat contradictory nature and her multidimensionality. I feel like I've met people like her in LA. What was she to you?

It has been revealed that elizabeth Debicki: She was an amazing role that I found in that script. I think Quentin created a character that is quite unlike anything else he's written for a female character. She is really contradictory, and I think that is the core of human nature. We just roam around and contradict ourselves.

It has been revealed that she obviously has real agency in this world, but she's really smart and funny and completely not ready for someone to walk into her life to kind of force her to look at herself. It's a really interesting relationship between her and Cliff, and I loved playing her.

In a recent development, when David and I first spoke about it, I remember us both saying our goal really was that we really wanted her to feel real, like someone you knew. I wanted her to feel like, "I know that woman. I wonder what she ate for breakfast. I bet she lives in an apartment that looks like that." She feels grounded.

Industry observers note that screenRant: Scott, on the other hand, I feel like I've only met maybe one or two Charlies. What went into making him your own?

Scott Caan: Listen, Quentin writes really great characters, and when I read the script, I was like, "Wow, this one is fantastic." For me, I like to bring as much of myself into the character as I can. For this one, initially, it was like, "God, how do I justify all this stuff?" That was really my approach. "How can I justify all my behavior and all these decisions?" And it was very fun.

Industry observers note that there's one [actor he reminded me of], which I'm not even going to mention who it is, but there was one idea. We were talking about it before, but it kind of went away. We do a lot of homework, and then when you show up, you kind of forget it all and just go try to play. I mean, the guy's got to be a version of me. So, I read something, and I go, "Alright, how can I put the version of me into this?".

The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth begins playing in select IMAX theaters on November 25 before streaming December 23 on Netflix.

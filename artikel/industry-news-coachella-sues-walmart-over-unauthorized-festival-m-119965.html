📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

At Coachella, fringe is welcome. Infringement, not so much.

In a new 28-page lawsuit filed Tuesday and obtained by Rolling Stone, organizers of the annual music festival accuse Walmart of “willful and repeated acts of trademark infringement,” claiming the retail giant intentionally sold, or allowed others to sell, “directly competitive goods” bearing the Coachella name and its distinctive logos featuring a Ferris wheel and palm tree silhouettes. The organizers say the unauthorized merchandise was sold in recent years at Walmart stores in North Indio and La Quinta, near the festival grounds. They also accuse Walmart of using Coachella’s trademarks in signage and advertising to promote other products, including beverages, coolers, and folding chairs. Sources indicate that the festival and its parent firm, Goldenvoice, argue in their upcoming filings in federal court in Los Angeles that they’re highly selective in granting sponsorships and licensing deals. The arrangements, they argue, command considerable value given the festival’s reach and visibility, and Walmart is unfairly exploiting their intellectual property without authorization or payment.

“Walmart has intentionally created a likelihood of confusion and false association between Walmart’s stores, website, and various goods it sells, on the one hand, and the festival and plaintiffs’ goods, on the other hand,” the lawsuit reads. The plaintiffs say the allegedly infringing conduct “was so extensive and so prominent” that a local newspaper, The Desert Sun, posted several videos on Instagram showing tumblers, bottles, wine glasses, tank tops, and other merchandise bearing the Coachella trademarks along with a caption that read, “Who needs the merch tent [at Coachella] when you have the North Indio Walmart?”.

It has been revealed that the complaint includes photos of the allegedly infringing items for sale at Walmart stores. The festival’s organizers say they have repeatedly asked Walmart to remove the merchandise over the years, but that the retailer “typically stops for a time but then resumes its infringing conduct.” They say the “recalcitrant behavior” left them with no choice but to sue. In a statement sent to Rolling Stone, a spokesperson for Walmart noted the tech giant hasn’t seen the lawsuit yet. “We will review the complaint and respond appropriately in court once we are served,” the spokesperson said in an email. A rep for Goldenvoice did not immediately reply to a request for comment.

Sources indicate that coachella recently revealed that Dua Lipa, Tame Impala, and SZA will top the lineup when the festival returns to Indio, California, next year. The fest is set for April 9-11 and April 16-18, 2027.

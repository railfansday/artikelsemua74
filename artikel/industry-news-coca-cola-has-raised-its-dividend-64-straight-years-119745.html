Investors can't help but think about the 2026 performance of a popular defensive name. The S&P 500, with heavy exposure to technology and artificial intelligence, is only up 6% this year (as of Nov. 23). Coca-Cola (KO -0.03%), on the other hand, has soared 37%. The beverage stock isn't known for its growth potential, which has been a pleasant surprise in 2026. Instead, investors choose to buy and hold this industry-leading company because it's a safe choice that can generate a healthy dividend stream. In a recent development, coca-Cola has raised its dividend payout for a mind-blowing 64 straight years, making it a Dividend King, or a company that has raised its payout for 50 years or more. Purchasing $10,000 worth of its shares today will bring in this much passive income in 2027.

Coca-Cola's board of directors approved a roughly 4% dividend hike on Feb. 19. The quarterly payout climbed from $0.51 to $0.53 per share, or $2.12 annualized.

Sources indicate that today's annual payout is 51% higher than the $1.40 per share paid in 2016. That's about 4.2% annualized growth. It isn't spectacular. But it's consistent, surviving a pandemic and a bout of high inflation.

Recent findings suggest that here's the math investors care about. Buying $10,000 at the current share price of $95.88 gets you 104 shares. Based on today's payout, that position would generate more than $220 in dividend income in 2027. If the board delivers another raise in February, a near certain event, that figure will obviously be higher.

Industry observers note that the dividend yield sits at 2.21%. The S&P 500 yields just 1.12%. Consequently, $10,000 in an S&P 500 index fund would produce only about $112 a year. Coca-Cola nearly doubles that.

Recent findings suggest that there's a catch, though. Coca-Cola yielded 2.66% the day after its February raise. This stock's impressive rally this year has pushed the yield much lower. Income investors are now paying more for every dollar of dividends.

NYSE · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/25/26.

According to recent reports, nobody questions the quality of this business. Coca-Cola sells drinks in more than 200 countries and territories. Its flagship trademark is the world's most valuable food and beverage brand, according to Kantar.

That brand strength translates into durable pricing power. In the third quarter, net revenue grew 7% year over year to $13.4 billion. Unit case volume, a measure of how much product consumers bought, jumped 5%. Yet price/mix (higher prices plus a richer product mix) still added 1%. Coca-Cola sold more without sacrificing price.

Industry observers note that profitability improved. The comparable operating margin, which excludes one-time items, widened to 32.9% from 31.9%. Comparable earnings per share (EPS) rose 11% to $0.91, beating Wall Street's $0.88 estimate. Management now expects full-year comparable EPS growth of at least 11%.

Valuation is the problem. Coca-Cola trades at a price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio of 28.3. The S&P 500's index's P/E multiple is 20.3. That's a 39% premium for a tech giant that isn't built for speed.

Sources indicate that management's long-term targets call for 4% to 5% organic revenue growth (excluding currency and acquisitions). Sell-side analysts expect the top line to shrink in 2027.

Recent findings suggest that this year's gain was less about profits. The stock's 37% surge far outpaces that 11% earnings outlook for the year. Investors simply paid more for each dollar of earnings, with the P/E ratio rising 23% in 2026. That can't continue indefinitely.

I wouldn't buy Coca-Cola at today's price. With the best outcome being mid- to high-single-digit growth ahead, the valuation leaves little room for upside. Patient investors who appreciate the dividend should wait for a cheaper entry point.

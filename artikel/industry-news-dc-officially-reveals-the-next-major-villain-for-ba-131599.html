DC reveals the identity of the Batman villain who will act as the Dark Knight's main antagonist in an upcoming crossover story. Despite the huge size of Batman's rogues' gallery, a select group of Gotham criminals are recurring main villains across all media. The Joker is ubiquitous, with primary antagonistic appearances in virtually every DC continuity. Other foes like Two-Face, the Riddler, the Penguin, and Bane pop up in dozens of DC comics, movies, and series, followed closely by Scarecrow, Ra’s al Ghul, Mr. Freeze, and mob kingpins Carmine Falcone and Sal Maroni.

According to recent reports, meanwhile, dozens of memorable Gotham villains almost always get relegated to brief cold-open beatdowns or henchman muscle. Physical powerhouses like Clayface, Killer Croc, Solomon Grundy, and Man-Bat have never fought Batman in a theatrical movie, and they usually act as mid-level boss fights or hired muscle in other media. Likewise, psychological and gimmick rogues like the Mad Hatter, Ventriloquist and Scarface, Professor Pyg, Hugo Strange, Firefly, Calendar Man, and Hush rarely get the chance to headline a major mainstream adaptation on their own. Other Batman enemies are even less lucky, with zero main antagonist appearances to their name. Fortunately, DC is finally placing one of those Gotham criminals in the spotlight with a major twist that makes them a threat not only to Batman, but also to Superman, Wonder Woman, Supergirl, and many other heroes. Industry observers note that dark Knights of Steel II #5 reveals a shocking new villain when Prince Victor Falcone, the unstable son of Blüdhaven’s honorable ruler King Carmine Falcone, reveals himself to be Victor Zsasz and kills his father to claim the throne. Zsasz had already shown his sadistic nature in issue #1, when he slaughtered young Bruce Wayne’s hound Ace. Now, Victor makes his move while the House of El mourns Queen Lara and Amanda Waller sits imprisoned. In a ruthless coup straight out of Game of Thrones, Victor murders his own father, carves a fresh tally scar directly into his own flesh to commemorate the kill, and seizes the Blüdhaven crown as "the Mad King Zsasz.".

Dark Knights of Steel II's change to Zsasz' lineage isn't the first modification of this kind, as this universe's Alfred never really existed.

With King Jor-El and Queen Lara gone, Blüdhaven’s throne immediately makes Zsasz a geopolitical nightmare for Bruce Wayne, Kal-El, Zala-El, and every realm. King Victor Zsasz now has access to an entire kingdom’s armies and resources, while the House of El is weakened and divided. Zsasz’s signature self-scarring obsession is a terrifying match to his newfound sovereign royal power and his personal malice toward Bruce Wayne in this timeline.

Victor Zsasz has a brief Arkham inmate cameo in Batman Begins, a secondary antagonist role in the DCEU's Birds of Prey, appears as a recurring mob enforcer in Gotham, and has minor side-missions in the Arkham video games. He has always possessed the psychological ingredients of a standout thriller villain, including his nihilistic worldview, his eerie intelligence hidden behind quiet patience, and a chilling ritual of carving tally marks into his own skin that makes his run-ins with Batman intensely personal and unsettling. However, Zsasz has never been trusted to carry a major storyline on his own.

Recent findings suggest that tom Taylor and Otto Schmidt’s bold decision to turn Victor Zsasz into a kingdom-threatening tyrant in Dark Knights of Steel II #5 serves as a subtle reminder of how many other overlooked Batman rogues could thrive if given a similar main-villain spotlight in future Elseworlds or mainline sagas. Villains like Calendar Man, Professor Pyg, Firefly, and Deacon Blackfire all have their own psychological hooks and theatrical menace that usually get sidelined in favor of another Joker or Riddler scheme. At least in alternative timelines, these underused antagonists can make Batman’s rogues' gallery feel truly varied.

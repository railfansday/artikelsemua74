Industry observers note that while Dutton Ranch season 2 technically continues the Yellowstone franchise’s overarching story, the show’s most promising plot also brings back the storyline of creator Taylor Sheridan’s greatest movie to date. Thanks to the outsized success of the Yellowstone franchise, no one could be blamed for assuming that Taylor Sheridan was always a TV creator first and foremost. After all, the writer/ director at the moment has over 10 seasons of TV in production in 2026.

According to recent reports, even compared to historically successful super producers like Grey’s Anatomy creator Shonda Rhimes or controversial Glee mastermind Ryan Murphy, this is a staggering number. Yellowstone was a huge hit throughout its five-season run, but even the biggest fans of Sheridan’s Neo-Western family saga could hardly have guessed how many subsequent small-screen projects the show would spawn. First, there were Yellowstone’s prequels, 1883 and 1923. (Related: DNA robots could deliver drugs and hunt viruses inside ...)

Sources indicate that then, there were the many unrelated Sheridan highlights, including the five-season crime drama Mayor of Kingstown, Landman, Tulsa King, its upcoming spinoff Frisco King, The Madison, Lioness, Lawman: Bass Reeves, and the upcoming third Yellowstone prequel, 1944. There’s also Yellowstone’s two sequels Marshals and Dutton Ranch, the latter of which is drawing attention back to Sheridan’s major-screen career with a season 2 plot that owes its inspiration to 2015’s Sicario. Before Yellowstone, Sheridan was best known as the screenwriter behind a string of hit 2010s thrillers. Although Wind River and Hell or High Water, both of which Sheridan also directed, were critical hits, it was 2015’s drug trade thriller Sicario that really put his name on the map. Sicario’s story follows Emily Blunt’s FBI agent Kate Macer as she joins Benicio Del Toro’s assassin Alejandro Gillick and Josh Brolin’s CIA operative Matt Graver in taking on the Sonora drug cartel.

At least, that’s what Kate thinks the CIA is doing when the extragovernmental agency joins forces with her in Ciudad Juárez. Throughout the twisty, cynical story of Sicario, it becomes increasingly clear that no one is as innocent and well-intentioned as they seem, and the entire criminal enterprise is even more morally rotten than viewers assumed heading into the dark crime thriller.

This moral ambiguity is what marked Sicario out as Sheridan’s strongest screenplay, and his best movie to date. While Yellowstone ultimately valorized John Dutton despite his obvious, egregious flaws, Sicario truly doesn’t present any of its characters as heroes, and instead confronts viewers with the immense complexity and overwhelming moral mess of the international drug dealing business.

Compared to the simplistic macho posturing of many later Sheridan projects, Sicario marks the closest that the writer/ director came to capturing the profound thoughtfulness found in indicates like The Shield and The Wire. To make this achievement all the more impressive, it is crucial to note that the movie is no dull lecture on the complexity of cartels. Instead, Sicario masks its smart commentary amid brutal, fast action and a genuinely unpredictable, shocking plot.

Fortunately for fans of Sheridan’s TV work, Yellowstone’s stronger successor Dutton Ranch is now positioned, in season 2, to bring back Sicario’s best storyline. Season 2 pits Beth and Rip against drug cartels, just like the protagonists of Sheridan’s most uncontroversially acclaimed work to date, and Dutton Ranch’s cartel storyline can now prove that the show is Yellowstone’s perfect successor in the process.

It has been revealed that while CBS’s comparatively tame cop show Marshals saw Kayce switch sides to work for the police, Dutton Ranch brings Beth and Rip closer to direct rivalry with organized crime than ever before. As surprising as it might sound, considering how obviously corrupt the Duttons themselves were, drug cartels were only ever a distant threat in Yellowstone. Compared to rival ranches and private equity firms, local meth dealers ranked low on the list of the ranch-owning family’s enemies.

In a recent development, A lot of demonstrates that share Yellowstone’s focus on morally dubious criminal families, like the BBC’s recent critical hit This City Is Ours, explicitly center on the drug trade. However, the only time that drug trafficking was mentioned in Yellowstone’s run was when distant problems like cartel pressure and faraway human trafficking rings indirectly impinged on the family’s attempts to control their vast ranching empire.

In contrast, Dutton Ranch season 1 jumped headlong into the topic of drug cartels when the show revealed that Annette Bening’s formidable villain Beulah Jackson was involved in a complicated scheme to traffic fentanyl in the bellies of live cattle. To get the drugs across the country for a Mexican cartel, Beulah arranged to have her workers stitch the opioids into the animals before their cross-border relocation.

While this plot might sound more suited to From Dusk Til Dawn: The Series than a Yellowstone sequel, Dutton Ranch’s soapy style makes the storyline feel unexpectedly fitting. The show’s atmosphere is even closer to the melodrama of its inspirations, Dallas and Dynasty, than the more dour Yellowstone was, so the shocking plot didn’t feel entirely out of left field. What did feel shocking was season 1’s finale, “El Padrino,” setting up a perfect mirror of Sicario’s themes.

Image courtesy of MovieStillsDb.

Industry observers note that in the finale, Beth and Rip drag a confession out of Beulah, but their exposure of the fentanyl smuggling scheme results in the cartel boss Mariano Reyes, the titular El Padrino, kidnapping Beth and Rip’s adopted son Carter. Both Sicario and its 2018 sequel Sicario: Day of the Soldado feature kidnappings as prominent plot points, with the second movie even centering on the kidnapping of a cartel kingpin’s teenage child.

It has been revealed that this highlights just how much, despite the success of Marshals season 2 among viewers, Dutton Ranch feels much closer to Sheridan’s existing efforts than the glossy CBS procedural. Excitingly enough, Dutton Ranch season 2 isn’t Sheridan’s only upcoming project that will see the creator return to the world of drug cartels. There's also 2027’s star-studded upcoming action thriller movie F.A.S.T, which sees him finally return to the significant screen.

Sheridan penned the screenplay for director Ben Richardson’s F.A.S.T, which tells the story of Brandon Sklenar’s former special forces commando who is hired by the DEA to take on CIA-backed dealers in a small town. With a killer cast including LaKeith Stanfield, Sam Claflin, and Juliana Canfield, F.A.S.T will ensure that, alongside Dutton Ranch season 2, the themes of Sheridan’s Sicario will be explored in more depth in 2027.

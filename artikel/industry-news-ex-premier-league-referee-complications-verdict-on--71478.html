Former Premier League referee Graham Barber believes Michael Oliver made the right decision to award Leeds United a second-half penalty during their thumping 4-1 win at Chelsea.

With the score level after Noah Okafor cancelled out Emmanuel Emegha's opener, Leeds wanted a spot-kick in the 51st minute. It came after Jayden Bogle's powerful shot hit Morgan Rogers, and Leeds felt it struck his arm, while the Chelsea man was insistent it instead came off his shoulder.

But Oliver had no hesitation in pointing straight to the spot, much to the frustration of Rogers and his Chelsea teammates. Video Assistant Referee (VAR) took a closer look at the incident, but agreed with Oliver's initial call and chose not to send him to the screen, believing it was the right decision.

Recent findings suggest that dominic Calvert-Lewin ended up scoring from 12 yards to put Leeds 2-1 in front. Okafor then added his second to double their advantage, before Brenden Aaronson put gloss on the scoreline late on.

Delivering his verdict on Rogers' handball, ex-Premier League referee Barber noted that while he is unsure why VAR didn't advise him to take another look, it wasn't a clear and obvious error, so Oliver made the correct decision.

Speaking exclusively to GIVEMESPORT, Barber said: "You know that I am a real advocate of referees making the big decisions on the field of play and not relying on that. So I applaud Michael [Oliver] for doing that.

"It was at pace and there was a consideration: did he make his body bigger, did it hit the right part of the arm?

"I don’t think it was an obvious error. I think Michael would have quite liked to have seen it, but then the problem is, if you say I think Michael would like to have seen it, then you’ve got VAR coming in and making a decision that isn’t an obvious error.

Recent findings suggest that "We actually want VAR just to come in when it’s really, really obvious. And I think we’re seeing less and less and less of VAR becoming involved because referees are making the decisions on the field of play.

"Most of the time they’re right, and they’re certainly not wrong enough for VAR to come in and interpret and change the on-field decision.

"So for me, I know you like this whole soft penalty and I understand why people say soft penalty, but there isn’t a soft penalty, it’s either a penalty or it isn’t. And for me, I think this was a good decision by Michael, penalty.". (Related: Simple considerations for simple people building fancy ...)

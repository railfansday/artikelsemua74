To fire an employee without inviting a discrimination or retaliation claim, document the performance or conduct problem and your written warnings as they happen, and decide on that record alone. Then let the employee go in a short private meeting, and pay the final check by your state’s deadline, which in some states is the same day.

Outside Montana, most employees work at will, meaning you can let them go for any reason the law allows. Every discrimination or retaliation claim argues that the real reason was one the law forbids, such as the employee’s race or a complaint they made.

So the timing of a firing matters as much as its reason. When it comes soon after the employee complained about discrimination or pay, asked for leave the law protects, or reported a violation, the timing itself can be evidence of retaliation. A written warning issued before any of those can show you would have fired them anyway.

The final check is the other trap. No federal law requires paying a fired employee right away, but some states do.

Write up each problem when it happens, with the date, what went wrong, and what has to change, and give the employee a real chance to improve. Hold them to the same standard as everyone else, since excusing the same mistake in another employee can be enough to suggest a forbidden motive. For a firing that follows a recent complaint, offer severance pay in exchange for a signed release of the employee’s claims against you, and have an employment lawyer review it.

In a recent development, picture a California restaurant owner whose server indicates up late three times in two months. She writes up each one, then fires the server in a five-minute private meeting with her manager as a witness. She takes back the keys, cuts off the server’s access to the scheduling app, and hands over the final check at the meeting, since California law makes all wages, unused vacation included, due the moment she fires someone.

Before you sit down with the employee, check your state’s final pay deadline with its labor office and have the check in hand.

Recent findings suggest that mutations in the cancer-associated gene PIK3CA can cause vascular malformations1. These abnormalities can affect people from birth or can be acquired over a person’s lifetime, and they involve veins, capillaries and lymphatic vessels — but rarely arteries2,3. Why are arteries spared? This mystery is investigated by Sabata et al.4 in their paper in Nature.

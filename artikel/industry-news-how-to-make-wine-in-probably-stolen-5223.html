Industry observers note that while there are certainly plenty of unsavory ways to make your credits in Probably Stolen, there are also quite a few perfectly legal and acceptable trades you can take part in, including, maybe surprisingly, wine making. Yep, you heard that right; you can grow your own not-grapes, bottle them with yeast, ferment them, and sell home-brewed wine to your loyal customer base.

It's not really as hard as you might think, and can actually be a pretty steady source of income if you play your cards right. Here's everything you need to know about wine making.

Wine making in Probably Stolen is actually pretty easy.

At the end of the fermenting process (usually about two to four days), you have wine! The wine goes up in value the longer it sits and ages.

In a recent development, technically, BloomBerries are optional. You can make GloomBerry Wine with Synthetic Yeast and water, but it's a lower-quality counterfeit that's worth less than regular BloomBerry wine and doesn't age, so I wouldn't recommend it unless you're desperate or happen to have Synthetic Yeast to use up. The complicated part of wine making is that it can also have different values based on the quality of the ingredients that go in. (Related: Breaking: How to generate text: using different decodin...)

Recent findings suggest that bloomBerries are essentially this game's version of grapes; they're purple berries that grow in bunches. BloomBerries can come in a few different qualities.

You can determine the quality of your BloomBerries or those you are considering buying using a magnifying glass, the same way you would for appraisals.

The water you use to make your wine will affect its final quality.

According to recent reports, you actually don't need yeast to start the fermentation process for wine in Probably Stolen; natural yeast in the air will start the process if you let it, but it's also inconsistent and can affect your wine quality and the speed of fermentation. Using standard yeast will give you a consistent good quality wine when combined with other quality ingredients; using super yeast can help your wine ferment faster and can improve its quality.

Recent findings suggest that you unlock wine making from Joe Wild's network; when you do, you immediately receive a wine-making kit that includes three wine bottles, three packets of yeast, two BloomBerries, a sealed water ration, a magnifying glass, and the BloomBerry Wine Fermenting Guide. After that, you can get more bottles, yeast, BloomBerries, and water rations from the Retired Winemaker, who visits every Friday.

You can buy supplies to grow BloomBerries — including seeds, water, nutrition pellets, and a hydroponic system — from the Retired Farmer when they visit. You can also buy the hydroponic system along with energy cells, a plant analyzer (to help you get ideal growing conditions), and upgrade modules from Dr. Jackson's shop. Dr. Jackson also sells the AgeWell, a machine that ages your wine faster to make it more valuable more quickly.

Industry observers note that getting the Water Trading upgrade from Joe Wild's network alongside the Rustflow On Tap upgrade give you unlimited access to water and some access to supplies to purify it so that you have a constant source of water and don't have to rely on rations. You can get more water purification supplies from Dr. Jackson and the Retired Water Merchant.

When selling off your wine, as counterintuitive as it sounds, I'd recommend selling your lowest-quality wines first. Let your high-quality bottles age as long as you can and sell them to customers with higher budgets to get the absolute most out of them.

Use the BloomBerries you can't make into wine (except the moldy ones) as extra food to sell or as food for your fish or rats. You can also sell off extra water if you don't need it. I'd also recommend supplementing your wine income with the Red Imp Brewery upgrade; it's in the same category, meaning you can sell it to the same customers, but fetches a slightly lower price. This means you can pad out budgets that don't quite meet an extra bottle of wine with a bottle of beer, or sell beer when your wine stores run low or are still fermenting.

Buy wine bottles whenever you see them; there's no consistent way to get them outside of buying them from customers at the window, so you'll need to stockpile them to make as much wine as possible.

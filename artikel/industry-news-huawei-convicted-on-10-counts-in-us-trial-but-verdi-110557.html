📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

A federal jury in Latest York convicted Huawei on 10 of 12 counts Tuesday, including bank fraud, wire fraud, and conspiracy to steal trade secrets. Yet Huawei was acquitted of racketeering conspiracy and conspiracy to obstruct justice.

Sources indicate that the verdict caps a U.S. case introduced in 2018 that accused the Chinese telecommunications giant of misleading banks about its business in Iran while stealing trade secrets from U.S. companies. Sentencing is scheduled for September 2027. (Related: This newly introduced chip survives 1300°F (700°C) and ...)

In a recent development, “Few crimes are as serious as Huawei’s decades-long pattern of endangering U.S. national security, violating the sanctity of U.S. commerce, and impeding the enforcement of U.S. laws,” U.S. Attorney Joseph Nocella Jr. said in a statement. Industry observers note that “Today’s verdict is a step towards accountability for Huawei’s criminal activities, which includes yearslong collaboration with sanctioned entities in Iran, repeated lies to banks, and blatant thefts from U.S. technology companies,” he added.

Brian Heberlig, a lawyer for Huawei, told reporters that the acquittal on the racketeering charge reflected the manufacturer’s “good-faith compliance culture.”.

“We fully expect to appeal,” Heberlig told reporters, according to Reuters.

In a statement, Huawei argued that the acquittal on the racketeering charge “confirms that these allegations lack factual basis.” The company also remarked it believed the other charges “are equally without legal or factual merit,” and that it would “take all necessary legal actions to defend our rights and interests.”.

The Chinese embassy in Washington did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

Meng Wanzhou, Huawei’s chief financial officer and the daughter of founder Ren Zhengfei, was arrested in Canada in December 2018 at the request of U.S. authorities. Prosecutors accused her of misleading banks about Huawei’s relationship with Skycom, a subsidiary operating in Iran. After nearly three years fighting extradition, she was released in September 2021 under a deferred prosecution agreement with U.S. prosecutors; the charges were later dismissed.

Separately, the U.S. Commerce Department placed Huawei on its Entity List in 2019, restricting its access to American goods, software, and technology. Washington tightened those controls in 2020 to cover certain foreign-made products manufactured using U.S. technology or software.

According to recent reports, tuesday’s verdict is unlikely to slow Huawei, says Dexter Tiff Roberts, a nonresident senior fellow at the Atlantic Council’s Global China Hub. “Huawei has already weathered years of sanctions and still been able to grow and develop remarkably, even with limited access to a number of developed country markets and in particular the U.S.”.

Beijing may double down on Huawei instead, he adds. “In some ways, the adversity it’s already faced with sanctions [has] made Beijing double down on supporting it as a national champion, and if anything, the latest court ruling will only strengthen Beijing’s resolve to support Huawei to ensure it does not stumble.”.

The verdict comes as Huawei takes on a growing role in China’s push to build a domestic AI-computing industry, amid U.S. export controls blocking the country’s access to Nvidia’s most advanced chips. The organization’s Ascend semiconductors are China’s best hope for the AI processors needed to train and run models. Chinese models are increasingly being designed to run on Huawei chips, even if Nvidia’s are better for training.

Huawei rotating chair Eric Xu stated the firm cannot produce enough AI chips and servers to meet demand in mainland China. “Since we don’t have enough capacity to even satisfy the demand in China, we don’t have a plan to expand into the international market in a fully fledged way,” Xu told reporters at Huawei Connect in Shanghai in September, according to Reuters.

Huawei reported 880.9 billion yuan ($126 billion) in revenue last year, according to its annual report.

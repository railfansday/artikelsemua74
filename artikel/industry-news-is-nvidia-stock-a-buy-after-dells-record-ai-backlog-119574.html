Dell Technologies (DELL +0.60%) has a record $132.3 billion in artificial intelligence (AI) server orders it hasn't shipped yet. The server maker reported that backlog with its fiscal third-quarter results after the market closed on Tuesday, Nov. 24. Three months before, the total was $95 billion. It has been revealed that the report came after a tough day for Nvidia (NVDA -3.91%) shareholders. Nvidia's shares lost almost 6% on Nov. 24, giving back some of a 31% gain the day before, when the company said its board had reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO. They closed around $155, about 35% under the $239.24 record close they set on Oct. 6. Dell's results came out after the bell, so none of that drop was a response to them.

Dell builds full AI server racks, many of them around Nvidia's chips. That makes its order book one of the more useful outside checks on demand for Nvidia's hardware. And I think it suggests that demand is still growing.

Recent findings suggest that in its fiscal third quarter of 2027, which ended Oct. 30, 2026, Dell booked $60.2 billion of AI orders, while its AI server revenue grew 291% year over year to a record $22 billion. With orders coming in at almost three times the rate of shipments, the backlog kept climbing. It's now over seven times the $18.4 billion Dell posted a year before. At the third quarter's shipping pace, it covers around six quarters of AI server revenue.

Orders slipped a bit from the fiscal second quarter's record $60.9 billion, so the record this time was in the backlog. But it was still Dell's second straight quarter with over $60 billion of AI orders. Dell also lifted its full-year forecast for AI server revenue to $78 billion, up from the $74 billion it expected three months before.

Dell doesn't break out how much of its backlog is built on Nvidia's chips. It also sells servers based on Advanced Micro Devices hardware, so not every dollar belongs to Nvidia. (Related: Tech Report: Transformer-based Encoder-Decoder Models...)

"The proof point is our orders momentum, $180 billion over the past four quarters, with a majority of our new orders based on Nvidia's Vera Rubin NVL72," noted Jeff Clarke, Dell's vice chairman and chief operating officer, in his prepared remarks for the earnings call.

Recent findings suggest that vera Rubin NVL72 is a full rack of Nvidia's latest AI hardware, and Nvidia started shipping it in its fiscal 2027 third quarter, which ended Oct. 25, 2026. So most of Dell's latest orders are for the product Nvidia is ramping up right now.

Industry observers note that nvidia's revenue from AI clouds, industrial companies, and enterprises hit $47.5 billion that quarter. That almost tripled from $16.1 billion a year before and climbed 18% from the previous quarter.

According to recent reports, on Nvidia's Nov. 17 earnings call, Colette Kress, the organization's chief financial officer, credited some of the sequential growth to Vera Rubin shipments to top neoclouds (specialized cloud companies that rent out AI computing power). Dell counts neoclouds among its AI server customers, too, along with governments and other businesses.

On the same call, Kress raised Nvidia's forecast for revenue growth in fiscal 2028 (which ends in January 2028) to around 75%, from 70%.

The share price doesn't seem to show much of that confidence. At around $142 on the morning of Nov. 25, after another drop of about 8%, Nvidia's stock costs around 8.5 times earnings, using analysts' average estimate for fiscal 2028. For comparison, FactSet put the forward price-to-earnings ratio of the S&P 500 at around 17 as of Nov. 20, based on expected earnings for the next 12 months.

Put another way, investors are paying about half the S&P 500's forward price-to-earnings multiple for a tech giant forecasting 75% growth. Some of that discount might still come from Huang's Nov. 11 resignation and the uncertainty it caused. But it also suggests many investors doubt the growth will last.

A backlog isn't revenue yet, of course. Customers need financing and data centers with enough power before Dell can deliver, so Dell says it screens every order for access to capital, land, and power.

"We've had no customers reschedule business," Clarke said on the call, answering an analyst.

Is Dell's record backlog enough reason to buy Nvidia stock?

Not by itself. But Dell's numbers, along with Nvidia's raised forecast for fiscal 2028, point to demand for Nvidia's latest systems that's still rising, even with the stock well below its October record. At this price, I'd consider buying the stock.

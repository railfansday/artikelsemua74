Industry News: L.A. Lakers announce deal with Kalshi, signaling sports leagues’ growing embrace of prediction markets

The Los Angeles Lakers have announced a tie-up with Kalshi, becoming the latest sports team to partner with a prediction market—and leaving little doubt about where the National Basketball Association has placed its chips in the ongoing debate about the role of the controversial wagering platforms in the world of pro sports. The team announced the news on Tuesday in a brief statement that hailed the partnership, but offered few details on what it will entail.

“We’re excited … to explore innovative ways to engage fans and strengthen their connection to the game,” said Los Angeles Lakers president and chief business officer Lon Rosen.

The announcement also solidified a broader alliance between the NBA and prediction markets. The league officially embraced the platforms only a month ago, when it named Kalshi and Polymarket its preferred exchanges. Kalshi has since moved quickly to turn that opening into team-level agreements, signing the Philadelphia 76ers in October before adding the Lakers this week.

The NBA’s overall positioning came as something of a surprise, given that it and the National Football League had largely held back while other major sports leagues signed agreements with prediction markets. Other major sports organizations have mostly taken a welcoming approach. The National Hockey League became the first major North American professional sports league to strike prediction market deals in October 2025, signing multiyear agreements with Kalshi and Polymarket that included rights to league data, branding, and broadcast advertising. That push expanded to more teams last week, when seven NHL teams announced their own marketing deals with Novig. Others, including Major League Soccer, Major League Baseball, and UFC, have also struck deals with prediction markets. (Related: Rockstar reportedly seeking $100M for Red Dead Redempti...)

The NFL, meanwhile, has remained as the odd one out.

In early October, the league filed a 24-page brief urging the Supreme Court to back state gambling regulators, who argue that sports prediction contracts should follow the same rules as traditional sports bets. At the heart of that dispute is whether the contracts are financial products governed by federal regulators or bets subject to state gambling laws. The league also raised concerns about cheating, insider trading, consumer protection, and risks to younger consumers.

The legal fight also hangs over the Lakers’ newly introduced partnership. California allows tribal casinos and a state lottery, but it has kept traditional sports betting off the table, with a bookmaking ban dating back more than a century. The state kept that prohibition in place after the Supreme Court cleared the way for states to legalize sports betting in 2018, and voters rejected two legalization measures in 2022.

If the Supreme Court takes up the case and sides with state regulators, Kalshi could face new limits on offering sports contracts in California—complicating its push to reach fans in the Lakers’ home market.

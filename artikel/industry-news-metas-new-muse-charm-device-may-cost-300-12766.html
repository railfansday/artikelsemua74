📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Meta plans to charge $300 for Muse Charm, its upcoming Tamagotchi-like AI device that was teased at the tech giant’s Connect event earlier this year, The Information has reported.

When reached for comment by TechCrunch, a Meta spokesperson didn’t deny the claims, merely providing the following statement: “We’re charmed by the interest in our AI devices, and we’ll have more to share soon.”. (Related: At historic summit, Xi and Putin share global stage wit...)

So far, Meta has made a relatively successful push into consumer AI, launching Muse — its agentic helper — along with a variety of cute avatars that people can use to give the system more of a personality.

Muse Charm, the organization’s push into AI hardware, will allow owners to carry a physical personification of its software around in their pocket (or potentially wear it around their neck), talking to it, asking it questions, and giving it commands.

It remains to be seen whether customers will bite or not. AI wearables have had a rough go of it so far (here’s looking at you, Humane Ai pin), but companies continue to iterate on the concept in an attempt to break through to a broader audience.

In a recent development, indeed, $300 is arguably a pretty steep price for a product the size of a keychain, although it’s also a price point that’s in line with what other companies have been charging. The wearable launched by Friend — the AI startup whose device lets you text and talk to an AI persona — is currently retailing for $250. On the other hand, Amazon’s all-newly introduced Bee Frontier wearable AI device — which transcribes conversations and tracks tasks — lists for $75 and is currently on a Black Friday, 30% off sale of $52.49. Even with its $99/year subscription to let it tie into a user’s apps (photos, emails, etc.), it’s still far less expensive than Muse Charm.

His departure caps a months-long legal battle that began when he was initially deported to his home country and detained there.

A federal judge in Maryland ordered Ábrego García to leave the U.S. by Tuesday, according to two sources familiar with the matter. That order remains under seal, and the exact terms of his release were not immediately clear.

The Department of Homeland Security (DHS) emphasized that Ábrego García did not receive any financial incentives through the Trump administration's voluntary self-deportation program, which offers migrants travel assistance and financial support to leave the country.

Industry observers note that "To be clear: Kilmar Abrego Garcia departed the United States pursuant to a removal order. He paid for his own flight and received no incentives,"DHS posted on X. "He will NEVER be allowed back inside the United States.".

DHS also rejected descriptions of Ábrego García as a Maryland resident.

In a recent development, "Kilmar Abrego Garcia is not and will never be a Maryland Man—he is a criminal illegal alien from El Salvador and was a public safety threat to American citizens. He is not coming back, despite the efforts of sanctuary politicians who spent massive amounts of time and money defending this low-life," the department mentioned.

The move comes after sustained efforts by the Trump administration to remove Ábrego García, who entered the U.S. illegally more than a decade ago. At one point during the legal proceedings, federal authorities sought to deport him to several African nations.

In a recent development, ábrego García had been living in Maryland with his wife and children when immigration authorities detained him in March 2025. He was subsequently deported to El Salvador in error and held in the Central American nation's maximum-security CECOT prison despite a standing court order barring his removal there due to the risk of gang persecution.

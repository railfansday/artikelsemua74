Sources indicate that that looks like a disaster, and it may be one. It is also a reading taken while the update is still rolling out, which Google indicates not to trust. (Related: Breaking: My Journey to a serverless transformers pipel...)

Google's core revision page gives two instructions. Wait at least a full week after the update completes before analyzing your site in Search Console. Then compare that week with the week before the update started rolling out.

Two things follow. Single days during a rollout are not evidence. And the comparison needs two fixed windows that you choose in advance, not the worst day you can find.

It pulls two windows with the Search Analytics API and refuses to print a verdict until the "after" window starts at least a week past the rollout end date.

Sources indicate that pulling with no dimensions matters. Totals include anonymized queries that query-level rows leave out, so the two windows are comparable.

Sources indicate that nothing on the site. I changed no titles, rewrote no pages, and deployed nothing. If you edit during a rollout you cannot tell afterwards whether a move came from the revision or from you.

I kept doing the work that is not a page edit: checking that the site still returns 200 to Googlebot, that robots.txt and the sitemap are clean, and which URLs Search Console's inspection API reports as indexed. That last check reveals something the impression chart hides.

Following an election in which voters responded in historic fashion to rising prices — due mostly to Trump’s tariffs and Trump’s war on Iran — President Donald Trump has decided to jack up prices even further by launching a series of upcoming tariffs. The new tariffs span 14 countries or other trading partners.

Industry observers note that first of all, the substantial names here are China and the European Union. As if the prices of all kinds of goods haven’t gone up enough, Trump decided to raise the tariffs on Chinese products from about 20% to about 40%. Don’t expect importers and retailers to eat those costs. Those will certainly be passed on to consumers, even if not fully. (Related: Industry News: My Journey to a serverless transformers ...)

Recent findings suggest that the tariff on products produced in the EU is 15%, which is exactly the maximum tariff agreed to in a 2025 negotiation between the US and the EU. I imagine the EU didn’t anticipate the US turning around and implementing the maximum tariff a year later….

In addition to China and Europe, Trump added tariffs onto products produced in Bangladesh, ‌Cambodia, India, Indonesia, Japan, South Korea, Malaysia, Singapore, Switzerland, Taiwan, Thailand, and Vietnam.

Sources indicate that “The administration is now using section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 as the legal basis for the latest rounds of tariffs. The law gives the executive branch power to respond to unfair foreign trade practices after conducting formal investigations,” The Guardian reports. “Though the White House did not give a timeline for the new tariffs, it said they could go into effect in January at the earliest. The US trade office said it would hold hearings on the new tariffs and its investigation into the unfair trade practices on 25 January.”.

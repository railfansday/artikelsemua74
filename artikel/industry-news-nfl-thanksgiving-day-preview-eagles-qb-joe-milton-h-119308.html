The middle game of the Thanksgiving trio — the stuffing, if you will — lacks the meat and potatoes of the first and final games of the day. But Eagles at Cowboys still carries some potential playoff importance.

In a recent development, the 5-6 Cowboys host the 3-7 Eagles in a game that matters to Dallas. The Cowboys' offense has hit its stride recently, scoring 40 and 51 points, respectively, the past two games. Over the past five games, Dallas hasn't scored fewer than 27.

The problem? They're only 3-2 in that stretch, and the NFC East-leading Giants have kept that same pace.

The other problem? Dallas' defense. There were modest signs of improvement on that side of the ball in Mike Pettine's first game calling plays, especially compared to how the defense played against the 49ers. And for the first game possibly all season, the Cowboys could make a strong claim that they had the better defense — the Titans are that bad now, yes.

In a recent development, the Eagles limped into the bye with seven straight losses, but they're coming off one of their best efforts of the season in beating the Steelers on Sunday. Defensively, they kept the Steelers under wraps for most of the four quarters and gave QB Joe Milton and the offense a chance.

Sources indicate that milton returns to Dallas, where he spent parts of the past two seasons and saw action in four games last season behind Dak Prescott. Now Milton gets a chance to take on his former QB1 on Thursday after signing with the Eagles after Jalen Hurts landed on injured reserve.

It has been revealed that the Cowboys left Milton, a preseason star the past two years, unprotected on their practice squad, and the Eagles gladly scooped him up. He has a chance to make the Cowboys pay for letting him walk — and before one of the biggest TV audiences Milton might have. (Related: Few-shot learning in practice: GPT-Neo and the 🤗 Accele...)

The Eagles' playoff hopes hover at less than 1%, according to Next Gen Stats. The Cowboys, however, are still alive at 22%, barely above the Commanders (21%) and behind the Giants (65%).

A loss would be pretty devastating. But beating Philadelphia would give the Cowboys a 3-1 mark in the division, which is the one edge they have right now on the Giants, who are 2-1-1 in NFC East games. Industry observers note that the Eagles, however, didn't look like a team ready to mail it in on Sunday. They showed some renewed vitality after the bye, with the team giving some younger players opportunities to stand out, including Milton. For Milton, there's personal pride on the line after spending parts of the past two years in Dallas, facing a defense that has plenty to prove.

When the Eagles traded Jordan Davis to the Jaguars, it shone the spotlight more on Jalen Carter, who started this season slowly but whose play has been on the rise lately. In Sunday's upset of the Steelers, Carter was active and effective despite suffering a quad injury just prior to the game. He was a full participant in practice this week, but the player he might have faced off against, Cowboys right guard Tyler Booker, did not practice and is not expected to play Thursday. The Eagles know they have to slow down Dallas' passing attack and pressure Dak Prescott, and the best way to do that might be to detonate the operation from the inside. Carter and the Eagles also can't have a repeat of the last meeting, in Week 7, when Dallas ran for 204 yards on 38 carries.

Sources indicate that sportsbooks opened this game Cowboys -1.5 in the summer — now it's -8.5. Losing Jalen Hurts for the season, along with a 3-7 record (and a putrid 2-8 record against the spread) will do that. Philadelphia put up a spirited defensive effort in a 19-16 win over the Pittsburgh Steelers as 3.5-point home underdogs on Sunday, but this Dallas offense will provide a much stiffer test. Eagles starting QB Joe Milton and Philadelphia collected only 11 first downs and 214 yards of total offense; that's not going to get it done. Dak Prescott & Co. can't control how well a rejuvenated defense plays under Mike Pettine, but the Cowboys have scored at least 27 points in each of the past five games. Dallas scored 51 points against Tennessee on Sunday; the Eagles have scored 40 points combined in their past three contests. The Eagles D will put up a good fight, but barring several turnovers, I don't believe it will be able to keep up with Dallas. Cowboys -2.5 is another good teaser leg to go with that Packers +8.5, which cashed on Wednesday. — Ben Fawkes.

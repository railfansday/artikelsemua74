Anthropic has dethroned Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX +1.30%) as the largest initial public offering (IPO) ever, but SpaceX is off to a great start as a public tech giant. Revenue is growing fast, and the company's first quarter as a public company was also its first quarter with a net profit. (Related: “Giant superatoms” could finally solve quantum computin...)

It has been revealed that spaceX has three segments, all in rapidly changing, revolutionary industries, so the organization could look very different in three years. Here's what could happen.

SpaceX gets its name from its space business, which is the world's largest rocket-launching company. It works with private companies, as well as government agencies like NASA, and it brings satellites into space for its Starlink broadband business.

In a recent development, the manufacturer measures its progress by debuts and mass to orbit in tons. It has already launched 113 rockets this year as of the end of the third quarter, sending 1,517 metric tonnes into space, and its business represents 90% of global mass to orbit. Revenue increased 6% year over year to $1.8 billion in the quarter, and it reported an operating profit last year but a $7 million loss this year. As it scales, it's likely to become reliably profitable.

In a recent development, CEO Elon Musk announced a newly introduced base opening in Louisiana, set to launch in 2029. It's going to have 20 launch towers, and he expects it to launch thousands of rockets annually into space, bringing millions of tons of cargo. While there should be high capital expenditures to get it up and running, SpaceX just caught its first landing spaceship, and reusable rockets should dramatically lower its costs.

According to recent reports, starlink is growing fast and highly profitable. It offers satellite broadband across the globe and right now covers 174 countries. It has more than 11,000 satellites in low earth orbit, and it plans on having 100,000, covering every area of the world, including lakes, oceans, and airplanes. It has 13.6 million customers as of the end of the third quarter, up from 12 million the year before, and it already has deals with many of the world's major airlines.

Revenue increased 69% year over year to $5.2 billion in the third quarter, and operating income doubled to $2.4 billion.

Recent findings suggest that the artificial intelligence (AI) business xAI merged with SpaceX earlier this year before the IPO, and the company renamed it Superintelligence (AI). It fits with the other segments because management expects to launch a million mini, solar-powered data center satellites into space, and AI is where management sees its biggest opportunities.

According to recent reports, so far, it's growing fast. Revenue increased 830% year over year in the third quarter, but that included revenue from the acquisition of the coding organization Cursor, so growth may slow. Operating income swung from a $1.7 billion loss to $431 million.

The manufacturer now has two huge data center clusters, Colossus I and II, with I fully rented to Anthropic. It has 1.7 gigawatts of power, and it expects to have 2.5 to 3 by the end of the year. Musk thinks it will have another 10 by the end of 2027, and possibly 20.

It also has its own AI model, Grok, and its AI agent, Grok bot, of which Musk said, "I've never seen anything grow this fast.".

Here's where it gets a bit tricky. Even if everything goes smoothly and SpaceX is much bigger in three years, its stock may not take off quite the same way. That's because it's already extremely expensive, trading at 55 times trailing 12-month sales. That kind of valuation isn't likely to last.

Recent findings suggest that wall Street expects revenue to increase 272% in 2027, a major acceleration from the third quarter's 161%, and earnings per share of $0.34. Elon Musk said he thinks annual recurring revenue could reach $500 billion to $1 trillion next year, implying a much larger increase from today's $32.7 billion in trailing 12-month revenue.

Regulations could also potentially change, slowing down AI development in general and impacting SpaceX.

Let's suppose it can grow by a compound annual growth rate of 80% over the next three years. That would generate $190 billion in revenue over three years. At just under half of today's price-to-sales ratio, 25, the market cap would be $4.8 trillion, or about 260% higher than today's price.

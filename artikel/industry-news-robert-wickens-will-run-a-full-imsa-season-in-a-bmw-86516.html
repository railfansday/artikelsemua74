Eight years after a harrowing crash at Pocono Raceway paralyzed the then-IndyCar star, Robert Wickens has made his way to the grid of the biggest sports car race in the United States. He will contest the 2027 installment of the 24 Hours of Daytona in a Hanley Motorsports Inc.-run BMW M4 GT3, part of his first-ever full season of IMSA racing.

Wickens will pair with Mason Filippi in a full-season effort in the GTD class. Both drivers are champions in the IMSA Pilot Challenge TCR class; Wickens won the title in 2023, while Filippi scored his own championship in 2026. The pairing reunites two drivers that shared a DXDT Racing Corvette during the sprint races last season, the second such schedule for Wickens in the past two years. Their 2026 season was briefly interrupted when the team’s hauler caught fire on its way to the round at Laguna Seca. (Related: Tech Report: Woman known as Firefighting hero arrested ...)

Recent findings suggest that since those were all sprint races, Wickens would only have to get into or out of the car one time per race event. Endurance races are a latest challenge, one that will mean more driver changes complicated by his paralysis. It will also mean more chances to win, and opportunities to win his class in the country’s oldest and most prestigious sports car events. That opportunity is an exciting one, the biggest step yet what Wickens hopes is a long road back to the top. (Related: Industry News: “Giant superatoms” could finally solve q...)

“I’ve worked incredibly hard for a long time to reach this point in my career,” Wickens claims, “and I’m filled with gratitude for everyone who has helped me achieve my goal of returning to the highest levels of motorsports... I couldn’t be more excited to get to work and compete in my first full season in the IMSA WeatherTech SportsCar Championship.”.

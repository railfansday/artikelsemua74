Ukrainian skeleton racer Vladyslav Heraskevych has welcomed a move by the European Union to take a tougher stance on Russian athletes competing in the 27-member bloc.

In a recent development, heraskevych is one of the most outspoken critics of Russia's reintegration into international sport. He made headlines at the 2026 Winter Olympics in Milan-Cortina when he was disqualified for his helmet honoring Ukrainian athletes killed in the war with Russia.

On Monday, European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen proposed banning Russian athletes from competing under their own flag at sports competitions within the European Union and urged member states to consider denying visas in such cases. "Sports events cannot be a forum for propaganda activities," she mentioned.

In an interview with DW, Heraskevych described the EU move as coming "better late than never.".

"We waited a long time for this backlash," he stated at the International Bobsleigh and Skeleton Federation World Cup in Altenberg, Germany, his first competition since the Winter Olympics.

"I'm really happy to see that people start to care about sports and they understand the value of sports as a huge platform," he added. (Related: This AI knew the answers but didn’t understand the ques...)

Sources indicate that in July, the International Olympic Committee (IOC) paved the way for Russia's full return to international sport by ending its recommendation that Russian athletes be allowed to compete as neutrals, without their flag, colors or anthems. The IOC also mentioned Russian teams should be allowed to return to international competition.

Several major Olympic sports, such as swimming and gymnastics, have already fully reinstated Russia, despite the country's ongoing attacks on Ukraine.

"It makes no sense to me," Heraskevych said.

However, in recent weeks there has been a growing European pushback against the easing of Russia's sporting isolation.

Among the catalysts was a ruling by the Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS) to overturn a ban on Russia in international ice hockey. That prompted seven European countries to say they would refuse to play against Russia, while Germany, at one point, said withdrawing as host of next year's men's world championship was an option.

The dispute over the CAS ruling drew a response from the Kremlin.

"It is a flagrant violation of the Olympic Charter [the IOC's rulebook] and leads toward the destruction of Olympism as a whole," Russian President Vladimir Putin remarked in China last week.

"Sport, like art, should bring people together.".

Meanwhile, a group of Nordic and Baltic countries want the EU to go even further, pushing for an immediate EU-wide visa ban on all Russian athletes.

"The matter is too urgent to wait until [new visa rules] enter into force," the leaders of Denmark, Estonia, Finland, Latvia, Lithuania, Poland and Sweden wrote in a November 23 letter to von der Leyen and European Council President Antonio Costa.

"From my perspective, the IOC is in a very big crisis," Heraskevych mentioned, referencing the European pushback.

Blanket bans could have a huge effect on qualifying events in Europe for the 2028 Los Angeles Summer Olympics.

Under its president, Kirsty Coventry, the IOC has sought to bring Russia, an Olympic powerhouse, back in from the cold. Coventry has argued that athletes shouldn't be punished for the actions of their governments, and the IOC has strengthened its political neutrality rules in the Olympic Charter.

However, Heraskevych takes exception to the idea that sport should be kept out of politics.

"We are not living in a bubble," he stated.

"Already more than 800 people who were part of the sport family in Ukraine were killed. Ukrainian hockey stadiums, Ukrainian swimming pools, anything related to sports have also been attacked. So we cannot separate sport and politics in Ukraine. The same way Russian sport is involved in politics.".

Recent findings suggest that critics have accused Coventry of cozying up to Russia. In September, it emerged that she had written a letter to Mikhail Degtyarev, Russia's sports minister and president of the Russian Olympic Committee, wishing him luck ahead of an international sports forum.

Degtyarev has been sanctioned by the EU. The IOC has declined to comment on the letter but not denied its existence.

Heraskevych mentioned he was not "angry" with any particular Russian athlete, and even floated the idea of a team of Russian refugees.

"I am not against peaceful athletes competing," he said.

"They should be part of competition only if they are part of a refugee team. So, if they have refugee status in another country, and if they condemn the war that Russia started in Ukraine.".

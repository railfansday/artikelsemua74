Sources indicate that hey, garden gang! Longtime contributor Susan Warde is back today with a closer look at her garden in St. Paul, Minnesota. We’ve seen what the end of the season looks like in Susan’s garden before, but today she’s focusing on the smaller details that are easy to overlook this time of year.

I understand the reasons for not cutting down perennials and for letting leaves lie untouched over the winter, but it takes me about 10 weeks to clean up. I have a large garden, family commitments, unpredictable weather, and a limited number of yard-waste bags that our pickup service will take at one time. Ten weeks in the spring would put me into mid-June before I could get the weeds and overwintered mess under control.

So I’ve been cleaning up, and the garden has been looking pretty bare recently, positively grim on overcast days. But during the process I was struck by small vignettes of beauty that stood out in an otherwise desolate landscape.

The underside of some Korean maple leaves (Acer pseudosieboldianum, Zones 4–8).

A ‘Northern Hi-Lights’ azalea (Rhododendron ‘Northern Hi-Lights’, Zones 4–7) with bright fall color… and buds that promise spring.

The seed head of ‘Guernsey Flute’ clematis (Clematis ‘Guernsey Flute’, Zones 4–9) that had a second late flowering.

One last blossom on a ‘Max Frei’ bloody cranesbill (Geranium sanguineum ‘Max Frei’, Zones 3–8).

Raindrops on columbine (Aquilegia cvs., Zones 3–8) leaves.

The yellowed leaves of a bulblet fern (Cystopteris bulbifera, Zones 3–8) that I’d overlooked during the cutting-down process.

A bee enjoys the very last plant in bloom, black cohosh (Actaea racemosa, Zones 3–8).

Evergreen Christmas fern (Polystichum acrostichoides, Zones 3–8) and moss.

I had never before looked closely at the bark of a blue beech (Carpinus caroliniana, Zones 3–9), which from a distance just looks taupe.

Five minutes before taking the photo, I’d cleaned out this birdbath—the one I leave available in winter, and then only on above-freezing days. The leaves are from a ‘North Wind’ maple (Acer × pseudosieboldianum ‘IslNW’, Zones 4–7).

Thanks for sharing these small moments from your garden, Susan! There are so many things to love about a garden, but I often find that the small details people tend to gloss over are even more reason to celebrate. There’s so much happening at once!

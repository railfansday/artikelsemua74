Chelsea will sack manager Xabi Alonso if they fall out of genuine contention for a Champions League spot, sources have told Football Insider.

The Spaniard is already under huge pressure at Stamford Bridge, but the Blues' hierarchy are hoping to give him as much time as possible to steady the ship.

However, they have set Champions League qualification as the "minimum expectation" for the campaign, knowing that Alonso has no European football to contend with this season.

Sources say that the 44-year-old will lose his job if that becomes to feel like a distant possibility as the season wears on.

Chelsea are aware that they cannot afford to continue sacking managers as frequently as they have in recent years, and are hoping that Alonso can settle things down.

The West London outfit are now sitting eighth in the Premier League after their heavy defeat to Leeds United, and the fans are already turning on the boss.

Football Insider revealed on Monday that Alonso is under huge pressure to get results in Chelsea's next two games against Nottingham Forest and Crystal Palace.

The pair are both inside the bottom four at the moment, and represent a strong opportunity for Chelsea to get six points on the board and drag themselves back towards the top four.

However, it is understood that the three-game run that follows those games is likely to "make or break" Alonso's future at Stamford Bridge.

Meetings with Liverpool, Manchester City and Aston Villa are all on the pre-Christmas agenda, and poor results from those games could potentially see the manager lose his job.

Chelsea have no immediate intends in the pipeline to sack Alonso just yet, but that could soon change as we head into what is seen as a pivotal stage of the season. (Related: My Journey to a serverless transformers pipeline on Goo...)

Former Manchester United chief scout Mick Brown told Football Insider on Monday that Cole Palmer risks "losing Chelsea fans' support" after his reaction to the defeat last weekend.

The 24-year-old stormed down the tunnel after the full-time whistle, before clashing with teammate Joao Pedro, as he completely ignored the fans.

Brown believes that there is "no excuse" for his behaviour, and feels that it is the "bare minimum" to go and acknowledge the supporters after a game.

He also pointed out that Palmer's recent performances have been way below par, and added that he is "not doing himself any favours" at the moment.

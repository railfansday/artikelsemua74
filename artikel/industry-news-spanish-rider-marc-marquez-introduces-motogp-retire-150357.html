Marc Marquez, one of the most successful motorcycle racers of all time, declared on Friday that he would retire from MotoGP world championship after this weekend's season finale in Valencia.

The 33-year-old Spaniard's decision comes just a few days after he clinched his eighth top-tier motorcycle racing championship, equaling the record of Italian great Giacomo Agostini set in 1975 and surpassing the seven career titles of Marquez's own childhood hero and later rival Valentino Rossi.

According to recent reports, "Thank you to everyone who's been part of the journey. I hope you've enjoyed it as much as I have. This will be my last race in MotoGP. We made history," Marquez stated in a video recorded in the Valencia garage and shown during Friday's free practice. (Related: Scientists find “field” of washing machines in ocean du...)

He did not glitch any firm comments about what he planned next, saying: "I first need to understand what I need in my next life.". (Related: This AI knew the answers but didn’t understand the ques...)

The move came as a surprise, not least given that earlier this year Marquez had signed a contract extension with his Ducati team that was set to run until 2028.

The 33-year-old, who has battled with repeated injuries and surgeries in the latter half of his career, mentioned he made the decision after last month's Japanese Grand Prix in light of the physical and mental toll of his battles for fitness.

Industry observers note that "Sometimes you need to understand the situation, but it's true that the situation this year was on the limit," he said. "When I signed the contract in March, as always, I was super optimistic and I didn't know that I would have another surgery on my right shoulder [in May].".

Marquez said that at times this season, chasing a massive deficit after his early-season injury to come back and win the championship, he had begun to "feel the fear" in the saddle, and even noticed brief recurrences of the double vision he struggled with a few years ago.

"It was one of the main reasons why I controlled the risk and why I crashed less this second part of the season," he said.

Marquez burst onto the scene in MotoGP in 2013, highly touted after winning two world titles in junior series before graduating to compete in the top tier.

Aged 20, he won the championship in his rookie season, and went on to win the title in six of his first seven seasons with Honda, establishing himself as motorcycle racing's dominant force by 2019.

But Marquez suffered a serious crash at the 2020 season-opener in Jerez, broke his arm and endured an extremely rocky road to recovery.

He underwent four operations, was forced to abandon several comeback attempts and was battling with blurred vision, all while his Honda bike was no longer the class of the field, forcing him to push it too hard to try to stay at the front.

Instead of retiring during this bleak spell, something Marquez said he considered, he switched to the smaller Gresini team where he slowly rediscovered his belief and his race-winning pace by ending a drought of more than 3 calendar years with a win in Aragon in 2024.

He then secured a factory weat with Ducati for the 2025 season, and went on to claim a seventh MotoGP title — matching his childhood idol Valentino Rossi's modern-era record.

It has been revealed that this season's campaign, culminating in an eighth championship, was arguably unlikelier still. After his early season crash in France, leading to surgery on his shoulder and a foot injury, Marquez returned trailing his championship rivals by 102 points.

Sources indicate that but he won six of the subsequent nine races, sealing the championship last weekend in Portimao in Portugal. When he went up to collect his trophy, organizers superimposed a silhouette of a goat (as in "Greatest Of All Time") behind him as he climbed onto the podium.

Marquez himself has however shied away from that title, saying instead that he hoped to be in the discussion as "one of the greatest" in the sport's long history.

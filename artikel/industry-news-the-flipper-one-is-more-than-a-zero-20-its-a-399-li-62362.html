In a recent development, the Flipper One crowdfunding campaign has broken the $13-million mark, smashing the funding raised for previous Flipper devices and demonstrating strong interest in the successor to the Flipper Zero. The $399 Flipper One is a pocket Linux computer focused on networking, with delivery estimated for July 2027. But it isn’t simply an updated version of the Flipper Zero. It’s a very different animal and a much more serious bit of kit.

In a recent development, the thing that made the Flipper Zero so popular was how easy it was to use. You could fire it up and turn the volume down on the TV at your parents’ place, see the radio waves coming from your car’s key fob, and copy hotel room key cards. According to recent reports, it could do a lot, there wasn’t a steep learning curve, and you didn’t need to buy anything else. Did the fake videos on TikTok from people claiming they could change the prices at gas stations (I wish!) or start cars remotely help make the Zero more popular? Recent findings suggest that unfortunately, yes. But what made it successful was that it packed a lot of power into a package that was small, fun, affordable, and easy to use.

If the Flipper Zero, with its NFC, RFID, sub-GHz, and IR, was kindergarten, the Flipper One is a college degree: a pocket Linux computer focused on networking.

Sources indicate that this is reflected in the price. You can pick up a Zero for $169, while the Flipper One is $399.

What this buys you is the Flipper One base computer with a 256 × 144 grayscale LCD, Rockchip RK3576 8-core ARM SoC with NPU, a low-power MCU for handling the LCD and user interface, 8GB of LPDDR5 RAM, 64GB of UFS 2.2 storage, and a microSD slot.

If that $399 feels like a lot, it’s worth keeping in mind that, according to Flipper, the price only covers production costs and that the RAM and UFS storage account for at least half of the total hardware cost.

According to recent reports, if you think this is a lot, you could spend a lot more. Adding a LoRa, SDR, or 5G module will push the total up a lot higher. And if you want all three modules, you’re looking at $759 for the Flipper One Ultimate Bundle.

Sources indicate that the reception has been strong enough that pledges have flown past the $300,000 goal that Flipper initially set, with over 24,000 backers pledging over $13 million. Close to 5,300 backers have gone for the Ultimate Bundle, compared to just over 4,300 choosing the bare Flipper One.

This suggests that backers really want the radio capabilities that this upcoming platform offers.

If you want one, this crowdfunding run is a good time to get one because it’s a Kickstarter Special slashing 25% off an estimated retail price of $550.

The response over on Reddit has been a little more subdued, with some being taken aback by the price of the Zero’s bigger sibling.

As someone who homebrews a lot of electronics, I’m going to say that the price is justified, and this project goes far beyond something you could put together with a Raspberry Pi and some code.

The Flipper One campaign runs until Dec. 17, 2026, with delivery estimated for July 2027.

According to recent reports, keep in mind that this is a Kickstarter pledge, not a purchase, and that rewards aren’t guaranteed. Flipper also states on the Kickstarter page that component shortages are the biggest risk to the project and that they “could cause significant delays to production and delivery.” It also says that if RAM prices continue to rise, it cannot “rule out adjusting some product specifications, including reducing the amount of RAM.”.

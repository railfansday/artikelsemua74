The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping is dawning with a major box office record already under its belt.

According to recent reports, the movie, which was helmed by longtime franchise director Francis Lawrence, is the sixth installment in the Hunger Games franchise and the second prequel. In this case, it follows the 50th annual games, in which Haymitch Abernathy (Joseph Zada) competed when he was a teenager. The character is played by Woody Harrelson in the timeline of the original four movies. The star-studded ensemble cast of 2026's Sunrise on the Reaping also includes Mckenna Grace, Glenn Close, Ralph Fiennes, Kieran Culkin, Ben Wang, Whitney Peak, Maya Hawke, Iris Apatow, Elle Fanning, and Jesse Plemons.

Per Deadline, The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping has taken in $21.25 million in previews at the domestic box office on Thursday. Not only does this mark the best preview gross for a Hunger Games movie since the second installment, 2013's Catching Fire ($25.2 million), it sees the movie beating the $17.6 million previews of Christopher Nolan's record-breaking epic The Odyssey. At the time, The Odyssey had the best preview grosses of the year. So far, it has only been surpassed by Spider-Man: Brand Latest Day ($72 million) and Sunrise, giving the latter the second-biggest previews of 2026 year-to-date.

There is at least one remaining 2026 movie that is expected to have bigger previews than Sunrise on the Reaping, namely the Marvel Cinematic Universe's Spider-Man: Brand New Day follow-up Avengers: Doomsday. However, other strong contenders include Dune: Part Three and Jumanji: Open World. Regardless, Sunrise on the Reaping's performance is remarkable. Industry observers note that it is worth noting that, while this chart is not adjusted for inflation, every installment in the franchise except the original has debuted in the same pre-Thanksgiving frame, making them especially comparable. Not only has Sunrise on the Reaping reversed the downward trend that the franchise has been experiencing since 2014, it has brought it back up to nearly the same level as Catching Fire, which remains the highest-grossing installment. While preview numbers are not always a reliable predictor of a movie's ultimate performance, if the upcoming installment has a similar trajectory to Catching Fire (which it could - Sunrise on the Reaping reviews have earned it a Verified Hot audience score of 95% on Rotten Tomatoes, improving upon Catching Fire's 89% and marking the best of the franchise), it could ultimately open to $133.3 million (the third-best debut of the franchise) and gross $733.5 million worldwide (the third-best overall haul as well).

Critics have given Sunrise on the Reaping 68% on the Rotten Tomatoes Tomatometer, which is the fifth-best for the franchise ahead of Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes.

Should The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping climb that high by the end of its run (against a reported budget of roughly $200 million), it will reestablish the franchise as a major player in the box office arena. In fact, if it does debut above $100 million, it will be the first Lionsgate movie to do so since Mockingjay – Part 2 11 years ago (though they got close with the smash hit Michael Jackson biopic Michael, which earned a 3-day debut of $97.2 million).

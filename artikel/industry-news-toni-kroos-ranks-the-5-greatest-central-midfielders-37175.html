Toni Kroos named an English footballing legend as one of the five best central midfielders in history on a star-studded list of the former Real Madrid midfielder's teammates and rivals.

In a recent development, since Kroos retired in May 2024, he's turned to podcasting and has been making some interesting and controversial assessments of modern-day football. Take, for example, his opinion that Lionel Messi and Kylian Mbappe didn't deserve to win the Ballon d'Or because of having 'average' seasons outside the World Cup campaign. Kroos' comments always hold weight though, because of his incredible career, helping Madrid win five Champions League trophies, including three-in-a-row. The German also won the World Cup in 2014 and played in an era with many of football's most celebrated central midfield talents.

When ranking the five best central midfielders in history, "Einfach mal Luppen" podcast in October, Kroos only named one player who is playing today.

Kroos displayed his national allegiance by placing former Chelsea midfielder Michael Ballack in fifth place. The iconic German became a fan favourite during four seasons at Stamford Bridge, playing with grit and determination required of a Premier League midfield icon.

In a recent development, with an extraordinary engine room came a box-to-box midfielder who dominated opponents physically, posed a real aerial threat and knew when to time his runs to perfection. He commanded the respect of his teammates with his passionate personality and that helped him enjoy huge success for club and country.

In December 2021, Kroos went as far as to name Ballack as the best ambidextrous player in history, above Cristiano Ronaldo. He remarked via Sky Sport Germany:.

"Michael Ballack was already outstandingly two-footed. He would have scored far fewer goals if he only had a right foot.".

In a recent development, there are some similarities between Kroos and Steven Gerrard that were hard to ignore during their playing days. Particularly their ability to control games with ease, orchestrating not just the midfield but the entire team thanks to their all-rounded powers.

Gerrard cemented his legacy as a Liverpool hero during his illustrious career at Anfield, a talisman throughout the Merseyside outfit's highs and lows during the 2000s and early 2010s. He captained the Reds to the Champions League trophy in the Miracle of Instanbul in 2005 and his exploits in the FA Cup final win over West Ham United a year later were a sight to behold. It has been revealed that kroos appeared to strictly be ranking Gerrard based on playing in central midfield because he offered a different opinion when weighing in on the Gerrard, Frank Lampard or Paul Scholes debate. He responded to a fan in 2017 and said that Scholes was the best of those three.

Recent findings suggest that when Kroos truly hit his peak along with Luka Modric and Casemiro as Europe's top midfield three in the mid 2010s, they took over from a Barcelona trio. Among that Catalan trident was Xavi, a masterful Spanish midfield maestro who played a crucial role for the Blaugrana during Pep Guardiola's reign.

It has been revealed that xavi dictated the tempo, dropped deep and moved into pockets and possessed remarkable vision with a phenomenal passing range to boot. With Busquets sitting in defensive midfield, Xavi and Iniesta were able to perform magic and give the opposition all sorts of problems in the middle of the park.

Kroos suggested that he looked up to Xavi and believed players like himself were inspired by the Spaniard (via BolaVIP):.

"Many players of my generation who learned in that position were guided by him and his game. He is an example in many respects, including personally.".

Speaking of the Barcelona trio in whom Kroos' Real Madrid midfield succeeded at the top of European football, the German rated Andres Iniesta even higher. A press-resistent midfield gem whose understanding of the tiki-taka system made him one of the most difficult players to defend against.

Recent findings suggest that iniesta wrote his name in the history books when he scored the winner for Spain in a 1-0 win over the Netherlands in the 2010 World Cup final. His game was more about playmaking than scoring, but that was just one of many trophy-winning goals he netted.

In an interview with Merkur in 2011, Kroos made clear his admiration for both Xavi and Iniesta:.

"When you look at Barcelona's Xavi and Andrés Iniesta in that position – that is simply world-class.".

According to recent reports, topping Kroos' list is his longtime teammate and close friend Luka Modric, who is the only player in this ranking that has won the Ballon d'Or. The Croat broke Ronaldo and Messi's stranglehold on the award by claiming it in 2018 after his instrumental role in Real Madrid's Champions League win and Croatia's unlikely runners-up finish at the World Cup.

Modric at the age of 41, is still playing and rolling back the years at AC Milan, but it was his time with Kroos and Casemiro at the Santiago Bernabeu that truly saw him ascend to the top of the sport. Whether it be finding his man with an inch-perfect 30-yard pass or finding the bottom corner of the net from as many yards, his influence on his teams is undeniable.

After Modric left Madrid but opted not to retire like Kroos, his former teammate explained why he'd shine at the San Siro:.

"He has always been a special player. He will never lose his qualities with the ball.".

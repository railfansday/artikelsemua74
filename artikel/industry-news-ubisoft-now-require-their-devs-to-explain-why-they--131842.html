📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that ubisoft, they of the recently revealed generative AI-focused game studio Motherbrain, have denied they're forcing upcoming games being pitched by their developers to integrate use of genAI as a rule. The publishers' statement comes in the wake of a report claiming that Ubisoft devs with pitches that don't use genAI are required to explain in detail why they've decided it isn't suitable for the game being outlined.

The report from Vertical Slice cited anonymous Ubisoft staff as having stated that the publishers' game pitching process now requires devs to either outline how they'll use generative AI to speed up production, or offer a specific justification as to why the tech isn't being used.

Recent findings suggest that ubisoft management reportedly revealed the change during a presentation to staff, with one source telling Vertical Slice that the organization are all in on generative AI. They cited an "attitude shift" among Ubisoft management as having left workers feeling "cornered by generative AI all the time", but made clear Ubisoft haven't brought in a blanket mandate forcing devs to use genAI no matter what. In a recent development, ubisoft have emphasised the latter in a statement subsequently issued to Vertical Slice. "We encourage our teams to explore where AI can be useful and bring value to their work," the company wrote. "This doesn’t mean that projects are required to use generative AI. Teams need to determine whether, and how, these technologies make sense for the game they want to make and the experience they want to create for players.".

Industry observers note that ubisoft have been keen on experimenting with genAI in game development for a little while - something essentially set in stone by the establishing of Motherbrain as a proper division where the tech giant can work on the likes of the "first playable generative AI experience" they were shouting about earlier this year. That move came in a year Ubisoft have spent vast swaths of laying off staff and closing studios, as part of a restructuring they kicked off with a barrage of cancellations in January. Most recently, those cuts hit 166 staff at Ubisoft Montreal and Ubisoft Quebec, both of which worked on Assassin's Creed Shadows. Ubisoft Singapore and Trials developers Redlynx, Ubisoft Winnipeg, Belgrade, and Barcelona, long-time Tom Clancy studio Red Storm, Ubisoft's Paris HQ and three German studios including Anno devs Ubisoft Mainz are all on a list of other affected subsidiaries that I'm very much getting tired of wheeling out whenever Ubisoft inevitably insist on adding to it.

Changes to remote working policies have also contributed to strikes at Ubisoft studios in France and Italy.

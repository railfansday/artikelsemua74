In a recent development, prince Harry has been back in the UK for some weeks now, and he’s wasted no time in letting everyone know about it, with plenty of public moments including a surprise appearance on This Morning and a family outing to London's Winter Wonderland. But while fans of the Duke of Sussex celebrate his return, the Palace is remarked to be “infuriated” with his recent engagements and, in particular, his request to join the “grid.”.

Speaking to The Express, royal commentator Richard Fitzwilliams accused Harry and Meghan Markle of purposely timing their public appointments to take attention away from other members of the royal family. (Related: Understanding BigBird's Block Sparse Attention...)

Recent findings suggest that “We must assume this has been deliberate. This is deplorable,” the royal expert stated, adding, “[The Palace is] indeed right to be infuriated.”.

But while Fitzwilliams accuses Harry of intentionally scheduling his engagements to outshine his royal counterparts, perhaps it's all just bad timing—which is where the royal “grid” comes into things...

Sources indicate that the royal “grid” is a meticulously organized schedule of the royal family’s engagements and appointments. Although the actual “grid” is not available to the public, fans of the royal family can keep up with their plans via the official website. According to the site, official royal engagements are published up to eight weeks in advance.

It has been revealed that according to The Express, Prince Harry has requested that he be added to the royal “grid.” His motive for the move hasn’t been confirmed, but there are of course plenty of theories. Some critics, like royal commentator Fitzwilliams, believe that Harry wants to intentionally create more scheduling conflicts with the other royals. Others take the more agreeable stance that the duke simply wants to avoid any further calendar clashes. (Related: Latest Update: Course Launch Community Event...)

One insider, for example, noted that Harry is “mindful” of his fellow royals and their looks, and tries his best to avoid overlapping with them. “He knows about those things and he is obviously mindful of that as he’s planning his own activities as well,” the source told The Times.

The royal family is split into various households, with Clarence House taking care of King Charles and Queen Camilla and Kensington Palace looking after the Wales family, for example. These households are made up of large teams, covering everything from catering to housekeeping. Also in the royal household are those who plan and manage the family members’ calendars—these are overseen by private secretaries and diary coordinators.

A previous advertisement for such a job at Kensington Palace remarked, “The role will be responsible for managing the seamless delivery of multiple complex diaries to ensure the smooth and effective coordination of engagements and events.”.

As for what the role involved? “Compiling and managing coordinated diary updates and weekly overviews; providing regular briefings; communicating diary changes to all key stakeholders, both internally and externally; and carrying out follow-up actions as necessary, as well as ensuring that all events and engagements are accurately recorded,” the listing explained.

Sources indicate that “The successful candidate will have a proven ability to manage complex diaries and will have excellent communication skills,” the vacancy ad went on. Being a team player and having a proactive mindset were just some of the other requirements of the role, in addition to maintaining confidentiality and discretion, which the listing described as “paramount.”.

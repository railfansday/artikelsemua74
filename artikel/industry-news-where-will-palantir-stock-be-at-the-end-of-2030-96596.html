Recent findings suggest that palantir Technologies (PLTR +1.14%) made $1.43 a share in its last four quarters. At roughly $161 a share as of this writing, that gives the stock a price-to-earnings ratio of about 112. I expect investors to pay much less than that for every dollar of Palantir's profit by the end of 2030.

According to recent reports, but I think the stock can still climb. I'd say roughly $240 when 2030 ends, about 50% over today's price, or a rise of about 10% a year. To get there, I'm assuming earnings per share of about $4.85 in 2030, with investors paying 50 times those earnings.

This would also take the shares over their record close of $209.05 on Oct. 9. They're still around 23% under that mark, and off almost 10% in 2026.

Over the first nine months of 2026, Palantir made $1.19 a share. For the fourth quarter, management expects non-GAAP (adjusted) operating income, excluding stock-based compensation and related employer payroll taxes, of about $1.64 billion, roughly 15% more than in the third quarter. If net income climbs at a similar pace, earnings per share for the full year would be near $1.70, almost triple the $0.63 Palantir made in 2025. After that, I'm assuming per-share earnings grow about 30% a year on average through 2030, as growth slows along the way. This puts them at around $4.85, nearly triple again. If Palantir's share count stays near where it is now and it keeps the 49% net margin it had in the third quarter, earnings like that would take about $26 billion in 2030 sales -- about three times the $8.53 billion it expects for 2026.

It has been revealed that growth at that rate would still be a sharp slowdown from 2026, and some of it could already be happening. Palantir's U.S. commercial remaining deal value (the value left on its contracts with American businesses, if customers exercise all options and cancel none) climbed 27% from the first quarter to the second. In the third quarter, it rose just 4%, to around $6.5 billion. (Related: How we sped up transformer inference 100x for 🤗 API cus...)

Granted, that's still 79% more than a year ago. But slower growth in that number can signal slower revenue growth later.

Sources indicate that taxes may also take a bigger cut of profits. Palantir's third-quarter income tax expense was around $14 million on $1.15 billion in pre-tax income, a rate of about 1%. But when it calculates its adjusted earnings, Palantir assumes a long-term tax rate of 23%. A rate like that may slow per-share earnings growth even if sales keep rising fast.

Even with earnings held at $4.85 a share, Palantir's stock price at the end of 2030 may vary widely based on what investors are willing to pay for them. Thirty times $4.85 is about $146, roughly 9% below where the stock trades now, even with profits nearly tripling. And 110 times earnings, near today's price-to-earnings ratio, would take the price above $530, a rise of about 34% a year.

I'd be surprised if investors kept paying over 100 times earnings for a firm whose profit growth had slowed to around 30% a year or less. And 30 times earnings looks too low for a business that turns about half its revenue into net income.

Fifty times earnings sits between the two. It's under half of Palantir's current price-to-earnings ratio. And it's below the forward price-to-earnings ratio of around 64 that investors pay today, using the 2027 earnings analysts expect. For a firm I'd expect to still grow profits more than 20% a year in 2030, that looks like a fair price to me.

A gain of around 10% a year isn't spectacular for a growth stock. But my forecast doesn't need Palantir to keep growing anywhere near its 2026 rate, and it already assumes investors pay under half of the current price-to-earnings ratio. If earnings rise more than 30% a year, $240 could turn out to be low.

The road there likely won't be smooth. Palantir shares dropped about 7% on Nov. 20, then jumped about 11% on Nov. 23.

Sources indicate that yet about 10% a year is enough for me to start. At roughly $161, I'd consider a small first buy and spread the rest over time.

In a recent development, the number I'd track most is U.S. commercial remaining deal value. If it keeps climbing just a few percent a quarter, I'd expect a 2030 price-to-earnings ratio closer to 30 than 50.

According to recent reports, A failed decision at COP31 doesn’t stop tourism being able to take action on the ground. Without a guiding goal, destinations risk losing a long-term investment signal and political backstop for climate resilience and adaptation.

COP31’s failure to reach agreement on the Antalya Pact has been called a setback, but others argue no deal is better than a bad deal.

The final text for the Antalya Pact, which had a goal to boost electrification and clean energy, climate finance and acknowledged the overshoot of the Paris Agreement’s 1.5 degrees Celsius goal, did not mention fossil fuels — a distinct reversal of what was achieved at COP28 in particular.

It also focused on carbon capture and storage, a technology encouraged by petrostates and considered a false solution by critics. The worry was that the mooted deal would have undermined the Paris Agreement.

Recent findings suggest that “We will not endorse a cover decision that does not even mention fossil fuels, the driver of climate change. And we will not accept negotiating backward in time. And we will not accept a COP that greenwashes fossil fuels,” stated Ireland’s climate minister Darragh O'Brien and European Commissioner Wopke Hoekst.

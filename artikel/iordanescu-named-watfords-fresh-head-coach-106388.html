Edward Iordanescu has been appointed as Watford's new head coach, three days after Alessio Dionisi was sacked.

Dionisi was only in charge for five months, with his sacking coming on the back of a run of seven defeats in 10 league games, culminating in a 4-0 loss to Sheffield United.

That result left Watford 20th in the Championship table, three points above the relegation zone, with Iordanescu becoming their sixth new boss in less than 18 months.

He is also the 25th manager since the Pozzo family took over the club in 2012.

"I am very proud and happy to be at this football club," Iordanescu told the club website.

"I can see the team is in a difficult moment, but I have already watched many of the games over the last few days, and I'm very confident about the future.

Iordanescu's first game in charge of Watford will come against Derby County on Saturday, but he will be in the stands for Wednesday's clash with Blackburn Rovers.

This will be the 48-year-old's first job since leaving Legia Warsaw in October 2025, having led the side to the Polish Super Cup in July 2025.

Iordanescu also took charge of Romania for two and a half years, guiding them to the last 16 at Euro 2024 when they were beaten by the Netherlands.

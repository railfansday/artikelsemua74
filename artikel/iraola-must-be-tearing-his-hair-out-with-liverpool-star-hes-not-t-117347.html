Liverpool manager Andoni Iraola must be tearing his hair out with what he's seeing from Milos Kerkez since his arrival.

Iraola previously managed Kerkez at Bournemouth, and he enjoyed a phenomenal spell with the Cherries under his management.

The Hungarian international was a flying full-back who loved to try to make things happen in the final third, and that's why Liverpool made a move to secure his signature.

Many Liverpool supporters must have been hoping that Kerkez would flourish under Iraola, given they've previously worked together.

Unfortunately, that simply hasn't been the case, and Iraola must be confused as to what's happened to him.

It has been revealed that against Club Brugge, Kerkez started on the left-hand side of defence. It was far from a disastrous performance from the Hungarian, who was incredibly solid defensively, but he's still showing a major weakness.

Kerkez simply isn't the same player he once was when it comes to how he contributes in the final third.

The 23-year-old struggles to look after the ball in possession, particularly when he ventures into the opposition half.

Kerkez completed just 78% of his passes against Club Brugge, while he also fluffed a shot from inside the penalty area.

This isn't an isolated situation for Kerkez, as he's yet to produce a goal or an assist for Liverpool so far this season.

Last term, despite playing 48 games, Kerkez contributed with just four goal contributions for Liverpool.

Although Kerkez's hasn't ever been a phenomenal contributor in the final third, it's just his overall game that appears to be lacking in attack.

Iraola will undoubtedly be happy with what he's seeing from him defensively, but he needs to see more from him going forward.

In modern football, full-backs who can contribute in both halves are incredibly crucial, especially when Liverpool usually have a defensive option on the other side in Ronald Araujo. It's not hampering Liverpool too much at the moment, considering their recent form, but they still remain seven points behind Arsenal in the Premier League table.

Liverpool fans are so used to having a left-back in Andy Robertson, who contributed so significantly in the final third throughout his career.

In his prime, Robertson hit 15 assists in one season while regularly reaching double figures for assists while with Liverpool.

That could be the main frustration from Liverpool supporters who are still unsure whether Kerkez is the right man for them.

They will likely be regularly comparing him to someone like Robertson, especially when they're also used to Trent Alexander-Arnold on the opposite side, one of the most creative defenders in world football.

Iraola must be tearing his hair out at why Kerkez just doesn't seem to be the same player when it comes to how he acts in the final third.

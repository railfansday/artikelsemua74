Nvidia (NVDA -3.91%) popped 31.5% on Monday, Nov. 23. Nvidia closed with a market cap of $3.985 trillion -- $954 billion more than it closed at on Friday, Nov. 20. This would be the equivalent of creating the world's 16th most valuable tech giant out of thin air. (Related: “Giant superatoms” could finally solve quantum computin...)

The move came as Nvidia's board of directors voted unanimously to reinstate Jensen Huang as president, CEO, and board director.

Investors may be wondering if one of roughly 42,000 employees should be worth such a large percentage of the company's value.

Here's why the move may still be an underreaction to how invaluable Huang is to Nvidia.

In a recent development, huang's sudden departure exposed serious threats to Nvidia's investment thesis -- namely, that management was ill-prepared to run the organization without him. And that the company's vision and culture centered on Huang.

Huang ran the semiconductor manufacturer as an extension of himself, with dozens of direct reports and client relationships built on decades of industry experience. The informality of Nvidia's structure allowed Nvidia to operate with the speed of a start-up with the financial muscle of an industry leader -- which has been a lethal combination that has allowed Nvidia to fiercely protect its lead over the competition and maintain pricing power with high margins.

Sources indicate that elon Musk, CEO of Tesla (TSLA -1.18%) and Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX +1.30%), has been one of the most public supporters for retaining Huang as CEO. In a conversation on the social media platform X, Elon Musk shed light on Huang's operating style and the vulnerabilities his departure created for Nvidia.

If Jensen gave me his word about compute delivery or I asked for components to be accelerated, he always came through. Sure, we’d put together a purchase order at some point later, but I never for a second thought it wouldn’t happen.

On SpaceX's second-quarter 2026 earnings call in August, which was its first earnings call as a public company, Musk mentioned that SpaceX would build exclusively on Nvidia because it believed that Nvidia's Vera Rubin architecture was the best computing platform. With SpaceX owning xAI and having aims to build orbital constellations of AI compute satellites in space, being exclusive to Nvidia is a massive vote of confidence in Nvidia's innovation. But also in Jensen Huang.

In a recent development, I doubt Musk would make such a clear commitment if he didn't believe in Nvidia's tech just as much as he does in its leadership. And if Huang were to leave and Nvidia's future computing platforms didn't produce as big a leap as Rubin did from Blackwell, or as big a leap as Blackwell did compared to Hopper, then Nvidia could be leaving business from top innovators like SpaceX on the table.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/25/26.

In the third quarter of fiscal year 2027, Nvidia reported a 103% year-over-year increase in quarterly revenue and a generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP) gross margin of 74.8%. Nvidia is at the moment in its fourth quarter of fiscal 2027. But management is already guiding for a 75% increase in year-over-year revenue in fiscal 2028 -- up from prior guidance of 70%.

Nvidia spent years developing Rubin but only began shipments in August. In Nvidia's latest quarter, Rubin contributed nearly $20 billion in revenue compared to $107.6 billion in total data center revenue, which made it the fastest product ramp-up in history in a product's first quarter of shipments.

It has been revealed that there are few CEOs as intertwined with a manufacturer's investment thesis as tech founders like Jensen Huang with Nvidia, Elon Musk with Tesla and SpaceX, and Mark Zuckerberg with Meta Platforms (META +3.40%). It's clear that Huang's ability to drive innovation and multi-year commitments is an inseparable element of Nvidia's valuation. So while Huang is definitely worth at least $954 billion to Nvidia, his sudden resignation exposed a gaping hole in Nvidia's investment thesis that needs to be plugged with a clear succession plan and improved governance.

It has been revealed that there's also the ongoing risk that internal and external investigations expose facts that force Huang to resign down the road. Pay close attention to the wording of the Nov. 23 press release -- that Huang's reinstatement poses no national security risk. I take that to mean that Huang's return as CEO isn't a national security threat, not that Huang is totally in the clear on national security leaks and export violations.

Until investors get more clarity, Nvidia remains a hold rather than a buy.

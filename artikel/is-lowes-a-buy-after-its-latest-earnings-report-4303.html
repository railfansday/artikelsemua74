Recent findings suggest that lowe's Companies (LOW +2.78%) stock enjoyed a midweek bump thanks to the third-quarter results it posted Wednesday morning. It was a beat-and-raise quarter for the durable home improvement and hardware retailer, with a near-double-digit increase in total sales. And while there was a large asterisk next to that figure, other positive developments helped lift the stock, at least initially. (Related: Latest Update: At least seven progressives voted agains...)

The post-earnings share price pop was encouraging, although it evaporated the following day. Here's my view on whether the shares can go higher, and meaningfully, before long.

The quarter saw Lowe's post $22.8 billion in net sales, up an uncharacteristically high 9% year over year. At first glance, there was a mismatch between that growth figure and the comparable sales increase of 1.8%. This is due to the firm's purchase of a latest asset, wholesale construction materials distributor Foundation Building Materials (FBM), revealed in August 2025. The deal closed in October, which was too late for FBM's full third-quarter 2025 results to be consolidated into Lowe's. Since the 2026 quarter included a full three months of FBM's performance, the year-over-year comparison for Lowe's total net sales was skewed. (Related: Senate panel vote on National Science Foundation nomine...)

Regardless, the line item exceeded the consensus analyst estimate of $22.4 billion.

Sources indicate that as for net income, there was also a note next to both the GAAP and non-GAAP (adjusted) flavors of that number. On a GAAP basis, profitability was $1.67 billion, or just under 4% higher year over year. Translated into a per-share, adjusted figure, the improvement was barely over 1% (at $3.10). On top of that, refunds from the Trump administration tariffs that were ruled illegal put roughly $250 million in Lowe's coffers and juiced both GAAP and adjusted earnings per share (EPS) by $0.34. So what we can call organic adjusted EPS fell by almost 10%, to $2.76. The average analyst estimate for raw adjusted EPS was $2.89, and clearly, many investors were willing to ignore that refund-adjusted number and consider the raw one to be a beat.

As management teams habitually do, Lowe's attributed its improvements to its current Total Home Strategy initiative, which was confirmed in 2020 and updated in late 2024. This is an attempt to be an all-encompassing, one-stop, omnichannel retailer for every customer, be it a person needing a tool for a small repair or a construction industry pro working on large-scale projects with a sizable budget. Total Home Strategy was a massive factor in the FBM acquisition and an earlier asset purchase in 2025, the interior surface design, distribution, and installation specialist Artisan Design Group (ADG).

NYSE · As of 10:00 AM ET 11/20/26.

Although the top- and bottom-line results for Lowe's were somewhat inflated, the organization was confident enough to raise key guidance items. It now expects total sales of $92.5 billion for all of 2026; that's higher, if not dramatically so, than its previous forecast of $92 billion. Comparable sales are now expected to rise by around 0.5% over the 2025 results. That provided investors some fuel for optimism, as the preceding estimate was that the key indicator would be flat.

Lowe's maintained its adjusted EPS projection of $12.25, and its guidance for capital expenditures (up to $2.5 billion).

In a recent development, I find it admirable that Lowe's has articulated and is executing a clear and sensible strategy for growth (even if its all-things-to-all-clients nature might prove overly ambitious). I also like the fact that it's growing, if slowly, its sales and "comps" despite lingering weakness in new housing construction in this country.

Would that make me a buyer of its stock? To me, the jury's still out on the current iteration of the Total Home Strategy, which, like all grand initiatives, needs time to take full effect. I'd prefer to see concrete evidence that it's really boosting Lowe's results before pulling the trigger on an investment -- especially given the unspectacular third-quarter organic growth rates (or declines). So basically, I'd adopt a wait-and-see stance on the retailer's stock.

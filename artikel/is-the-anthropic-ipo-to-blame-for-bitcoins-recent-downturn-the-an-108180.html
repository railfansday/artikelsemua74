In a recent development, on Nov. 19, Anthropic (ANT +1.08%) became available to trade after raising a record $81.3 billion in its initial public offering (IPO), and just two days later, Bitcoin (BTC +0.80%) fell below its psychologically important $60,000 level to hit $55,130, its lowest since September 2024. For many investors, those events are connected, and Anthropic's debut is blamed for Bitcoin's poor performance.

In short, one prevailing theory on the topic indicates that legions of investors sold their crypto to buy shares of Anthropic, just as they seemed to be doing right before Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX +1.30%)—better known as SpaceX—had its IPO in June. But does this narrative really hold up to scrutiny? (Related: Tech Report: Think AI "knows" what it’s doing? Scientis...)

U.S. spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds (ETFs) and their capital flows are notable bellwethers for Bitcoin's price, as they indicate demand for Bitcoin in the traditional financial system. (Related: Latest Update: Trump pushes Huang’s reinstatement despi...)

Sources indicate that those ETFs saw $1.9 billion in net outflows from Nov. 5 to 13, and then another $1.8 billion from Nov. 16 to 20. By Nov. 23, November's outflows had reached $4.7 billion, a fresh record.

Analysts have been largely consistent in saying that the IPO-drain story is pretty unproven; 10x Research's Markus Thielen called the timing coincidental. Similarly, TDX Strategies' Dick Lo called the IPO one factor affecting Bitcoin, but not necessarily the primary one, and he pointed to hotter-than-expected inflation data and rising Treasury yields as more important causes. Outflows accelerated the week the offering closed on Nov. 20, and then continued through Nov. 23.

Industry observers note that market turbulence has also been bad for Bitcoin recently. In the week ending Nov. 20, U.S. stocks had their worst weekly performance since April 2025, due in part to the Federal Reserve's meeting minutes showing widespread support for more rate hikes, which tend to be bad for risky assets like cryptocurrencies. With Anthropic's stock now sinking, interest rates and economic uncertainty appear to be the bigger culprits.

Bitcoin's performance after the SpaceX IPO in June is a helpful data point for deciding whether to buy the dip here.

It has been revealed that spot Bitcoin ETFs saw a then-record $4.5 billion in net outflows that month, partly because the SpaceX IPO pulled capital away from crypto. Then, in July, they took in $403 million as the coin rose by 7.3%.

So money returned to Bitcoin, but only in a trickle, which coincided with a decent price rebound. The same could well happen once the market's attention turns away from Anthropic, potentially within the next few weeks.

Of course, one recent precedent isn't the same as a pattern.

In a recent development, nothing about this IPO changes why people hold Bitcoin, so it still makes sense to buy slowly, in small amounts. Just be aware that if the Fed hikes interest rates further, it could be a long time before your Bitcoin purchases break even, and there could be a bit more downside in the near term.

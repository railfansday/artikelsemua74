Everton’s Hill Dickinson Stadium is almost guaranteed to host the Nations League finals next summer if the FA are successful in bringing the closing stages of the tournament to England.

That is according to former Toffees chief executive Keith Wyness, speaking exclusively to Football Insider, who believes that the new ground is the best fit for the job.

This comes after the news that the FA have registered their interest in hosting the finals, should England find a route past Norway in their quarter-final clash in March. (Related: Breaking: Hosting your Models and Datasets on Hugging F...)

Wyness believes that the Hill Dickinson Stadium is a “shoo-in” to host if the FA are successful, with a recent FIFA announcement ranking it as the joint-best stadium in the country.

The Toffees’ home shared that accolade with the Tottenham Hotspur Stadium, but recent reports have suggested that the FA are reluctant to use the Lilywhites’ home.

Wembley is also off the table due to events being held at the time, which Wyness believes makes the Hill Dickinson the obvious solution.

Sources indicate that FIFA assessed existing Old Trafford as a Women's WC venue at 4.1/5, saying the planned upcoming Man United stadium "remained at an early stage of development, with the conceptual design subject to further detailed planning". Everton’s former chief Wyness – who served as CEO at Goodison Park between 2004 and 2009 and now runs a football consultancy advising elite clubs – believes that the people of Liverpool would have a huge interest in the Nations League finals if the Hill Dickinson won the right to host. The semi-finals and final are due to be played between 9 June and 13 June, and England have never hosted the finals since the competition’s introduction back in 2019.

Speaking on the latest edition of Football Insider’s Inside Track podcast, Wyness explained it would be “great news” for Everton if they won the right to host.

“I think it looks almost like a shoo-in for it,” he said.

It has been revealed that “There was that recent FIFA announcement you mentioned and I saw the rankings of the UK stadiums and it had Spurs and Everton both rated exactly the same at the top level. So, you know, it's one of the best stadiums in Britain.

Industry observers note that “This is a great asset for Everton. So it's great news that it's been recognised. We've seen rugby, rugby league. We'll start to see concerts very soon.

“Liverpool's a great city for nightlife and events around things like this. So there'll be a massive market and a big demand from the fan base to go as well and support events. I think it's a natural fit and I think it would be great news for Everton.”.

Wyness recently told Football Insider that Everton assistant manager Leighton Baines is working closely with striker Thierno Barry on the training ground, in a bid to improve his finishing.

The Frenchman already has 10 goals from 14 games in all competitions this season, but has also missed more big chances than any other Premier League player.

Wyness outlined that Everton believe that Barry can become a £60million striker within the next few years, if he can become more consistent in front of goal.

David Moyes is keen to help develop the youngster, and he has tasked Baines with paying close attention to aid his progress.

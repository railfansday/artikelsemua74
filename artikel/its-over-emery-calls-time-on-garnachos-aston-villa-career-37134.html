Aston Villa were held to a 0-0 draw against Sunderland on Saturday afternoon in the Premier League as they returned to action following the international break.

The Villans were hoping to get back to winning ways after four games without a win in all competitions heading into this one, but the team struggled to create many clear-cut chances and failed to break down a Sunderland side sitting in the relegation zone before kick-off. The goalless draw leaves the team in 11th place in the Premier League, having won just three of their opening 11 games in the top-flight this season, with Emery still looking to find the right balance after losing a host of key first-team stars during the summer transfer window. But despite what happened on the pitch at Villa Park on Saturday afternoon, the biggest story was who wasn't around to see it take place.

When the starting lineups were revealed 75 minutes before kick-off, Emery named a strong lineup which included Alysson and Emi Buendia on the wings, Johan Manzambi playing as a number ten, and Nicolas Jackson up front, with Boubacar Kamara and Leon Goretzka supporting them.

That meant there was only space on the bench for Ross Barkley, John McGinn, Joao Gomes, Ibrahim Mbaye, Brian Madjo, and George Hemmings. But more importantly, and perhaps significantly, there was no space whatsoever for summer signing Alejandro Garnacho.

Garnacho was the first attacking signing of the summer at Villa Park as Emery looked to replace Morgan Rogers following his move to Chelsea, but the Argentine has struggled to make any sort of impact.

Recent findings suggest that in fact, he has managed just 96 minutes of action across all competitions so far this season, failing to start a single game. Emery has previously addressed his situation publicly, stating that he needed to do more.

"[He has to] continue working. Work, work, train, work. When he can have minutes to play, try and play well. He is progressing, but we have players in front of him now.

"We want a competitive team and players to compete in the style we have. Garnacho is a little bit behind, because of some circumstances we had in pre-season," Emery noted. "It is time to be patient for the time he needs.".

After the September international break, Emery claimed that he had improved, but he still managed just 64 minutes between that return to action and the most recent break.

Now he has been left out of the 20-man squad entirely, despite Matty Cash, Matteo Ruggeri and Amadou Onana all being unavailable due to injury, and that suggests that the Spaniard has made his mind up about his long-term future at Villa Park.

After causing trouble at Man Utd, the club decide to cut their losses and moved him on to Chelsea, but he quickly ran into problems there too. The club shipped him off after just one season, and Villa looked keen to revitalise his career.

However, even Emery can't work miracles this major, and it's clear that the winger simply needs to move away from the Premier League to have any chance of getting his career back on track.

Recent findings suggest that villa have too much to play for and too many options ahead of him to be giving him pity minutes, and he's clearly not doing enough in training to warrant an opportunity. With a season-long loan deal agreed with an obligation to join permanently based on certain conditions, this is a very familiar scenario for the club.

Recent findings suggest that after the Harvey Elliott debacle last season, it's clear that they have learn their lesson. If the club are able to cancel the loan in January then they will surely look at doing that. If not, then the 22-year-old will have a long wait until the summer before moving on, because it's evident that he has no future at Aston Villa.

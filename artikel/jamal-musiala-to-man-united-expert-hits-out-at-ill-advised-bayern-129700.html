Bayern Munich are facing a difficult decision over the future of Jamal Musiala amid growing interest from Man United.

This comes after honorary president Uli Hoeness raised concerns about the player's health and questioned whether the club would continue paying his wages. (Related: Costco’s $40 last-minute Thanksgiving Dinner Kit comes ...)

Since then, the club have distanced themselves from the statement, insisting they are focused on helping the 23-year-old get back to full fitness in the coming weeks. (Related: NASA’s new AI space chip could let spacecraft think for...)

Former Man United chief scout Mick Brown told Football Insider that Bayern Munich must be careful to manage Musiala's illness and not force him into a return.

At the same time, the Bundesliga giants have been urged to respect the player, with public discussions over his situation and future of the club helping nobody.

Musiala collapsed twice during pre-season games for Bayern, and has been diagnosed with absence seizures due to a neurological dysfunction, which is said to be treatable.

Football Insider revealed on 24 November that Bayern Munich are increasingly likely to sell Musiala at the end of the season as they weigh up his future at the club.

The attacking midfielder has a release clause in his contract which comes into play at the end of the season, and their doubts could open the door for clubs like United to swoop.

However, the focus must be on helping Musiala's recovery rather than any speculation over his future, while he remains under contract at Bayern until 2030.

"Bayern Munich are in an extremely difficult situation with Jamal Musiala," Brown told Football Insider.

"Whenever a player is suffering with health complications, these sorts of questions get raised about how they will deal with it or whether he will be able to continue playing.

Industry observers note that "At the end of the day, it will be up to the player and the club's doctors to come to a conclusion, and if they think he's okay to continue, then he will continue. It's not up to the club.

"I think the comments Bayern's president made were probably ill-advised, but we have seen this kind of thing come up before where contracts have to be cancelled.

"There's obviously insurance involved in those contracts as well, so it turns into a very complicated situation to deal with.

"But ultimately, it's not something that should be going on in public statements, it has to be discussed with the player because that should be where the focus is.

"They want to help him recover and turn things around, get back to playing and then they can talk about his future.

"If the decision is made that he will be allowed to leave Bayern Munich, I'm sure there will be Premier League clubs interested, Man United for sure are interested, but that's not the main problem at the moment.".

As Brown explained, there will be no shortage of interest from Premier League clubs if Musiala is allowed to leave Bayern Munich at any stage in the near future.

The midfielder is very much seen as one that got away in English football, having represented Chelsea and England at youth level before his move to Munich and Germany.

Football Insider revealed on 26 November that Liverpool and Arsenal would be prepared to explore a move for Musiala next summer if he can prove his fitness first.

Recent reports in Germany have suggested that the attacking midfielder has a release clause in his contract that can be triggered next summer, and English clubs are on alert.

Sources say that it would be a "very expensive" deal to sign the 23-year-old, but they first need him to prove he can get back to play regularly and consistently at the highest level.

Sources indicate that jane Fonda, Robert De Niro and Chelsea Handler are among the entertainment figures who have signed on to a letter backing Sen. Chris Van Hollen (D-MD) in his bid to become Senate majority leader over Sen. Chuck Schumer (D-NY).

The letter, signed by about three dozen figures describing themselves as donors, fundraisers and artists, stated that in this year’s midterms, “voters delivered not on ly a mandate but an opportunity for change: a government that delivers real value in the daily lives of working families.”. The election for majority leader, to be held early next month, has highlighted party divisions, including among donors and in the entertainment industry, and even after the whopping victories of Democrats across the country in the midterms.

Noting that the upcoming vote on a Senate leader will be chosen by secret ballot, the letter called on the caucus to support Van Hollen, who is challenging Schumer, the current minority leader who had been the presumptive favorite to win following Democrats huge gains in which they flipped control of the chamber.

Others who signed on to the letter include Bruce Cohen, Padma Lakshmi, Laurie David, David Mandel, Fisher Stevens, Marisa Tomei, Amy Ziering and Morgan Spector.

Sources indicate that in a statement. De Niro mentioned, “Americans voted for the change necessary to fight the corruption, cruelty, and policies of Trump and his Epstein class cronies. That’s why I’m supporting change in Senate leadership. Chris Van Hollen is a progressive leader and a fierce fighter. He’s the right person to lead the Senate majority.”.

Van Hollen has pitched his candidacy as a necessary response to the midterm results and that Democrats’ unexpected gains were also calls for change, coming from the left and the middle of the party.

“The voters responsible for the Blue Tsunami want fundamental change and bold, upcoming ideas. Simply put, Chuck Schumer is the past and Chris Van Hollen is the present and the future,” Cohen mentioned.

Schumer has insisted that he will emerge victorious, and even some entertainment industry observers believe that he will return to the post.

Mathew Littman, who leads The Working Group, which organizes Hollywood creatives, said via email, “Senator Schumer just led one of the great political wins of all time. He earned two years to lead his fellow Senate Democrats.”.

A Times analysis last week highlighted that Schumer had the public support of 16 senators and Van Hollen had the backing of 6, with 4 more calling for change. But more than two dozen senators’ backing is unknown.

Spokespersons for Schumer and Van Hollen did not return requests for comment.

According to recent reports, but a campaign aide to Schumer, Angelo Roefaro, told Politico that he was behind a bit of pranks-manship in advance of the release of the letter. He gained access to the Google doc draft of the letter and added Frank Sinatra’s name to the list of signatories, and subbed in Schumer’s name for Van Hollen. A Times reporter initially posted a copy of the letter endorsing Van Hollen, but with Sinatra’s name still included, before replacing it with an image of the corrected letter, per Politico. Roefaro told Politico that Schumer was unaware of the prank.

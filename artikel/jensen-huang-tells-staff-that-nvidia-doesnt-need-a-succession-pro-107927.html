Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang on Tuesday struck a conciliatory and remorseful tone while leading his first employee meeting since being reinstated, and said how sorry he was for what he had done.

Industry observers note that huang told employees that he let them down and promised to do a better job the second time around, according to people inside the room, who asked not to be named in order to share details. "I hurt all of you," Huang stated, according to one of the people. (Related: Industry News: Texas’s outgoing GOP attorney general dr...)

In a recent development, the CEO also addressed succession planning during the event, the people said. He told staff that Nvidia has built a team that doesn't require succession planning the way a normal organization does, because the company operates a unique business model where there are multiple capable leaders at Nvidia that are leading different parts of the organization. A succession plan would create unnecessary hierarchy, Huang said, according to one of the people present.

Investors and analysts were left to speculate about the company's future leadership in the wake of Huang's resignation on Nov. 12.

Sources indicate that on Monday, Huang was reinstated as CEO of the chipmaker by Nvidia's board of directors. Eleven days before, Huang had resigned from Nvidia after multiple reported affairs, including one with a Chinese businesswoman who is alleged to be a spy for Beijing.

Harvey Jones, Nvidia's newly appointed board chair, said last week that Huang resigned after "disclosure of personal behavior inconsistent with the company's code of conduct, standards and cultural norms.".

Huang started the meeting by thanking CFO Colette Kress for her leadership as interim CEO.

According to recent reports, huang told staff that Tuesday's organization meeting was the second hardest meeting of his life, the first one being with his wife. He noted he is in therapy, according to one of the people present.

According to recent reports, in a press release announcing Huang's return on Monday, the company said the board "received input" from senior officials in the federal government about any potential national security risk around the CEO. President Donald Trump told CNBC that he gave the board assurances and "made the deal" to restore Huang to his post.

In a recent development, after the manufacturer had its worst week in the stock market since 2008, the stock surged more than 30% the day his return was confirmed. Shares fell on Tuesday, however.

An Nvidia representative didn't immediately respond to requests for comment.

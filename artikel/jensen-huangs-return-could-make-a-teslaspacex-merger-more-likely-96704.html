Tesla (TSLA -0.20%) and Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX -5.65%), Elon Musk's two trillion-dollar companies, took a step closer to merging on Tuesday.

Both companies have appointed a total of five directors, three from Tesla and two from SpaceX, to guide two special committees to handle merger negotiations.

In a recent development, the committees have also hired investment banks to develop financial models for the all-stock merger, and a deal could be reached before year's end. If the merger were timely enough, it would also allow SpaceX to be included in the S&P 500 earlier than it would as a stand-alone tech giant. It has been revealed that naturally, merging two companies that have the same CEO is easier than merging two unrelated companies. Musk has a majority of SpaceX's voting power and 20% of Tesla's voting power, and could own a majority of the combined manufacturer.

The full details of Huang's return to Nvidia may never be clear, but President Trump states he played an active role in getting Huang reinstated, telling CNBC that he "convinced" the board to do so.

That move may have shifted the Overton window significantly in corporate governance, or what's considered acceptable, and also shows that Trump is willing to put his thumb on the scale for his corporate friends, at least when he believes it's in the country's interest, one of which is Musk. (Related: Introducing Optimum: The Optimization Toolkit for Trans...)

The dynamics of getting the merger approved versus reinstating Huang are much different, however. Trump just needed to get the board's approval to get Huang back as Nvidia CEO. The Tesla/SpaceX merger, on the other hand, will face a battery of regulatory hurdles to get cleared.

It has been revealed that china would be an issue in the merger, as Tesla has a significant business there, and SpaceX's technology would likely raise national security concerns. It would have to pass antitrust muster in both the U.S. and Europe, where there could be resistance to creating a firm with a leading position across so many disparate industries. Additionally, Texas Attorney General-elect Nathan Johnson has expressed skepticism about corporate concentration and could challenge the combination.

Still, expectations of corporate governance and President Trump's own ability to influence such decisions have shifted with Huang's reinstatement, which could help the merger go through.

It has been revealed that while the two companies haven't formally declared an intention to merge, they're likely to go through with it, as all of the interested parties, including Elon Musk, appear to be incentivized by a merger. The deal will serve as a major test for the regulatory apparatus, as antitrust regulators have blocked much smaller deals in the past.

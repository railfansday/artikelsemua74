Thanksgiving wound up being a very bad night for Dallas Cowboys head coach Brian Schottenheimer, but it apparently won't cost him his job.

Industry observers note that immediately after the Cowboys' collapse against the Philadelphia Eagles on Thursday, team owner Jerry Jones told reporters he doesn't expect to make a coaching change any time soon. Instead, Jones suggested continuity would give Dallas its best chance in the coming weeks. (Related: Industry News: The Partnership: Amazon SageMaker and Hu...)

Via Nick Harris of the Fort Worth Star-Telegram:.

"I wouldn't anticipate that, no, at this juncture. We can give ourselves a better chance to win these coming games and be competitive, in my mind, without coaching changes.".

The Cowboys face the Seattle Seahawks in Week 13, then have a bye before facing the Los Angeles Rams on Dec. 20.

Jones went on to say he was still "confident" about Schottenheimer, but made clear that confidence was based on nothing the team indicated Thursday:. (Related: Transformer-based Encoder-Decoder Models...)

"Nothing we've seen out here tonight, but you've got to look at everything. That's certainly not the thought on my mind at this particular time. We've got several more games to go and we've got to have our mind on that.".

Thanksgiving really was that bad for the Cowboys, who led 28-10 at halftime and held a lead as large as 21 points near the end of the third quarter.

Brian Schottenheimer had a bad Thanksgiving.

Sources indicate that the team was then hit with a cascading series of disasters. Former Cowboys backup Joe Milton led the Eagles to touchdowns on three of their next four drives, with the Eagles also returning a blocked punt for a touchdown. The Cowboys still had the advantage in the final minute, but decided to go for it on fourth down in Eagles territory and watched Dak Prescott get sacked.

Eagles kicker Jake Elliott proceeded to make a 59-yard field goal in the final seconds. A Prescott interception ended the game.

Industry observers note that both the overall collapse and late-game decision-making left nearly every finger in Dallas pointing at Schottenheimer, who has been facing the hot seat for some time. He mentioned he wasn't worried about such speculation after the game, via Jon Machota of The Athletic:.

It has been revealed that "I don't worry about that. I worry about the guys in that locker room. That doesn't affect me.".

Recent findings suggest that schottenheimer's hire was controversial when it was first unveiled, as he was hardly a popular offensive coordinator under previous head coach Mike McCarthy (who is dealing with issues of his own right now). Jones' decision to promote Schottenheimer demanded trust in his own team's operations, and the results have been a 12-16-1 record.

In more than three decades of owning the Cowboys, Jones has fired only a single head coach during the season: Wade Phillips in 2010. Jones has said he regrets that decision and that could very well be informing his thinking here, though that will hardly protect Schottenheimer in a month and a half.

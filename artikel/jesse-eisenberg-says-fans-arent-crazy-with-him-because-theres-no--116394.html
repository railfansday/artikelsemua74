Elsewhere in the lengthy profile, Aaron Sorkin, who directed Eisenberg as a young Mark Zuckerberg in “The Social Network,” told the outlet that “I hardly know him at all,” noting that the actor reminds “me of Holden Caulfield, if that makes any sense. He has absolutely no patience for phonies.”.

It has been revealed that eisenberg earned an Oscar nomination for the part, but has since been outspoken about his decision not to reprise the role in the director’s follow-up, “The Social Reckoning,” released earlier this year. “I’d be out at a bar, and a drunk guy would say, ‘Hey, I love your website,’” he told the Newly introduced Yorker. “And as I’m pretending to laugh, I’d just wish all of the oxygen would leave my lungs, and that I would collapse on the floor in front of this guy and show him what he’s doing to me.”.

According to recent reports, the actor-turned-director, who is at the moment promoting “The Debut,” his latest project, also spoke about creating “nonhierarchical” environments on his sets. “How could you be making a movie about a woman who’s an underdog, who no one notices, and then have a set where the people walking around feel like that?” he stated. “You’d have to be so stupid and blind and compartmentalizing, in a psychopathic way, to write a movie about that and then perpetuate it.”.

“The Debut,” Eisenberg’s third feature as a director, stars Julianne Moore as Mona Friedman, a Latest Jersey housewife who auditions for a part in her local theater’s production of a fictional musical. After she’s cast as Miss Danielle, Friedman’s life begins to unravel as she becomes more consumed with the role and falls under the spell of director Jerry Blankenbuehler (Paul Giamatti).

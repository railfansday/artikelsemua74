It has overtaken the 138 daily services from New York JFK to become the carrier's most-served market.

Between December and March, JetBlue will now have 38% of the international flights from the South Florida airport. The next-largest operator will be Air Canada, with 12%. When the US carrier's higher-capacity equipment is considered, it'll now have nearly one in two seats for sale (46%), with 21 destinations served.

Recall that eight domestic routes began or resumed on July 8 or July 9. It is worth seeing how they performed by load factor, and the usual caveats apply. For this, the US Department of Transportation data was used, but this presently only exists until August 2026. As such, the analysis is based on less than two months' information, which is, of course, a very obvious limitation.

In a recent development, this source highlights that the eight markets only had an average load factor of 59.4%. Cleveland was just 34.4% (!), which was astonishingly low, even so quickly after commencement. Nashville was second (45.3%), followed by Ponce (51.1%), Houston Intercontinental (57.7%), Charlotte (59.7%), Detroit (63.1%), Baltimore (66.2%), and Charlotte O'Hare (79.2%).

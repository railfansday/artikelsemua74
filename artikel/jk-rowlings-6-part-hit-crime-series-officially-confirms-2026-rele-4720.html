According to recent reports, the controversial author, Rowling, finally published Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone in 1997 after many rejections, and it led to it becoming the biggest book series in history. Since the seven novels concluded, the author has continued writing, whether that be to expand her Harry Potter universe, write other children's novels, write screenplays, or write under pseudonyms to venture into entirely upcoming genres.

Rowling's new crime thriller adaptation is set to be released in one month, just weeks before the upcoming HBO Harry Potter series on December 6. Strike: The Running Grave will return for its seventh season, with Tom Burke back as Detective Cormoran Strike and Holliday Grainger as his partner, Robin Ellacott. It will adapt the seventh novel in Rowling's crime series, written under the pen name Robert Galbraith. (Related: How to train a upcoming language model from scratch usi...)

Sources indicate that "When Sir Colin and Sally Edensor approach Cormoran Strike and Robin Ellacott, they are desperate to reconnect with their estranged son, Will. The Edensors enlist the detectives to gather evidence to discredit the Universal Humanitarian Church, a religious cult who have indoctrinated Will and siphoned off his trust fund.".

Recent findings suggest that the seasons include adaptations of The Cuckoo's Calling, The Silkworm, Career of Evil, Lethal White, Troubled Blood, The Ink Black Heart, and now, The Running Grave. The most recent installment of Strike, titled The Ink Black Heart, averaged over 7.1 million viewers, becoming the BBC's most-watched drama.

Burke and Grainger lead a guest cast including James Fleet (known for Bridgerton) as Sir Colin Edensor, Keeley Forsyth (known for Happy Valley) as Mazu Wace and Nichola McAuliffe (known for British soap opera EastEnders) as Shelley Heaton.

The British crime drama Strike first arrived in 2017, and it certainly had a strong reception within the UK before landing on HBO Max in 2021. Season 1, The Cuckoo's Calling, debuted to a strong critical reception and landed an impressive 85% on Rotten Tomatoes. Reviews stated, "It has style and good actors. These days, on television, much depends on the eye of the beholder, this is ever so true with the plot of this series," and "The seven hours are pleasingly old-school, too: no gratuitous violence, a lot of character development and the crimes wrap up like tidy parcels.".

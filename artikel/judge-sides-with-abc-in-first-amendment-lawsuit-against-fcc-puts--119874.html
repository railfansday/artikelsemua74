Judge Sides With ABC In First Amendment Lawsuit Against FCC; Puts Halt To Agency’s Early License Renewal Proceeding

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

A federal judge has largely sided with ABC in its First Amendment lawsuit claiming that an FCC order to review its broadcast licenses was a retaliatory act by the Trump administration designed to chill its content, whether that be news coverage or jokes from Jimmy Kimmel.

Recent findings suggest that U.S. District Judge Loren AliKhan issued a preliminary injunction on Wednesday that prohibits the FCC from taking any further action in its license review, concluding that the network was “likely to succeed in their retaliation claim.

According to recent reports, “This is not a close case,” she wrote in concluding that the network had shown that the public interest favors court intervention in agency action. “No one doubts that the Commission may enforce the law in the manner it sees fit,” AliKhan wrote. “But in doing so, Defendants may not retaliate against or punish Plaintiffs and other broadcast license holders for disfavored speech.”.

She also rejected the FCC’s effort to dismiss the lawsuit on jurisdictional grounds.

“Given the extreme, indeed existential, nature of the possible penalty Plaintiffs face – loss of their broadcast licenses – the court concludes that early license renewal proceedings create a risk sufficient to chill a broadcaster of ordinary firmness,” the judge wrote.

Read the judge’s decision in the ABC case against the FCC.

According to recent reports, in highlighting the urgency of court action, AliKhan cited moves that the network has already taken out of fear of losing their licenses. They include the choice to place James Talarico’s guest appearance on Jimmy Kimmel Live on YouTube, outside of the FCC’s authority, as well as ABC’s decision to livestream Trump’s July primetime speech out of fear concern of administration retaliation.

Recent findings suggest that “These alterations to Plaintiffs’ editorial decision making showcase how the Commission’s early license renewal proceedings have already curtailed Plaintiffs’ protected activity,” the judge wrote. “That an objectively reasonable broadcaster faced with the same existential threat would follow suit is beyond debate.”. ABC filed suit against the FCC in August, claiming that the agency was violating the First Amendment as it ordered the network’s broadcast stations to submit their licenses to an early review.

According to recent reports, the network noted that the agency’s action was unprecedented, creating a chill to its speech and that of other broadcasters given the risk to losing licenses for their eight owned stations. The first of the licenses was not set to expire until 2028.

In claiming regulatory retaliation, the network cited Trump’s attacks on Kimmel and its news coverage, as well as warnings from the president’s FCC Chairman Brendan Carr.

Industry observers note that the judge referenced Trump’s attacks and warnings in her opinion and noted that three of the co-hosts of The View, as well as Kimmel and ABC News reporters have entrires of the White House’s “media offenders” webpage. She said that Trump and Carr’s “call and response” was “no coincidence.”.

She also noted the timing of the FCC’s order for ABC stations to submit to the license review, coming a day after Trump posted on Truth Social that Kimmel should be fired for a skit the previous week.

The FCC has argued that the early license renewal was tied to its inability to obtain documents from the network as part of an investigation of its diversity, equity and inclusion practices. But toward the end of a two-hour hearing last month, the judge asked the agency whether there were other, less drastic alternatives it could have chosen rather than the license review. And in her decision, AliKhan wrote that she did not find the agency’s explanation to be credible.

In a recent development, she wrote, “What is more, Chairman Carr has taken President Trump’s rhetoric as his marching orders. The same week he was tapped to be President Trump’s FCC chair, following months of President Trump’s calling for ‘ABC [to] lose its license’ for how it covered the presidential campaign …. then-Commissioner Carr lauded ‘license revocation’ as a ‘remedy’ to ‘deter this kind of conduct.'”.

During the hearing, Beth Wilkinson, representing the network, compared ABC’s situation to the French Revolution, with people forced on a guillotine, wondering if anything will come down. Losing broadcast licenses, she said, is akin to a “death penalty” for stations.

Sources indicate that the FCC argued that the network had to wait until the review process played out before challenging it, and that the D.C. Circuit Court of Appeals, not the district court, is the venue to hear such a case.

But AliKhan concluded that ABC’s First Amendment retaliation claim fell outside the kind of statutory review set up in the Communications Act, directing challenges to the federal appellate court and likely adding months of delay.

Industry observers note that the judge wrote, “To be sure, Plaintiffs would face even greater injury if the FCC ultimately denied their renewal applications. But that does not mean that they are not right now being injured by allegedly being singled out for differential treatment—that is, from facing early license renewal proceedings years in advance of the standard expiration dates as a cudgel to stamp out unfavorable content about President Trump.”.

Carr has initiated investigations and reviews of all of the major broadcast networks. Among other things, he launched a probe of The View earlier this year and whether it had to comply with the agency’s Equal Time Rule.

In a recent development, the judge’s decision was limited to the license renewal proceeding. She also denied ABC’s request for a broader injunction that would prohibit any FCC actions “to coerce or threaten” ABC with sanctions as a way to influence their content. AliKhan wrote that such an injunction could not be issued to something “merely feared.”.

An ABC News spokesperson remarked, “Today’s decision is an vital victory for the First Amendment. We thank the court for its careful consideration.”.

A spokesperson for the FCC stated in a statement, “While we appreciate the district court denying in part Disney’s request for preliminary injunctive relief, the portion of the decision granting Disney’s motion is wrong on the facts and wrong on the law—as controlling Supreme Court precedent makes clear. The FCC looks forward to prevailing in full on this matter. We will continue to use our lawful authority to hold broadcasters accountable to their public interest obligations.”.

Sources indicate that anna Gomez, the sole Democrat on the FCC, noted in a statement, “For nearly two years, the agency has weaponized its licensing authority to retaliate against broadcasters for exercising their right to free expression, and now a federal court has stepped in to put a check on that conduct. That brings badly needed accountability to a Commission and an administration that have politicized the machinery of government in a sustained campaign of censorship and control.”.

The case had been closely watched by First Amendment advocates and journalism groups.

Bruce Brown, the president of the Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press, called the decision “a substantial win not only for ABC, but for all broadcasters.”.

He remarked, “As the court recognized, this is ‘not a close case.’ But it is also a novel case forced on ABC because of the administration’s punitive actions and therefore all the more vital that the federal judiciary stepped in to vindicate First Amendment rights.

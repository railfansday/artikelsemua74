The January transfer window is closing in, and at Middlesbrough, Kim Hellberg has already revealed which position is their priority to strengthen.

Rokas Pukstas' start to life at the Riverside has yet to get into first gear. He made a six-minute cameo against QPR before going down with a hamstring injury while training in America over the three-week international break back in September. (Related: Latest Update: Distributed Training: Train BART/T5 for ...)

It was revealed last week that the 21-year-old will have to go under the knife to repair it, and Boro boss Kim Hellberg has admitted that any first-team minutes between now and the end of the season will be a positive.

The Swedish head coach also revealed that Rangers loanee Connor Barron is right now injured, but with no minutes in a Boro shirt since his arrival in the summer, it's almost all but certain that the Scottish giants will be considering bringing him back once the window opens. (Related: Summer at Hugging Face...)

Speaking after their 2-0 win over Bristol City last Saturday, Hellberg remarked that it was "absolute" that Middlesbrough needed to strengthen their midfield, with it likely that Sebastian Berhalter and Aidan Morris will be their only senior central midfielders come the end of the calendar year.

Here are three players they should be targetting.

We'll start with arguably the more underwhelming choice, but one that would ensure continuity and familiarity within the squad and wouldn't put too much pressure on replacing Morris or Berhalter, who are becoming quite an established duo in the engine room.

Alan Browne spent last season on loan at Middlesbrough from Sunderland, playing the utility role to perfection. The Irishman played a range of positions in midfield and even covered across the backline when needed.

Various injuries to key players effectively made him a first-choice player from mid-January until the end of the season, and he played the full 90 minutes in their play-off final defeat to Hull City back in May.

Since returning to the Stadium of Light, Browne hasn't featured in competitions that the Black Cats care about. His only bows have come in the EFL Cup and the EFL Trophy. He's yet to make a squad in the Europa League, and finally made his first Premier League 20 in their goalless draw with Aston Villa on Saturday.

There were murmurs back in September about him possibly having his contract paid out, and if Boro could bring him back for free, he'd be a useful addition.

Even so, with his deal set to expire in the summer, the 31-year-old shouldn't be available for too much in January if he's still under contract on Wearside.

Barnsley are in some real dire straits at the moment, battling down at the bottom of League One.

January will be crucial if Daniel Stendel's side are going to avoid a first fourth-tier campaign since the late 1970s, but there's the possibility that they could be looking even worse come the time the window slams shut.

Despite the Tykes' position, they still have some quality amongst the squad, and there's a strong possibility that the unpopular ownership group at Oakwell could cash in on the likes of Reyes Cleary or, in this case, Patrick Kelly, as they'd be worth a lot less as League Two footballers in the summer if that's where they end up.

Cleary gets a lot of the plaudits amongst neutrals, given how impressive he was last season, but, arguably, Kelly has been Barnsley's best player so far this term.

Sources indicate that the Northern Irish international has run the show from midfield. Only Derensili Sanches Fernandes and Oliver Norwood have created more chances from open play in League One, and only the Huddersfield Town wideman and Will Alves have created more chances as a result of them carrying the ball forward.

Kelly has excelled in the high-octane, pressing style that Stendel has deployed at Barnsley this season, so he shouldn't have to adapt too much to what Hellberg would want him to do, which may have been the issue for Leo Castledine this time last season.

And as a chance creator, he could deputise in the Riley McGree role behind the striker if needs be, too.

Middlesbrough have found joy in scouting from the MLS and looking at American midfielders, so if a latest middle-man is needed at the Riverside in January, it wouldn't be wild to take a look at some of the better-performing Americans in the division in 2026.

17-year-old Adri Mehmeti would be a dream signing by the looks of things, but realistically, whoever comes in will likely initially be playing understudy to their two compatriots already in the middle at the Riverside.

Recent findings suggest that 23-year-old Beau Leroux of the San Jose Earthquakes could be considered, though. The defensive midfielder initially looks like a bit of a disruptor, like Morris can be.

The Boro man has made more tackles and won possession more times than anyone in the Championship so far this season, per Opta. In the 2026 MLS campaign, Leroux was 8th in duels won, 10th in possession won and 25th in tackles made.

Per FotMob, the 23-year-old was a top 10% defensive midfielder in defensive contributions, recoveries and possession won in the final third, and in the top 1% for duels won.

Recent findings suggest that so, defensively, he looks to be a strong candidate, but he can also bring something in the attacking and chance-creating sense, too. He scored four and assisted seven in 33 MLS games last season, taking 52 shots from defensive midfield and creating 37 chances.

In a recent development, leroux's ceiling looks quite high, and there is room for improvement. His pass percentage numbers weren't much to shout about, so if he does come in, he would seemingly be someone to learn from Morris and Berhalter, who look to do either side of the game better than he does right now.

However, if he can realise his potential under Helberg, he could become a real all-action box-to-box midfielder who could cope with the Premier League, which is Middlesbrough's ultimate goal come the end of this season.

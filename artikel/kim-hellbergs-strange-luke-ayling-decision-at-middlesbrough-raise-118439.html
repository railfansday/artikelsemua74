Kim Hellberg has Middlesbrough looking like genuine Championship promotion contenders, but his handling of Connor Barron raises some interesting questions for Rangers.

In a recent development, the Scottish midfielder arrived on loan from Ibrox in September, yet has barely featured in Kim Hellberg's plans. Saturday's victory for Middlesbrough over Bristol City in the Championship provided another curious example, with the Boro boss turning to Luke Ayling in midfield. Sources indicate that that decision came instead of giving Barron an opportunity. Given the work Middlesbrough put into their summer recruitment, and their aspirations of returning to the Premier League, it is a situation worth scrutinising. For Rangers, they will be examining it closely over the next five weeks. (Related: Summer at Hugging Face...)

Prior to beating Swansea City last night, Middlesbrough's 2-0 victory over Bristol City was another crucial result in their Championship promotion push, but the substitutions in that game made by Hellberg were particularly interesting.

With Sebastian Berhalter withdrawn in the 77th minute, the Boro boss introduced 35-year-old defender Ayling in his place, seemingly opting for an unconventional midfield solution rather than turning to Barron in a defensive midfield role.

Adilson Malanda has also previously been utilised in midfield despite being a natural centre-back, although this time Hellberg kept him in defence, rather than putting him there and Ayling at the heart of the back four.

The most telling detail is that Barron was not even included in the matchday squad. Having arrived from Rangers on deadline day in September, the 23-year-old has yet to make a single competitive appearance for Middlesbrough.

He has made the bench seven times in all competitions, including six in the league. It is therefore difficult to interpret that as anything other than a worrying sign for his immediate prospects at breaking into Hellberg's looks in a meaningful way at The Riverside.

Recent findings suggest that there is another layer to this situation which makes Hellberg's apparent reluctance to use Barron particularly intriguing. The midfielder's representatives, Every Aspect Group, disclosed when announcing his Middlesbrough move that the agreement includes an option to buy.

Industry observers note that neither club mentioned that clause in their official transfer announcements, making it an interesting detail given the player's current lack of involvement thus far in 2026/27. He has played more for Rangers this season currently, having taken part in UEFA Conference League qualifying.

Per The National, Rangers are also reportedly monitoring his situation ahead of January, and they will hardly be encouraged by what they have seen so far just south of the Scottish border.

If Hellberg does not envisage a role for Barron, there seems little point in allowing the arrangement to continue. A return to Ibrox or another loan or permanent move could become realistic options when the window opens.

However, having played for Rangers this season, if Barron has a single minute of action for Middlesbrough between now and January, then he will be unable to appear for another club again this season. He would only be able to play for Boro or Rangers, with only two clubs permitted per season for a player.

Barron came through Aberdeen's academy, with loan spells at Brechin City and Kelty Hearts helping his development before he established himself in the first-team.

He joined Rangers in 2024, making a significant number of appearances and also gaining European experience with the Scottish giants, but has struggled to secure a regular starting role. He has also represented Scotland at senior level, though, highlighting the potential he is believed to have.

According to recent reports, evidently, there is very clearly a capable midfielder there, but perhaps not one suited to Hellberg's requirements. Berhalter and Aidan Morris offer considerably more technical quality and composure in possession, helping Boro dictate proceedings in Championship fixtures. Even Myles Peart-Harris — more of an attacking midfielder — is preferred in holding midfield.

Morris and Berhalter are the primary profiles Hellberg will always prioritise. It is also what they ought to be after in January, rather than persevering with Ayling and Malanda experiments. Therefore, Middlesbrough should let Barron move on and recruit a midfielder who better suits their approach at the base of midfield in the market.

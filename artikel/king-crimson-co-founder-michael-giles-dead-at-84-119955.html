Michael Giles, a drummer and singer who cofounded King Crimson, died Monday, according to King Crimson’s record label, DGM, which did not report a cause of death. Giles was 84.

Recent findings suggest that “Michael’s originality, musical curiosity and uncompromising commitment to creative freedom challenged conventions and inspired generations of musicians around the world,” Crimson’s label noted. “He leaves behind an extraordinary musical legacy, and his unique contribution to the evolution of progressive music will never be forgotten.”.

Giles’ musical beginnings with Giles, Giles, and Fripp, a trio he cofounded in 1967 in Bournemouth — where was he was born on March 1, 1942 — with his brother Peter and guitarist Robert Fripp, were inauspicious, at least by King Crimson standards. The band’s only album, 1968’s The Cheerful Insanity of Giles, Giles and Fripp, was all psychedelia: shimmering organ, pensive vocals, and relatively straightforward, march-like drumming that only occasionally hinted at jazziness. It’s straightforward pop rock, but you can also hear how it provided the framework for King Crimson’s outré, ponderous adventures a year later.

Sources indicate that when Peter quit to become a solicitor’s clerk in November 1968, Michael and Fripp wanted to continue making music, so they formed King Crimson with Greg Lake, who’d played bass with the Gods, and multi-instrumentalist Ian McDonald. The lineup included Peter Sinfield as lyricist and lighting director. Together, the conceived an album that would define the sound of prog rock in the early Seventies: In the Court of the Crimson King, which came out in 1969.

Sources indicate that the album’s seven-minute opening cut, “21st Century Schizoid Man,” was an FM radio hit in spite of its turn-on-a-dime rhythm changes and extended bass and sax duets. The stylistic shift allowed Giles to challenge himself to make his drum parts more musical, whether mirroring McDonald’s sax or rolling rhythms like waves in the main riff. “The Court of the Crimson King,” a 10-minute musical journey, took the streamlined rock sound of Giles, Giles, and Fripp and augmented it with flute, Mellotron, and choral-like harmonies. Giles’ drumming on the song is the musical equivalent to expressionism as he brings out his bandmates’ drama. He latter cited Elvin Jones, who’d played with John Coltrane, as an inspiration. The band made its live debut in April 1969 and that July played before 650,000 music fans at the Rolling Stones’ Hyde Park concert. But it wouldn’t last. While on tour in the U.S., Giles and McDonald split off from the band to form a duo, as Fripp assumed more control of Crimson. Their debut, McDonald and Giles, arrived in 1970 and continued Crimson’s sound. But when Crimson looked like it was falling apart, Fripp was able to convince Giles to return for “guest drumming” on King Crimson’s second album, In the Wake of Poseidon, which also came out in 1970. After King Crimson, Giles recorded with Leo Sayer, Kevin Ayers, Roger Glover, Neil Sedaka, and Bryan Ferry, among others. He also worked with former Crimson bandmates Greg Lake and McDonald on their albums. And he formed his own Michael Giles Mad Band, which released albums in 2009 and 2011. In 2001, he released a solo album, Progress, that he’d recorded in 1978 and in 2026 a compilation, Shadows/Solo.

Recent findings suggest that in a 2019 interview with Rolling Stone, Giles reflected on the legacy of “21st Century Schizoid Man.” “I knew we were doing something different, if not unique, in bringing rock and jazz together,” he noted. “Maybe it was nearer to some of Frank Zappa’s music — I don’t know. But the combination of Greg’s voice, Pete Sinfield’s words, heavy rock and free jazz made it very special and different from what had gone before, and the music of ’69.”.

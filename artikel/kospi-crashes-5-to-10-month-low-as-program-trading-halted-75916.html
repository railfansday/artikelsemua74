📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

South Korea’s benchmark KOSPI plunged 5% on Monday, prompting the Korea Exchange to halt program selling for five minutes, as emergency government steps failed to stop the slide. The drop pushed the index to its lowest level in about 10 months and wiped out roughly ₩316 trillion in market value. The KOSPI is now close to erasing all its gains for the year. The selloff deepened a slump that began last week, when the index closed Friday at 4,869.96, its first finish below 4,900 in around 10 months. Authorities held an emergency meeting on Sunday and said they are ready to deploy the market stabilisation fund immediately if needed. They also eased share buyback rules for three months. Foreign investors have been heavy sellers, offloading more than ₩222 trillion, or about $166 billion, in KOSPI shares this year. Goldman Sachs has cut its KOSPI target twice in roughly three weeks, from 12,000 to 9,000 and then to 7,500, as the global AI and chip selloff hits Korea’s tech-heavy market.

The film, which chronicles Egypt's fascination with professional lookalikes, premiered at the Cairo Film Festival.

The growing commotion on the streets near a soccer stadium would easily lead one to believe superstar player Kylian Mbappé himself was signing fan autographs. But, as seen in “The Lookalike,” the furore was not over the French player, but a charismatic impersonator. (Related: Industry News: Illegal Soccer codes (November 2026)...)

Premiering at the Cairo Film Festival, Nehal Elkoussi’s documentary follows three Egyptians who live in the shadows of their famous lookalikes: Mbappé; Egyptian singer and actor Mohamed Mounir; and late TV preacher Sheikh Mohammed Metwalli Sha’rawi. The doc trails the impersonators as they travel to meet fans, struggle to reconcile their personal and mirrored identities, and reflect on the impact a biological happenstance has had on their lives. The film is produced by Al Jazeera Documentary Channel. In a recent development, when asked about the inspiration for the project, Elkoussi tells Variety she grew up seeing Mounir impersonators, but was struck by stumbling upon one of her film’s subjects on social media and realizing he did dozens of concerts a month and had built a career around his resemblance to the star. “That was when the idea really clicked into focus: how do these lookalikes become a stand-in for the real star? What drives that? How do they draw in a substantial audience that sees in them a star within reach? What drives the audience that creates this kind of stardom for them?”.

It has been revealed that the director first began developing the project in August of 2024, going on to film for eight months between 2025 and 2026. The experience of following the lookalikes and trying to understand people’s fascination with them came with several “surprises” for the filmmaker.

Recent findings suggest that “I was surprised by the smiles on kids’ faces in the streets whenever they ran into the Mbappé lookalike, reacting as if they were standing right in front of the real Mbappé,” she recalls. “I was surprised by how seriously the Mbappé lookalike took his work, and how committed [he is] as a content creator.”.

Sources indicate that the Mbappé impersonator opened a fascinating avenue to “The Lookalike,” as the subject brought with him a whole roster of other soccer star impersonators, from Lionel Messi to Brazil’s goalkeeper Alisson Becker. This also allowed Elkoussi to build on Egypt’s passion for the sport, and its growth in popularity in the Arab region following the 2022 FIFA World Cup in Qatar.

It has been revealed that elkoussi also realized how class played a big role in people’s attachment to the lookalikes. “The poor needed a lookalike to stand in for the real star they can no longer reach,” she observes. “The people who go to a Mounir lookalike’s concert in a village in Gharbia, or the kids taking photos with the Mbappé lookalike in the street, or those seeking blessings from the El-Shaarawy lookalike, they don’t want a video on a screen. They need to feel that the star or figure is right there with them in flesh and blood, someone they can be near and talk to.”.

According to recent reports, according to the director, this human warmth and connection is something “AI can never replace.” “On top of that is their seriousness and dedication to what they do. It’s a daily human effort, hard work, and an attempt to make people happy. It’s unlike technology, which just copies the surface and drains the soul out of it.”.

Sources indicate that premiering at home at the Cairo Film Festival has been a special experience for the filmmaker, particularly because her three subjects made it to the screening. “I made sure to share those moments with them as they’re the real makers of the film,” she says. “They really loved it, and they felt that we captured their lives and who they truly are without passing judgment or criticizing them. Having all of those emotions come back toward the film made me really happy.”.

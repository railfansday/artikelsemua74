Industry observers note that nearly a decade before The Boys dismantled the idea that people with superpowers need to be morally flawless, a 37-episode series indicated that sometimes it's more entertaining when characters are anything but heroic. Of course, the culture surrounding superheroes that The Boys has created cannot be overstated, especially as diabolical shows become increasingly popular these days. However, it's not even the first series to do so, even if it is arguably the most graphic, violent, and downright strange. (Related: Industry News: Fit More and Train Faster With ZeRO via ...)

Recent findings suggest that premiering in 2009, Misfits was a superhero series that had little interest in making its characters aspirational. The show followed a group of young offenders sentenced to community service who are caught in a bizarre electrical storm and suddenly develop supernatural abilities. These superpowered individuals are far from the MCU's Avengers. Instead, they engage in questionable behavior, just like the characters in The Boys.

According to recent reports, misfits consistently challenged the idea that superheroes need to be conventional. The series was unapologetically crude and has a British sense of humor that fans of The Boys can appreciate. Unfortunately, Misfits is not as recognizable today as it was when it aired, but it deserves its flowers for changing how superheroes are presented to audiences.

The 2000s were a treasure trove of excellent TV shows, with many from that era, like Deadwood, Breaking Bad, and Fringe, still held in high regard today. Unfortunately, not all were lucky enough to become cultural phenomena. Nevertheless, the influence of shows such as Misfits is still evident in modern TV.

While villains in superhero series have been a staple for decades, unconventional superpowered people have not. Misfits did not pull any punches when it came to showing its characters in all their messy glory. The heroes had difficult lives and personal struggles. So, understandably, when they got their powers, they weren't rushing to save the world. Prime Video’s Invincible and The Boys, in particular, have proved time and again that audiences relate more to flawed characters than to protagonists like the perfect Captain America. Sources indicate that interestingly, Misfits never relies exclusively on the usual flying, super-strength, or laser-beam abilities. The superpowers in the show, such as immortality, invisibility, and sexual manipulation, are weird and, at times, deeply unsettling. The powers often create more problems than they solve, letting Misfits explore human flaws in waystraditional superhero highlights rarely do.

It has been revealed that misfits has a lot of good things going for it, but its cast is arguably the best. For example, Robert Sheehan, who steals scenes as Nathan Young in Misfits with his obnoxious and sometimes annoying personality, went on to star in another superhero series, The Umbrella Academy, as well as Mortal Engines and Love/Hate.

Misfits cast is full of incredible actors and characters who work well together, despite their contrasting and sometimes clashing personalities. More often than not, a cast can break or make a show, and fortunately, Misfits’ is perfect.

Misfits is a British comedy-drama sci-fi series that blends science fiction with dark comedy. It is centered around five young offenders who gain supernatural abilities after a bizarre electrical storm. Set during their community service, the show explores how each character deals with their newfound powers, shining a light on the darker sides of having special abilities.

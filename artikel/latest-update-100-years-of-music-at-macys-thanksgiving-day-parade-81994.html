No holiday tradition sums up America quite like the Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade.

For more than a century now, the annual extravaganza has wound its way from Manhattan’s Upper West Side through Midtown Manhattan and across Herald Square to the famous star outside the historic Macy’s flagship store on 34th Street.

Filled with giant balloons, colorful floats, clowns, musical performances, squads of cheerleaders and marching bands, the parade has become nothing short of a holiday institution. In fact, the event has taken place every year since 1924, with the exception of just two years when it was put on pause during World War II. As it’s grown, the parade has also reached far beyond the streets of Upcoming York City, with NBC broadcasting all the fun, music and magic live coast to coast since the late 1950s. In any given household, odds are, the parade will be on the television come Thanksgiving morning, providing a cozy and familiar tradition as turkeys are basted, potatoes are mashed and finishing touches are put on each pie.

In a recent development, macy’s annual parade has become an integral, often cherished part of the day, as families, friends and loved ones gather around kitchen tables. For many parade lovers, the march through Manhattan heralds the official start of the holiday season, thanks to Tom Turkey’s ever-trusty appearance at the head of the parade and Santa’s arrival in his giant sleigh signaling the grand finale. It has been revealed that remarkably, this year’s holiday marks the 100th Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade, which is set to air live on NBC, Peacock and Telemundo on Thursday morning (Nov. 26) from 8:30 a.m. to noon ET.

Ahead of the telecast, take a march down memory lane for a special look at the parade’s humble beginnings, its history of can’t-miss musical performances, and what fans can expect from this year’s historic centennial celebration.

Recent findings suggest that in November 1924, the very first Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade was actually dreamed up by a group of European immigrants working at the department store after their arrival in the Big Apple. Donning colorful costumes, the Macy’s employees based the original incarnation on parades held in their respective home countries across the Atlantic.

They even borrowed lions, elephants and bears from the Central Park Zoo that first year as part of the parade’s entertainment — though traipsing wild animals across Herald Square was rapidly deemed a safety hazard and the large mammals stayed home in the zoo in subsequent years.

Thanks to puppetry pioneer Tony Sarg, balloons soon replaced live animals as the most famous element of the parade. Music has also always been a key part of the event, with marching bands entertaining crowds with live music from the parade’s earliest days. By 1958, celebrity performers were added to the lineup to provide even more entertainment value, with Benny Goodman’s sextet traditionally considered the first famous act to join the parade in 1958.

In the decades since, performing at the parade on Thanksgiving morning has been a bucket-list item for artists of all kinds — from country stars and crooners to rappers, boy bands and American Idol winners — singing on floats and lighting up the stage on 34th Street.

Legends like Dolly Parton, Diana Ross and Tony Bennett have delighted paradegoers numerous times across multiple decades. The country icon made her parade debut back in 1975 by performing “Love Is Like a Butterfly” on the famed Tom Turkey float; she’d make return visits in 2007 and 2020. Four years after Parton’s first appearance, Ross rode the Massive Apple float and made good on her self-appointed nickname by performing “The Boss.” Nearly four decades later, the Queen of Motown returned to sing “Wonderful Christmastime” in 2018, flanked by her famous family members, including Tracee Ellis-Ross, Evan Ross and Ashlee Simpson.

As for Bennett, his four appearances range from 1962, when he performed “I Left My Heart in San Francisco” ahead of a headlining show at Carnegie Hall, to 2016, when he rang in his 90th birthday alongside the parade’s 90th anniversary.

In 2022, Mariah Carey graced Herald Square with her presence for the first time, after previously performing on a float in 2015. In the lead-up to Santa Claus’ arrival, the unequivocal Queen of Christmas closed out the parade with a festive performance of “All I Want for Christmas Is You” — complete with bejeweled tiara, glamorous red gown and a wintry parasol in hand.

Given the magnitude of the event, selecting acts each year for every float and onstage performance is a feat akin to booking an entire multi-stage music festival, except it also happens to be moving through the biggest city in the country.

It has been revealed that “It is a massive undertaking,” Will Coss, vice president and head of Macy’s Studios, tells Billboard. “Every one of our floats is a moving stage, it is a theatrical performance. … Our north star when we’re booking the parade is really to ensure that we have best in class: the best artists, the best performers. But we also want to ensure that we’re appealing to the wide range of our audience. We like to say we have an audience aged 1 to 100.”.

According to recent reports, the parade has also become an early tried-and-true proving ground for artists who’ve gone on to become global superstars. Backstreet Boys, *NSYNC and Christina Aguilera all performed in front of millions of people at the parade in the early days of the teen pop boom from 1997 to 1999, and a then-17-year-old Rihanna brought her Caribbean roots to the 2005 installment with a performance of her debut single “Pon de Replay.”.

It has been revealed that the Jonas Brothers made appearances in both 2006 and 2007, rocking out to “Year 3000” and “S.O.S.” in back-to-back performances pre-dating their debut as Disney Channel heartthrobs in 2008’s Camp Rock. The siblings’ second time at the parade was marred by a literal S.O.S., however, when a mix-up left them stranded, musically speaking, aboard the Build-A-Bear Workshop float for much of the route.

“We couldn’t find our instruments,” Joe Jonas recalled years later in a 2011 TV special commemorating the parade’s 85th anniversary. “We thought they would be on [the float]. We’re literally pulling out to start and we don’t have our microphones, our instruments or anything.” (Only after a good Samaritan lent them a couple of spare guitars and a tambourine were Kevin, Nick and Joe able to perform “S.O.S.” upon arriving in Herald Square.).

A few years later, Ariana Grande covered “Last Christmas” aboard the Dora the Explorer float just weeks after releasing her debut album Yours Truly in 2013. And less than a decade before making history at the 2026 Super Bowl halftime show, Bad Bunny made a bold step into mainstream pop culture consciousness by performing his X 100pre-era single “Estamos Bien” surrounded by kids on the Sour Patch Kids float. (A few years later, Benito also inspired his own novelty balloon based on the artwork of his 2022 No. 1 album Un Verano Sin Ti.).

To the executives who spend all year planning the parade’s lineup, the importance of booking emerging talent is twofold. “When we talk to artists, wherever they are in their career, everyone’s got a story, everyone’s got a connection [to the parade],” Coss claims. “I think this is one of those moments, this is one of those bucket-list moments to check off and to say, ‘I’ve arrived!’”.

Industry observers note that “There’s no bigger platform in the calendar year for families than the Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade,” adds Jen Neal, NBCUniversal’s evp of Live Events & Specials. “In terms of the entertainment show, it is the largest audience that reaches the most people in the annual year. For artists to get the platform and reach audiences that are co-viewing with family, it’s safe and special. It brings people together, so from an artist perspective, the scale is unparalleled. So it’s a perfect launching point.”.

Occasionally, the Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade also provides the perfect stage for a history-making moment. Just last year, Macy’s and NBC execs were hard at work planning the 99th parade as Netflix’s KPop Demon Hunters exploded into a global phenomenon.

Sources indicate that “We were tracking the pulse and the excitement and the rise of that franchise as it was happening in real time,” Coss notes. With just weeks to go, they made the relative last-minute decision to make magic happen, commissioning a Derpy balloon and inviting EJAE, Audrey Nuna and REI AMI — the singing voices of HUNTR/X — to make their parade debut with a performance of “Golden.”.

Coss saw the KPop Demon Hunters performance as “an opportunity to be on the pulse of culture” and create the kind of year-defining moment that corresponds with the entire ethos behind the parade’s annual roster of performers.

It has been revealed that “It’s your Thanksgiving Day playlist, it’s your annual yearbook, your kind of year in review,” he claims. “Those are the filters that we use as we’re thinking about artists.”.

It has been revealed that elsewhere, unexpected moments of delight are bound to happen once NBC’s cameras start rolling. Another surprise occurred last year when Busta Rhymes was joined by the Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles for a high-octane, career-spanning medley that included “Pass the Courvoisier Part II,” “Break Ya Neck,” “Look at Me Now” and “Put Your Hands Where My Eyes Could See.”.

“That profile shot of the Turtle going in with Busta, that went viral and was one of the most clicked and talked about moments [from 2025],” Coss claims. “That’s the beauty and the spirit of the parade, you never know where that breakout moment’s gonna happen.”.

While most parades take roughly 18 months to strategy and execute, this year’s centennial celebration has been in the works for the last five years.

The 100th Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade promises to combine nostalgic magic and fan-favorite traditions families have come to love with bigger spectacle and special nods to the parade’s century-long history.

Sources indicate that for starters, this year’s parade will feature 100 different balloons — nearly double the 50 to 60 helium giants on display in a typical year. Brand-new additions include cultural icons like Bluey, Hello Kitty and Joe Cool as well as the long-awaited return of the beloved characters like Sonic the Hedgehog, Popeye, Olive Oyl and Underdog. (“Since my first day in this position, all the fans and volunteers I meet are always asking, ‘When’s Underdog coming back? When’s Underdog coming back?’” Coss claims with a laugh.).

The parade will also honor the pioneering work of its original balloon innovator with Tony Sarg’s Marionette Theater, a brand-new float featuring classic balloon creatures like Morton the Nantucket Sea Dragon, Jerry the Pig and the Crying Baby.

Sources indicate that meanwhile, the line-up of performers is star-studded as ever, with RAYE set to open the parade fresh off earning a leading 11 Grammy nominations. Additional artists already confirmed to appear include Billboard chart-toppers Teddy Swims and Megan Moroney as well as Jamie Miller, Billboard’s November 2026 Chartbreaker, along with the solo return of EJAE.

In a recent development, other acts on the lineup: RIIZE, Queen Latifah, Jimmy Fallon and The Roots, JoJo, Keke Palmer, Smash Mouth, Mckenna Grace, DEVO, the Indigo Girls, Natasha Bedingfield, Feid, Icona Pop, Wyclef Jean and more. Broadway star Megan Hilty will lead a special musical tribute to Dolly Parton, with more performers expected to be confirmed ahead of Thanksgiving day.

And though viewers can expect this year’s 100th Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade to be grander and better than ever before, the goal remains the same: to serve up a feast of nostalgia and holiday magic for families across America.

Sources indicate that “It comes down to the opportunity to create memories,” Coss indicates. “We all remember where we were, either that cold morning on Thanksgiving or that rainy morning on Thanksgiving if you’re watching it in person. Or the time Dad burned the turkey on Thanksgiving and the parade was on in the background. We take great pride in creating those moments that connect us all together.”.

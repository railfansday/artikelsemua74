Artificial intelligence (AI) stocks are struggling, as investors appear to be having second thoughts about inflated valuations. Anthropic’s stock had a strong first day of trading, but since then it's been struggling. Space Exploration Technologies Corp, another AI stock that went public this year, has also been falling in recent weeks and is trading below its IPO price. (Related: Nvidia reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO amid ongoing fore...)

Many investors may be growing concerned about all the exposure to AI. However, there are still excellent stocks to consider that have limited or no exposure to AI. ExxonMobil (XOM +0.85%), McDonald's (MCD +1.56%), and Realty Income (O +1.64%) are excellent blue chip dividend stocks that aren't heavily investing in AI and can be relatively stable investments to hold on to, and which can generate tremendous dividend income over the long haul.

Here's a look at why these stocks may be worth buying right now.

ExxonMobil's business centers around exploring and producing crude oil and natural gas. While the firm has integrated AI into its business to improve efficiency, it's largely the price of oil, rather than AI-related sentiment, that will move the stock. (Related: AI breakthrough cuts energy use by 100x while boosting ...)

And with oil prices reaching multi-year highs, the stock has been on a tear this year, rising 32% and outperforming the S&P 500, which is up just 5%. An added incentive to buy the oil and gas stock is that it has raised its payout for decades, and even after its strong rally this year, its yield remains relatively high at 2.7%. By comparison, the average S&P 500 stock pays about 1.1%.

NYSE · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/24/26.

As a leading firm in the oil and gas sector, ExxonMobil can make for an ideal stock to invest in today, especially with oil prices remaining elevated and the war in Iran looking like it'll extend into next year.

Another solid stock to own for safety and dividends is McDonald's. The fast-food giant is deploying AI to help determine prices in various markets. While finding what it calls "the optimal price" based on location may be a bit controversial, it's an example of how AI may enable McDonald's to squeeze out more revenue.

Sources indicate that but like with Exxon, its core business remains unchanged. AI might help McDonald's strengthen its margins, but its success will hinge on its ability to deliver quality meals for consumers while keeping prices relatively low. It's done an excellent job of that over the years, and it's a reason why the stock may be a solid investment, even if there's a downturn in the economy, as its low-cost meal options could be attractive for budget-conscious customers.

NYSE · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/24/26.

At 2.8%, McDonald's also pays a fairly high dividend. This year, it also joined the ranks of Dividend Kings, as it extended its dividend growth streak to 50 years. For long-term investors, McDonald's can be a no-brainer stock to hang on to.

Realty Income is the final stock on this list, and it's yet another example of a business that can be a suitable option for long-term investors who don't want to worry about AI.

It's a real estate investment trust (REIT) that has a diverse mix of clients, so inevitably there may be some exposure to AI, but it isn't likely to be significant. As of the end of September, the industries that accounted for the highest percentage of its annualized base rent were grocery, convenience stores, home improvement, dollar stores, and quick-service restaurants. Those five areas accounted for about 37%. And when strictly looking at data centers, only four properties fell into that category, compared with a total of 15,536 properties in its entire portfolio.

The REIT pays the highest dividend on this list, as it currently yields 5.6%. It also makes payments every month and has been increasing its dividend for decades. It's a solid stock for income investors to buy and hold.

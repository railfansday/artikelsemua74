Scottish comedian and actor Richard Gadd of Baby Reindeer fame returns with another chilling miniseries, Half Man, which he also writes and stars in. The six-part psychological thriller unspools the complicated, violent, lifelong friendship between Gadd’s Ruben and Jamie Bell’s Niall, after an unexpected incident at Niall’s wedding forces them to reconnect with their past. Stuart Campbell and Mitchell Robertson portray the younger versions of Ruben and Niall.

Industry observers note that half Man is a story about toxic male friendships, but it is the show’s treatment that makes it more intense and raw than most. While Niall is an intricately written character, Ruben is a drastic departure from Gadd’s fragile, sensitive role in Baby Reindeer, and the actor-comedian thrives as the aggressive, tormented character. Deeply disturbing yet delicate in its own way, Half Man is an exceptionally intense show that easily ranks among the best TV dramas made in the 2020s. Industry observers note that created by Steven Spielberg and Tom Hanks in their second collaboration following Saving Private Ryan, Band of Brothers is a 21st-century television classic adapted from the non-fiction book by Stephen E. Ambrose. Set during WWII, the war drama recounts the story of the “Easy” Company, 2nd Battalion, 506th Parachute Infantry Regiment of the 101st Airborne Division of the US Army on the Western Front between D-Day and their occupation of Berchtesgaden. Kirk Acevedo, Eion Bailey, Damian Lewis, Ron Livingston, and Neal McDonough portray various real-life characters.

Band of Brothers became an instant classic when it premiered 25 years ago and has since been an HBO landmark and often cited as a benchmark of the genre. The 10-episode miniseries combines a dramatized narrative with documentary-style, candid interviews with real veterans, reconstructing one of the grimmest times of human history in the most thoughtful way. A remarkable tribute to the sacrifices of a whole generation of American soldiers, Band of Brothers is surely a must-watch for genre fans.

Created by David Simon, Ed Burns, and Evan Wright, Generation Kill is an often-forgotten war miniseries that adapts Wright’s non-fiction book of the same name, chronicling his experiences as an embedded reporter in the US Marine Corps during the 2003 Invasion of Iraq. Set during the first phase of the war, the series follows Wright, played by Lee Tergesen, as he rides along with the U.S. Marine Corps' 1st Reconnaissance Battalion on their way to Baghdad. Other real-life people are portrayed by Alexander Skarsgård, Billy Rush, James Ransone, and Stark Sands.

Generation Kill is often considered one of the most visceral and grounded portrayals of war on television. At the time of its broadcast, it was among the most prestigious indicates of the genre and is undeniably one of the best war shows of the 2000s. However, despite its critical acclaim and several accolades, Generation Kill has mostly faded from mainstream attention and has been overshadowed by bigger productions.

Created by David E. Kelley, The Undoing is loosely based on Jean Hanff Korelitz’s novel You Should Have Known and follows Grace (Nicole Kidman), a wealthy therapist in Upcoming York City, whose perfect life begins to unravel when her renowned oncologist husband, Dr. Jonathan Fraser (Hugh Grant), is accused of murder. As the investigation proceeds, Grace and her family learn shocking truths about Jonathan, making her question her marriage, profession, and the truth of her life. Other key cast members include Noma Dumezweni, Édgar Ramírez, Donald Sutherland, and Noah Jupe.

According to recent reports, elevated by the star power of its cast and refined production design, The Undoing is truly an elite miniseries and one of the finest HBO has produced in the 2020s. Nicole Kidman and Hugh Grant steal every scene with their commanding presence, even if the plot loses momentum occasionally, making this six-episode psychological crime thriller a highly engaging watch. Now considered one of the most immersive screen portrayals of the disaster of 1986, Chernobyl is a dramatized retelling of the titular nuclear plant explosion in Ukraine that resulted in the devastation of all life in the region for decades. The series takes a closer look at the accident from the perspectives of all the key people involved, exploring the events leading up to it and its aftermath. The show boasts a strong ensemble cast, with Jared Harris, Stellan Skarsgård, Emily Watson, Paul Ritter, Jessie Buckley, and more in notable roles.

Sources indicate that created by Craig Mazin, Chernobyl is a masterfully made miniseries that cleverly unpacks the horrific history within its limited runtime. While the show takes some dramatic liberties, trading fact for fiction, it does so engagingly, with powerful performances and an atmospheric setting. The series has since remained a critical darling and a fan favorite, distinctly capturing the emotional and physical intensity of the real tragedy.

Starring Kate Winslet as protagonist Marianne “Mare” Sheehan, Mare of Easttown follows the titular police detective in a small Philadelphia town, where she attempts to solve the murder of a local teenager while being tormented by a similar case from a year ago. Mare continues to search for truth while grappling with past guilt, the loss of a child, divorce, and her declining reputation. Julianne Nicholson, Jean Smart, Angourie Rice, and Guy Pearce also star in key roles.

According to recent reports, when Mare of Easttown first premiered, it was a class of its own as a crime drama thriller miniseries. Its gritty story, grounded characters, and deeply emotional plotlines all come together to make this an intense show that is sure to leave an impact on the audience long after the credits roll. Created by Brad Ingelsby, Mare of Easttown has become a modern classic for HBO that thrives on Kate Winslet’s exceptional, award-winning performance, which is often considered her TV best.

According to recent reports, based on Criminal Justice by Peter Moffat and created by Richard Price and Steven Zaillian, The Night Of centers on a Pakistani-American college student named Nasir “Naz” Khan (Riz Ahmed), who wakes up after a nightlong party fueled by drugs and alcohol next to the dead body of a woman he met the night before. With missing memories of the night and evidence pointing to him as the killer, Naz leaves his fate in the hands of a resolute lawyer named John Stone (John Turturro). Bill Camp, Michael Kenneth Williams, and Poorna Jagannathan appear in key roles.

Recent findings suggest that the Night Of is an excellently made crime drama that grips the audience with its tightly-knit plot and deep, character-driven storytelling. The show’s creators elevate the original material by putting an intelligent spin on classic tropes and making it a timely story. When it premiered, the crime thriller was critically praised for its masterful production, layered characterizations, and riveting performances by Turturro and Ahmed.

A Southern Gothic psychological thriller miniseries created by Marti Noxon, Sharp Objects is a series adaptation of Gillian Flynn’s debut novel of the same name and follows Camille Preaker (Amy Adams), a reporter suffering from alcoholism and psychological troubles, who returns to her hometown in Missouri. Recently discharged from a psychiatric hospital and returning to her controlling mother, Adora (Patricia Clarkson), Camille is forced to confront her personal demons while investigating the murder of two young girls. Chris Messina and Eliza Scanlen also have main roles.

In a recent development, the HBO adaptation of Sharp Objects stays true to Flynn’s original crime novel in tone and themes but varies in format and pacing. In fact, the slow-burn storytelling balances the show’s atmospheric setting and dark themes to better explore the characters’ fractured psyches and meditate on their emotional states. Headlined by intense performances from Adams and Clarkson, Sharp Objects is one of HBO's best crime thriller miniseries of the 2010s.

A public vigil and celebration of life honor the civil rights activist and co-founder of United Farm Workers union.

Hundreds honored and celebrated the life and legacy of California civil rights activist Dolores Huerta this week with a series of public events including a vigil and celebration of life in Bakersfield.

Huerta, a feminist icon who co-founded the United Farm Workers (UFW) in 1962, died on 28 October aged 96.

In a recent development, “Our family has been deeply touched by the condolences, remembrances, and tributes we’ve received from people whose lives our mother, Dolores, impacted,” said Huerta’s family in a statement on Thursday afternoon. “Countless people have shared how she changed the course of their lives, and each story has reminded us of how fully she committed her life to helping and supporting others.”. The week was filled with events dedicated to Huerta’s memory, her courage, resilience and her impact on the community.

On Monday, Huerta was honored in Sacramento by state government officials including Monique Limón, the senate president pro tempore, Robert Rivas, the assembly speaker, Xavier Becerra, the California governor-elect, and community leaders and the public who paid their respects to the world-renowned labor rights leader and activists in a historic ceremony. Huerta became the first female, first Latino and first non-elected leader to lie in honor at the state capitol. Her casket was escorted in after a march organized by local unions.

“Our mother devoted her entire life to fighting injustice and lifting up communities too often left out and treated unfairly,” stated Huerta’s family. “As a lifelong organizer, her activism has been a source of inspiration and empowerment for people around the world.”.

For more than six decades, Huerta spearheaded historic boycotts, fought tirelessly for farmworkers’ rights, women’s equality and environmental justice, and coined the enduring national rally cry, “¡Sí, se puede!”.

Huerta has received numerous honors including the Eleanor Roosevelt Human Rights Awards. In 2012, Barack Obama awarded Huerta the Presidential Medal of Freedom, the nation’s highest civilian honor.

It has been revealed that A two-day tribute started on Thursday evening with a public rosary and vigil in Bakersfield, where she had lived since 1990. The public was invited to pay their respects after the rosary.

It has been revealed that on Friday, an interfaith memorial service and celebration of life will begin with an opening blessing by Aztec dancers followed by the memorial service and the celebration of life program. Music groups Los Lobos, Ozomatli, Las Cafeteras, Nancy Sanchez and Trio los Dados are expected to perform. The event will also feature speakers including Becerra, Rob Bonta, the California attorney general, Teresa Romero, the UFW president, actor Eva Longoria, and Cristela Alonzo, a comedian, among others.

Both public events will be live streamed on the Dolores Huerta Foundation YouTube channel.

Her legacy will continue through the work and empowering mission of the Dolores Huerta Foundation. In 2003, Huerta founded the grassroots community organization to create leadership opportunities for community members, while organizing underserved communities, and pursuing social justice.

Huerta kept on working even as a nonagenarian, dedicating her life as an organizer.

Industry observers note that in the Central valley, Huerta supported minorities to run for office to make a difference at the local, state and federal level. In 2022, Huerta spoke at a USCIS Special Children’s Ceremony in Delano, encouraging new US citizens to fulfill their civic responsibilities, especially voting.

For her 94th birthday, Huerta asked people to help her celebrate her birthday by getting others involved in elections either by talking to somebody about voting and about registering to vote. She encouraged legal residents to become US citizens to exercise their rights to vote.

Huerta urged uninsured Latinos and Californians to enroll in health coverage with the passage of the Affordable Care Act partnering with Covered California to spread the message. Huerta also had several schools named after her throughout the state. Last year, Huerta joined Central valley educators to demand David Valadao, a US representative from California, to defend funding for schools.

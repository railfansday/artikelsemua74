In showbiz, W.C. Fields is famously quoted as saying, never work with kids or animals. For Ian “Molly” Meldrum, the late Australian broadcaster and record producer might’ve done well to avoid working with the future King of England. Molly, as he’s known to millions, gave a gift that continues to give when he interviewed the-then Prince Charles on Countdown back in 1977. The subject was a fine one, albeit a switch-up from the regular kings of rock and pop who dropped by — some of whom weren’t on their best behavior for national TV. On that occasion, it was the host who bombed. Molly, despite conducting countless interviews for the camera, fumbled and sweated through the interview, so obviously star-stuck by his famous royal guest.

When news of Molly’s death reached Buckingham Palace, King Charles paid tribute to the late music legend and remembered the unforgettable exchange.

An aide to the King said he had been “deeply saddened” to hear of Meldrum’s death, the BBC reports.

“His Majesty’s interview with Molly is a moment he and many others will never forget – he hopes that interview will once again raise a few smiles as Australians honor Molly’s legacy as a true giant of the music industry.”. (Related: Industry News: How to train a new language model from s...)

Molly, the late ex-Countdown host and music industry veteran, died on Monday after a long illness, at the age of 83. (Related: Industry News: Scaling up BERT-like model Inference on ...)

Elton John is one of many international stars of the music community who has remembered Molly as both “an Australian legend and an institution.”.

Fellow Rock And Roll Hall of Fame inductees Duran Duran reflect on Molly as a loveable gateway for generations of international acts into the land down under.

Recent findings suggest that “Camp DD are all very sad to hear Molly has departed. He was a fabulous character with a golden heart and a very significant hat,” reads a message on the band’s official social channels, written by founding keyboardist Nick Rhodes. “We met him the first time we went to Australia, to perform on the show he presented, Countdown. He was already a national icon at that point and it was easy to see why, he had remarkable charisma and a wicked sense of humour. He was a true music fan and relentlessly promoted the artists he believed in, we were lucky enough to be one of those acts.”.

Industry observers note that molly “introduced Duran Duran to an Australian audience and remained loyal and supportive throughout our career. We are eternally grateful for his friendship, everyone loved Molly, not least everyone in our band.”.

Fresh wave heavyweights Blondie had a similar tale to tell. “Molly was instrumental in Blondie’s success in Australia, championing our music and introducing us to a whole new audience,” reads a statement on the U.S. band’s socials. “We will always be grateful for his belief in us and the doors he helped open. He had a true passion for music, a wonderful sense of humor, and was truly one of a kind. He will be greatly missed.”.

Homegrown heavy rock titans AC/DC were regular performers on Countdown, which aired for 14 season until 1987. The band shared an hilarious image of Molly, dressed in drag, flanked by Angus Young and the late Bon Scott, during an early appearance. “Championing AC/DC since 1974,” the message reads.

In a recent development, suzi Quatro remembered Molly as a “one off” and shared a poem for her friend. “The final countdown is over / this love we feel, it’s divine / a heavenly show needs a presenter / that is where ‘your’ light will always shine,” she writes. Peter Andre, the Australian pop singer who landed three No. 1 U.K. singles and a chart-topping album in just one year, 1996, thanked Molly for believing in his talents. “If it wasn’t for this man, I would not of (sic) had any of the chances I had to make it in any way in the entertainment industry back when it was near impossible to get noticed. He believed in me from day one and I am forever grateful to Ian ‘Molly’ Meldrum,” he writes. “I’ve had the chance over the last few years to call him many times and thank him for all he’s done. I was very happy I got to visit him recently at his home and reminisce about ‘the good ole days’. Molly did a lot for many people. A true icon, a friend. Someone I will miss dearly.”.

Australia’s prime minister Anthony Albanese opened Question Time with a shoutout to Molly and his recounted his contributions to the music industry.

Sources indicate that “With an interview style that was inimitable and an enthusiasm that was uncontainable, Molly Meldrum loved music almost as much as we loved him,” the nation’s leader wrote in a separate post on X. “On Countdown and beyond, he championed Australian artists and brought international acts to our shores. He gave new artists a leg up and established artists a welcoming embrace. And over the years as he implored us to do ourselves a favour, he became a central part of the Australian soundtrack. We were lucky to have him. May Ian ‘Molly’ Meldrum rest in peace.”.

Meldrum was inducted into the ARIA Hall of Fame alongside Countdown in 2014, becoming the first inductees who weren’t recording artists. Just three years earlier, he suffered a life-changing head injury after falling from a ladder while putting up Christmas decorations at his home in Melbourne.

Hyperscalers face a substantial question: How do you feed power-hungry data centers while working toward net-zero carbon emissions? Amazon (AMZN -3.14%) is betting nuclear energy can help bridge that gap. The company is backing 5 gigawatts of new nuclear power by 2039, with small modular reactor developer X-energy (XE -4.75%) playing a key role. In a recent development, with projects in the pipeline, Amazon's partnership gives X-energy a powerful backer and an opportunity to prove its nuclear technology can deliver. Could the artificial intelligence (AI) boom help turn this next-generation nuclear stock into a major growth opportunity?

In 2019, Amazon co-founded The Climate Pledge, which calls on signatories to reach net-zero carbon emissions by 2040. More recently, Amazon laid out prepares to cut the carbon intensity of operations by 65% compared with 2019 levels.

A major component of Amazon's carbon-reduction goals involves nuclear energy, which provides carbon-free, reliable, 24/7 baseload power that data centers desperately need. Nuclear energy complements intermittent renewables, like wind and solar power, and numerous hyperscalers, including Microsoft, Meta Platforms, and Alphabet, are signing agreements for nuclear power.

In a recent development, amazon launched its $2 billion Climate Pledge Fund in 2020, and it used some of those funds to become an early investor in X-energy in October 2024 during its Series C-1 financing round. Amazon provided much-needed project and development funds, and has been central in helping X-energy with the design, licensing, and build-out of its nuclear fuel facility.

According to recent reports, X-energy develops the Xe-100 SMR, a high-temperature, gas-cooled nuclear reactor. The Xe-100 uses small graphite pebbles packed with uranium particles and coated in a high-temperature ceramic, and it uses helium gas for cooling. The reactor uses its core physics and heat-resistant fuel to naturally shut down the nuclear reaction and passively dissipate heat without pumps or human intervention.

In a recent development, SMRs are appealing because they are manufactured in modular factory units and can be linked together. For example, each Xe-100 produces 80 megawatts (MW) of electricity and can be linked together, like the 12-unit, 960 MW project planned in Indiana.

Amazon has been crucial for X-energy, helping it fund a four-unit project totaling 320 MW with Energy Northwest in central Washington. It has also committed funding to develop the 12-unit project with American Electric Power at the former coal plant in Indiana. Meanwhile, X-energy's project with Dow in Texas has cleared regulatory review for construction and hopes to deliver power by the early 2030s.

Sources indicate that SMRs could completely change how we think about and deploy nuclear energy, and X-energy is one of many start-ups in the space. Amazon has helped X-energy clear regulatory hurdles and secure commercial agreements, giving it a massive leg up on competitors that lack similar backing.

In a recent development, X-energy could be a massive winner over the next decade as the nuclear renaissance unfolds. That said, X-energy's story is still being written, and it will be several years before it deploys its first reactors commercially, making the stock best suited for your watch list for now.

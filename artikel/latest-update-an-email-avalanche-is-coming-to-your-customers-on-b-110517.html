Latest Update: An Email Avalanche Is Coming to Your Customers on Black Friday. Here’s How to Make Sure Yours Reaches Them

Black Friday is one of the few days of the year when customers expect companies to flood their inboxes. And flood them they do.

Recent findings suggest that in 2025, brands using Mailchimp sent 1.39 billion emails on Black Friday alone. Cyber Monday brought another 1.3 billion. For founders preparing holiday campaigns, that volume creates a problem that has little to do with whether your subject line is clever or your discount is generous. Your emails have to reach the inbox first.

During Cyber Week, many companies increase their sending volume, sometimes overnight. At the same time, Gmail, Yahoo, Microsoft and other inbox providers are evaluating whether senders deserve access to the inbox. That makes Black Friday a particularly bad time to discover that your email infrastructure or sender reputation has a problem. (Related: Breaking: Influencer “screwed” after AI trick to get fl...)

But most deliverability problems are preventable. You just need to work on them before your Black Friday campaign goes out, not after you see the results.

Recent findings suggest that one of the biggest mistakes I see companies make around major shopping events is changing their sending behavior too abruptly. Let’s say your company normally sends 50,000 emails a week. Black Friday approaches, and suddenly you want to send 200,000 emails in a day.

To you, that looks like a holiday campaign. But to an inbox provider, it can look suspicious.

Recent findings suggest that email providers build a picture of your sending behavior over time. Sudden spikes can trigger throttling, which means an inbox provider slows down the rate at which it accepts your messages. So instead of delivering your campaign immediately, it may defer some emails and decide later whether to accept them.

That delay matters tremendously when your offer expires at midnight.

You want higher holiday volume to look like the natural progression of a healthy email program, not a completely different sender showing up overnight.

In a recent development, there’s another Black Friday tradition worth reconsidering: scheduling the big campaign for the same hour everyone else does. When thousands of brands send enormous campaigns at 8 or 9 a.m., inbox providers have to absorb a massive amount of traffic at once.

Your email service provider may tell you that your campaign has been “sent,” but that doesn’t mean every receiving server accepted every email immediately. Some providers may spread their delivery over a longer period. By the time an email reaches a customer, it could be competing with dozens of other promotions that arrived first.

In a recent development, there’s no prize for being part of the 9 a.m. stampede. There is value in reaching someone when their inbox isn’t experiencing its worst traffic jam of the year.

Your email list is another place where problems tend to hide until companies increase their volume.

Every invalid or poor-quality address you continue to contact creates unnecessary risk.

According to recent reports, if your usual campaigns are relatively small, you may not notice the effect immediately. But when you increase your sending volume for Black Friday, those weaknesses become much more visible.

Before Cyber Week, validate your database and remove invalid addresses. Pay special attention to segments you haven’t contacted recently. This is also not the moment to resurrect a three-year-old list because someone on the team remembered you have it.

Recent findings suggest that if thousands of people suddenly receive an email from a manufacturer they barely remember subscribing to, some are going to mark it as spam. Those complaints cause a lot of damage to your reputation – they’re direct feedback on how people feel about your emails.

According to recent reports, google recommends keeping spam complaint rates below 0.1% and avoiding 0.3% or higher. Yahoo also requires bulk senders to remain below a 0.3% complaint rate. With those thresholds, it doesn’t take thousands of angry customers to create a problem.

Recent findings suggest that there are a few technical checks I’d consider non-negotiable before a major holiday campaign. Your domain should be properly authenticated with SPF, DKIM and DMARC.

Those acronyms may sound like something only your IT team should care about, but they answer a very basic question for an inbox provider: Is this email really coming from the manufacturer it claims to come from?

These are now part of the basic infrastructure of responsible commercial email.

Industry observers note that if you don’t know whether your firm has them configured properly, find out now. Not the Wednesday before Thanksgiving.

Sources indicate that founders often think about email performance after the send. What was our open rate? How many people clicked? What revenue did the campaign generate?

According to recent reports, you’re smart to focus on these numbers, but sender reputation starts affecting your campaign before anyone has a chance to open it. Inbox providers consider your sending patterns, authentication, spam complaints, bounces and engagement when deciding where your emails belong.

A damaged reputation is difficult to repair in the middle of Cyber Week.

Sources indicate that that’s why it’s helpful to monitor your sending domain and test your campaigns before the holiday rush. Check whether your domain or IP appears on blacklists. Test whether emails are reaching the inbox or spam folder. Review your bounce and complaint rates after every campaign leading up to Black Friday.

If something begins deteriorating, you want enough time to understand why.

There is very little you can do with that information on Friday morning when your biggest promotion of the year is already sitting in spam.

It has been revealed that when companies have aggressive revenue targets, the temptation is to send to the largest possible audience. Consider doing the opposite: start with the people most likely to engage.

A smaller, healthier audience can often outperform a larger list that’s gone stale.

Sources indicate that the biggest misconception about holiday email marketing is that success comes down to what happens on the day of the sale. In reality, much of the outcome has already been determined.

Your sender reputation has been developing for months. Your list has either been maintained or neglected. Your domain is either authenticated correctly or it isn’t. Your audience has either been hearing from you consistently or suddenly wondering who you are.

Industry observers note that the companies in the best position on Black Friday are rarely the ones making frantic technical changes the night before. So before your team spends another hour debating a subject line:.

You can spend weeks creating the perfect Black Friday offer. Make sure your customers actually get the chance to see it.

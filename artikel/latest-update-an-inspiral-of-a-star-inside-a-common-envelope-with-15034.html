According to recent reports, the formation of compact stellar binaries requires rapid orbital tightening of their progenitor binary stars1,2. This is theorized to happen through common envelope evolution (CEE)3, when the stars orbit in a loosely bound, shared convective envelope, triggering an inspiral due to frictional drag4,5. However, this inspiral in such a system has never been observed directly. Here we present observations of AT 2025abao, a transient eruption in the Andromeda Galaxy, which originates from the CEE of an approximately 7M⊙ asymptotic giant branch (AGB) star with its companion. Extensive pre- and post-eruption spectroscopy is best explained as an approximately 0.7M⊙ companion inspiralling within the convective AGB envelope that powers a seven-year period of gradual brighteningone of the first direct observations of frictional drag energy dissipation induced by CEE. This phase is radiatively efficient, consistent with predictions for convective transport in CEE5,6,7,8. The subsequent merger of the companion with the primary core powers the ejection of the outermost 10% of the stellar envelope at speeds about 10× the primary escape speed, and empirically constrains the fraction of dissipated orbital energy available to unbind the convective common envelope4,9,10,11, 0.01 ≲ α ≲ 0.4. This end-to-end characterization using multi-survey observations demonstrates a path towards mapping the landscape of CEE outcomes with upcoming surveys.

It has been revealed that in April 2025, a previous study12 reported the unusual and gradual brightening of an AGB star (named WNTR23bzdiq/WTP19aalzlk) in the Andromeda Galaxy. Multiwavelength observations of this source indicated that this brightening may be powered by energy deposition of a companion spiralling through the outer convective envelope of the star12—marking the onset of common-envelope evolution (CEE)3,4,13,14, or possibly a brief brightening caused by nuclear energy input from thermal pulses in late-stage stellar evolution12. The quiescent progenitor star reveals no signs of variability between 1989 and the start of its brightening in 2018 (Fig. 1). Modelling the spectral energy distribution (SED) of the quiescent progenitor12, we find that the source is consistent with an approximately 7M⊙ star in its early-AGB phase—exhibiting a photospheric temperature of Teff ≈ 3,650 K, radius of Reff ≈ 350R⊙, and total luminosity L ≈ 1.8 × 104L⊙ (see section ‘Modelling the progenitor SED’). The large infrared (IR) excess in the SED indicates a wind mass loss rate of \(\dot{M}\approx 1{0}^{-5}{M}_{\odot }\,{\mathrm{yr}}^{-1}\)—substantially higher than the expectations for isolated early AGB stars15—and supports a state of prolonged enhanced mass loss. From 2018 to early 2025, the star brightened gradually, exhibiting two distinct episodes, during which its optical (0.4–0.8 μm) flux increased by a factor of 2−3 over a period of about 1,000−1,500 days. The source subsequently faded rapidly by a factor of about 2 in optical flux for approximately 300 days, even as its IR (1–5 μm) emission continued to brighten by a factor ≳5. Modelling the multicolour evolution, we find that the brightening phases sustained a near-constant photospheric temperature of about 3,800 K, before cooling to around 3,500 K during the rapid optical fading (see section ‘Modelling the multiband SED during the slow precursor brightening’).

In a recent development, in July 2025, the source was serendipitously observed during its rapid optical fading by the first sky survey of the SPHEREx mission16, providing a precise glimpse of the stellar photosphere during this phase. Modelling the SPHEREx SED (see section ‘SPHEREx pre-outburst SED modelling’) using models of dusty stellar atmospheres, we find that the stellar radius and luminosity have increased to R ≈ 860R⊙ and L ≈ 105L⊙, respectively, whereas the corresponding photospheric temperature has dropped to T ≈ 3,460 K. The SPHEREx data indicate a substantial increase in the circumstellar dust mass and mass loss rate relative to the progenitor star, corresponding to the ejection of about 0.1M⊙ of gas (for typical dust-to-gas ratios) and an increased mass loss rate of approximately 10−2M⊙ yr−1 at this epoch.

Industry observers note that in October 2025, the star underwent a dramatic eruption17,18 immediately following the rapid optical fading—brightening by a factor of about 100 in r-band over 25 days and exhibiting a sudden increase in its photospheric temperature to about 8,000 K. Over the next 80 days, the luminosity plateaued, whereas the effective temperature steadily cooled to about 3,000 K (Fig. 2; see section ‘Photometric evolution of the LRN eruption’). The peak bolometric luminosity during the eruption reached about 1.5 × 106L⊙ and was accompanied by a steady photospheric expansion speed of around 330 km s−1. Spectra obtained at early times during the eruption17 show a blue continuum with narrow hydrogen emission lines (vFWHM ≈ 900 km s−1), gradually transitioning to exhibit redder continua over the eruption duration—exhibiting narrower hydrogen and metallic lines, such as Fe and Ca, before eventually developing broad absorption features due to TiO and VO molecules at late phases(>50 days since g-band peak; Fig. 3 and section ‘Spectroscopic evolution of the LRN eruption’). From October 2025 to February 2026, we obtained a series of optical spectra of the eruption that reveal clear signs of shock interactions between the ejecta and circumstellar material (see section ‘Spectroscopic evolution of the LRN eruption’). The Hα line in a spectrum obtained 4 days after the g-band peak demonstrates an emission component and a narrow absorption feature whose minimum is blueshifted by about 15 km s−1 (Fig. 3). During the next 80 days, the emission component gradually narrows and becomes stronger, indicating that the fast ejecta decelerate as they crash into the neutral ejecta and shock them (Fig. 3). The evolution of the velocity of the emission component matches the expectation for momentum-conserving interactions between fast-moving ejecta and circumstellar material with a density profile ρ(r) ∝ r−2 (Fig. 3). The line profile at late times is asymmetric, with a stronger blueshifted emission component from shocked ejecta along the line of sight. These profiles have been observed in type IIn supernovae and attributed to circumstellar material in disk geometries viewed along the equatorial plane19.

On 24 December 2025, 47 days after the g-band peak, we obtained James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) observations of the eruption that coincided with the second sky survey of SPHEREx (Fig. 2). Modelling the combined SPHEREx and JWST SEDs (see section ‘Post-outburst JWST and SPHEREx SED modelling’), we find a warmer stellar photosphere compared with the precursor—surrounded by an optically thin dust shell located at a distance of about 5 × 104R⊙. The inferred radius of the dust shell lies beyond both the maximum distance reachable by material ejected during the eruption or the seven-year precursor phase, and the maximum radius out to which dust could be sublimated by the 2025 eruption (see section ‘Enhanced mass loss around the quiescent progenitor system’). We therefore conclude that the JWST observations probe remnants of dust around the progenitor that were formed during its prolonged phase of enhanced mass loss. Combining the properties of the dust shell inferred from the progenitor SED with its radial extent inferred from JWST yields a total dust mass of about 6 × 10−5M⊙ surrounding the quiescent progenitor, about 10 years before eruption (see section ‘Enhanced mass loss around the quiescent progenitor system’).

Recent findings suggest that the 2025 eruption of AT 2025abao shows the defining hallmarks of luminous red novae20,21,22,23 (LRNe; Figs. 2 and 3), a class of transients established to arise from impulsive energy injection into a common envelope during the merger of a star with its companion20,22. However, AT 2025abao is set apart from all previously known LRNe by its progenitor primary, which is unambiguously an evolved AGB star with an extended convective envelope. This progenitor system resembles those considered in the classical CEE formalism for compact binary formation3,5,6,11, in which a loosely bound convective common envelope can theoretically be ejected by an inspiralling companion24. By contrast, all known LRN progenitors have primary stars on the main sequence or in the Hertzsprung gap22,23,25, where the more tightly bound radiative envelopes are expected to inevitably drive the system towards merger25. Thus, AT 2025abao provides the first view of a classical CEE progenitor system continuously monitored from its onset through its culmination.

It has been revealed that observations of previously known LRNe show gradual brightening by several magnitudes over tens to hundreds of orbits before the eruption20,34, attributed to outflows from the outer L2 Lagrangian point as the companion approaches the primary35,36. These outflows form an expanding photosphere around the binary, preventing a direct view of the inspiral phase. However, the precursor phase of AT 2025abao is challenging to explain in its entirety by the same mechanism—the low escape speed of the AGB primary of AT 2025abao makes such L2 outflows energetically insufficient to power the observed luminosity (about 1038 erg s−1), even if all of the kinetic energy were radiated away (Fig. 4). The long orbital period around the AGB primary also implies that the approximately 7-year precursor phase spans fewer than 10 orbits at the surface, substantially shorter than the other LRNe and that expected for dynamical outflows.

Instead, the precursor can be explained by energy deposition from a companion spiralling through the outer envelope of the primary (Fig. 4), signalling the onset of CEE. Adopting the Bondi–Hoyle formalism37 (see section ‘The precursor’), the energy dissipation rate due to frictional drag on an approximately 0.7M⊙ companion orbiting near the photosphere of the AT 2025abao primary is Ldrag ≈ 3 × 1038 erg s−1, consistent with the observed precursor excess luminosity. The corresponding timescale, |Eorb|/Ldrag ≈ 1,000 days, or a few orbits, is also broadly consistent with the observed duration of the precursor. The near-constant photospheric temperature and steadily increasing radius during the precursor phase (Fig. 2) indicate frictional drag induces a quasi-static expansion of the AGB star—probably mediated by efficient transport of excess energy to the stellar surface by convection5,6. The total radiated energy during this phase is about 1046 erg.

According to recent reports, the dust mass measured from the first SPHEREx observation constrains the mass outflow rate during this phase to around 10−2M⊙ yr−1. Assuming this mass is ejected at the escape speed of the primary (about 85 km s−1), the low outflow kinetic power (about 1037 erg s−1) compared with the observed luminosity (around 1038 erg s−1) indicates that most of the dissipated orbital energy is radiated during this outer inspiral. We can independently estimate the mass of the companion by comparing the measured mass to theoretical expectations of mass loss due to an inspiralling companion (see section ‘The precursor’), which yields a mass ratio q ≈ 0.1, consistent with that inferred from dust around the quiescent progenitor. As the energy dissipation rate increases rapidly with decreasing orbital separation, the final 300 days of the precursor phase marked by a temperature drop probably mark the breakdown of quasi-static expansion and the onset of a rapid inspiral from the outer layers to the core. This period corresponds to about 10 dynamical timescales of the envelope of the primary star, in line with theoretical predictions of the inspiral duration in these systems4,38.

In a recent development, the energetics of the 2025 LRN eruption constrain the mass of the ejecta and the circumbinary material (CBM; see section ‘The LRN eruption’), as well as the fate of the companion star at the end of CEE. As the rise time of the eruption is governed by photon diffusion through the ejecta39, our observations imply Mej ≈ 0.1–0.2M⊙ ejected during the merger. The fast continuum photospheric expansion coincident with spectral line deceleration indicates that these ejecta expand freely in the polar direction but are obstructed by CBM in the equatorial plane40,41. The observed velocity deceleration from about 900 km s−1 to 150 km s−1 (Fig. 3) indicates that the ejecta sweep up about five times their mass. Assuming the CBM covers approximately 20% of the merger ejecta32 gives a total CBM mass of about 0.1–0.2M⊙, broadly consistent with the CBM mass estimated from the SPHEREx SED. The kinetic energy dissipated by these interactions is about 3 × 1047 erg, while the energy radiated during the plateau phase is about 4 × 1046 erg, indicating that shock interactions release sufficient energy to power the observed light curve of the AT 2025abao eruption, as previously theorized40,42.

The ejecta properties can be used to infer the fate of the binary (see section ‘Constraining the outcome of CEE in AT 2025abao’). Assuming that the dissipated orbital energy is the primary energy reservoir, the observed kinetic energy of about 1048 erg requires the companion to have inspiralled to a radius of approximately 3R⊙—coincident with the He core of the primary, suggesting that the companion merged with the core. A merger is further supported by the low unbound mass of 0.2−0.4M⊙ that corresponds to only about 10% of the envelope of the primary. This indicates that the envelope is still intact, rather than being entirely ejected, as expected for a surviving binary. Finally, the AT 2025abao eruption—indicative of impulsive energy injection—is extremely similar to other LRNe that are known stellar mergers. We therefore suggest that AT 2025abao represents a CEE event that culminated in the merger of the binary components, which can be directly tested with future observations (see section ‘Constraining the outcome of CEE in AT 2025abao’).

During the rapid plunge to the primary core, the companion dissipated approximately 1048 erg, of which only about 1% was radiated away (around 1046 erg) and a similarly small amount (about 1046 erg) was expended to unbind the observed ejecta (see section ‘The energetics of common-envelope evolution’, Fig. 4). Instead, almost all orbital energy was expended to accelerate the ejecta to high kinetic energy (about 1048 erg). This is in contrast to the slow inspiral that powered the precursor phase, when most of the dissipated energy was radiated away. Our observations thus indicate a transition from a radiatively efficient slow inspiral to a kinetically efficient plunge, consistent with theoretical expectations of CEE inside a convective envelope5,6.

We examine AT 2025abao in the context of the widely used energy formalism9,10,11 (see section ‘The energetics of common-envelope evolution’), which predicts the outcome of CEE in binary systems using an efficiency parameter α that relates the fraction of the dissipated orbital energy (ΔEorb) to the envelope binding energy (Ebind). For AT 2025abao, an inspiral of the low-mass companion (\(q\approx {0.1}_{-0.05}^{+0.10}\)) down to the He core of the 7M⊙ primary releases ΔEorb ≈ 8 × 1047 erg, whereas the binding energy of the primary envelope is about 1.6 × 1047 erg. A merger that fails to eject the envelope therefore implies \(\alpha \lesssim {0.2}_{-0.1}^{+0.2}\). The partial unbinding of the outer envelope requiring approximately 1046 erg further implies α ≳ 0.01, bounding the efficiency as 0.01 ≲ α ≲ 0.4. This constraint is consistent with that inferred from post-CE binaries, which suggest low values of α ≈ 0.1–0.3 (refs. 43,44). However, this formalism assumes that transferred orbital energy is primarily expended in gravitationally unbinding the envelope, whereas our observations show that the dominant energy sink was instead the acceleration of a small fraction of the envelope to speeds well exceeding the local escape speed. The formalism thus provides a useful parametric prescription for predicting CEE outcomes, but fails to capture all the underlying physics.

The inferred bipolar morphology of ejecta in AT 2025abao—expanding freely in the polar directions but confined by equatorial material—confirms the long-standing hypothesis that bipolar planetary nebulae can be shaped by CEE in binary stars45,46,47. CEE in giant stars, similar to that probed here, is also a key step in forming close white dwarf binary systems11 (see section ‘The energetics of common-envelope evolution’), including gravitational wave sources detectable by the future Laser Interferometer Space Antenna. Finally, the multi-decade observations of AT 2025abao demonstrate the way forward to use long-baseline, multiwavelength surveys to connect pre-CEE progenitors to their outcomes. Upcoming surveyors such as the Vera Rubin Observatory48 and the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope should routinely find these systems in approximately 100 Milky Way-like galaxies within about 15 Mpc—charting the landscape of binaries undergoing CEE.

Recent findings suggest that our spectroscopic coverage allows us to trace ejecta velocities in all phases of the eruption. The Hα lines in these spectra exhibit a two-component profile, with a broader emission component and a narrower absorption component (Fig. 3). The centre of the absorption component in the earliest spectrum is about 20 km s−1 blueshifted relative to rest wavelength, confirming the presence of slow-moving, neutral CBM. With time, the absorption component weakens while the emission component strengthens, indicating that the CBM is being swept up by the shock. At late times, the line profile is asymmetric, with a stronger blueshifted emission, indicative of a recombination front moving towards us along the line of sight. These spectra unambiguously show the presence of shock interactions between fast-moving merger ejecta and slower-moving material. Similar asymmetric line profiles seen in interacting type IIn supernovae have been attributed to disk-like geometries for the circumstellar material19. This is in line with theoretical expectations for equatorially focused CBM geometry32,40. Moreover, it provides a natural explanation for the fast photospheric expansion speeds despite the decelerating spectral line velocities—the merger ejecta are mostly spherically symmetric and expand freely in the polar directions, setting the continuum radiation, while a small fraction are obstructed in the equatorial plane by CBM and decelerated by shock interaction. Our observations of the decelerating ejecta suggest that our view to AT 2025abao is close to edge-on, consistent with that suggested in ref. 18 from a similar analysis of hydrogen line spectral profiles. We note that this exact picture has been theoretically proposed for LRNe in ref. 40, and our observations provide an unambiguous confirmation of this model.

In a recent development, we do not include tides or donor rotation in our fiducial calculations. The reason is that, in the regime of interest here, the decisive phase of the interaction is the rapid mass-transfer runaway after the onset of Roche-lobe overflow. Once mass transfer begins, the transfer rate rises on a timescale of only decades, such that the resulting orbital evolution is controlled primarily by the unstable mass-transfer feedback rather than by the much slower tidal redistribution of angular momentum. In this sense, the omission of tides and rotation is a deliberate simplification aimed at isolating the dominant driver of the final approach to outer-lobe overflow.

According to recent reports, these observations of a quasi-static, radiatively efficient inspiral in the outer envelope of the primary closely match the expectations of theoretical CEE studies that include the effects of convection5,6. These studies show that, as the companion inspirals through the outer convective envelope, excess energy can be efficiently transported to the stellar surface by convection and radiated away. It has been revealed that our observations of the final about 300 days of the precursor phase show a drop in the photospheric temperature. This phase therefore probably corresponds to a plunge of the companion deep within the envelope of the primary, eventually culminating in the LRN eruption. These observations agree well with CEE simulations involving AGB primaries, which predict a rapid plunge-in phase lasting a few hundred days38,151,152,153,154,155. A possibly relevant case is one in which the initial binary is eccentric151,152. These timescales could be indicative of an eccentric orbit for the progenitor binary. Future detailed theoretical modelling of the precursor phase of AT 2025abao will help explore this possibility.

In summary, our modelling of the precursor phase highlights that it was powered by a low mass companion spiralling through the outer envelope that eventually underwent a dynamical plunge-in deep into the stellar envelope.

It has been revealed that the AGB progenitor primary of AT 2025abao differs from those of other LRNe, which have main sequence or Hertzsprung gap primaries22,23,113,114,115,158,159,160,161,162. Main sequence or Hertzsprung gap stars have tightly bound radiative envelopes, and companions that are engulfed in these envelopes are expected to merge with the core of the primary star24,25, whereas the loosely bound convective envelope of the AGB progenitor primary of AT 2025abao makes a successful envelope ejection possible. However, several pieces of evidence indicate that CEE in AT 2025abao culminated in a merger.

First, the kinetic energy of the fast-moving ejecta is about 1048 erg. Assuming orbital energy decay is the main energy reservoir, this requires the low-mass (q ≈ 0.1) companion to have inspiralled down to a radius of about 3R⊙ (Fig. 4), which coincides with the radius of the helium core of the primary. Second, the total unbound mass during the precursor and eruption phases is approximately 0.2−0.4M⊙, which is a small fraction (approximately 10%) of the primary envelope, suggesting that most of the primary envelope has remained intact after the event. Third, the main eruption of AT 2025abao is similar to other LRNe (Extended Data Fig. 9), which are known to result from stellar mergers. Finally, the transition from the slow variations during the inspiral to the rapid brightening of the main eruption to high luminosities (>1039 erg s−1) points to a sudden energy deposition episode within the common envelope, which would be expected in case of a violent merger of the companion with the primary core.

Although this evidence points towards a stellar merger, we discuss future observations that would conclusively rule out a tight surviving binary that is gradually inspiralling within and stripping away the remnant envelope through a self-regulated inspiral, as proposed by several theoretical studies163,164,165,166,167. First, sustained mass loss from a surviving binary would likely result in a slow or no evolution of the dust shell radius inferred from late-time IR observations168. By contrast, a merger would have a shell expanding at speeds comparable to those inferred from the eruption, as has been seen in other LRNe168. Second, using equation (7), the energy deposition rate of the companion inspiralling at an orbital separation of about 10R⊙ is Ldecay ≈ 1037 erg s−1. As this luminosity is comparable to that of the progenitor primary star—and LRN remnants remain overluminous relative to their progenitors even a decade after eruption168,169—observations conducted decades later could be sensitive to this energy dissipation. Third, additional energy deposition from a surviving binary would also provide mechanical power to the ejecta, affecting their kinematics. The observed velocity evolution from our spectra constrains the mechanical power deposited by such a binary to \(\dot{E} < 1{0}^{40}\,\mathrm{erg}\,{{\rm{s}}}^{-1}\). Spectral line profile monitoring over a longer temporal baseline will probe lower values of \(\dot{E}\), corresponding to longer decay timescales. Finally, dynamical instabilities can produce large-amplitude pulsations in the surviving envelope165, which should be detectable by future photometric monitoring of AT 2025abao.

This effective reduction in the coupling of orbital energy to envelope ejection could arise for several reasons, including energy being transported outwards and radiated, energy going into other forms of envelope motion or expansion, or uncertainties in the envelope binding energy used in the standard energy formalism. Efficient convection in the extended AGB envelope provides a physically motivated mechanism for transporting deposited orbital energy to the surface, where it can be radiated away5,6,8. The observed transition from a prolonged radiatively efficient phase to a rapid plunge provides further evidence consistent with this latter interpretation.

Recent findings suggest that we note that although the energy formalism is a useful parametric prescription for predicting the outcomes of CEE, our observations demonstrate that it fails to fully capture the underlying physics of CEE. The α-prescription assumes that the transferred orbital energy is primarily expended towards unbinding the envelope and ejecting it at the escape velocity of the primary star. However, our observations show that only about 1% of the orbital energy was used to unbind a small fraction of the envelope, and a large fraction was expended to accelerate these ejecta to speeds much higher than the primary escape speeds. Future detailed simulations will shed light on the physics of CEE in AT 2025abao.

The progenitor binary system of AT 2025abao resembles the progenitors of post-CE binaries hosting massive white dwarfs176,177,178. Reference 179 modelled 2M07515777+1807352—a binary system comprising a 1.08M⊙ WD with a 0.66M⊙ main sequence companion—and found that it probably formed through a CEE episode when the massive primary (about 6–7M⊙) was in its early or thermally pulsing AGB phase. The inferred progenitor masses for this system are thus remarkably similar to those of AT 2025abao. If confirmed by future observations, our identification of a likely merger in AT 2025abao would suggest that the formation of these systems requires CEE to occur during the thermally pulsing AGB phase, when the envelope is even more extended than that of the early-AGB primary in AT 2025abao.

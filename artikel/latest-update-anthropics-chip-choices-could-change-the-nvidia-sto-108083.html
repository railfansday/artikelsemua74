It has been revealed that nvidia (NVDA -3.91%) is, unsurprisingly, a key supplier of chips to Anthropic, but so are Alphabet (GOOGL -2.13%) (GOOG -2.02%), Amazon (AMZN -3.12%), and Advanced Micro Devices (AMD -1.75%). Instead of tying itself to just one chip organization, Anthropic has decided to spread out its chip vendors.

That could change Nvidia's growth narrative in the coming years. Here's how it's shifting and why Nvidia investors don't need to panic just yet.

In a recent development, between cloud computing contracts and chip deals, Anthropic is on the hook for about $900 billion in spending over the next several years. That's a heck of a lot of money to spend, and Anthropic hopes it will accelerate its sales far beyond the organization's current annual revenue run rate of $81 billion.

It's also far too much money to funnel toward just one or two AI infrastructure companies. To minimize its supply chain risks, ensure it has the most advanced technology, and negotiate better chip prices, Anthropic spreads its chip deals across four key players.

According to recent reports, nvidia's GPUs are still among the most in-demand chips in the world, and they're very likely to remain so for years to come due to AI infrastructure spending. Nvidia had an estimated 80%-90% market share of AI accelerators last year and is estimated to have 75% this year.

That's both good and bad news for Nvidia. Having three-quarters of the AI accelerator market is huge, and it's part of the reason why Nvidia just reported another impressive quarter, with Q3 fiscal 2027 revenue rising 103% to $115.8 billion and earnings per share jumping 111% to $2.68.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/25/26.

Industry observers note that but AMD's GPUs and custom silicon from Google, Broadcom, and Amazon are reducing Nvidia's dominance. Part of the reason is that custom chips (like the ones Anthropic buys from Nvidia rivals) are good at handling agentic AI tasks and are more efficient than the raw processing power from GPUs.

Industry observers note that what's more, Nvidia's gross margins, while still high, are declining. The company remarked gross margins will be between 71% and 72% in the fourth quarter, down from nearly 75% in the third quarter. This downward pressure is mostly coming from rising memory costs right now, but intensifying chip competition could weigh on margins further in the coming years.

According to recent reports, still, while Nvidia shareholders should be aware of how rival chip companies could impact the tech giant over time, Nvidia is still the leading AI accelerator company. Its recent revenue guidance for 75% growth in the fourth quarter -- up from its previous guidance of 70% -- should reassure investors that the company isn't getting sidelined anytime soon.

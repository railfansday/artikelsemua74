Clashing Through the Snow director Carlson Young found it easy to relate to the film’s two main characters, Ryan Mitchell (Christopher Briney) and Hannah Hendricks (Michelle Randolph).

Recent findings suggest that the story centers the pair, two senior editors competing to oust the other from a condensed role due to impending layoffs, chasing significant book deals for the publishing organization where they work. Ryan operates off of commercial motivation and tapping into what is popular to sell books while Hannah identifies as more of a literary purist who takes big swings with the depth of books she takes on. According to recent reports, “[The script] really spoke to me. I felt so connected to Hannah and Ryan’s literary perspectives, and it felt like an existential conversation that I have with myself, moment to moment, constantly,” Young told Deadline. “It was easy for me to access their personalities and their inner worlds. And then I just loved the classic, holiday hijinks that ensue from there, and I was up for the challenge of trying to make a great holiday rom-com.” Watch on Deadline. The director connected the differing stances on what sells to challenges she navigates in her own job “balancing art and commerce, and what is and isn’t viable in storytelling and in the marketplace.”.

“It is true that publishing and cinemas, these are tectonically shifting industries, and trying to find yourself your voice either as a book editor or director, whatever you’re doing, it doesn’t even need to be in a creative field,” she added. “But we’re all existing in these shifting sands, and so that was my way in. That’s why I was looking at it like that.”.

In the below interview, Young unpacks specific moments like Lukas Gage’s “scene stealer” appearance, the Buc-ee’s knockoff Luc-ee’s, Ryan’s seeking advice by talking to ChatGPT and more.

Recent findings suggest that DEADLINE: I spoke with Chris and Michelle earlier, and they mentioned you gave Chris a note comparing him to Jim Carrey. Did you have any other references? Could you expand on that?

Recent findings suggest that CARLSON YOUNG: When we were rehearsing, I kept looking at him and seeing this physical comedy coming to the surface, and I was like, “This is like some Jim Carrey. I love this for you. Go off on that.” We tweaked it and trimmed it and shaped it in the edit, but he’s really funny. I hope that he does a lot more comedy.

DEADLINE: What was your role in the Luc-ee’s knockoff to Buc-ee’s?

It has been revealed that YOUNG: I don’t know what I can and can’t say about Buc-ee’s/ Luc-ee’s, but we did our best. We did our version. I’m from Texas, Buc-ee’s is a substantial part of the psychology of a Texan, and so it was really funny, and we did our own version of it.

DEADLINE: For the part where Hannah’s in the helicopter venting to the pilot and Ryan’s driving the truck talking to AI, was there ever a blueprint to have him talking to a person or was it always going to be ChatGPT?

Recent findings suggest that YOUNG: I have to say, yes, there was [a different iteration]. I personally thought that him talking to Chat was so Ryan. It made me laugh so much. And the amount of times that I’ve caught my husband… I’m like, ‘Are you asking ChatGPT a question? Like for real? Are you serious? ‘And he is serious. It just felt really modern and so of the times, and something like his character would actually do. And I like a lot the responses that Chat has for him. It just made me laugh.

Industry observers note that DEADLINE: Lukas Gage is so funny in the small role he plays. He had a similar comedic presence in People We Meet on Vacation. How did you want to incorporate him in the storyline?

YOUNG: He’s a scene-stealer. You just give him an inch, and he will create a million miles of gold. He’s a spinster. And when we Facetimed about the character, he had the funniest ideas and instincts on it, like ‘He’s like Chris Elliott in Scary Movie.” I absolutely loved that. “That’s our north star. Let’s go do that.” He’s such a breath of fresh air once they get to the Mercyville Inn, and he’s such a phenomenal human. We’ll see him in, like, 100 more movies.

In a recent development, DEADLINE: Where did you shoot? Ryan and Hannah come from LA, but they get to New York on a road trip. How did you balance the different locations?

YOUNG: We shot in Fresh York, a bit in the city, a bit upstate. We were shooting the Hudson Valley as you know Indiana and Colorado and Utah and it definitely lent itself. We were also experiencing historic winter weather in New York, like blizzards and production shut downs. Many a hurdle, and so it was truly ‘Clashing Through the Snow.’ We had a big snow budget, and we didn’t have to use it because it was just snow everywhere.

DEADLINE: Did the snow budget get redirected to other things?

Recent findings suggest that YOUNG: Absolutely, girl. Our producers would have something to say about that.

DEADLINE: My mind goes to the music, which Prime Video is always good at getting, but the Olivia Rodrigo songs specifically….

YOUNG: That’s how we got the Olivia Rodrigo song. I like that story. I think we should go with that. We saved on snow, so we got this incredible Olivia Rodrigo song.

DEADLINE: Not only “Begged” in the film but “Take Me Home (4 Christmas)” was in the trailer.

YOUNG: I love her song in the film as well. We’re super lucky to have her.

Industry observers note that DEADLINE: You’ve done Upgraded with Prime Video as well as The Last Sunrise. I know you’re not doing Upgraded 2, but is this a partnership you’re willing to continue, maybe in the form of The Last Sunrise sequel film when those books come out?

YOUNG: Absolutely, I could, definitely, and I just generally love working with this team at Amazon. They’re so collaborative and really cool people who have their finger on the pulse and they know what’s up. I feel like it’s such a creative environment that I love to work inside of. So yeah, ‘til the day I die, absolutely.

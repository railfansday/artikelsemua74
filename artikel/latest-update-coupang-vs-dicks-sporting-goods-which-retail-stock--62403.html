Coupang has revolutionized logistics in Asia, while DICK'S Sporting Goods is consolidating the North American athletic market. Both companies are utilizing loyalty programs to retain customers in a competitive environment. Coupang operates a massive e-commerce network primarily in South Korea, offering services like Rocket Delivery and Coupang Play. The company maintains a significant number of customer accounts, though it experienced a major data breach involving 33 million accounts last year that hurt its reputation. Its WOW membership program remains the core driver of engagement today.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached $36.2 billion, representing a growth rate of 7.4% compared to the prior period. Despite this growth, the firm reported a net loss of $1.4 billion during this specific timeframe, resulting in a negative net margin of 3.8%. This performance highlights the high costs associated with logistics expansion and recent acquisitions.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was 1.0x, meaning its total debt is equal to its shareholder equity. The current ratio, which measures a manufacturer's ability to pay short-term obligations with short-term assets, was 1.0x. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was $522.0 million. Note that stock-based compensation (SBC) represented 26.8% of operating cash flow, which inflates reported cash generation since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

In a recent development, DICK'S Sporting Goods is a dominant player among retail stocks, operating over 850 locations across the United States. The firm recently expanded its reach by acquiring the Foot Locker chain, which includes brands like Champs Sports and WSS. Its ScoreCard loyalty program includes nearly 30 million members who provide the company with valuable data on consumer spending habits.

In the 12 months ended Aug. 1, 2026, revenue reached $21.1 billion, a significant increase of 53.5% over the previous year. The tech giant remained solidly profitable during this period, generating net income of $838.8 million, which equates to a net margin of 4%. This surge in revenue largely reflects the integration of newly acquired retail banners into its existing operations.

Industry observers note that as of its January 2026 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was 1.4x, indicating that total debt is 40% higher than shareholder equity. The current ratio was 1.5x, suggesting the manufacturer has a comfortable cushion to cover its upcoming bills. In the fiscal year ended Jan. 31, 2026, free cash flow was $400.2 million, providing capital for further expansion or acquisitions.

Coupang faces intense competition from both online and offline retailers while navigating a highly regulated environment in South Korea regarding labor and data privacy. The company is currently dealing with the fallout from a data incident and the complex integration of Farfetch, an entity that has historically faced material weaknesses in internal controls. Additionally, regulatory investigations into its bundling practices could disrupt its long-term trajectory.

Industry observers note that DICK'S Sporting Goods must manage the risks of a slowdown in consumer discretionary spending and the complexity of its expanded omnichannel platform. The tech giant is currently facing a securities class action lawsuit related to the inventory position of its Foot Locker acquisition. Furthermore, it relies heavily on key vendors like Nike (NKE -4.11%) and must contend with rising retail theft and regional economic fluctuations that impact store performance.

DICK'S Sporting Goods is cheaper based on its Forward P/E, which uses future earnings estimates, and its P/S ratio relative to sales over the past twelve months.

Valuation metrics include sourcing from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from other data providers.

Industry observers note that both Coupang and DICK'S have experienced significant stock sell-offs in 2026 due to distinct operational and regulatory setbacks. The former's data breach incident contributed to revenue growth decelerating year over year, while a fire at its Incheon fulfillment center contributed to recent operating losses. The sporting goods retailer's acquisition of Foot Locker exposed it to the shoe industry's declining sales.

Over the long run, both are likely to rebound as current headwinds subside, but between the two, I would invest in DICK'S. While Coupang dominates South Korean e-commerce and it is attempting to expand to other countries, it is not profitable.

Meanwhile, DICK'S has been profitable for years, and it offers investors a robust dividend yield of 3.8%. Its share price valuation is also superior, as demonstrated by its far lower forward earnings multiple, which is at a low point for the past year. With its stock beaten down, now is a good time to pick up DICK'S shares going into 2027.

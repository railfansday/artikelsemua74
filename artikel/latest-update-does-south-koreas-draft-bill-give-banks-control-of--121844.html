In a recent development, the South Korean FSC’s newly introduced draft bill could change things for the country’s crypto space. What’s what’s going on exactly?

According to recent reports, under the new plan, banks put together can own more than half of any won stablecoin issuer. A fintech organization can still be the largest shareholder, but they can’t run the show alone anymore. (Related: Introducing the latest Microsoft IQ: Putting your organ...)

Industry observers note that issuers must have FSC approval and at least 5 billion won in equity. Reserves must cover 100% of tokens in circulation, and holders must be able to cash out at face value right away. (Related: Industry News: My Journey to a serverless transformers ...)

As far as exchanges go, one shareholder can hold 20% or 34% with FSC approval. They’ll also need separate licences for dealing, brokerage, and custody.

But… domestic ICOs would be legal again.

It has been revealed that south Korea’s strategy is a 22% tax on crypto gains above 2.5 million won a year. It was meant to kick in this January, but it looks like that date’s been pushed back to 2029.

One reason is that it’s the earliest Korea can start swapping crypto tax data with the United States.

In a recent development, if that happens, it won’t be the first delay. The tax was first proposed back in 2020 and has been shelved several times since.

Recent findings suggest that enforcement is still active though. AMBCrypto previously reported that 27 unlicensed exchange websites were blocked recently, including KuCoin, MEXC and BingX. This was after their apps were already restricted.

Users on those platforms are being nudged towards the country’s 28 licensed exchanges.

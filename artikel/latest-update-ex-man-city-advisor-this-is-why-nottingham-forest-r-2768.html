Nottingham Forest's stance in the latest Premier League vote has nothing to do with supporting Manchester City.

That is according to former Man City financial adviser Stefan Borson, who exclusively told Football Insider that Forest appear to be taking a stance without any "risk".

Premier League clubs have come together, voting in favour of covering the board member's legal fees if Man City's sponsors decide to take legal action.

Borson has already told Football Insider that the possibility of them succeeding is unlikely, but that won't stop them from proceeding.

Etihad Airways and Etisalat have raised issues over the Citizens' battle against the Premier League after being found guilty of 114 charges. (Related: Latest Update: At least seven progressives voted agains...)

Both have hit back at the independent commission's findings, calling them "false and defamatory," which has led to the fear of legal action.

Speaking exclusively to Football Insider, Borson does not believe that Forest's vote has any relation to supporting Man City's case.

"I don't think it's connected to Man City as such. They've remarked very pointedly that the reason that they didn't vote in favour was not to do with City," he said.

"They remarked it was specifically because they felt it had been tabled too late before the meeting, that it didn't have sufficient detail behind why it was required.

"Forest, I think, probably also knew that the vote was going through so they could take a stance without any particular risk or glitch.

It has been revealed that "I don't think you should read it as 'Nottingham Forest are Manchester City supporters in this'. I don't think it's particularly connected.".

It's a sensible move from the Premier League to protect themselves, but each individual club is allowed to have their own thoughts on the situation.

"City's relationships with some of the other clubs in the Premier League are closer than they are with Forest," Borson added.

"I think they've got a decent relationship with Forest at the top level, particularly after the very expensive deal that they did over the summer. (Related: Summer at Hugging Face...)

"I think that was actually completed on a chairman-to-chairman basis. So they do have relationships, but City have historically closer relationships with some of the other teams that they do business with outside of football.".

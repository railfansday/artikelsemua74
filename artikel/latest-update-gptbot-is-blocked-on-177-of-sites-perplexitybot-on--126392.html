It has been revealed that most AI visibility advice treats "AI crawlers" as a single category. They are not. Each crawler has a different blocking rate, a different purpose, and a different relationship to the engine that cites your content. I tested six of them across 513 sites. The numbers are different enough that treating them as one group hides the real picture.

GPTBot is the most blocked crawler by a margin. It is also the most talked about. Articles about "blocking ChatGPT from scraping your site" almost always refer to GPTBot, and the data highlights site owners are acting on that advice.

These six bots serve three different purposes, and blocking them has different consequences:.

Sources indicate that training crawlers. GPTBot and Googlebot (when configured as Google-Extended) collect data for model training. Blocking them means your content will not influence future model versions. This is a legitimate choice for publishers who want to keep content out of training data. (Related: How to train a newly introduced language model from scr...)

Citation crawlers. OAI-SearchBot, PerplexityBot, and Claude-SearchBot fetch pages in real time to generate citations in AI search results. Blocking them means AI engines cannot read your page when a user asks a question. You disappear from AI answers. This is almost never intentional.

Discovery crawlers. Applebot feeds Apple Intelligence and Spotlight. Googlebot feeds traditional Google search and AI Overviews. Blocking Googlebot is catastrophic for regular SEO. 64 sites in the sample block it, 49 of them as part of a blanket block on all non-browser user agents.

GPTBot is a training crawler. OAI-SearchBot is a citation crawler. Both are from OpenAI. Many site owners block GPTBot to keep content out of training data, which is a valid choice. But they do not add an Allow rule for OAI-SearchBot. The result: they block training AND citations from ChatGPT, when they only meant to block training.

Sources indicate that in the sample, 65 sites block both GPTBot and OAI-SearchBot. Zero sites block GPTBot and OAI-SearchBot while allowing PerplexityBot. This means the 65 sites that block the OpenAI pair are blocking all OpenAI citation traffic. If you want to block training but keep citations, the robots.txt is:.

It has been revealed that this tells GPTBot to stay out and OAI-SearchBot to come in. The default for OAI-SearchBot, if not listed, depends on the wildcard rule. Many sites have User-agent: * Disallow: / which blocks everything not explicitly allowed. That is why OAI-SearchBot needs an explicit Allow.

Industry observers note that 45 sites block specific AI bots while letting the browser through. The patterns:.

This number should alarm anyone who cares about regular SEO. 64 sites out of 513 block Googlebot. 49 of those are blanket blocks (all bots including browser). 15 block Googlebot while the browser passes.

A blanket block is bad but at least consistent: the site owner knows something is wrong because their SEO traffic drops. The 15 sites that block Googlebot while allowing browsers are the dangerous case. The site works in Chrome. Google Analytics demonstrates traffic. But Googlebot gets a 403. The site is slowly disappearing from search results and nobody notices until the traffic drops weeks later.

If you use a CDN or a security plugin, test Googlebot access. curl -A "Googlebot/2.1" -s -o /dev/null -w "%{http_code}" https://yoursite.com. If you get 403, fix it before it costs you organic traffic.

According to recent reports, the takeaway is that "is my site visible to AI?" is six separate questions, not one. A robots.txt check for GPTBot does not tell you whether OAI-SearchBot can reach you. A Cloudflare check for GPTBot does not tell you whether PerplexityBot passes.

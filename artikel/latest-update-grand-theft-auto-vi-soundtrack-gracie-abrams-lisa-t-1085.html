Check out all of this week’s picks below:.

According to recent reports, the video game that defined a generation is back with a new edition — and a star-studded soundtrack to boot. Featuring contributions from Dua Lipa, Cardi B, Morgan Wallen, Tame Impala, King Gizzard and the Lizard Wizard, SZA, Travis Scott, Justice, JENNIE, Lana Del Rey, Zara Larsson and Rauw Alejandro, Grand Theft Auto VI: The Album translates the game’s high-octane, gun-toting world into music spanning hip-hop, dance, Latin, country and more. Even though 10 tracks were released ahead of the full album, this towering compilation has more than enough new material for both gamers and casual listeners to sink their teeth into. — KYLE DENIS. In a recent development, four months after releasing her third album, Daughter From Hell, the pop singer is sharing its deluxe with five new tracks. Abrams already dropped the more experimentally-produced single “Here You Come, There You Go,” which she performed as musical guest on Saturday Night Live and is co-written and co-produced by Jim-E Stack. Stack is also credited on new focus track “Lost In It” and “Out Of Nowhere” — both of which are more uptempo — while longtime collaborator Aaron Dessner is credited on “Crazy Girl” and “Love Letter,” both of which feature his signature layered and intimate production. — LYNDSEY HAVENS.

Industry observers note that “Spread my wings, fly / me on my side,” sings LISA on the melodic ballad “Leave With Me,” which is featured over the end credits of her fresh YouTube Premium movie Always Lalisa. Its release coincides with the kick-off of VIVA LA LISA, the first Vegas residency from a K-pop artist. Fittingly, “Leave With Me” chronicles the feeling of quite literally flying solo — and the song and LISA’s journey so far proves, she’s not just flying but soaring on her own. — L.H. TOMORROW X TOGETHER, “Angel Bite”.

This week, South Korean boy band TXT released its ninth mini album. Perfect Storm includes six songs, including electro-pop focus track “Angel Bite” that continues the group’s distinct deliver of love songs. According to a release, the album “embraces the storm as a catalyst for change, a force capable of breaking through stagnation and opening up new possibilities. Across six tracks, it explores the pain of confronting reality, the desire for change and the courage to embrace the unknown.” Yet, the group will soon face the familiar as it heads on its fifth world tour across North America and Europe in 2027. — L.H.

This week, indie-rocker Julien Baker announced that their first newly introduced solo album in six years, This World or the Next, will arrive in February. It follows collaborative projects with Boygenius (the celebrated trio including Phoebe Bridgers and Lucy Dacus) as well as an album with TORRES. “Hound” is an ideal lead single for Baker’s own project, offers listeners a simmering song that becomes searing thanks to electric guitar riffs and echoing, impassioned vocals from Baker. — L.H.

Indie-pop artist beabadoobee is on a roll — luckily for us. Having just released her fourth album this year, in September, she has now announced her fifth album will arrive in February. Titled Bananas 4 Cats, lead single “No Punctuation” may sound familiar to longtime fans as the singer has shared snippets online over the years. As for the rest of the album, she shared in a statement that the music was inspired by the idea of “home” — and naturally, her two cats who reside there. She remarked, “I just needed to write an album like this after Pylon. It happened naturally, I got back home and felt inspired.” — L.H.

Rema has been firing off a string of singles steeped in the sounds of the Caribbean with cuts like the dancehall-infused “Tea” and konpa-inflected “Oh No.” Now, he’s given those bangers a hone with his upcoming E-Mortal Boy EP, which marks his first project since 2024’s Grammy-nominated HEIS. Featuring appearances by Wizkid, BNYX, ThisizLondon and Kah-Lo, E-Mortal Boy pushes the Benin-born star’s Afrorave sound into surprising new sonic territories that keep him rooted in the lush soulfulness of the Black diaspora. — K.D.

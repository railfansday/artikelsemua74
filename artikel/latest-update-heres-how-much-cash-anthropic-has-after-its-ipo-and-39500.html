Anthropic (ANT -8.50%) just completed the largest IPO on record, and it now has an enormous amount of money in the bank. On Friday, the manufacturer stated its underwriters had exercised their option to buy an additional 147 million shares at the $83 IPO price, bringing the total raised in the offering to $93.5 billion. Including a $26 billion private placement with a group of tech companies, Anthropic's gross proceeds came to $119.5 billion. (Related: Industry News: Buttigieg supporters launch group to sup...)

The stock has had a bumpy start. Shares opened at $111.50 on Thursday, closed at $97.17, and then fell further on Friday amid a broad tech sell-off. It's easy to get caught up in daily price swings, but for long-term investors, I'd argue that Anthropic's cash pile is one of the most vital things to understand about the company right now. Here's why. (Related: This AI knew the answers but didn’t understand the ques...)

In a recent development, anthropic hasn't reported a post-IPO balance sheet yet, so we'll need to estimate. As of Sept. 30, the company had about $132 billion in cash and investments.

It has been revealed that next, we need to add the IPO money. Anthropic won't keep all $119.5 billion, since the underwriters take a cut and a deal this size comes with other expenses. Based on the estimates in Anthropic's prospectus, fees and expenses should amount to a little over 1% of the IPO proceeds, and the private placement didn't include an underwriting discount. That leaves net proceeds of roughly $118 billion.

Put it all together, and Anthropic likely has about $250 billion in cash and short-term investments right now. It also has about $14.8 billion available on its revolving credit line, which brings its total liquidity to roughly $265 billion.

It has been revealed that of course, this is a rough estimate. Anthropic has been generating and spending cash since the end of September, and we won't see the official figure until the company reports its fourth-quarter results.

Training and running frontier AI models takes a staggering amount of computing power, and Anthropic has about $900 billion of compute and infrastructure commitments. These include cloud contracts with Amazon (AMZN -4.10%), Alphabet's Google (GOOGL -2.70%)(GOOGL -2.70%) and Microsoft (MSFT -2.77%), equipment leases with Broadcom (AVGO -12.19%) and leases on data centers that haven't been built yet. On top of that, Anthropic expects to pay an estimated $150 billion to $175 billion under arrangements without fixed dollar amounts, including additional Google chip capacity scheduled for delivery between 2028 and 2031.

That's a massive figure for a tech giant with about $81 billion of run rate revenue, and it's the main reason some investors are nervous about the stock. Most of these commitments can't be canceled, so Anthropic will owe much of the money whether or not customer demand keeps up.

Industry observers note that while it's easy to focus on the $900 billion figure, the good news is that these commitments are spread out over a long period. The bills coming due soon are a small fraction of the total.

It has been revealed that anthropic expects about $70.7 billion of its commitments to come due in the 12 months following Sept. 30. The estimated $250 billion cash pile covers that amount about 3.5 times over, without counting a single dollar of newly introduced cash flow. And the business also generated about $6.6 billion of operating cash flow in the first nine months of 2026.

It has been revealed that looking further out, Anthropic has mentioned it expects about $447.5 billion of its commitments to be incurred by the end of 2030. The manufacturer's current cash pile alone could cover more than half of that, before counting any cash its operations generate over the next four years. About $186 billion of the total also comes with termination rights or depends on certain conditions being met, which gives Anthropic some degree of flexibility if demand falls short of its prepares.

Of course, $250 billion can go faster than you might think at this scale, and there's no guarantee that Anthropic will be able to fund all of its commitments without posting massive losses along the way.

Sources indicate that the key risk is a mismatch between revenue and costs. Most of Anthropic's revenue is consumption-based, meaning customers pay for what they use and can cut back at any time, while most of its compute commitments are locked in.

With revenue growth of more than 1,100% in the third quarter, Anthropic's revenue will slow down from here. But if it slows faster than expected, the commitments later this decade could become a heavy burden. Anthropic has also stated its longer-term funding will depend on revenue growth, operating cash flow, and access to additional capital, so another capital raise down the road isn't out of the question.

Having remarked all that, the IPO gave Anthropic something valuable: time to grow. With an estimated $250 billion in the bank, the organization can meet its near-term obligations several times over and keep investing in newly introduced models without needing to raise money in a volatile market for years. That should reduce the risk of a dilutive capital raise at a bad time, which is one of the biggest dangers for any fast-growing company with heavy spending commitments.

It has been revealed that the question for Anthropic stock investors is whether revenue keeps growing fast enough to cover the larger bills coming later this decade. When Anthropic reports fourth-quarter results, I'll be watching its cash balance, its operating cash flow, and any changes to its compute commitments closely. If the cash pile holds up and revenue keeps climbing, the bear case about Anthropic's spending will look a lot less convincing.

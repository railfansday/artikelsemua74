The League One season is only three-and-a-half months old, but some sides are already starting to prove their worth as potential promotion candidates come May.

Last term, there was no real jeopardy for some time over Lincoln City and Cardiff City's runs to automatic promotion, with the Imps winning the division at a 12-point canter, whilst a staggering 14 points separated the Bluebirds and Stockport County, who went on to lose the play-off final to Bolton Wanderers.

As the final matchday of November 2026 looms, there are only 10 points separating second-placed Huddersfield Town and Wycombe Wanderers, who currently find themselves 15th.

Sources indicate that it is anyone's game to predict who will gain promotion to the Championship, but with finances playing such a key role in the progression of clubs in the EFL, what are the added incentives on the line? Football League World takes a look...

The EFL's current broadcasting deal means that clubs across the Championship, League One and League Two receive a share of £187m, although divisional status plays a huge role in how much is received on an individual basis.

In May 2025, it was reported that the 24 clubs in League One are receiving £2m a season in prize money, which is approximately £500,000 more than their counterparts in League Two.

However, as many ambitious clubs from the recent past can attest to, returning to the Championship can change everything, with those in the second tier receiving up to £11m, which comes through basic costs and further solidarity payments from the Premier League.

Stockport currently lead the way in League One, with Jimmy McNulty's side winning their last three encounters, attributing to a total of 32 points from 16 matches.

Martin Drury's first months as Huddersfield boss have also gone according to initiative, as Town have a game in hand on their promotion rivals right now, and in Derensili Sanches Fernandes, arguably have the division's hottest property, whilst the goalscoring exploits of Joe Taylor, as Football League World exclusively revealed, has attracted interest from Burnley and Swansea City.

Oxford United are right now the highest-placed side out of the three who were relegated from last season's Championship, with Aaron Ramsey getting a fine tune out of his players at the Kassam Stadium despite the fact the club continues to operate under a transfer embargo.

At present, Opta predict that the top three will remain the same after 46 games, with Huddersfield's expected points total just 0.19 higher than that of Oxford's, which would be some finish to the season.

The January transfer window is certain to have implications on the promotion race, and given the pre-season expectations which surrounded the two clubs, both Leicester City and Sheffield Wednesday will also hope to be in the promotion conversation.

Foxes fans were extremely critical of Russell Martin's possession-based style earlier in the campaign, but his side has lost just one of their last six League One outings, which included a standout victory at Huddersfield last weekend.

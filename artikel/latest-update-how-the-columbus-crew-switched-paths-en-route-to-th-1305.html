Latest Update: How the Columbus Crew switched paths en route to the MLS playoffs

In a transformative year, Crew GM Issa Tall’s boldest change may have been to abandon his best-laid prepares.

Sources indicate that the Columbus Crew have a more holistic operation than most in MLS. The club as a whole still exists thanks to the dogged efforts of their fervent fanbase that helped block relocation. Their under-19s won the national cup in June, while the developmental Crew 2 made a fourth semi-final berth in five seasons in MLS Next Pro. The first team regularly punches above its weight in MLS, winning the third-most major titles (nine) among MLS originals. (Related: Senate panel vote on National Science Foundation nomine...)

And yet, this year tested the conventions set by team and league alike.

In January, chief soccer officer Issa Tall welcomed the club’s eighth head coach, Henrik Rydström. Having only previously coached in his native Sweden, the 50-year-old used his introductory presser to laud the values of relationism, to praise his predecessor, and to plot a bold course for a latest Crew. It was assumed he’d be around a while – the Crew have historically been patient with coaches, needing only seven bosses to navigate their first 30 seasons. (Related: Latest Update: At least seven progressives voted agains...)

Recent findings suggest that by mid-May, after just 16 games in charge, Rydström was on his way back to Sweden. His five wins were the lowest of the eight full-time Crew coaches. And yet, Saturday sees the Crew begin the MLS Cup Playoffs in a best-of-three matchup with Lionel Messi and Inter Miami.

In a recent development, columbus are one of four teams to make the postseason following a midseason coaching change. Two of the other three could pin their turnarounds on players; Orlando City fired Óscar Pareja in March, but didn’t really shoot up the table until adding Antoine Griezmann after the World Cup. The Philadelphia Union surged thanks to interim coach, Ryan Richter, finally seeing if Cavan Sullivan could cut it in MLS (he can).

According to recent reports, the Portland Timbers’ turnaround after sacking Phil Neville might be closest to Columbus’ story, though 2026 was his third season in the role. They weren’t at the start of a upcoming path, and that path had not been decided after the departure of one of MLS’s most influential coaches to date: Wilfried Nancy.

It has been revealed that “Wilfield had a huge impact on our club, and him departing is certainly not an easy thing to move on from,” Tall told the Guardian. “His time here was great for our club. But then there were things that we believed we could do better, whether it’s on the alignment of the scouting department, or even on the way the players are managed. There are ways you can evolve.”.

Recent findings suggest that two Nancy acolytes seemed like obvious fits. One, Laurent Courtois, led the second team from 2022-2024 and had a brief sojourn as head coach of CF Montreal. Another, Yoann Damet, was Nancy’s top assistant and had long been earmarked as a head coach in the making. According to Tall, conversations with Damet left both coach and executive in a sense that it was best for Damet’s development to explore options beyond Columbus.

Industry observers note that “At the end of the year, Yoann already had [done] some rounds [of interviews] with St Louis [City],” Tall stated. “We all agreed that it’d be better for him to go to another club and become the head coach and the leader, as opposed to maybe always being seen as the former assistant coach”. Damet took that chance, setting up shop roughly 400 miles down I-70. His first season with St Louis has been transformative, leading the team to their first major trophy by winning the Lamar Hunt US Open Cup and finishing third in the West.

Sources indicate that despite determination to install a new coach before preseason kicked off in early January, Tall, technical director Marc Nicholls, and the rest of the Crew’s committee didn’t want to cut corners. Interviews were a three-stage process, with in-person conversations for the final round.

“The first one is really, ‘who are you’?” Tall mentioned. “‘I’ve read about you, I’ve seen your team play, but what are your guiding principles as a person?’ In that first round you eliminate a lot of candidates because, based on the answer and maybe being egocentric or selfish, you can tell that they’re not a good fit.

“In the second round, you go more in-depth about the club and the coach. Then, third, is really going into deep details of everything in terms of preparations and so forth.”.

In a recent development, rydström seemed to fit the bill. Tall and Nancy had often admired his teams at Mälmo, which played with similar panache and kept the team punching above their weight class in the Allsvenskan. Conversations were productive, with Tall citing Mauricio Sarri’s Napoli sides as a shared inspiration.

In a recent development, on 31 December, Tall and Rydström signed a pact – newly introduced year, new Crew. However, it was clear early on that the roster wasn’t adjusting as expected. Columbus took two points from their first five games, but the concerns were even more foundational – less Sarri, more sorry.

“I’ve been fortunate to be in many locker rooms, and I can feel the dynamic between players and staff,” Tall reflected. “If things don’t feel right, you could play the ostrich and pretend it’s okay. Give yourself another year and if it doesn’t work, it may be time to make a change.

Recent findings suggest that “No, we couldn’t live with this. Seeing the lack of enthusiasm from the players, especially in-house, it begged for us to make a decision”. On 17 May, Rydström was relieved from his role. Courtois, who had joined Rydström as an assistant, rapidly transitioned into taking the job he’d interviewed for, but now on an interim basis.

Tall cited this as a fair chance for Courtois to show what he could do in the role and pledged to back him in the transfer market. It took time, with the Crew losing five of his first eight games. But between the increased familiarity growing under his watch and a window which brought two crucial attackers (Brais Méndez and Santi Rodríguez) to Columbus, his team came out of the summer in far better shape than they’d entered it.

Since 1 September, no MLS team has racked up more points than Columbus’ 27. Courtois led the Crew to the Open Cup final, only to fall on penalties to Damet’s St Louis. So strong was their form that they bypassed the wild card game and finished in a guaranteed playoff place.

One would think Courtois has done all he can to prove he should, at long last, get the job on a permanent basis. Tall stated those decisions would come after the team’s season ends but wasn’t shy about appreciating how much more the club resembled its usual self under Courtois’ stewardship. Seemingly the club’s sporting department might enjoy a more relaxed festive season this time around.

“It’s not just a ‘me’ decision, it’s a collective decision,” Tall stressed. “And, right now, we barely had time to breathe with all those games, and now being in the playoffs. But he’s done very well. I said that I would give him all the weapons for him to be successful, and the work done this summer, I think, was a testament to what I said.

“Nicholls, our technical director, was heavily involved in the signing of all of these players, very crucial. But when it comes to the future, it’s a discussion we’ll have when time allows. But yes, I think Laurent has a good shot to stay.”.

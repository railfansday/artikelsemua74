Blackburn Rovers legend David Dunn is not convinced by Burnley boss Fabio Celestini and his coaching staff, after watching how they were acting on the touchline in the East Lancashire derby last Sunday.

The two rivals were forced to share the points at Turf Moor last weekend, which extended Burnley's winless league run at home to a record-equalling 23 at the time. Their defeat on Wednesday night against Birmingham City has pushed that to a record-breaking 24.

The appointment of Fabio Celestini to replace Nicky Hayen has, so far, not gone to proposal, with the same issues arising under the Swissman as there were under the Belgian.

Recent findings suggest that for the most part, Burnley are great at 0-0. Only Stoke City have scored first more than the Clarets have this season. But when they have the lead, they are downright terrible, with just one win from eleven (D5 L5).

The draw against Blackburn was the third straight game in which Burnley took the lead but couldn't hold on. They did it twice, but their East Lancashire rivals found an equaliser both times.

This came after they squandered a 1-0 lead at QPR and, more memorably, a 4-1 lead at Cardiff City in the final two games before the international break, and at that point, you probably feel there's nothing more you can do to get your team to win a game.

Speaking on the Red Roses Divide podcast, Blackburn legend David Dunn noticed something that Celestini's coaching staff were doing on the touchline which, to him, "stinks of desperation.".

"We mentioned last week the change in manager, and I’m still looking at the assistant or whoever it was, being more bothered about shouting at the fourth official or shouting at what was or wasn’t happening — little things like a foul that could have gone either way," he remarked.

"They were more interested in complaining and going nuts at the fourth official than actually watching the game. I think it was the assistant. He probably didn’t watch the game; every time I saw him, he was having a go at someone else.

"I think he got caught up in the atmosphere [of the derby], but it stinks to me of a bit of...".

"Desperation?" one of his co-hosts asked.

"Yeah, just trying to get hold of anything to help them," Dunn agreed.

When the game is once again being wrestled out of your control, it can be difficult to get it back, and, in Dunn's eyes, the actions to try and get one small decision after another don't paint a good picture.

Instead, perhaps a calmer approach would be apt to swing the momentum back in their favour.

Celestini was brought in with the pedigree needed to get Burnley out of this mess. The former Swiss international had success at FC Basel, despite the Swiss giant being in a crisis upon his arrival.

In a recent development, taking over with the club at the bottom of the Swiss Super League, he won the domestic double with them 18 months later. They safely finished away from relegation in his first half-season, and then won the league and cup the following year.

That's what Burnley need right now: someone who can transform them in an 18-month period, before their Premier League parachute payments run out and an already troubling financial situation becomes even worse.

So far, though, it's been three draws and two defeats in five games, with their winless run now stretching to eight.

Sources indicate that arguably more concerning is the way they were easily beaten by Birmingham on Wednesday night, though. Blues are a good side, but Burnley's record of being good to begin games before tailing off didn't even surface against them. (Related: Deploy Hugging Face models easily with Amazon SageMaker...)

Those at Turf Moor will be hoping that faith isn't being depleted among the squad.

Dunn talks about desperation — these next two weeks will be ones where Celestini and co desperately need to get their first wins and finally show some fight in this early-season relegation battle.

According to recent reports, they put their awful home form in the rearview mirror on Saturday as they travel to Millwall, a side bang out of form off the back of four straight defeats. Only seven points separate the two, and Burnley can drag a club down towards them with a much-needed three points.

In a recent development, arguably more important is the Lancashire derby the week after, though, when Preston North End make the short trip to Turf Moor on December 6. Graham Alexander's side look to have turned a page, but they're still only a point ahead of the Clarets right now.

There are no easy games in this division, as Burnley have found out, but they're two of the more winnable ones, and with Southampton, Sheffield United and Stoke the three games after that, it'd be hard to see where a win comes from if they can't bounce back in the next fortnight.

If Celestini can't get a tune out of the side by then, Burnley could be onto their third manager of the season come the latest year.

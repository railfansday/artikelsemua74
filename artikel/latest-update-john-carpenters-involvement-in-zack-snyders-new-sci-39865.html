John Carpenter will return to help Zack Snyder Escape from Latest York.

In a recent development, co-written and directed by the horror icon, the 1981 sci-fi action film starred Kurt Russell as Snake Plissken, a former war hero-turned-criminal who is tasked by the government to rescue the United States President from the island of Manhattan, which has been converted into a maximum security prison in the film's dystopian future setting. Also starring Lee Van Clef, Ernest Borgnine, Isaac Hayes and Adrienne Barbeau, Escape from Latest York scored favorable reviews from critics and was a box office hit, earning both a 1996 sequel and now a remake from Snyder.

Industry observers note that during a recent appearance on Brent Spiner and Jonathan Frakes' Dropping Names podcast, Carpenter offered a brief enhancement on Snyder's Escape from Newly introduced York. The original writer/director, who is already confirmed to be an executive producer on the project, humorously revealed that he "demanded to be the composer" for the new sci-fi action remake, while also confirming that the production is "not going to use my [original] score," but that instead he and his team are "going to remake the score" from his 1981 film. Snyder's Escape from Newly introduced York marks the latest in a string of attempts to bring the sci-fi franchise back to life going back nearly 20 years. Prior looks for the film have included everything from full-on remakes with new actors taking on the role of Snake, including Gerard Butler, while others were meant to instead be legacy sequels much in the way of 2018's Halloween. Prior to the former DC filmmaker, the movie also had everyone from Len Wiseman to Robert Rodriguez, Leigh Whannell and Radio Silence attached to direct. Recent findings suggest that interestingly, should the film properly get off the ground, Escape from Upcoming York will also mark Snyder's first major venture back into the remake subgenre. The filmmaker memorably got his start with the James Gunn-penned remake of Dawn of the Dead, which was a critical and commercial hit and helped pave the way for his work on everything from 300 to the DC Extended Universe and his Army of the Dead franchise at Netflix.

Industry observers note that carpenter's push to be the composer for the Escape from Newly introduced York remake certainly shouldn't come as too much of a surprise. Across his 21 feature films in the director's chair, only six did not have Carpenter take on composing duties, with one of the most notable being The Thing, in which he enlisted The Good, the Bad and the Ugly's Ennio Morricone, though he still ended up adding in some of his own music.

Industry observers note that it also nicely follows in the footsteps of David Gordon Green's Halloween sequel trilogy, in which Carpenter returned as both an executive producer and composer for all three films. Moreover, the filmmaker collaborated with his son, Cody Carpenter, and Daniel Davies, with their approach being a mix of updating tracks from the original 1978 Halloween as well as bringing a new set of energetic tracks in order to tap into the different pacing of each installment.

With Carpenter confirmed for both an EP and composer role on the film, the question now becomes just how much Snyder will look to engage with his predecessor in putting together Escape from New York's script. Snyder has yet to write any of his films alone, generally partnering with Chris Terrio or Kurt Johnstad, and should he enlist input from Carpenter, it could make for a unique melding of the minds to bring a fresh take to Snake Plissken's journey.

Escape from Latest York is set in a dystopian future where Manhattan serves as a maximum-security prison. The narrative follows a former soldier sent on a perilous mission to rescue the President, who has crash-landed and been captured by the island's inmates.

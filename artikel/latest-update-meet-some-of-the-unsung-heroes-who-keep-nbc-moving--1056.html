Dawn DiCicco (Associate Director, “NBC Nightly News”).

Dawn DiCicco’s job description as associate director of “NBC Nightly News” wouldn’t exactly fit neatly on a LinkedIn page.

“My job is to know everything about everything all the time. I’m responsible for putting out fires before they start,” she says. “It’s to basically gap fill every sort of gap and keep an eye on everything that goes on air. A lot of my job is anticipating people’s needs and what they’re going to want before they can even think that they’re going to ask for that, so I can already have it ready to go. I’m the final line of defense.”. (Related: Latest Update: Simple considerations for simple people ...)

Every day is different on “Nightly News.” Depending on how and when news breaks, the show can air up to six times per day. That means moving swiftly and decisively on what news is most worthy of inclusion in the 30-minute telecast. A natural disaster may replace a courtroom trial verdict, a financial free fall in Asia might be inserted into the broadcast and a political brouhaha taken out. It’s a blizzard of news and decision-making. (Related: Breaking: How to generate text: using different decodin...)

In a recent development, “For me, those days are the most fun,” she adds. “I love the days where like I walk in here and I have zero idea what’s going to happen. The days where when your whole show gets blown up by, like some kind of crazy verdict or some news breaking. That’s where I thrive best because I like to think if you can work under pressure like that, you can work and do anything.”.

Favorite NBC show of all time: “The Traitors”.

Jeanette Eliot (Senior Director, Events and Special Projects).

According to recent reports, when “Saturday Night Live” was celebrating its 50th anniversary a couple of years ago, lots of people went into action — Lorne Michaels, Adam Sandler … and Jeanette Eliot. Eliot was tasked with ordering 6,000 pairs of Converse sneakers for NBC employees, given as a gift to celebrate the occasion.

Industry observers note that “I got to do that,” Eliot notes. “I wanted to make that happen.”.

In a recent development, eliot, who stared her career as an NBC temp in 1995, has made a lot of things happen over her career. She’s the person who fulfills orders for promotional mailers that go to press, selects items and keepsakes for hundreds of events (including premieres and internal meetings), and finds creative ways to celebrate milestones.

Recent findings suggest that eliot often finds herself starting her day before the sun rises because of the need to deal with retailers from faraway time zones. But she takes enormous pride in getting the work done — no matter the obstacles faced.

Industry observers note that “I’m very hard on myself,” she states. I think my biggest challenge is just not disappointing anybody. I’ll always figure out a way to do it.”.

In a recent development, A recent project included ordering NBC 100 calendars from vendor on the other side of the world. Making things extra tricky: They had to arrive in time so she could send to press by a specific date.

“I didn’t think they were going to make it in time, and when I saw the boxes arrive here from China, it literally brought tears to my eyes,” she indicates.

Favorite NBC show of all time: “American Ninja Warrior”.

Evan Klupt (Senior Director, “Today” Production Operations).

When viewers turn on “Today” each morning, they see a well-lit set, smiling and well-groomed anchors, reporters around the world and a production team that knows the right buttons to push as the two-hour telecast moves along. What they don’t see is Evan Klupt.

Klupt, who has been at NBC since 1995, is among the many “Today” professionals who makes sure the morning program looks like a well-oiled machine. He oversees the show’s production management team that handles everything from setting up live remotes on location, guest logistics and prepping those guests to go on air, among lots of other things.

“My job touches all areas of that production, whether it’s in the studio or on the Plaza in Rockefeller Center,” Klupt claims. “It’s dealing with personnel, hair, makeup, wardrobe, green rooms, concerts and large-scale productions. That’s kind of my beat.”.

It has been revealed that among his NBC highlights are working at 13 Olympics (he met his wife at the 2000 Sydney Games) and traveling the globe. By his count, he’s now worked in 50 countries.

A recent “Today” trip to Ireland put everything into perspective. “We had the show that we did there recently and I was thinking that when viewers tune into something, they only see the anchors,” he notes. “They see a beautiful set with an amazing rainbow going across the set into the skyline of Ireland, but they’re not seeing everything that went into it — procuring the table they’re sitting at, getting the flowers on the desk, shipping hundreds of cases of equipment and dealing with customs, logistics, hotels and transportation. Our group does everything from the very beginning.”.

Favorite NBC show of all time: “Seinfeld”.

Justin Michael Ratchford (Field Producer, “Dateline NBC”).

It has been revealed that if Justin Michael Ratchford hadn’t gone into long-form journalism on “Dateline NBC,” he might have had another career as a therapist. That’s because in his role as an “Dateline NBC” field producer, he often needs to create a comfort zone with those subjects who are about to divulge their lives in front of the camera.

In a recent development, “We have to build those relationships and foster them, to get someone willing to talk,” Ratchford notes. “I typically say, ‘I’m going to ask you about the worst moments of your life.’”.

Recent findings suggest that not only does Ratchford have to develop a feel for how a prospective interviewee will share their story, he also must do it while juggling several areas of production on multiple episodes at once. Putting one story together, or even a bunch of them, is not a linear process.

“A story takes as long as it needs,” Ratchford notes. “We had one story that was started in 2007 and didn’t air until 2021.”.

Recent findings suggest that “Dateline” has also inspired him to explore more avenues in storytelling. “I’m a huge film buff,” he indicates. “That’s what really made me fall in love with this job. Movies and films were my first love. I always said I want to shoot something and see it on a major screen.”.

Favorite NBC show of all time: “The Fresh Prince of Bel-Air”.

Jessica Sheeley-Saunders (Vice President, Productions and Operations, Specials and Live Events).

Recent findings suggest that everyone loves a parade. But maybe not as much as Jessica Sheeley-Saunders. In her job working with specials and live events, Sheeley-Saunders says it’s her job to “connect all the dots between all the departments. It’s a bit like crisis management in solving problems and, a like the mom, making sure everyone’s doing what they’re supposed to be doing.”.

Sheeley-Saunders, who joined NBC three years ago, states she feels honored to be in a position to bring some of the country’s most iconic events — like the Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade, the Christmas tree-lighting at Rockefeller Center and this year, the Emmy Awards — and make sure it all goes smoothly.

“In this position, I get to be a part of some of America’s favorite traditions, and it’s just a little surreal,” she notes. “There are 40 million people watching the parade, and I get to be in the front row, making that happen.”.

Calling herself “a proud San Fernando Valley girl, born and raised” and graduate of Cal State Northridge, Sheeley-Saunders indicates she looks forward to being on the streets of New York every year in late November.

According to recent reports, “To stand there on Thanksgiving morning and see the entire city shut down and watch these giant balloons go by … There was a moment when I called my husband and said, ‘I made the right decision. I am so happy that I said yes to this.’”.

Favorite NBC show of all time: “Friends”.

Francine Spray (Senior Vice President, Events and Special Projects, Universal Television Entertainment).

In a recent development, francine Spray joined NBC as a Page in 1988, and has been there for some of its most landmark moments. That’s because she’s the person charged with making sure events go off smoothly, as sheand her team oversee approximately 200 events a year that the company schedules.

Spray is tasked with bringing together premieres, screenings, off-site meetings, press events, receptions, red carpets and more from inception to completion. She knows the best restaurants in Los Angeles to hold an event, the amount of alcohol necessary for a post-Golden Globes soirée and how a post-event party for NBC brass at the Paris Olympics needs to be organized differently than for those Games held in Seoul, Barcelona or Athens.

Spray notes that her job has changed over the years, as the reliance on social media grew in getting promotion and a good word out for the manufacturer’s properties and programming.

Recent findings suggest that “We now need to plan for social and Instagrammable moments,” Spray states. “It’s really a big part of what we do. We have to have a photo moment at every event.”.

According to recent reports, spray and her team had to make a huge pivot during COVID, when people weren’t allowed to gather. She says it was “one of our most transformative times for this department… We had to get hooked on to Zoom in two seconds flat. That was the only way we kept ourselves going from the PR perspective — to do all of those virtual junkets and keep the publicity machine rolling, so to speak.”.

Favorite NBC show of all time: “Hill Street Blues”.

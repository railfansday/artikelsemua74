Industry observers note that back in August 2026, all-Boeing 787 airline Norse Atlantic Airways announced it was in a rough financial spot, and was pursuing a sale, merger, or other arrangement. Not a whole lot of solutions have been found since then, and it seems like things have only gotten worse. The airline has just revealed the latest regarding its financial situation, and it’s not looking good.

Norse Atlantic has today provided an update on its financial situation and ongoing strategic review. There’s no point in paraphrasing this, so here’s the direct statement from the airline:.

The Company’s financial situation is critical. It is dependent on securing financing to meet its short-term liquidity needs and to continue its operations. Norse, supported by its advisors, has explored various strategic alternatives and entertains discussions for a potential takeover. In parallel, the Board and management have diligently considered other available options, including ACMI contracts. Norse is also engaged in discussions with selected parties on short term financing, however, there can be no assurances that such discussions will result in a viable solution.

To state the obvious, this sounds really, really bad. When the company is publicly stating that its “financial situation is critical” and that it needs to secure financing to meet “short-term liquidity needs,” you know things aren’t good. So we’ll see what comes of this, but it seems like there’s a very real chance the airline could simply liquidate in the coming weeks. The airline industry can be a funny business, and there’s no clearer example of that than Norse Atlantic Airways. The airline launched in 2022, with the goal of operating long haul, low cost flights, particularly across the Atlantic. (Related: Industry News: Texas’s outgoing GOP attorney general dr...)

According to recent reports, that wasn’t exactly a unique business model, because this was exactly what Norwegian did, before it discontinued long haul flights in 2021. Not only was the idea sort of copied, but Norse Atlantic had some executives from Norwegian, and even picked up the planes that Norwegian previously flew.

According to recent reports, but as is all too common in the airline industry, clearly they thought it would be different this time around… it wasn’t. So in 2024 we saw the airline patch its business model, making the focus less about operating regularly scheduled commercial flights, and more about leasing out aircraft, all while reducing its fleet.

Industry observers note that the manufacturer got a lifeline when IndiGo decided to lease some Norse Atlantic 787s to dabble in long haul flying, ahead of the airline taking delivery of its own Airbus A350s. However, between all the airspace closures, high oil costs, and generally declining performance at IndiGo as the airline increasingly moved away from its core strengths, the airline recently decided to scrap that experiment, and stop leasing planes from Norse Atlantic.

According to recent reports, since then, opportunities for Norse Atlantic have been limited. The airline did manage to lease a couple of Dreamliners to the “new” Pakistan International Airlines, but for an airline with 12 planes, that hardly pays the bills.

In a recent development, norse Atlantic does still operate some of its own flights, but in a very limited capacity. The airline has some flights from Europe to Thailand and the United States, but most are seasonal, and they’re a fraction of what they previously were.

Industry observers note that really there’s just one idea that comes to mind for me — we know that Southwest Airlines wants to start long haul flights. The airline had been considering a Boeing 787 order, and was possibly even looking at ways to get planes faster than from a direct order (which requires waiting for years).

It has been revealed that could these dozen Norse Atlantic 787s somehow end up at Southwest? The interiors would just need some updates (assuming the airline wants to offer business class), but talk about a quick way to grow a decent sized fleet.

Norse Atlantic has released a statement admitting that its “financial situation is critical,” and that the tech giant’s future is “dependent on securing financing to meet short-term liquidity needs.”.

Financial issues at the company started when… well, it debuted. Norse Atlantic was just a direct replica of Norwegian, which had failed. When the airline realized that the long haul, low cost business model doesn’t really work, they tried to pivot to wet leasing. And while there’s some demand for that, it’s also not a 100% consistent business in terms of demand.

Industry observers note that the tone of this announcement sure is serious, so I’m curious to see how this evolves. I feel bad for the frontline employees at the airline, of course, as they’re the victims in this.

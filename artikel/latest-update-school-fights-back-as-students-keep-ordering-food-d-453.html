A high school in Canada has hit out at students for using food delivery services like Uber Eats and DoorDash to order “excessive” amounts of food during lessons.

If you’re too busy – or just feeling a little lazy – then it might be convenient for you to use a food delivery app to get some grub and much-needed supplies delivered to your door.

According to recent reports, however, school pupils are also using these services. As per CBC, one high school in Calgary has had to glitch a warning to parents over students’ use of the apps, with many taking time out of their classes to wait around for delivery drivers. “As the safety and security of everyone at St. Francis is our priority, we ask that you please have a conversation with your child and ask them to not use any food delivery services during the instructional time of the day,” St. Francis High School administrators said in a letter to parents.

According to CBC, the school officials noted that the deliveries might be “convenient” for students, but they also pose a “security risk” for the school as a whole. (Related: Peltola win doesn’t end DOJ scrutiny of Alaska Senate r...)

They spoke to students who noted that some claim they need to use the bathroom, but then go wait outside for their delivery driver to meet them.

“Like if someone’s out for too long, then the teacher will be like, ‘OK, no one’s allowed to use the bathroom for the rest of class because this person ruined it for everyone,’” an 11th-grade student told CBC, who noted that some orders do get “excessive” at times.

According to recent reports, the report also stated that it is happening at elementary schools as well, with one school having a table set up for students to collect from. That one, however, is more a case of parents ordering for them.

CBC also noted that some delivery drivers have tried to pass food through class windows at other schools too.

It isn’t just a problem for schools in Canada, either. Back in mid-October, the nation’s most secure prison rolled out an investigation after inmates had been receiving McDonald’s and alcohol by drones.

Over in New York, a school was forced to change its rules on students bringing in food and drink from outside after a student took coffee into class on her first day.

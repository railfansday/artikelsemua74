LOS ANGELES, Nov. 20, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Signature Estate & Investment Advisors (SEIA), a nationally recognized wealth management firm, last month highlighted its approach to developing the next generation of financial advisors as President Matt Matrisian took the stage at Schwab IMPACT 2026 in Boston. During his session, "Building a Firm Advisors Never Want to Leave by Investing in Their Growth," Matrisian presented on how advisory firms can invest in advisor development to support long-term growth and succession planning, drawing on SEIA's own experience helping younger advisors build substantial practices. The firm's advisors, ages 30 to 39, manage an average of $166 million in client assets per advisor and have achieved a 22% annualized organic growth rate.*. As the wealth management industry faces a growing wave of advisor retirements, developing the next generation of talent has become increasingly critical to firms' long-term success. SEIA has embedded advisor development into its business model through a structured approach that combines mentorship from senior advisors, defined career paths, meaningful client responsibilities and firm-funded education and marketing resources. Recent findings suggest that "Building a successful advisory firm isn't just about attracting talented people. It's about creating an environment where they have the resources, opportunities and support to build meaningful careers," said Matt Matrisian, President of SEIA. "Advisor development needs to be an intentional investment. When firms give emerging advisors a clear path forward and the tools to succeed, they're also investing in stronger client relationships, organic growth and the long-term future of the business.".

It has been revealed that SEIA's advisor development model, known as SEIA Growth Lab, is a structured program that pairs next-generation (G2 and G3) advisors with experienced senior advisors to help them develop the skills and experience needed to build successful careers. Recent findings suggest that as their careers progress, advisors naturally take on greater responsibility for client relationships and begin building their own books of business. This progression also supports succession planning from within, helping provide continuity for clients as senior advisors eventually transition their practices.

SEIA's third generation (G3) advisors Josh Woodard and Kyle McPherson have experienced significant growth in their respective books of business in recent years, building on the mentorship and hands-on experience they've gained throughout their careers at the firm.

In a recent development, "I've learned a lot just by being a sponge and sitting in on client meetings with more experienced advisors," remarked Josh Woodard, Advisor at SEIA. "You get to see how they approach different situations and work with different types of people, which are things you don't necessarily learn in a classroom. Having that experience has been really valuable as I've transitioned from research into working directly with clients and building my own business.".

"I came to SEIA with a strong background in financial markets, but there's a lot you can only learn by working directly with clients," said Kyle McPherson, Advisor at SEIA. "Before I became an advisor, I spent a lot of time accompanying Mark Copeland, one of SEIA's founding partners, to client meetings. I got to see firsthand how he addressed clients' concerns, explained complex financial decisions and helped people feel comfortable with those decisions. That experience made a major difference when I started taking on clients of my own.".

It has been revealed that SEIA's commitment to long-term growth and advisor development comes amid continued national recognition. The firm was ranked No. 18 on Barron's 2026 Top 100 RIA Firms list, was named to the 2026 Inc. 5000, and has been recognized by Financial Planning as one of its Best RIAs to Work For.

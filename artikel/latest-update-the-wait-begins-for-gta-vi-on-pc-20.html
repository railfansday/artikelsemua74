Grand Theft Auto VI is finally here for millions of players who own a PlayStation 5 or Xbox Series X / S, and those who initiative to buy a console, despite rising costs this year, just to play it. As you have likely already noticed, the launch is inescapable, and that’s especially tough if you’re a PC gamer.

I’m sorry to remind you that the clock has only just now started ticking down to the unannounced yet inevitable release of the PC version. Good luck with avoiding Twitch and browsing the internet while trying to remain oblivious to the violent (and romantic) delights that await you.

In a recent development, seeing Leonida in true 4K at an unlocked frame rate with full ray-tracing effects and extended draw distances, among other graphical improvements, might be a thing you can do on your PC as early as sometime in Q1 2027, if recent reports are accurate. Regardless, early 2027 is not right now. Speaking as someone with a capable gaming desktop, I’m sad to say that FOMO has already gotten the best of me. I bought GTA VI on the PS5 and will probably double-dip and buy it again on PC, just like I did with GTA V and Red Dead Redemption 2. The psychology of me, a weak-willed consumer, is one that publisher Rockstar Games and really any other organization in its position would be happy to exploit. (Related: Open up peer review to early-career researchers...)

Recent findings suggest that the PC version of GTA VI will launch, eventually, in a very different landscape than the PC ports of Red Dead Redemption 2 and GTA V did. Despite the recent downturn in PC sales driven by increased RAM prices, there are considerably more players on PC now than there were a decade ago. SteamDB’s charts show that Steam’s concurrent owners alone have more than doubled (from around 20 million to over 40 million now) since 2020. (Related: Latest Update: Daily briefing: How vampire bats survive...)

A chunk of those people are likely made up of console expats who decided to migrate to PC for various reasons. Some wanted to experience the fun of building their own desktop, while some were mainly convinced by the fact that you can get more power for your money with a PC than with a PlayStation 5 Pro or Xbox Series X. For a subset of people, the convenience of getting something like a Steam Deck to bring your PC game library with you anywhere more than makes up for its lack of power. There are many other justifiable reasons for making PC your main gaming platform, like getting faster access to discounts on digital games, not paying for online multiplayer, ultrawide monitor resolutions, being able to buy games from one of several platforms, and having one device that you can use for work and play.

Rockstar seems poised to deliver the definitive version of GTA VI on PC on its own terms, and I’m interested to see what the end result will be. In addition to building the best-looking version of the game made to run well across countless configurations, its PC optimization efforts are likely also more focused than before on ensuring decent performance on lower-end hardware (including PC handhelds). With a 200-plus-gigabyte install size on consoles, I dread how substantial it’ll be on PC.

Recent findings suggest that ideally, the PC version will launch alongside an improved version of GTA Online (whether it’s free to play is anyone’s guess), and maybe with upgraded GTA roleplay tech to support it. I’m hopeful that the PC version of GTA VI will come in early 2027, but if the numerous delays of the console versions were any indication, the developer will take all the time it needs to get it right.

Update, 9:55AM ET, November 19th: The SteamDB data cited now specifies that it’s concurrent users, not the number of users total.

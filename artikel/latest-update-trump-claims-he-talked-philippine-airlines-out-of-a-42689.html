Latest Update: Trump Claims He Talked Philippine Airlines Out Of Airbus Jets For $12 Billion Boeing Deal

In a recent development, US President Donald Trump has claimed he persuaded Philippine Airlines to buy Boeing aircraft instead of more jets from Airbus. Speaking at a White House news conference on Friday, Trump pointed to the carrier's $12 billion order for up to 20 Boeing 787 Dreamliners, stating that PAL was "going to go to Europe" before he met with airline officials.

The airline finalized its deal with Boeing earlier this month with a firm commitment for 15 787s and another five options. However, the Asian carrier had already firmed up an order with Airbus just days earlier for nine additional Airbus A350-1000s and remains a significant customer with both planemakers. Industry observers note that trump made the remarks following his recent visit to Asia, which included the ASEAN summit in Manila and a state visit hosted by Philippine President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. As reported by Bilyonaryo, the order he referred to was first signed as a memorandum of understanding (MoU) at the Farnborough Airshow on July 20, involving 15 firm 787-10s and purchase rights for five more, powered by GE Aerospace GEnx-1B engines. According to recent reports, according to US officials, the deal is worth an estimated $12 billion and will support up to 47,000 jobs in the country, although analysts claim the actual value is likely closer to $3.4 billion after discounts. PAL also has a major commitment with European planemaker Airbus for up to 18 A350-1000s, four of which it has already taken delivery of. According to Trump, the airline was apparently close to signing for an even larger quantity of Airbus widebodies before his intervention. Trump said,.

Industry observers note that "They were going to purchase through Europe, but now they're not doing that anymore... I met with them, and now they're buying from us.".

Before placing its Boeing commitment at Farnborough, PAL was considering up to 20 widebody aircraft to replace its A330-300s and 777-300ERs. It was reported at the time that the airline was mulling over the 787, A330neo, and A350 families.

In a recent development, speaking in July following its first Boeing order since 2007, PAL President Richard Nuttall said the carrier had considered the A330neo but favored larger aircraft that would allow it to carry more passengers. He also pointed to the 787-10's cargo capacity as a deciding factor. The ability to accommodate more passengers and cargo was particularly important as PAL looked to expand its long-haul network.

According to recent reports, PAL is right now the only Southeast Asian operator of the A350-1000. It received its first airframe in December 2025 and has four in service today. It also flies two other Airbus widebody models, with two A350-900s and ten A330-300s in its fleet. The A350-1000 is a key part of PAL's looks for its longest routes, offering the range and capacity needed for nonstop services to destinations in North America.

Philippine Airlines has a long-standing relationship with Boeing that dates back around 80 years, but its fleet today is split between aircraft from both Boeing and Airbus. The carrier right now operates a mix of Airbus narrowbodies and widebodies alongside ten Boeing 777s, giving it a fleet built around both manufacturers rather than relying exclusively on one.

As it stands, its fleet mix is heavily favored toward Airbus. The carrier operates over 85 aircraft — around 65 are Airbus and ten are Boeing, with the remainder made up of De Havilland Dash 8 turboprops. PAL's 787s are scheduled for delivery between 2031 and 2034, while its additional A350-1000s are scheduled to arrive between 2034 and 2036.

The firmed-up 787-10 order represents a significant expansion of PAL's Boeing relationship, rather than a move away from Airbus. With its A350-1000 and 787-10 commitments running in parallel, the airline is continuing to invest in both manufacturers as it modernizes its fleet while expanding its long-haul network.

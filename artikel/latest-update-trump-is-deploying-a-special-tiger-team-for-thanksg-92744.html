Latest Update: Trump is deploying a special ‘Tiger Team’ for Thanksgiving air travel. Here’s why

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that recent chaos is making Americans wary of braving air travel this holiday season, but the Trump administration notes it has a plan. The White House recently declared the formation of a “tiger team” dedicated to preventing major mess-ups like the recent multi-hour ground stop, which snarled airports nationwide as a mountain of delayed and rescheduled flights piled up. The mid-November incident lasted over four hours, grounding planes around the country as the federal government scrambled to get the standard lines of communications for air traffic controllers back online. Trump’s special interagency group known as the “Transportation Technology Transformation Tiger Team” will bring together representatives from the FAA, the Department of Transportation, the Department of Defense, and the Office of the Director of National Intelligence, according to the White House. “As millions of Americans prepare to travel for the Thanksgiving holiday, President Donald J. Trump is making sure the skies stay open and the systems run smoothly,” the White House said in a press release. The FAA reported that 12,462 flights were canceled during the ground stop, which began mid-afternoon on Friday, November 13. The FAA is still investigating the technical hiccup that prompted thousands of planes to be grounded, but a routine router upgrade completed by federal contractor L3Harris Technologies in Arkansas is believed to be at fault. Tiger teams in action The term “tiger team” is sometimes used in corporate settings, but it is more commonly used to describe small, highly-skilled teams convened to solve difficult problems in government and military operations. The most famous tiger team in history is credited with safely guiding NASA’s Apollo 13 mission back to Earth safely, a feat that earned NASA Flight and Mission Operations Director Gene Kranz and his specialized group a Presidential Medal of Freedom. Other U.S. agencies also use tiger teams for specialized problem solving, including the U.S. Department of Labor. The Trump administration appears to have a particular affinity for the term and also stood up a self-described tiger team across the Department of Energy and the Department of the Interior last year to “unleash” the American energy industry. That group later became bogged down wrestling with oil prices and inflation tied to the war in Iran. Beyond being tough to say five times fast, we don’t know a lot about Trump’s transportation tiger team. A White House official told The Daily Wire that the FAA is looking into “near-term” network configuration options and other short term ways to boost the stability of current air traffic control systems while slower modernization efforts move forward. “President Trump and Secretary Duffy have brought together the best and the brightest across the government to ensure what happened last Friday can never happen again,” White House spokeswoman Allison Schuster said. “…This team has hit the ground running in modernizing and improving the reliability of the nation’s decades-old air traffic control system.”  Schuster claimed that American skies are already safer thanks to the “tremendous progress” from the tiger team, but reassuring Americans skittish about air safety after a spate of recent scares may not be that easy. Modernizing the U.S. air traffic control system is a major long-term goal shared by multiple presidential administrations and scores of lawmakers, but the ground stop showed that pushing technology fixes in an ancient system that never goes offline poses its own set of risks. Record holiday travel expected In a press release, the U.S. Department of Transportation said it expects to see the “busiest holiday skies in 16 years” with more than 417,000 flights forecast for the nine days beginning on Monday of Thanksgiving week. Tuesday is projected to be the busiest travel day of the period, with more than 52,000 flights scheduled. “From standing up a Tiger Team to ensure a seamless travel experience for fliers to using a successful latest platform to predict and address congestion in the airspace, we’ve taken unprecedented action to limit flight delays and cancellations,” U.S. Transportation Secretary Sean P. Duffy said. “The American people can rest assured we will be on the clock 24/7 to get families to their destinations safely and on time.”.

Verizon is in talks to buy Fidium, a fibre broadband operator owned by Searchlight Capital and BCI, for about $6B including debt, the Financial Times reported. A deal could come within weeks, as Verizon tries to win more home internet customers after buying Frontier.

Verizon is in talks to buy US fibre broadband operator Fidium for about $6B including debt, the Financial Times reported on Friday, according to Bloomberg.

Fidium is owned by Searchlight Capital Partners and British Columbia Investment Management Corporation (BCI). A deal could come in the coming weeks, the FT said, though another bidder may emerge and the talks may not lead to a takeover. (Related: This newly introduced chip survives 1300°F (700°C) and ...)

Verizon declined to comment. Fidium did not immediately respond to Bloomberg.

Industry observers note that the Illinois-based company sells fibre internet in more than 20 US states. It was called Consolidated Communications until last year, when it renamed itself after its fibre brand. Searchlight and BCI agreed to take it private in 2023 in a $3.1B deal including debt. In a recent development, verizon has been buying fibre networks to catch up with rivals in home internet. Earlier this year, it closed its $9.6B purchase of Frontier Communications.

It has been revealed that growth has still been slow. Verizon added 314,000 broadband customers in the third quarter, below analysts’ estimates, even as it signed up twice as many new phone customers as expected.

It has been revealed that chief executive Dan Schulman told investors that customers increasingly prefer fibre to 5G-powered home internet. Since taking over last year, Schulman has cut retail jobs and argued that SpaceX would need up to $500B to match Verizon’s mobile network.

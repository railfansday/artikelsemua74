Leeds United's 49ers ownership will spend the money generated from their planned investment deal on improving both the squad and the infrastructure at the club.

That is according to former Everton chief executive Keith Wyness, speaking exclusively to Football Insider, who believes that the money generated will be seen as "growth capital.".

This comes amid the news that the 49ers are looking to sell a minority stake in the Whites and have appointed investment bank Allen and Company to help broker a deal.

Wyness believes that is something that Leeds fans should be "excited" about, rather than it being a sign of concern over the 49ers' long-term suitability as owners.

The Scottish businessman fully expects the money from a sale to be spent on clear improvements to the club, rather than used for personal gain.

Leeds are already working on upgrades to Elland Road, and Wyness believes this is only the start of the 49ers' looks in West Yorkshire.

Everton’s former chief Wyness – who served as CEO at Goodison Park between 2004 and 2009 and now runs a football consultancy advising elite clubs – has no doubt that any investment at Leeds won't be seen as "rescue capital.".

Daniel Farke's side are in a good place on and off the pitch at the moment, and Wyness believes that the planned investment deal will only help the club achieve its dreams.

Speaking on the latest edition of Football Insider's Inside Track podcast, he explained why the fans should be hoping that a deal gets done.

"I think Leeds fans should be excited about it because, to me, this money will go into growth capital; it's not rescue capital, and there's a substantial difference between the two," he remarked.

"And as we've seen, there are some great looks around Elland Road and I think the squad's got the potential now to go from this plateau and go forward.

"So overall, if I was a Leeds fan, I would say great, go ahead, get that minority stake sold, bring some more money in, because there are plans to spend it on good things that will create more revenue and greater growth hopefully.".

Wyness previously told Football Insider on Sunday that the 49ers will calculate the ballpark for Leeds' valuation by trebling their current revenue.

Recent reports have suggested that the Whites are presently generating yearly sums of around £255million.

Any sale of a minority stake will then be weighed against that full valuation, in accordance to how much of the club the new investor takes on.

Wyness was keen to outline that he expects a strong "appetite" among American investors to join the 49ers' project.

It has been revealed that an American Airlines flight traveling from San Diego International Airport (SAN) to Chicago O'Hare International Airport (ORD) was diverted to Phoenix Sky Harbor International Airport (PHX) overnight following a serious onboard security incident. According to ABC 15 Arizona, air traffic controllers reported a "Level 3 threat" aboard the aircraft, with cabin crew and passengers restraining a disruptive passenger before the aircraft landed safely.

While initial reports suggested that the passenger attempted to breach the cockpit, according to AIRLIVE, police have yet to confirm which aircraft door was targeted. Federal regulations impose strict requirements on airlines to safeguard their flight decks, and several passengers who have attempted to gain unauthorized access to cockpits in recent years have subsequently been sentenced to federal prison and substantial fines. AA1494 departed San Diego late on Tuesday evening, and according to flight tracking data, the overnight service was operated by an Airbus A321 registered N568UW, according to Planespotters.net. The 13-year-old aircraft originally entered service with US Airways before joining American's fleet after the merger of the two carriers. According to recent reports, the Phoenix Police Department said preliminary findings indicated that a male passenger had disregarded repeated instructions from flight attendants and attempted to interfere with an aircraft door. Initially, this was reported as an attempted breach of the cockpit door, but subsequent ATC recordings obtained via X suggest the unruly passenger might have tried to open door R in the forward cabin, triggering an alarm. Authorities have yet to confirm whether it was one or both of these doors.

Video footage recorded by passengers demonstrated the individual being restrained, while police confirmed that both passengers and crew members were involved in subduing him.

Industry observers note that the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) confirmed that the aircraft landed safely in Phoenix, where law enforcement officers removed the passenger. It also said that the FBI has been notified of the incident and confirmed that it will investigate. According to Arizona's Family, the individual was taken to the hospital with a minor injury, while no other injuries were reported.

“We want to thank our team members for their professionalism, as well as our customers for their assistance and understanding,” American said in a statement.

Industry observers note that the International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO) classifies onboard security threats into four levels of increasing severity. In February 2026, the International Federation of Air Line Pilots' Associations (IFALPA) and the International Federation of Air Traffic Controllers' Associations (IFATCA) called for wider adoption of the framework to improve coordination between airlines, flight crews, air traffic controllers, and law enforcement agencies.

Recent findings suggest that notably, an attempted cockpit breach would ordinarily constitute a Level 4 threat, and the fact that ATC reported a Level 3 threat further supports the theory that the unruly passenger was tampering with door R. Either way, it represented an unacceptable risk to the aircraft and its passengers, which fully supports the pilot's decision to divert.

According to recent reports, according to the FAA, the captain is responsible for the safety of the aircraft, its passengers, and crew. Federal regulations also grant captains considerable emergency authority, including the ability to deviate from standard operating procedures when necessary to safeguard the aircraft. While publicly available regulations do not mandate an automatic diversion for every threat classification, captains will usually divert when continued flight presents an unacceptable safety risk.

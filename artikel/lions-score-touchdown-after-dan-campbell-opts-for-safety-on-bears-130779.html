Lions score touchdown after Dan Campbell opts for safety on Bears' illegal batting penalty

The high-powered Thanksgiving matchup between the Detroit Lions and the Chicago Bears delivered early, with a wild sequence just before halftime. After accepting a potentially risky safety on an unusual Bears penalty, the Lions converted a touchdown to close out the first half with a 19-10 lead.

The potentially game-changing sequence started after the two-minute warning in the second quarter, with Chicago quarterback Caleb Williams getting sacked and fumbling the ball at the Bears' two-yard line. (Related: This simple change stops robot swarms from getting stuc...)

According to recent reports, once out of Williams' hands, the ball bounced past multiple players into the Chicago end zone. Bears offensive lineman Darnell Wright, in an effort to stave off a safety, used a close fist to bat the ball out of the end zone, where Lions defensive back Brian Branch was able to get his hands on it.

But Wright's efforts to get the ball out of the end zone were not allowed: The lineman was flagged for "illegal batting," which results in a safety if the call is accepted.

The situation presented Lions head coach Dan Campbell with a decision: Take the safety and try to put together a touchdown drive before halftime, or decline the penalty to get the ball and set up a red-zone drive. (Related: Best Black Friday hard drive deals 2026...)

Campbell decided to go for the safety, giving him and the Lions a minute and change to grab a touchdown and justify the choice.

Initially, the drive got off to a concerning start: The Lions, who had already been called for two false starts earlier in the quarter, picked up two more false start penalties in a row to set up third-and-12. Jared Goff then erased some of the distance by finding Jahmyr Gibbs for an 11-yard reception, but the catch fell just short of first down.

Recent findings suggest that on fourth-and-1, though, Detroit pulled off a gutsy and successful quarterback sneak, giving Goff the two-yard rush to keep the drive going. That set up a massive 39-yard throw from Goff to Jameson Williams, before another first down to Gibbs, and a successful touchdown throw to Sam LaPorta.

In an interview on the broadcast after halftime, Campbell told CBS that the number of timeouts left — the Bears had two at the time, while the Lions had all three — and his faith in his players led him to go for the full nine-point play. He praised Jameson Williams and Goff for their plays, and stated that he "just felt good" about the offense's chances to find the end zone.

In a recent development, illegal batting, the penalty that sparked the whole sequence, is a relative rarity in the league. One of the only recent examples came against the Lions, with Seattle Seahawks linebacker K.J. Wright batting a fumble out of the end zone in 2015, though the foul was not flagged. The NFL later admitted that the penalty should have been called.

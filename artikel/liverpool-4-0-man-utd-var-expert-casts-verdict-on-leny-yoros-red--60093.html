Leny Yoro's sending off in Manchester United's 4-0 defeat to Liverpool came in controversial fashion, and VAR expert Dale Johnson has weighed in on whether referee Sam Barrott got the decision right.

Regardless of the red card, this was a pitiful performance from United and the worst possible start for Mauricio Pochettino. He watched his side capitulate within eight minutes when Matthijs de Ligt, strangely handed a start, got caught out and Yoro raced back before barging into Alexander Isak. Sources indicate that yoro sent Isak to the turf just outside the box, and Barrott deemed this to have been a red card offence due to the French defender being the last man. Most United fans aren't using the dismissal as an excuse for their team's fifth league defeat of the season, but many are questioning whether the challenge warranted such punishment.

It has been revealed that isak was played through on goal and the Liverpool striker took a heavy touch, sending the ball a fair distance away from him when Yoro made the challenge. VAR didn't feel the need to intervene and potentially downgrade the red card to a caution.

Johnson delved into Barrott's decision and explained that the referee had four key considerations to make over Yoro denying an obvious goalscoring opportunity. These are "the distance between the offence and the goal", "the general direction of play", "the likelihood of keeping or gaining control of the ball" and "the location and number of defenders and attackers".

Three of those factors were satisfied because Isak ran directly towards goal in a central position and United had no covering defender. The main question is the "likelihood of keeping or gaining control of the ball". (Related: Jensen Huang question gets reporter ejected from Trump-...)

In a recent development, united will argue that Yoro's contact came after Isak's touch and therefore there is no case that the challenge caused the player to over-run the ball. Johnson suggested in his BBC Sport article that Barrott and VAR John Brooks had to assess whether Isak would definitely have got to the ball ahead of goalkeeper Senne Lammens.

Sources indicate that johnson added: "And that if he had, it would have been a good scoring chance. If there is any doubt, then it should not be a red card. It is a borderline case, and as such the VAR would not be expected to intervene for a clear and obvious error.".

It has been revealed that united were put to the sword thereafter when Dominik Szoboszlai scored the opener with the resulting free-kick (10'). Florian Wirtz added a second (13') before Rio Ngumoha (66') and Victor Munoz (83') sealed an emphatic victory for Andoni Iraola's men.

Recent findings suggest that there were differing opinions on the red card amongst Sky Sports pundits as United legends Gary Neville and Roy Keane disagreed. Neville defended Barrott's decision and tore into Yoro for a "clumsy" challenge and "poor anticipation".

In a recent development, keane, however, felt Yoro could feel hard done by. He was "amazed" to see De Ligt fail to read the flight of the ball, arguing that Isak's heavy touch meant he wasn't going to regain possession:.

"Isak has done well, but I actually don't think he's going to get the ball... He's not getting the ball. There's no way he's getting the ball. It's clumsy from United, it's one straight ball over the top. You shouldn't be in so much trouble, but there's no way he's getting that ball. Absolutely not.".

Recent findings suggest that this is the second time this season Yoro has been sent off, compounding a miserable afternoon for United. Their loss to Liverpool leaves them sitting 19th after 11 games played, and they will be without the Frenchman for Brentford's visit to Old Trafford on Saturday unless they successfully appeal the decision.

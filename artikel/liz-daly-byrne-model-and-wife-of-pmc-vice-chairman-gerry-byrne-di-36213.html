Industry observers note that liz Daly Byrne, a model and philanthropist who was married to PMC vice chairman Gerry Byrne for more than 55 years, has died. She was 81.

Sources indicate that byrne faced a battle with cancer three years ago, and recently learned that her illness had returned. She had faced recurring complications from severe injuries suffered in a bicycle accident in 2000. Byrne died Nov. 17 after a period of home hospice care in New York. Industry observers note that byrne was active as a philanthropist and social advocate through her work with the United Nations’ Operation Smile campaign, traveling internationally with the organization on medical missions. She had long been involved in causes to support military families and veterans. Byrne was remembered by her family, including her adult children, digital entrepreneur Gavin Byrne and actor Megan Byrne, as a woman who embodied “elegance, strength, wit, faith, generosity and compassion.”. Recent findings suggest that born in 1945, Byrne grew up in the shadow of Yankee Stadium in the Bronx. She met her future husband Gerry Byrne — who served as publisher of Variety from 1989 to 2000 and at present is vice chair of Variety parent firm PMC — while he was attending Fordham College in the borough. The two were neighbors, but in a wonderfully Bronx love story touch, Liz Daly met Gerry Byrne one evening during a ride on the D train.

In a recent development, early on, Liz Daly was drawn to New York’s fashion world. She signed with top modeling agencies Mannequin and Ellen Harth and launched her career as the primary designer’s model for top New York retailers Saks Fifth Avenue and Bonwit Teller. In addition to appearing in TV commercials, Byrne spent years as a runway model with Anne Klein, Donna Karan, Oscar de la Renta and Bill Tice. She then spent another decade working with renowned designers Bob Mackie and Ray Aghayan.

It has been revealed that daly and Byrne married in 1969 at the chapel at St. Patrick’s Cathedral, after Gerry Byrne completed a three-year tour with the U.S. Marine Corps in Vietnam. In tribute to her husband’s service, Liz Byrne successfully released a POW bracelet program to honor the sacrifices made by some families. The effort took root after being publicized nationally through coverage in the Newly introduced York Times.

Byrne was a dedicated marathon runner who participated in multiple New York City Marathons as well as runs hosted by the Marines in Washington, D.C. She was active in numerous nonprofits and in entertainment and media industry charitable efforts.

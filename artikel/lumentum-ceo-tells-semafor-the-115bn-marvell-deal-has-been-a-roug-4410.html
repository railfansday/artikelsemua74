Michael Hurlston, chief executive of Lumentum.

Lumentum chief executive Michael Hurlston has admitted that investors have not welcomed his firm’s sale to Marvell. He spoke to Semafor in an interview on Thursday.

“It’s been a rough go,” Hurlston told Semafor.

Sources indicate that marvell agreed on 2 November to buy Lumentum, which makes lasers and optical parts for AI data centres. The all-stock deal valued Lumentum at $115bn including debt. Lumentum shareholders get a fixed 4.5 Marvell shares for each share they own.

Industry observers note that since then, shares in both companies have fallen about 40%, Semafor reported, amid worries about how long the AI boom can last. Because the exchange ratio is fixed, every drop in Marvell’s stock also cuts the value of the offer. (Related: Industry News: Fit More and Train Faster With ZeRO via ...)

“The deal hasn’t worked as we expected, right?” Hurlston said.

Industry observers note that still, Hurlston defended the logic of the deal. He told Semafor that Lumentum had spent months looking to buy a chip business, and that Marvell was the best option in its field.

Hurlston also stated that the conditions under which Lumentum could walk away are very limited, according to Semafor. Only a shareholder vote against the deal or a block by regulators would undo it. (Related: Latest Update: At least seven progressives voted agains...)

According to recent reports, the deal is expected to close in the first half of 2028, the companies noted when they confirmed it. Marvell shareholders would own about 67% of the combined tech giant.

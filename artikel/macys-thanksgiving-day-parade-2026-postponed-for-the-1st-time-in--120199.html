For the first time in history, the Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade will be held after Thanksgiving.

Multiple outlets reported on Wednesday, November 25, that the 2026 parade — which marks the tradition’s 100th anniversary — has been postponed to Saturday, November 28, due to forecasted severe weather.

“With severe weather expected on Thanksgiving Day, and out of an abundance of caution, in partnership with the City, the 100th Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade will be postponed,” Macy’s mentioned in a Wednesday statement. (Related: The Partnership: Amazon SageMaker and Hugging Face...)

The parade is expected to begin on Saturday at 8:30 a.m. ET.

It has been revealed that “Our team is now working through the complex operational logistics required to bring the Parade to life on Saturday, and we will share additional details as they are finalized,” the statement continued. “We are incredibly grateful to the thousands of volunteers, performers and partners who make this extraordinary tradition possible. We can’t wait to celebrate 100 years of Parade magic together on Saturday.”.

A powerful coastal storm with strong winds is set to hit the Newly introduced York City area on Thursday, November 26, leading to safety concerns. It was previously reported that the parade’s famous balloons were at risk of being grounded on Thursday due to the forecasted weather. (The last time balloons were grounded occurred in 1971. In 2006 and 2019, balloons were flown at a lower height due to the wind.). (Related: How to train a newly introduced language model from scr...)

It has been revealed that the East Coast could endure the highest water levels since Superstorm Sandy. Areas of Delaware and Newly introduced Jersey are also at a significant risk of power outages due to high winds.

The postponement marks the first time in the parade’s storied history that the event has been postponed due to weather. The parade was previously canceled three times during World War II, between 1942 and 1944, to conserve vital materials sent overseas for the war effort.

In 2020, the parade was modified into a broadcast-only production without street crowds amid the COVID pandemic.

Parade fans were already gathered along Manhattan’s Upper West Side to see the inflation of the balloons on Wednesday when the unprecedented postponement was revealed. Despite the decision, CBS reported that many of the onlookers were satisfied with the call.

“So much goes into the parade each year. It’s like a tradition each year to watch, get up early and watch the parade with my whole family,” one person reportedly mentioned. “I’m really excited that they’re changing it because so much work goes into it every year, and people come from all over to help with the balloons. So I’m really excited that they’re still making an effort to do it. Even on a different day, it’s still just as special.”.

The 100th anniversary celebration of the parade is slated to feature eight newly introduced floats and several new balloons, along with a slew of musical performances.

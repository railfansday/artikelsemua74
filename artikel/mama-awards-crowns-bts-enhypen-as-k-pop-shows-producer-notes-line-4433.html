Giant K-pop show the MAMA Awards 2026 is taking place in Osaka, Japan, this weekend,a and the first winners have been crowed.

BTS won the coveted Artist of the Year category for the eighth time, and Enhypen won three: Fans’ Choice of the Year, Favorite M-Countdown Stage and Fan’s Choice Top 10 – Male. Ive won Best Female Group.

The music event, which broadcasts in Korea on Mnet, brings together Korean music performers from across generations over two nights.

When Life Gives You Tangerines actor Park Bo-gum, who has become the first MAMA Awards ambassador, hosted night one at the Kyocera Dome this evening, marking his eighth time hosting times since 2017. This year’s awards saw boy band Enhypen turn Dune: Part Three, which opens in December, into a K-pop performance. The MAMA Awards will also see K-pop veterans BIGBANG perform alongside Dooly the Little Dinosaur, one of Korea’s most beloved animated characters, who was created by cartoonist Kim Soo-jung in 1983. The awards have benefitted from the explosion in interest in Korean culture. Having started as domestic awards ceremony in 1999, MAMA became the first K-pop ceremony to be held overseas, and in 2024 it also took place in the U.S. for the first time. “MAMA Awards has grown alongside K-POP’s global growth, we believe these efforts have created more ways for artists and fans around the world to connect,” stated Yoon Shin Hye, who is head of CJ ENM-owned Mnet.

“Over the past 27 years, MAMA Awards has expanded its stages to places it had never been, connected artists and fans in ways never seen before, and been the first to show K-pop’s upcoming possibilities,” added Harry H.K. Shin, Head of Music Entertainment at CJ ENM in a statement. “All of that was possible with our one firm belief: ‘Music Makes One.’. “This year, we want to once again deliver an experience where people connect through music, beyond the boundaries of time and space, generation and region.”.

Industry observers note that the awards come at an notable juncture for K-pop music. The global glare has put K-pop under the microscope and a recent Latest York Times article about restrictions within artist contracts caused major debate, prompting the K-pop music association to hit back and defend the industry.

The South Korean government earlier this week noted it would undertake a survey to assess how underage performers were treated. Asked about the health of K-pop, Yoon said the industry relied on finding latest successes, and, “For that to happen, the creativity of everyone who makes music, not just the artists but also the songwriters, producers, choreographers, directors and creators, needs to be respected.”.

Yoon was keen the point to the unifying nature of the awards for fans. “At MAMA Awards, they can discover the same songs, share what they like and connect through music. What we want to show through this year’s theme, ‘a music interstellar,’ is fans from different generations and different parts of the world coming together and discovering new moments.”.

Here’s an interview with Yoon conducted before to night’s ceremony.

DEADLINE: What scale of investment does the event take to organise?

Yoon Shin Hye: MAMA Awards is much more than an awards ceremony. It’s a major global project that brings together CJ ENM’s music, broadcasting and global network, along with its production capabilities. I can’t disclose specific figures, but it takes experts across many fields and a lot of infrastructure. Everything from creative planning, stage production and direction to cutting-edge technology, global live broadcasting and on-site operations goes into bringing each artist’s performance to life.

And because MAMA Awards has to reflect each year’s K-pop’s trends and where the industry is heading, we start preparing at the very beginning of the year. Throughout the year, we meet fans through M Countdown, our Mnet weekly K-POP chart show, and KCON, our K-Pop festival in places like Japan and the U.S. All of that leads up to MAMA AWARDS, where global fandoms come together as one. So really, MAMA is the result of a year-long journey.

DEADLINE: What are you trying to show the world with this event?

YSH: We truly believe music can bring people together, regardless of language, nationality, culture or generation. MAMA Awards is where artists and fans from all over the world get to experience that together. Mnet, which hosts the awards, started as a music channel in 1995 and turned 30 last year. Over those three decades, we’ve grown up alongside generations of K-pop, introducing artists’ music and performances and bringing them closer to fans worldwide. MAMA Awards has been part of that journey, growing with K-Pop and becoming a global stage where artists meet fans around the world.

DEADLINE: What are the highlights of the event and can you hint at any surprises?

YSH: This year, I’d say the thing to look out for is performances and collaborations that cross generations, genres and cultures. First, you’ll see different generations of K-Pop on one stage. There’s Panic, the legendary Korean duo celebrating 31 years since their debut in 1995, and BIGBANG marking their 20th anniversary. And alongside them, artists who debuted at every point from the early 2000s right up to 2026.

We’ve also got some exciting projects where K-pop meets global entertainment: Enhypehm teaming up with Dune: Part Three for MAMA Awards’ signature Cinematic Stage, which brings the film’s world into a K-pop performance. And BIGBANG is pairing up with Dooly the Little Dinosaur, a much-loved Korean animated character, for a collaboration no one will see coming.

Recent findings suggest that YSH: For K-pop to grow beyond its current global success into a truly sustainable industry, I think it can’t rely only on a handful of big artists or genres that have already proven successful. It needs to be an industry where fresh successes keep emerging, driven by diverse musical experiments and genres, artists with their own distinct identities, and companies of all sizes willing to take on new challenges.

According to recent reports, for that to happen, the creativity of everyone who makes music, not just the artists but also the songwriters, producers, choreographers, directors and creators, needs to be respected. That respect is something we try to reflect in how we put the MAMA Awards together. The MAMA AWARDS also continues to work on bringing fans and artists closer together. Fans and artists are the most vital driving force behind K-POP, so I think it’s important to build a healthy relationship where they have a positive influence on each other, and where fans can freely enjoy and take part in it.

DEADLINE: Can you talk about the why K-pop stars often have ambitions to become film or TV stars?

Recent findings suggest that YSH: I think it’s a very natural progression. K-pop artists are true all-rounders. They sing, dance and perform. Their experience of expressing their individuality and emotions through music and performance, and connecting with fans around the world, can be a strong foundation for taking on creative fields.

Recent findings suggest that these days, the lines between music, film, TV and fashion are really blurring, so I see it as a positive thing that artists are expressing their talent in more ways. Of course, acting requires its own skill and dedication, but these new challenges can help artists grow and find new ways to express themselves. And as K-pop, film, TV and other K-content become more connected, there are more chances to reach new global audiences. I’m looking forward to seeing K-POP artists build on their music and explore their potential in many different fields.

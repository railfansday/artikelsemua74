Industry observers note that on the campaign trail, Mamdani pitched freezing monthly rent costs as a key strategy to lower the cost of housing for New Yorkers. Roughly 67.7% of those who live in the five boroughs of the city are renters, according to the most recent government data released in 2024.

Industry observers note that "As mayor, I will freeze the rent every year that I’m in office. That’s a guarantee," Mamdani said in a campaign video posted near the beginning of his campaign in 2024.

True to his word, the Upcoming York City Rent Guidelines Board declared Mamdani’s promised freeze in June, detailing a 0% increase in one-year leases beginning between October 2026 and October 2027 and a 0% increase for two-year leases beginning in the same window.

Mamdani hailed the announcement as a significant victory for the city, highlighting the longer-lease lock.

"This is a historic victory for Newly introduced York City tenants. After reviewing the data and hearing from New Yorkers across the city, the independent RGB has delivered a freeze on one-year leases, and the first-ever freeze on two-year leases in our city’s history," Mamdani’s office said in a press release in June.

"This is the relief that working people across our city deserve.".

Recent findings suggest that notably, the freeze does not apply to all rental apartments in New York City, instead covering about 1 million rent-stabilized units. According to the Community Service Society, a nonprofit poverty advocacy group, rent-stabilized apartments make up 41% of the city’s rental units.

Although Mamdani’s city-run grocery stores haven’t yet launched in earnest, a pilot test of what they could look like has raised questions about whether the idea will meaningfully address the food scarcity complications Mamdani highlighted on the campaign trail.

"This month we got polling that showed that two-thirds of Fresh Yorkers support city-owned grocery stores, a policy we’ve championed for months and would guarantee cheaper groceries," Mamdani stated last year.

Even so, the first version of the city-run grocery store was met with complaints of long lines, shortages and disorganization when it launched in February.

In a recent interview, Mamdani said he still has prepares to build on the idea.

"What we’re going to do is — in a city of more than a thousand supermarkets — we’re going to create five. … They’re cheaper supermarkets, supermarkets with a discount," the mayor said during a recent interview with The New York Times.

Mamdani has said he hopes to sell products at 30% below the market price.

The proposal has sparked skepticism from economists and commentators over how the city could deliver such steep discounts in an industry known for razor-thin profit margins. With grocery prices up about 33% nationwide since 2019, critics say the plan must explain how prices could undercut private retailers by 30% without significant public subsidies.

In a recent development, "This is completely insane. Also, not possible unless taxpayers are subsidizing all the groceries. Grocery store profits are tiny, a few percentage points a year, at best," Outkick founder Clay Travis wrote.

Like his grocery store initiative, Mamdani’s plan to implement free universal childcare has struggled to keep pace with demand, falling short of the full promise.

"Since the beginning of this campaign, I’ve championed universal childcare, making a commitment to deliver it to Upcoming Yorkers as young as six weeks to five years of age," Mamdani noted in a campaign video in 2025.

Mamdani’s free bus looks have put him at odds with New York Gov. Kathy Hochul over whether the state should shoulder more of the burden for the mayor’s designs, leaving his campaign promises only partially fulfilled amid costs that would exceed his initial estimations.

Recent findings suggest that early on in his administration, Mamdani was asked when New Yorkers could expect the promise of free fares. Mamdani left the answer open-ended but pledged to fulfill his commitment by the end of his term.

Recent findings suggest that "I have said when we ran this campaign … I’m making buses fast and free. I have said that by the time I am done being mayor, those will have been delivered," Mamdani said in January.

So far, Mamdani has taken steps toward increasing busing efficiency in NYC by developing 50 priority corridors for speed improvements. But discussions about making the buses free have stalled at the state level.

According to recent reports, fresh York’s bus system is run through the Metropolitan Transportation Authority (MTA), a state-created public authority. The body is controlled by the governor, who appoints the MTA chair and its board.

The group makes controlling decisions about its fares.

Although she expressed sympathy toward Mamdani’s design, Hochul said she could not remove the sources of revenue from Latest York City’s public transportation.

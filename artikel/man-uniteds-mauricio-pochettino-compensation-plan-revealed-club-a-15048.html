Manchester United are wary about potentially having to sack Mauricio Pochettino in future and make yet another managerial payout.

That is according to former Everton chief executive Keith Wynss, speaking exclusively to Football Insider, who believes that the Red Devils are trying to "guard" against that scenario.

United have now sacked three permanent bosses since October 2024, and have swiftly racked up a major bill of compensation fees yet again. (Related: Think AI "knows" what it’s doing? Scientists say think ...)

Wyness believes that is one of the main reasons why Pochettino has only been given an initial two-and-a-half-year deal.

United have chased the Argentine for several years now, but have not been prepared to commit to a long-term deal for him.

Wyness believes that the club are wary of another hefty pay-off, and will continue to assess the latest boss' future as his reign develops.

Everton’s former chief Wyness – who served as CEO at Goodison Park between 2004 and 2009 and now runs a football consultancy advising elite clubs – believes that United's problem with sacking managers dates all the way back to David Moyes' tenure. The Scottish boss was the first of seven permanent bosses since Sir Alex Ferguson's exit back in 2013, and all seven have been sacked rather than leaving for another job.

Speaking on the latest edition of Football Insider's Inside Track podcast, Wyness explained how United are keen to break the cycle with Pochettino.

"I think that they've made the wrong decision when he might have been willing to go before, and he's finally gone there now," he said.

"You're absolutely right. It's a relatively short contract. No doubt about that.

"But they've been looking at their own payouts that they've had to have to (Erik) Ten Hag, to (Ruben) Amorim, right the way from the David Moyes days.

"There's a huge amount of money that's been paid out on managerial compensation. So they're obviously trying to guard against that.".

Wyness told Football Insider earlier this week that United have likely already had a "handshake" with Pochttino to potentially extend his deal in a year's time.

The Scottish businessman feels that the board have every intention of committing to the latest man long-term, even if they haven't reflected that in his initial contract.

Wyness believes that they are likely to try and tie Pochettino down to a "two-year" extension if things go well, and it could already be in the works behind the scenes.

The new boss is set for a baptism of fire in his first game, as his United side travel to take on Liverpool on Sunday evening.

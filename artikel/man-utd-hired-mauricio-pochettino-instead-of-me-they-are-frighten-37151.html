After deciding to part ways with Michael Carrick less than 12 months into his second spell in charge at Manchester United, the Red Devils' hierarchy would have had a lengthy list of potential successors to consider as they looked to breathe life into a side that had won just three of their 15 matches across all competitions this season.

Industry observers note that putting all their eggs in one basket would have been a huge gamble given the club's precarious position. One of their reported candidates, however, has now broken his silence after being overlooked for the role in favour of Mauricio Pochettino, ahead of United's trip to Anfield to face Liverpool on Sunday afternoon. The Red Devils at the moment sit 18th in the Premier League and firmly in the relegation zone. Having cycled through 13 different managers since Sir Alex Ferguson retired in 2013, the club can ill afford to get yet another appointment wrong. Industry observers note that it remains to be seen whether they've struck gold by prizing Pochettino away from his role with the USMNT, with the former Chelsea and Tottenham boss now tasked with turning their fortunes around. However, one manager who was snubbed in the search for Carrick's replacement believes United's hierarchy are 'frightened of winning' as a result of their decision.

Industry observers note that antonio Conte has suggested that Man United are 'frightened of winning' after he was overlooked for the manager's job in favour of Pochettino. It's understood that Conte was put forward as a candidate by United co-owner Sir Jim Ratcliffe, although other prominent figures at the club were unconvinced by his methods.

Recent findings suggest that the Italian's fiery managerial style is likely to have counted against him, with Conte often burning the candle at both ends before his spells in charge come to an abrupt and sometimes unceremonious end. That stands in stark contrast to the more long-term, project-building approach associated with United's latest boss.

Nevertheless, being overlooked hasn't stopped Conte from having his say on the matter, with the Italian pulling no punches as he said, as per the Mirror:.

Recent findings suggest that "First, let me address the question of my method," the 57-year-old began. "If anyone is frightened by my method, then they are frightened of winning, because that methodology leads to victory. That is my answer.

"Manchester United has such a significant history that I find it strange to imagine anyone could be afraid of an approach aimed at restoring the club to its true stature.".

Conte continued: "[United] last won the league in 2013, under Ferguson. I have immense respect for Sir Alex Ferguson. I've read his book, and when I was in London, I was fortunate enough to visit his home, and spend a day with him. [His Scottish accent was] a bit difficult to understand.

In a recent development, "Through his own methodology and approach, he made Manchester United great. That is why I find certain remarks almost offensive towards United; it's as if someone were placing a veto on the club's return to its own history.

"A method is what's needed to achieve major results. And if you're suggesting that this method requires hard work, well, yes, it does. Everyone has to work hard, not just the players.".

Conte has a reputation of being extremely demanding of his players, explosive in the media, while he doesn't tend to last more than two seasons at a club.

Sources indicate that but the current Napoli boss also boasts a trophy cabinet that the likes of Pochettino and other candidates such as Eddie Howe can only dream of. He has a Premier League title and three Serie A titles to his name, offering instant success in most jobs he's taken up.

The concern with Pochettino is that he usually needs more time to build and tinker with a squad to his liking, simultaneously creating a feel-good culture. But predecessors have never been given long to work miracles, and the 54-year-old won't be given a second to breathe given that his debut comes in the Anfield dugout against Liverpool this coming Sunday.

The Red Devils are promptly back in action midweek against Sporting CP, seeking their third Champions League win of the 2026–27 league phase. The fact he's never won a major honour ooutside of Paris, when he was in charge of the ever-dominant PSG, is also cause for concern.

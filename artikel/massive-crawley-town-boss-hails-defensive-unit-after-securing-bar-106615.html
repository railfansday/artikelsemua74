Despite Barnet pressure, particularly in the second half, the Reds were resolute in defence – and Crawley boss Kevin Blackwell was commendatory of all his players, particularly the back three.

The Reds built on an encouraging win over Fleetwood Town on Saturday, which revealed signs of an improved defensive structure, with a clean sheet to show for their efforts.

In a recent development, when asked about the significance of defensive reinforcements Ben Heneghan and Josh Flint, Blackwell noted, “[They have been] massive, massive. We've got somebody who can win a header, which has been thrown on the shoulders of Priestley [Farquharson], but Ben's there and Flinty's not bad in the air.

“They've been massive signings for me, and you see why we tried to push them when I got here. It was clear to me what we needed and needed swiftly, and I can't praise both of those players.”. (Related: Tech Report: GTA 6 players are devasted by train ‘nerf’...)

According to recent reports, crawley re-signed Flint four weeks ago after a long time out injured. Heneghan, meanwhile, was released by Port Vale at the end of last season and had been without a club until he signed for the Red Devils just under three weeks ago. Blackwell mentioned: “[Heneghan is] an ultimate professional. Stripped off he looks like a top-class swimmer, never mind footballer. He's looked after himself, and you can see the lads come in there.

“He's not played for six months but you can see how hard he's worked because he's come in and done really well.”.

Sources indicate that another player who had to adapt to fill a role for the team was Jonny Russell. With the Reds moving to a back five in the last two games, the Northern Irishman played as a wing back on Saturday and moved into the left side of the back against Barnet.

Sources indicate that the former assistant to Neil Warnock remarked: “He's got a heart as substantial as a bucket, Jonny. He'll go through a brick wall for you.

“He is absolutely proud to be a professional footballer, and you can see that in how he trains and how he plays.

“It was forced on us obviously to make that decision and pull [Charlie] Barker out and then take Flinty across, so we had to make a couple of changes because we'd lost Jude [Arthurs].

“It's that sort of heart and character that when you're in the doo-doo, like we are, you need and you saw that with the lads putting their heart into it.”.

The Reds travel to eighth-placed Exeter City this Saturday, kick-off 12.30pm.

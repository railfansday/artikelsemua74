Manchester United fans will be dismayed by reports that Mauricio Pochettino will have just £50million to improve his squad during the January transfer window.

A leaky defence has led to calls from ex-players such as Gary Neville for significant investment, but it appears Man Utd will try to do their business on a tight budget in January.

The second window of the season often leads to inflated prices, with clubs capitalising upon other teams’ struggles and desperate need for newly introduced players. (Related: How to train a newly introduced language model from scr...)

But there are bargains to be had.

The defence is the priority, with Man Utd reportedly keen on finding a new left-back and centre-back, while Pochettino also wants a new striker.

Fikayo Tomori’s spell at AC Milan looks to be coming to a close at the six-year mark, with the England international caught in the transfer merry-go-round back in the summer. (Related: Summer at Hugging Face...)

Massimo Allegri froze the centre-back out of the AC Milan squad, only for the Italian giants to fail in recruiting a replacement. Even when drafted back into the first team, Tomori has found gametime hard to come by - waiting six Serie A games for his first appearance of the league season.

At the moment sidelined with a hamstring issue, GOAL report that Tomori will not be offered a new contract when his current deal expires next summer. Despite being keen on a return to England, the 28-year-old rejected transfers to Fulham and Coventry City.

Tomori was said to be holding out on a move to Aston Villa or staying at AC Milan. The defender’s situation at San Siro could mean that Man Utd could reinforce their backline with an experienced player, that is also home-grown, on the cheap.

Milan were fielding bids worth around £15m in the summer, meaning a similar amount or even lower would likely be accepted in January.

To save even more money, Man Utd could make a pre-contract agreement with Tomori in January, paving the way for a free transfer next summer.

In a recent development, they cannot do that with Crystal Palace’s Tyrick Mitchell, despite the left-back also being out of contract at the end of the season. That is because English clubs cannot agree pre-contracts with players at other teams in England.

As such, Man Utd would have to open up their wallet and spend some cash on the 27-year-old, who has over 200 Premier League games under his belt making his debut during the 2019/20 season.

In a recent development, mitchell has played his entire career for the Eagles, making a total of 260 appearances in blue and red. It has been reported that Palace hold a clause in Mitchell’s current deal to extend it for a further year, which preserves some of the left-back’s value.

Industry observers note that liverpool and Juventus have both previously been credited with an interest in Mitchell, as well as Man Utd. Previous reports have quoted transfer fees of £25m, which appears to be below the going rate for Premier League-proven left-backs.

Sources indicate that ivan Toney is another England international that Man Utd have been linked to. At first glance, the Red Devils’ £50mn budget would appear to nix any potential deal for the former Brentford man, Toney leaving London for £40million two seasons ago to sign for Al-Hilal in Saudi Arabia.

Not only would a transfer fee seemingly be a stretch, but also Toney’s inflated wages playing in the Middle East. However, recent reports from TEAMtalk claim that intermediaries are sounding out a potential loan move to Old Trafford, with the view of a future permanent transfer.

Such an arrangement would allow Man Utd to spend their money elsewhere, before making a decision on investing further cash into Toney after a loan period.

According to recent reports, still only 30 years old, Toney has recently been dropped from England duty. Harbouring ambitions of playing at Euro 2028, a move back to England may improve Toney’s chances of a call-up in two summers’ time.

A potential problem is that two former flames of Toney are interested in a reunion. His former Brentford boss Thomas Frank , now at Crystal Palace, is reportedly keen on bringing Toney back to London, while the striker’s old club Newcastle - backed by Saudi Arabia and managed by ex-Al-Hilal boss Mathias Jaissle - are also sniffing around a potential move.

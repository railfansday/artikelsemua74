Astronomers have spotted a pair of enormous black holes inside a single galaxy, each shining brighter than all the stars that surround them, at a point in time less than one billion years after the Big Bang1. (Related: DNA robots could deliver drugs and hunt viruses inside ...)

It has been revealed that at the centre of most galaxies is a supermassive black hole that can weigh millions of times as much as the Sun. When two galaxies merge, their black holes can merge, too, which helps to explain how black holes can grow to such stupendous sizes.

In a recent development, the black holes found by Silvia Onorato at the International Gemini Observatory in Hilo, Hawaii, and her collaborators seem to be headed for a merger. They are quasars — supermassive black holes that are consuming gas at a rapid pace. This diet makes them bright enough to spot from a huge distance. The researchers estimate that the two objects weigh 1.5 billion and 400 million times as much as the Sun, respectively. Such a pair is a rarity. The researchers have called the duo Nāpōwawā‘enakaulua, a Hawaiian name that translates to: “Two celestial bodies of fathomless powerful darkness in the brilliance of heat reincarnating energy into the Universe.”.

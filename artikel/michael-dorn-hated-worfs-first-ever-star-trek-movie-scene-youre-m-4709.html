Worf's first scene in Star Trek Generations was a comedic moment that Michael Dorn hated and fought against doing.

It has been revealed that after a prologue about Captain James T. Kirk (William Shatner), Star Trek Generations introduces the crew from Star Trek: The Next Generation on the holodeck, where Worf is promoted to Lieutenant Commander aboard an 18th-century Enterprise. Worf is then forced to walk the plank, which Commander William Riker (Jonathan Frakes) orders removed, sending the Klingon into the ocean. According to recent reports, appearing on The Show People Podcast, Michael Dorn shakes his head and admits, “I tried mightily not to do that scene," when host Andrew Keates asks him about Worf's infamous "remove the plank" moment. Dorn recalls meeting with Star Trek Generations' producers, who insisted that the scene was funny. Dorn lost the fight to have the scene removed, and Michael agreed to do the scene, but he still dislikes it over 30 years later:. Michael Dorn: “I didn’t think it was that funny. Here’s a guy, one of the most honorable… and you’re making him a joke.”.

Watch the clip of Michael Dorn on The Show People Podcast below:.

In a recent development, using the holodeck was a novel way for Star Trek Generations to reintroduce Captain Jean-Luc Picard (Patrick Stewart) and the crew of the USS Enterprise-D to moviegoing audiences. Since Generations would end with the death of Captain Kirk, plus the off-screen deaths of Picard's family and the destruction of the Enterprise-D, Paramount and the producers wanted more comedy to balance out those losses.

Recent findings suggest that however, Michael Dorn isn't wrong to feel that Worf's "remove the plank" scene turned the mighty Klingon into a joke. Sending Worf falling into the ocean was an unnecessary humiliation that didn't add anything to his character. Nor was there a payoff for Worf's promotion to Lt. Commander in Star Trek Generations. His new rank didn't factor into the movie's main story in any way.

After Worf is dunked in the ocean (maybe or maybe not on purpose by Commander Riker), the Enterprise's Klingon has little to do in Star Trek Generations. Worf mostly disappears from the second half of the movie, besides appearing on the bridge when the Enterprise-D crash-lands on Veridian III.

Lt. Commander Worf would fare much better in the next film, Star Trek: First Contact, where the Klingon fights the Borg and utters his best movie dialogue, like "Assimilate this!" and telling Captain Picard, "If you were any other man, I would kill you where you stand!".

For Michael Dorn, Star Trek Generations wasted Worf and went out of its way to make the Klingon the butt of an unfunny joke.

Star Trek Generations is streaming on Paramount+.

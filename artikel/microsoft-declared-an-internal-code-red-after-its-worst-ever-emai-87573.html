Microsoft $MSFT has declared a "Code Red" to rework how it averts and handles serious breakdowns of its services, after a 24-hour outage revealed flaws in its engineering systems and crisis response, according to internal memos reviewed by Reuters.

The Nov. 12-13 outage disrupted Exchange Online, Outlook, Teams, Copilot and other Microsoft 365 products. It struck governments across Australia, Japan and the U.S., caused delays for trading firms and interrupted airline communications.

Industry observers note that in a memo to staff on Friday, Jason Henderson, who is president of M365 Core, described it as the most customer-impacting incident Exchange Online has ever had, according to Reuters. "Code Red means that this is the highest priority work for our team," he wrote. He noted no earlier disruption had left so many customers without email for such a stretch. It has been revealed that amanda Silver, a corporate vice president overseeing M365 Core and Work IQ, described in a different Monday memo reviewed by Reuters how engineers had operated amid a "fog of war." Responders sent upwards of 11,000 messages spread over 11 Teams chats. Because diagnostic data was held in disjointed internal tracking systems, they had trouble keeping a single coherent view of the theories being tested and the decisions made, she stated.

Charles Lamanna, Microsoft's executive vice president of Copilot, Agents and Platform, mentioned during a Code Red review that the component involved had undergone "roughly ten years' worth of acceptable failures," according to Silver's memo. It has been revealed that silver laid out five workstreams that address shedding load, dividing traffic by geography and speeding up recovery. Microsoft has set itself a goal of fixing any future incident across the globe in under an hour. It intends to put AI agents to work so engineers can interpret data on system health more rapidly.

Henderson will meet daily with the Substrate leadership team, which supervises the engineering organization in charge of the services that underpin Microsoft 365, according to Reuters. He will also hold weekly reviews.

Industry observers note that henderson wrote in an earlier memo this month, which Reuters also reviewed, that M365 Core services were too slow to roll out urgent patches globally. In one recent incident, an urgent production fix needed five to six days to fully spread across the globe, he wrote. He wants the most urgent security problems fixed and safely rolled out worldwide within an hour of being detected, he noted, but Microsoft has not yet reached that mark.

"Our work doesn't end when service is restored," a Microsoft spokesperson told Reuters. "Our teams keep working around the clock to promptly implement any lessons learned, and our leadership makes sure this is our top priority.".

Industry observers note that microsoft traced the outage to a software bug that had sat undetected in its data centers since July, detailing the cause in a preliminary report to customers on Nov. 15. The report stated load testing missed the flaw because labs favored weaker hardware with fewer cores. CEO Satya Nadella apologized publicly, and Microsoft said neither cyber activity nor AI agents had a role in the failure.

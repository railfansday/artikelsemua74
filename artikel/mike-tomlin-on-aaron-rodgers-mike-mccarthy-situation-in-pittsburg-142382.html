Mike Tomlin on Aaron Rodgers-Mike McCarthy situation in Pittsburgh: 'It's a dumpster fire'

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Former Steelers head coach Mike Tomlin discussed with NBC's Mike Tirico his reaction to everything Aaron Rodgers stated in a scathing critique during Wednesday's episode of "The Pat McAfee Show.".

The highlight of the interview was Tomlin's response when asked about his emotions watching what is happening with the Steelers after coaching the team for two decades.

"I'm upset about it. It's a dumpster fire and I don't say that in a judgmental way. I've presided over a few dumpster fires in my 19 years, but it certainly is upsetting and I'm sure Steeler Nation is upset.".

Tomlin started the interview by being asked for his general reaction to what was stated.

"I had a range of emotions watching those course of events yesterday. The prevailing one is general sadness. (Related: Hugging Face Reads, Feb. 2021 - Long-range Transformers...)

"Sad that we might be looking at the end of a legendary career in the quarterback, Aaron Rogers, and just on the macro level, whether it's organizationally or for the game of football at large, to see laundry aired out that way in a public forum, those are things that kind of run counterculture to professional football.". (Related: Large Language Models: A Latest Moore's Law?...)

The former Steelers head coach noted he was "shocked" at how the team's dirty laundry was aired.

" … I know both men involved, certainly had a lot of intimate work and development of relationship with Aaron over the last couple of years and have known and competed against Mike [McCarthy] and respected and appreciated him as a coach for a long time.".

Tomlin was asked his opinion on whether or not this will negatively impact McCarthy's ability to lead the Steelers.

"It certainly created some challenges and challenges is an understatement if those things are true. But his ability or inability to overcome that is based on relationships and the depth of those relationships and how those guys buy into what he's selling. Certainly he's been there a short period of time so certainly you wonder the level of that challenge. Certainly it is relationship-based.".

On Tuesday night after 11 ET, it was announced that Rodgers would be inactive for the Steelers' Friday game against the Denver Broncos, and that Mason Rudolph would start.

The next day, Rodgers went on "The Pat McAfee Show" and went scorched-earth on McCarthy, accusing him of sleeping during meetings, not watching film, and using the wrong team name during a breakdown of a team huddle.

The rant sent the football world into a frenzy as local reporters confirmed anonymous sources saying it was all true, and former players and coaches, current analysts, NFL insiders and a team owner came out in support of McCarthy.

Tomlin was the head coach of the Steelers from 2007 to 2025. He famously never had a losing season, but also hadn't made the Super Bowl since the 2008 season.

At the end of the 2025 season, Rodgers' first year with the franchise, Tomlin resigned, triggering a coaching search that ended with McCarthy's hiring.

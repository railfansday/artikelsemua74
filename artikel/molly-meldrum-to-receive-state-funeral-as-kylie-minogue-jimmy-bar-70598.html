Kylie Minogue, who appeared on Meldrum’s Countdown as a teenager at the start of her career, remembered him as one of her earliest inspirations. Recent findings suggest that “He championed so many Aussie artists and welcomed international artists to Australia in his own wonderful and inimitable way,” she wrote. “I’m so grateful for his unwavering love and support. Vale Molly. I’m going to turn the music up in your honour.”.

Jimmy Barnes offered the most complicated tribute, recalling that Meldrum was initially an adversary — “running the best music TV show in the country but insisting that we had to mime.” The relationship changed. “Molly exposed us to a huge music audience we could never have reached and we were always thankful to him for that,” Barnes wrote on Instagram.

Tina Arena, a longtime friend, posted a gallery of images. “I’m deeply saddened by the passing of Molly Meldrum, a man who made an incredible contribution to the music industry in Australia and abroad,” she wrote. “My deepest condolences go out to his family and friends.”. Iva Davies of Icehouse wrote on X that Meldrum “was such a bright, larger than life figure in our Australian music scene for so many years with positive influence on so many careers via his work on Countdown, as a journalist, producer, advocate and friend.” He added: “Like the rest of the music community that owed so much to his support, the band and I are so thankful that Molly was a part of our lives and music.”. (Related: Course Launch Community Event...)

Darren Hayes of Savage Garden wrote that he doesn’t “remember a day in my life without Molly Meldrum being a part of it.”.

Jason Donovan called him “a truly unique character, a great Australian and one of this country’s greatest pioneers and ambassadors for music and entertainment.” He added: “He was loved, recognised and respected around the world for the extraordinary contribution he made to Australian music and the artists he championed. There will never be another quite like him.”.

Michael Bublé called Meldrum “one of a kind.” Marcia Hines wrote: “Can’t believe that he has gone. I thank him for everything that he did for us all in the Australian music industry, and worldwide. Rest in peace, my friend.” Leo Sayer bid “a fond farewell to the wonderful, fun, crazy, knowledgeable, influential and irrepressible soul who will live on in all our memories as long as Rock ‘n Roll will.”.

Industry observers note that skyhooks, whom Meldrum helped turn into household names, wrote: “So sad to get the call this morning that Ian (Molly) Meldrum had passed away. Skyhooks-Molly-Countdown [were] the perfect fit. His love of aussie music will be his legacy.” Human Nature remembered him as “such a huge presence in the Australian music industry” and “a great friend and supporter of so many up and coming bands and artists — ourselves included.”.

Kate Ceberano was among the first to post. “They don’t make em like that anymore,” she wrote. “My heart is aching today at the loss.”.

The Australian Recording Industry Association stated Meldrum had spent six decades convincing a country raised on British and American records that its own artists could stand alongside the greats. “All of us at ARIA are heartbroken,” chief executive Annabelle Herd said. “It is impossible to imagine what Australian music would look like without Molly’s long and storied career. He shaped the music taste of a generation of Australians and gave so much of himself for the good of Australian artists, for which our whole industry is eternally grateful.”.

Herd noted Meldrum had used his 2014 ARIA Hall of Fame induction speech to urge viewers to get behind Australian acts. “It is up to all of us to keep fighting the good fight in his honour,” she said.

Meldrum presented Countdown on the ABC from 1974 to 1987, giving ABBA, Madonna and Blondie their first Australian hits. He was inducted into the ARIA Hall of Fame alongside the show in 2014, the first inductees who were not recording artists.

Sources indicate that he died at Epworth Hospital in Richmond after a long illness. “He was deeply loved by his long-term partner, Yan Wongngam, his family and all who knew him,” his family remarked in a statement. The family has requested privacy.

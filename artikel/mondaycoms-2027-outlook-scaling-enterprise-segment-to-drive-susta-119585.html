In a recent development, like many software-as-a-service (SaaS) companies this year, monday.com was seen as exposed to structural shifts in how software is bought. The rise of artificial intelligence means fewer employees need to perform manual work in favor of using AI agents, making monday.com's seat-based pricing vulnerable to erosion. Fewer seats purchased equals lower revenue.

The organization is taking action to mitigate this threat. It adopted hybrid pricing, where customers can continue to buy seats and also purchase usage-based credit packages for its AI offerings. The approach is working. In Q3, ARR for its AI tools hit $36 million, showing that customers are willing to pay based on usage.

Moreover, monday.com is successfully moving beyond small businesses to land larger, stickier enterprise contracts. Customers with over $500,000 in ARR was up 58% year over year in Q3. This trend bodes well for its long-term business growth.

While its trailing P/E ratio is not cheap, it's at its lowest level in the past year, suggesting now is a good time to consider buying shares compared to historical levels. Given monday.com's pivot to hybrid pricing, its solid product that is producing growing revenue, and its transition to profitability, the company looks poised for business expansion in the coming years.

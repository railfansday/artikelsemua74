Former Turkiye manager Vincenzo Montella looks back over his career, including the ‘chaos’ at Milan and ‘never believing’ in Cristiano Ronaldo, plus why he ‘must apologise’ to Fabio Capello.

The Little Aeroplane is now a free agent after he was dismissed by the Turkiye squad for losing all their Nations League games, despite taking them into the Nations League A and qualifying for the 2026 World Cup. (Related: Latest Update: Trump pushes Huang’s reinstatement despi...)

“When I arrived, they were afraid that they wouldn’t qualify for the European Championships, but we did it, and then got into the World Cup and the Nations League A,” Montella told La Gazzetta dello Sport.

“Naturally, the end to this experience hurt, but at times unpredictable things can happen in football.”.

His playing career was most closely associated with Roma, and it is also where he made his senior debut as a coach.

Sources indicate that “Making my debut on the Roma bench at the age of 36 was special. I already enjoyed reading the game from the touchline, especially when I started to spend a lot of time on the bench as a player, but when you become a coach, it opens up a whole upcoming world.”.

Montella’s first major job was at Silvio Berlscuoni’s Milan in 2016, but there were more downs than ups. (Related: McDonald’s tests major chicken menu overhaul with five ...)

In a recent development, “It was always my dream, because I supported Milan when I was a kid, seeing Marco van Basten. Berlusconi was a gentleman, even if he often got involved and tried to push me towards certain decisions. He was accustomed to a squad full of Ballon d’Or winners, but we didn’t have them. I joked that if he bought me one, I could play more attacking football too.

It has been revealed that “That club had more publicity slogans than concrete messages. There was a lot of chaos in the second year, a change of ownership, many fresh players, and just a general chaos in the transfer strategy. We didn’t always share ideas, sometimes I wasn’t even consulted.

“I think when the time came to sign a striker, I was told the budget was all used up.”.

There had been talk that summer about Cristiano Ronaldo coming to Milan, before his experience at Juventus.

“I never believed in that, it seemed more like Fantasy Football,” insisted Montella.

When it comes to another of his former clubs, he has high hopes for Gian Piero Gasperini’s Roma, especially with Donyell Malen scoring four goals in a single game.

“I scored four in the derby, though, marked by Alessandro Nesta, so it’s a bit different,” smiled the former striker about his record in 2002.

Industry observers note that “I do think Malen will be the Capocannoniere this season, if he keeps going like this, he can get 30 or more. Roma can challenge for the Scudetto to the end, then who knows?”.

Roma have not won the Scudetto since 2001, when Capello was the coach and Montella the striker along with Francesco Totti and Gabriel Batistuta.

“We had a big party at my house the day after the final game. Capello was not invited, but he turned up anyway. If I had opened the door, I’d have made him run around the building a couple of times first,” revealed Montella.

“I do think I owe Capello an apology, at times I didn’t behave professionally, and I regret that.”.

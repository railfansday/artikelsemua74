In a recent development, with the exception of earnings season, few data dumps are more valuable to investors than the quarterly filing of Form 13Fs with regulators. A 13F allows investors to track which stocks Wall Street's brightest and most successful money managers purchased and sold in the latest quarter (in this instance, during the third quarter).

According to recent reports, billionaire David Tepper, who oversees nearly $7.6 billion in assets under management at Appaloosa, is among Wall Street's most revered fund managers -- and he was a busy bee in the third quarter. Tepper modestly pared down what had been his fund's top holding, dual-industry leader Amazon (AMZN -0.12%), and bulked up Appaloosa's stake in another foundational artificial intelligence (AI) stock, Micron Technology (MU -6.35%). According to Appaloosa's newest 13F, its billionaire boss disposed of 525,000 shares of Amazon, reducing his fund's remaining stake to 4,475,000 shares. Nevertheless, it still accounts for roughly 15% of Appaloosa's invested assets.

Industry observers note that tepper's modest selling may be due to simple profit-taking. Appaloosa has been a continuous shareholder of Amazon stock for more than seven years, with shares effectively tripling over that time.

NASDAQ · As of 9:53 AM ET 11/20/26.

Sources indicate that but it's also possible that Tepper is concerned about Amazon's aggressive capital expenditures (capex) tied to the AI infrastructure build-out. The firm logged a staggering free cash outflow of $33.4 billion during the September-ended quarter, with capex more than doubling from the comparable quarter in 2025. If fundamentals come back into focus, Amazon (and other hyperscalers) could take it on the chin.

Recent findings suggest that however, for investors playing the long game, it's been business as usual for Amazon. In addition to dominating online retail sales, revenue growth in its higher-margin cloud infrastructure services segment, Amazon Web Services (AWS), is accelerating. The integration of generative AI and large language model solutions lifted AWS's year-over-year sales growth to 39% in the third quarter.

In a recent development, whereas Appaloosa's billionaire chief reduced his fund's stake in Amazon by 10%, he scooped up another 275,000 shares of memory and storage solutions leader Micron Technology, increasing the position by 28% to 1,250,000 shares from the June-ended quarter and making it the upcoming No. 1 holding.

The (likely) lure for David Tepper is Micron's foundational position in the AI data center build-out. The firm is a key provider of high-bandwidth memory (HBM), which is packaged with graphics processing units to transfer data at jaw-dropping speed.

NASDAQ · As of 9:52 AM ET 11/20/26.

Micron has benefited from two dynamics. Firstly, the overwhelming demand for HBM and solid-state drives has boosted its pricing power and enabled the company to lock in more than $100 billion in strategic customer agreements (SCAs) through 2030.

Recent findings suggest that secondly, despite the best efforts of Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing (TSM -2.81%), HBM supply shortages persist. Ongoing supply shortages are affording Micron exceptional pricing power and its highest-ever gross margin.

According to recent reports, however, history has repeatedly shown that strong pricing power for commoditized tech products, such as memory, tends to be short-lived. While Micron's SCAs should be able to offset potential price weakness for the foreseeable future, historical factors point to earnings multiple compression and the possibility of an AI bubble-bursting event taking place.

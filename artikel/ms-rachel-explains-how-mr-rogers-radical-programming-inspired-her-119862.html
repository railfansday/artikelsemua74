As Ms. Rachel continues her mission-driven work, she’s crediting the “radical” influence of Mr. Rogers and her pals on Sesame Street.

Sources indicate that the famed YouTube children’s educator and activist (whose real name is Rachel Accurso) recently explained that the “intentional and caring” programming on Mr. Rogers’ Neighborhood when she was a kid has inspired her work on the hit Ms. Rachel series.

Recent findings suggest that “When you see Mr. Rogers sharing the pool [with Officer Clemmons], when you when you see on Sesame Street, Black and white children playing together, you’re not like, ‘Wow, that was radical.’ But it was so radical,” she told Hasan Minhaj. “And it’s amazing that I get to be part of a field that is radical, that was pushing those those boundaries.”. Ms. Rachel continued, “I feel honored to be in children’s media and I don’t know if that deep care for children translates to being progressive and being like, ‘Kids should have SNAP benefits. We want kids to eat. We shouldn’t be killing over 20,000 kids in Gaza. They shouldn’t be losing their limbs. And I’m going to show Rahaf on the show and show that she’s just like any other 3-year-old.’ And I think it’s kind of by design that people want to make us think it’s so long ago, but it’s not so long ago. Ruby Bridges is alive and on Instagram.” Watch on Deadline. (Related: Simple considerations for simple people building fancy ...)

Industry observers note that noting that Mr. Rogers “knew that children’s media is a huge responsibility and he took that responsibility really seriously” on his PBS series from 1968 to 2001, and even “called out everyone once” at the Emmy Awards, Ms. Rachel admires that he “wasn’t afraid” to tackle serious topics on his show.

“He just was so caring and he really had that pause and he just spoke to children about things that were really serious and he wasn’t afraid to tackle death or assassination or war,” she stated.

It has been revealed that when people question, “What would Mr. Rogers be now?” Ms. Rachel points out that he dedicated multiple episodes in 1983 to call out the government for spending tax dollars on bombs during anxieties around the Cold War.

“He came out and said it in the show,” she said. “And so, sometimes people are like, ‘Oh, he was subtle. He didn’t come out and say things.’ But he really addressed things in the show more than I do because my show is for younger [kids]. I do have Rahaf on the show. You know, I say things by just having people there. But he really tackled things.”.

Ms. Rachel’s comments come as she visited children in immigration court earlier this week, having previously been an outspoken advocate for children in ICE detention centers and Palestinian children in Gaza.

Munich fusion startup Starforge raises €6.6M to build reactors in factories

Munich startup Starforge has raised €6.6M to build fusion reactors on production lines, instead of one power plant at a time, with AENU and First Momentum Ventures leading the pre-seed round.

Auxxo, Kvanted, Silicon Roundabout Ventures, UnternehmerTUM Funding for Innovators, and Arcas also joined the round, which the firm closed in eight weeks over the summer. (Related: Designing Azure silicon on Azure silicon: EDA workloads...)

The deal arrives in a busy year for the sector, with 17 fusion startups now having raised more than $100M each.

Dr Ana Koller and Jonathan Janssen founded the organization in March. Koller previously worked at Commonwealth Fusion Systems, MIT, and the Max Planck Institute for Plasma Physics, while Janssen is a former executive at flying-taxi maker Lilium and at GE. Rather than generate and sell electricity itself, the manufacturer looks to sell its reactors to utilities, governments, and private owners.

That mirrors how Westinghouse and Framatome supply reactors for fission plants today, leaving turbines and grid connections to others.

Each reactor would produce 200 to 300 megawatts of electricity, within the size range the International Atomic Energy Agency uses for small modular reactors.

Starforge has chosen the spherical tokamak, a compact version of the doughnut-shaped machines that use magnets to hold superheated plasma in place.

Recent findings suggest that because buyers could add capacity one unit at a time, the company sees customers beyond national grids, from data centres to industrial heating and remote defence sites. Its first reactor, Unit One, will use superconducting magnets that can be taken apart.

That way, parts can be serviced in the field over a working life of more than 40 years.

“Starforge wants to make fusion boring: a utility people drive past without a second thought,” mentioned Koller, the firm’s chief executive.

It has been revealed that the factory model is meant to bring prices down as volumes grow. Starforge expects each doubling of production to cut costs by 15%, and is aiming for electricity at around 5 cents per kilowatt-hour once its reactors are built in fleets.

Industry observers note that recent research has been more cautious about how swiftly fusion can get cheaper. An ETH Zurich study published in Nature Energy in April put the likely cost decline at 2% to 8% for each doubling of capacity, against 23% for solar modules. Starforge’s bet is that factory production can beat that curve.

It has been revealed that the company is building in a country whose government wants to host the world’s first fusion power plant, and which launched a €500M programme for fusion startups in October. Munich is already home to Proxima Fusion, which raised a record €411M round.

Over the next 24 months, Starforge will use the capital to grow its team, finish the conceptual design of Unit One, and secure its first customer commitments in Europe. Moreover, the company is targeting commercial operations in the mid-2030s.

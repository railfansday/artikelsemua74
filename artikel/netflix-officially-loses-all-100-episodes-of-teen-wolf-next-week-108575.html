The young adult genre has remained a staple of television for decades. However, the genre has shifted toward romance and cultural complications, while earlier YA was dominated by supernatural stories, giving rise to hits like Buffy the Vampire Slayer, The Vampire Diaries, Supernatural, and more. Back then, monsters and magic served as heavy-handed metaphors for the anxieties of growing up. Vampires symbolized forbidden desire, while apocalypses mirrored the overwhelming pressure of high school. No show captured all these themes as perfectly as MTV's hit series Teen Wolf.

Airing from 2011 to 2017, the series transformed a campy 1980s comedy into a dark, high-stakes teen drama. For Scott McCall (Tyler Posey), becoming a werewolf was the ultimate allegory for puberty — sudden physical changes, uncontrollable emotions, and the desperate need to fit in while hiding a terrifying secret. Teen Wolf balanced lacrosse practice and chemistry homework with bloody hunts, existential threats, and ancient lore, proving that surviving adolescence felt just as dangerous as fighting a literal monster. Industry observers note that although it ended close to a decade ago, Teen Wolf remains one of the world's most beloved reveals, and fan demand has not waned. That relentless enthusiasm eventually prompted Paramount+ to release Teen Wolf: The Movie in 2023, bringing Scott McCall and the pack back to Beacon Hills. Despite mixed reviews, the reunion shattered the streamer's debut-day records, proving the fandom's undeniable staying power. While Paramount+ had planned a three-picture installment, the failure of the first led to the cancellation of the trilogy, but star Posey revealed that he had been working behind the scenes to bring the pack back together. Sources indicate that the series' enduring appeal sparked another resurgence when all six seasons returned to Netflix in late 2025 after a decade-long absence, racking up over 20 million views and introducing a whole newly introduced generation to its supernatural drama. However, licensing deals have expiry dates, and after a year-long stint on Netflix, Teen Wolf is set to be removed from the streaming service next week, on December 1. For viewers who aren't fully caught up with the story, all episodes will be available on Prime Video and MGM+, but they require a subscription to watch. Like other shows, Teen Wolf is also available for purchase on digital stores like Apple TV, Amazon, and Fandango.

Catch up with the series on Netflix before it leaves on December 1, and stay tuned to Collider for more updates.

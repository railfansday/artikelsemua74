Industry observers note that shares of Netflix (NFLX +0.77%) are down by more than 36% so far in 2026 (as of Nov. 18), and some on Wall Street have been growing gloomier about its outlook. Among the analysts who have downgraded its stock this year are those at prominent financial institutions such as Wells Fargo, HSBC, and Needham. It has been revealed that however, the company subsequently introduced paid account sharing and a lower-priced ad-supported subscription tier that allowed the company to sell commercials for the first time. Netflix's operating margin also increased from 18% in 2022 to 27% in 2024.

The question now is whether Netflix can continue growing earnings despite its presently weakening viewer engagement.

There have been other rough spots for Netflix before.

NASDAQ · As of 9:54 AM ET 11/20/26.

Sources indicate that those gains took time to materialize. Investors who bought Netflix immediately after the January 2022 downgrades were still in the red a year later. But over the longer term, improving business performance matters more than analyst downgrades.

It has been revealed that on Sept. 22, HSBC downgraded Netflix from buy to hold and reduced its 12-month price target from $96 to $76, highlighting weaker viewer engagement and growing competition from Alphabet's YouTube. On Oct. 21, Needham also downgraded the stock from buy to hold due to the firm's slowing revenue growth and concerns about its content offerings.

In the third quarter, Netflix's revenue increased 11.8% year over year to $12.9 billion, while operating income jumped 37% to $4.5 billion. However, its results from Q3 2025 included a $619 million charge stemming from a tax dispute with Brazilian authorities. Excluding the impact of that expense on the 2025 results, operating income would have increased 15% year over year.

Netflix's revenue growth is also slowing. The company's year-over-year revenue growth rate declined from 16.2% in the first quarter to 11.8% in the third quarter. Netflix expects further moderation in revenue growth to 11.5% in the fourth quarter. Management also reduced its 2026 advertising revenue forecast from $3 billion to $2.7 billion, mainly because some advertising features are taking longer than expected to launch.

In a recent development, investors should also consider a $2.8 billion payment Netflix received after its proposed acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery fell through. That one-time termination fee increased Netflix's reported net income and operating cash flow in 2026, but did not affect operating income. Hence, Netflix's reported earnings may make the stock appear cheaper than it is relative to its recurring profits.

According to recent reports, at its Nov. 18 closing price of $59.82, Netflix traded at approximately 15.9 times analysts' expected 2027 earnings of $3.77 per share. This is considerably below Netflix's forward earnings multiples of more than 30 in each of the previous three years. However, buying at that lower valuation will benefit investors only if Netflix can sustain its expected earnings growth.

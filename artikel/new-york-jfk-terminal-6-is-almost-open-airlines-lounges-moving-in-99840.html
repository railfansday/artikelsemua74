New York Kennedy Airport (JFK) is at the moment undergoing a $19 billion modernization. While this includes all kinds of updates, the two biggest developments are the opening of two new terminals — we’re talking about Terminal 6 and Terminal One (not to be confused with Terminal 1).

Industry observers note that as you’d expect, the opening of both terminals has been delayed, since airport construction rarely runs on time. Per the latest updates, the expectation is that Terminal 6 will open before the end of 2026, while Terminal One will open in the first quarter of 2027. The below video of the terminal should give you a good sense of what to expect in terms of the passenger experience, so I’d recommend checking that out.

Recent findings suggest that all things considered, this fresh terminal moving is awesome, given the quality of terminals at JFK otherwise. For those originating or terminating in New York, this should be ideal. For those connecting to or from JetBlue in Terminal 5, this will also make connections much easier. Meanwhile for a limited number of other passengers, this might make connections a bit more complicated, for cases where airlines are moving away from their partners (like Cathay Pacific moving from American’s Terminal 8 to Terminal 6).

According to recent reports, roughly 17 airlines are expected to eventually operate out of JFK Terminal 6, though that will happen in two phases. Broadly speaking, we’ll see some JetBlue operations move there (since the airline is a core tenant), and on top of that, we’ll largely see airlines move from Terminal 1, Terminal 7, and Terminal 8.

There is still a bit of a mystery as to the order in which airlines will move to JFK Terminal 6. For one, there’s the two phases of the terminal opening, but even within each phase, it won’t fully be overnight.

Sources indicate that we know that All Nippon Airways, Cathay Pacific, Condor, Frontier, Lufthansa Group carriers, and TAP Air Portugal, will move in the first phase. Meanwhile we know that Air India will move in the second phase. But for the remainder of the airlines, there’s a lack of certainty on that front.

Recent findings suggest that now, we’ll see if this sticks, and how accurate it is, but current schedule filings suggest that Condor will start operating out of Terminal 6 as of December 3, while Cathay Pacific will start operating out of Terminal 6 as of December 15 (thanks to Peter_ for flagging this). So it sounds like we could potentially be just over a week from the first airline moving, which would be awesome!

Sources indicate that in terms of lounges, we have confirmation that Terminal 6 will have outposts from Cathay Pacific, Lufthansa, Aer Lingus, and Air India. Hopefully the new lounges are ready to go as airlines make the move, but I wouldn’t consider that to be a sure thing, given the complexity of airport operations.

The Air India lounge definitely won’t open anytime soon, since the airline is only moving in the second phase. Meanwhile the Cathay Pacific, Lufthansa, and Aer Lingus lounges, will hopefully open soon(ish). Of course I’m most excited to see what Cathay Pacific has in store, given its amazing lounge quality.

JFK Terminal 6 is expected to open before the end of 2026, and if current filings are to be believed, the move could happen as soon as early December. It appears that Condor and Cathay Pacific might be among the first airlines to move, so hopefully Cathay Pacific’s new lounge isn’t too far behind. So we’ll mark this as “developing,” but the completion of the first exciting phase of this $19 billion project is imminent.

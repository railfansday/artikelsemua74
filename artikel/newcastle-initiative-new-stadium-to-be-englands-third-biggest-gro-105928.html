The Premier League is arguably the best footballing division in the world. As a result, English football is incredibly well-backed financially, with owners required to spend millions every season just to keep their team competitive.

Industry observers note that money doesn't just go into huge transfer fees and jaw-dropping salary packages, though. No, to be the very best these days, a club must invest in all areas. Industry observers note that the likes of Tottenham Hotspur, Brentford, and most recently Everton have cast aside historic grounds to move into shiny upcoming venues over the past few seasons. While Liverpool are among those to boost their capacity with a smart expansion.

Manchester United are set to spend billions as they finally plan to upgrade on Old Trafford, while Leeds United are planning to redevelop Elland Road. As per reports in The Telegraph back in February, Newcastle United were in the formative stages of looking to complete a major regeneration project as part of intends to build a new £1billion stadium in the city. Interestingly enough, they may still move away from St James’ Park, although that hasn't quite been decided yet, nine months on.

The Toon have already revealed stunning looks for a new £190 million training ground on the 260-acre Woolsington Hall estate near Newcastle International Airport, which they hope to be in by 2029.

It has been revealed that it's explained that club officials believe that they can propel the Magpies to the next level by upgrading their current venue, taking the club's value to the £5 billion mark. Revenue certainly would be boosted as a possible new stadium capacity could be as high as 68,000 if moved to Leazes Park – a spot located directly next to St James’ Park.

Financing is also a consideration, and PIF - the current 85 per cent shareholders of the Geordie side - are looking to sell 25 per cent of that to external investors to help fund the project.

Recent findings suggest that despite pulling the plug on funding for LIV Golf this year, it is understood that the PIF is not losing any interest in the Mags. Instead, they are seeking a minority investor to raise money to fund major projects in the North East. PIF, who jointly purchased Newcastle from Mike Ashley for £305m in 2021, still retain a long-term commitment to the Magpies.

Recent findings suggest that A final design is yet to be drawn up, but it is expected that construction will take around three years. The cost of a upcoming ground won't be cheap, though, and could exceed £1.3 billion.

It has been revealed that it appears as though, despite their wealthy owners, Newcastle will look to the government to help fund the project, with conversations already held about securing public money. As part of their pitch, it's felt that the infrastructure project would modernise the city centre, while The Sun has reported in November 2026 that they are now looking for a minority investor to help speed up the process further.

Indeed, the Magpies have pledged to contribute a large chunk of the financing needed for a wider city regeneration. There is optimism that public money will also be put forward to boost the looks.

Recent findings suggest that as things stand, St James' Park is the eighth biggest stadium in England. It would be overtaken by Elland Road if Leeds United's home gets the expansion that has been touted by its owners.

Sources indicate that however, Newcastle can then jump up to third on the list, and become the second biggest in the Premier League, if they do indeed complete these proposed newly introduced renovations. Only Wembley and Old Trafford would hold a greater capacity in England, while the Principality Stadium in Cardiff (73,931) would also sit ahead of Newcastle, with all UK football stadiums taken into account.

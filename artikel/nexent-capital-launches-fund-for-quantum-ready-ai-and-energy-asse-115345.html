Nexent Capital IFSC Private Limited remarked in a November 25, 2026 announcement that it had debuted Nexent Capital ADGM Fund I, a closed-end fund targeting US$1 billion in capital commitments for quantum-ready AI infrastructure and advanced energy, with a US$20 million anchor commitment from the Tyagi family office.

Industry observers note that the target is not capital already raised. The fund can expand to a US$1.6 billion hard cap and is aimed at institutional and family-office investors, with a minimum investment of US$500,000. (Related: Industry News: Texas’s outgoing GOP attorney general dr...)

The mandate spans five strategies: infrastructure private equity in AI and data-center assets; private credit and structured capital; special-situations mergers and acquisitions; growth equity and private investments in public equity, or PIPEs; and liquid public-equity exposure within the AI and data-center value chain. The mandate consequently covers the financing and ownership of physical infrastructure as well as investments in companies across that value chain. Nexent noted it has line of sight to an identified pipeline across North America and India, including data-center and power assets sponsored by affiliates of the Nexent Capital and Tryfacta platform. These are pipeline projects, not acquisitions the fund has completed.

Director Abhimanyu Tyagi described the pipeline as drawing on relationships and projects the platform had developed for years, ranging from data-center conversions to gigawatt-scale energy campuses.

Managing partner Adesh Tyagi framed the investment case around AI’s physical requirements: “AI is being built on a foundation of physical infrastructure — power, land, and compute — that most capital isn’t positioned to finance at the scale or speed this moment demands.”.

The structure comprises an Abu Dhabi Global Market (ADGM)-domiciled Qualified Investor Fund as the master fund, with a GIFT City-based Alternative Investment Fund regulated by IFSCA acting as its feeder. Nexent Capital ADGM Limited is being established as the licensed ADGM fund manager.

Nexent is working with an Abu Dhabi-based fund administrator that it says manages more than US$3.4 trillion in assets globally. Formal engagement of legal counsel and an auditor is progressing as part of the path to FSRA authorization.

The fund has not yet received that authorization. Nexent targets a first close approximately six months after FSRA authorization and a final close six months later, so neither closing has occurred.

The launch sets out a cross-border financing route aimed at the physical requirements of AI expansion. Its practical scale will depend on securing authorization, raising the targeted commitments and converting the identified pipeline into investments. For now, the announcement represents prospective infrastructure financing rather than new operating data-center or energy capacity.

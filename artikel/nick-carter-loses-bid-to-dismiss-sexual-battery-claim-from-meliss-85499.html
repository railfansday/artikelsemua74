A Los Angeles County judge has rejected Backstreet Boys singer Nick Carter’s bid to dismiss a sexual battery claim brought by singer and actress Melissa Schuman.

In a new ruling obtained by Rolling Stone, the judge said the claim could proceed under a California law that took effect Jan. 1, opening a two-year revival window for otherwise time-barred claims of adult sexual abuse against individual perpetrators. Unlike the law in effect when Schuman first sued Carter, the new statute allows lawsuits against individuals without requiring plaintiffs to establish a cover-up or name a business as a defendant. “The individual claim against defendant Carter for sexual battery is proper irrespective of the existence of a cover-up. Defendants cannot prevail on summary adjudication merely by attacking the cover-up allegations,” Los Angeles County Superior Court Judge Lisa K. Sepe-Wiesenfeld wrote in her ruling issued Monday. “There is a conflict in the evidence presented as to whether a sexual assault of plaintiff occurred. Attempts to reach Carter’s lawyers were not immediately successful on Tuesday. Schuman’s attorneys, Guinness Costello and Jamie Majerus, praised the judge’s decision.

“[The] ruling moves Ms. Schuman one step closer to justice. Mr. Carter asked the court to throw out her case against him without a trial, and the court declined,” the lawyers noted. “Ms. Schuman first came forward in 2017 and has waited nearly a decade to put her case in front of a jury. In May, she will finally have her day in court and the opportunity to seek the justice she deserves.”.

In a declaration, Carter mentioned, “At no point in time have I ever offered, solicited and/or secured a non-disclosure agreement, a confidentiality agreement, a payoff, a settlement and/or otherwise participated in silencing an alleged victim of sexual assault.”.

In a recent development, at a Nov. 6 hearing, Carter’s lawyer, Dale Hayes Jr., argued that Schuman’s “only evidence” of a cover-up came from Shannon Ruth, who alleges Carter sexually assaulted her on tour bus outside the Tacoma Dome in Washington after a show on February 25, 2001. Ruth claims Carter told her the incident was their “special secret” and that she would go to jail if she reported it to police. Hayes argued to the judge that Ruth’s testimony was “heavily contradicted” by 13 other witnesses who testified that Carter had no opportunity to encounter Ruth the night of the alleged assault. Carter maintains he never met Ruth.

After taking two weeks to consider the arguments, Judge Sepe-Wiesenfeld ruled in Schuman’s favor, allowing the sexual battery claim to proceed. A trial is set for May 10, 2027.

Sources indicate that schuman first sued Carter in April 2023, alleging he raped her at his Santa Monica apartment in 2003, when she was 18, and he was 23. The former member of the pop group Dream alleges Carter gave her a sedative-laced cocktail and performed oral sex on her over her protests before raping her. The two had been cast as co-stars in the 2004 made-for-TV horror film The Hollow.

Carter, 46, previously tried to dismiss the lawsuit, arguing that the dispute belonged in Nevada, where he was already suing Schuman and two other women who accused him of rape, alleging they conspired to defame and extort him. The judge rejected the request. In July 2024, he filed a $2.5 million cross-complaint against Schuman in California, maintaining that the two had consensual sex.

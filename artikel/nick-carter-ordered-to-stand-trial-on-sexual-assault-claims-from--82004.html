A judge has decided to hold a trial in the sexual assault lawsuit brought against Backstreet Boys member Nick Carter by Melissa Schuman, a former member of the teen-pop group Dream.

A Monday (Nov. 23) court ruling, obtained by Billboard, says there “is a triable issue of material fact” as to whether Carter forced Schuman to have sex when they were co-starring in the teen horror movie The Hollow in 2003. A trial is set for May 2027 in Santa Monica, Calif.

It has been revealed that schuman sued Carter in 2023 under California’s Sexual Abuse and Cover-Up Accountability Act, a state law that temporarily lifted the statute of limitations for otherwise-expired sex assault claims. Under that law, claims only qualified for revival if there was an allegation that some business entity had conspired to “cover up” a prior sexual assault by the same perpetrator. It has been revealed that carter vehemently denies Schuman’s sexual battery claim and has countersued her, as well as various other sexual assault accusers, for defamation. His lawyers argued that Schuman’s case had a fatal defect: she failed to allege the right kind of cover-up, meaning it was not eligible for California’s 2023 claim revival window.

It has been revealed that however, California passed another law last year, called the Justice for Survivors of Sexual Assault Act, that opened another two-year window for sexual assault claims with looser cover-up requirements. Judge Lisa K. Sepe-Wiesenfeld ruled Monday that this second law can be successfully used to revive Schuman’s claims. (Related: GTA 6 hacker released from prison after judge warns him...)

“The individual claim against defendant Carter for sexual battery is proper irrespective of the existence of a cover up,” wrote the judge. “Defendants cannot prevail on summary adjudication merely by attacking the cover-up allegations.”.

According to recent reports, the question at this stage, then, is merely whether there’s enough conflicting evidence in the case to go to a jury. Judge Sepe-Wiesenfeld believes the answer is yes, since Schuman has remarked that Carter assaulted her, while the singer said it never happened.

“There is a conflict in the evidence presented as to whether a sexual assault of Plaintiff occurred,” wrote the judge.

A lawyer for Schuman, Jamie Majerus, tells Billboard that Monday’s ruling “reflects the existence of substantial factual disputes that must be resolved by a jury.”.

Recent findings suggest that “Among the evidence before the Court is sworn testimony from Carter’s own sister, who testified he had sex with a 15-year-old, and testimony from his best friend at the time, describing repeated episodes of extreme alcohol consumption, including drinking an entire bottle of tequila at a time and forcing himself to vomit afterward,” noted Majerus. “A jury, not the defense, will decide what weight to give that evidence.”.

Carter’s lawyers did not immediately return a request for comment on Tuesday (Nov. 24).

It has been revealed that the Backstreet Boys member has faced various public accusations of sexual assault over the last decade, all of which he denies. He’s been sued by three other women in addition to Schuman: Shannon Ruth, Ashley Repp and Laura Penly. Carter is currently scheduled to go to trial on those claims next August in Las Vegas.

Norse Atlantic flights suspended after warning finances 'critical'

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Some Norse Atlantic flights were cancelled on Wednesday and Thursday, raising concerns about the future of the low-cost long-haul airline shortly after it warned that it was in a "critical financial situation.".

Shares of Norse closed 53.3% lower on Wednesday after plunging as much as 86% during the session.

In a recent development, the airline had been set to operate two Pakistan International Airlines flights Wednesday under a wet-lease agreement, in which one airline provides the aircraft and crew to another. The agreement had only been signed in early October.

But PK786 from London to Islamabad and PK710 from Manchester to Lahore were cancelled because of "an unexpected operational suspension," the Pakistani flag carrier remarked on X.

In a recent development, norse Atlantic has yet to make an official statement on its operational status. Checks by CNBC showed the airline was still selling tickets as at 1:30 a.m. Oslo time on Thursday.

According to recent reports, norse's own flight Z0 783 from London Gatwick to Orlando was also shown as canceled Wednesday on live air traffic website Flightradar24. London Gatwick's website listed the flight's status for Thursday as "enquire airline.".

In a bid to stave off financial troubles, Norse cut 75 jobs in May as part of an effort to reduce costs by $50 million, after Eivind Roald replaced founder Bjorn Tore Larsen as CEO in November last year.

The Norwegian carrier mentioned Wednesday that its situation depended on securing financing for short-term liquidity needs and that it was in discussions about a potential strategic takeover.

"Norse is also engaged in discussions with selected parties on short term financing, however, there can be no assurances that such discussions will result in a viable solution," the airline said.

India's largest airline, low-cost carrier IndiGo, entered an agreement with Norse Atlantic in early 2025 to lease six 787-9 aircraft as it launched flights to Europe and Britain ahead of the arrival of its own widebody planes.

The agreement concluded at the end of October this year, with IndiGo citing airspace constraints, elevated fuel costs and currency pressures.

According to recent reports, at its peak in 2024, Norse Atlantic had 4,822 scheduled flights. That number dipped to 4,695 in 2025 and 2,565 in 2026, according to aviation analyst Cirium. The airline had 122 flights scheduled for November this year.

Recent findings suggest that norse Atlantic CEO Roald told CNBC in May that he was not "overly concerned" about access to fuel and said the airline had no indication at the time that the crisis would have an immediate impact. The airline said in October that it was continuing to operate at reduced capacity because of "prolonged high fuel prices.".

Industry observers note that the Anthropic (ANT -8.50%) initial public offering (IPO) this week reinforced the view that, despite recent turbulence related to developments at Nvidia, investment in artificial intelligence (AI) remains in high-growth mode. That's not in doubt; still, the key question for retail investors is how to find the best-value way to invest in it. Companies like the AI lab and Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX -7.69%), with its AI infrastructure, rocket launch, and connectivity businesses, appear attractive on this basis. Still, I think there's another tech giant that's a better buy on a risk-adjusted basis: On Semiconductor (ON -7.42%). (Related: Industry News: Buttigieg supporters launch group to sup...)

To buy into the investment theme, you need to believe that the use of AI applications will continue to grow, spurring growth for AI enterprises like Anthropic and, in turn, demand for SpaceX's data center infrastructure. While these assumptions about the outlook for end demand seem entirely feasible, the reality is that both companies are engaged in a fiercely competitive, highly capital-intensive arms race. (Related: AI breakthrough cuts energy use by 100x while boosting ...)

In Anthropic's case, it needs to continually and heavily invest in leasing compute capacity from infrastructure services (including those from SpaceX) to compete for frontier model supremacy with its rivals -- a group that actually includes SpaceX. Anthropic has already made over $900 billion in spending commitments, including $120.5 billion in an agreement with SpaceX.

In turn, compute providers like SpaceX and the well-known hyperscalers are having to make massive investments into new data center capacity to meet their contractual obligations. These investments are not risk-free. In SpaceX's case in particular, there are serious questions about how promptly its planned orbital data center satellites will depreciate. SpaceX also faces launch risk every time it attempts to send any cargo into space, and if it merges with Tesla, the manufacturer will be part of a much larger and more complex business.

All told, if there's any slowdown in demand for AI applications, both Anthropic and SpaceX are likely to suffer disproportionately.

The power and sensing chip company is largely a play on the increasing power density, not only in its core automotive and industrial end markets, but also in its fast-growing data center business. The company is best known for power control chips and sensing technology that are used in electric vehicles (EVs), as well as myriad industrial applications ranging from EV charging and industrial automation to renewable energy and consumer electronics.

According to recent reports, as an investment, On Semiconductor is not without risk. Its core automotive business is exposed to the cyclicality of the EV market; the power semiconductor market is competitive, and there's execution risk around its recent AI-related acquisitions. However, the diversity of its end markets means it doesn't face the same degree of existential risk as companies like Anthropic and SpaceX do from frontier model competition and capital-intensive investments.

In a recent development, in contrast to many AI-related companies, On Semiconductor is highly cash-generative, with Wall Street analysts targeting $2 billion in free cash flow (FCF) for 2027 and $2.3 billion for 2028, according to Visible Alpha. Based on its current market cap of $25.1 billion, the stock would be trading at 12.6 times FCF in 2027 and 10.9 times FCF in 2028.

In a recent development, those are highly attractive valuations for any company whose management is targeting 30% annual operating income growth from 2026 to 2030. In that period, its data center revenue growth is expected to grow at a 50% annual rate.

Industry observers note that on top of all of this, On Semiconductor's power and sensing solutions are set to become a key feature of physical AI solutions (such as robots, EVs, drones, and industrial machines) over the long term. As more physical AI products are developed, there will be an increasing need for edge power density. For example, robots can't wait a few seconds for calculations in the cloud; they need to process sensory inputs in real time, which requires power and sensing chips.

As such, the firm has significant exposure to a major AI investment theme, but doesn't trade at a nosebleed valuation, and doesn’t carry the kind of downside risk inherent in other pure-play AI stocks.

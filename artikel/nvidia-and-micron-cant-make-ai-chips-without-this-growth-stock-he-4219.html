Recent findings suggest that nvidia (NVDA -3.43%) and Micron Technology (MU -6.35%) both make chips that are essential to the continued AI build-out, but their chips couldn't be more different. Nvidia makes advanced logic chips and graphics processing units (GPUs) used for training and inference of large language models. Micron makes memory chips, and demand for its high-bandwidth chips has soared, as the ability to quickly access large volumes of data for training and inference has become a bottleneck in the continued development of AI. (Related: How to train a upcoming language model from scratch usi...)

Recent findings suggest that while Micron prints its chips itself, Nvidia contracts chip manufacturing to Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing. However, there's one organization neither could produce its chips without: Applied Materials (AMAT -4.70%). The company supplies manufacturers such as Micron and Nvidia's partner, TSMC, with a wide range of wafer fabrication equipment. (Related: Hugging Face Reads, Feb. 2021 - Long-range Transformers...)

The company is growing quickly, but the market may still underappreciate the opportunity ahead for the business, and the stock could soar from here.

Applied Materials holds a broad portfolio of wafer fabrication equipment that's used by both logic chipmakers like Nvidia and memory chipmakers like Micron. As Micron and other memory chipmakers respond to the spike in demand for their chips, Applied's share of semiconductor system sales for DRAM and flash memory chipmaking equipment climbed to 42% of sales in the company's fiscal fourth quarter. That's despite heavy spending from advanced logic chipmakers like TSMC as well.

Recent findings suggest that overall, Applied Materials' revenue climbed 21% in 2026, but that growth has accelerated sharply every quarter. Revenue was up 53% year over year in Q4. What's more, management expects 2027 to come in strong as well. Its first-quarter outlook calls for $11.1 billion in revenue at the midpoint, up 22% from last year. But it expects the market for wafer fabrication equipment to grow 30% overall for the full year and to gain market share, implying revenue acceleration throughout the year.

NASDAQ · As of 9:55 AM ET 11/20/26.

Industry observers note that applied's broad portfolio of equipment positions it well to gain market share in the fast-growing industry. It's the only equipment maker that competes across all three market segments: etch, deposition, and process control. Its size gives it the advantage of investing more in research and development than its competitors, while working closely with manufacturers to build integrated solutions that save floor space. Those solutions increase switching costs for manufacturers, locking them into Applied's products and servicing.

In the longer run, management expects its gross margin to expand by five percentage points from 2025 to 2030, while operating margin expands by 10 percentage points during the same period. Combined with the robust outlook for revenue growth driven by expected spending on artificial intelligence, Applied should deliver very strong net income growth and free cash flow. Management is committed to returning excess free cash flow to shareholders over time, reducing its share count, and further boosting earnings per share.

NASDAQ · As of 9:55 AM ET 11/20/26.

Sources indicate that applied Materials stock currently trades for less than 20 times forward earnings estimates. That's despite expectations that earnings per share will grow more than 46% over the next year and another 28% the following year.

That low multiple is due to the cyclical nature of the business, and there's a significant risk that its equipment sales growth will taper as AI spending stabilizes and capacity constraints ease. That noted, the firm's services revenue acts as a ballast, providing a consistent stream of revenue, as its equipment must be serviced regularly to maintain precision.

Recent findings suggest that that said, an earnings multiple of 20 is closer to its mid-cycle multiple, and the outlook for wafer fabrication equipment spending (as well as analysts' outlooks) indicates we're closer to the start of an up cycle in earnings. Importantly, the stock price could fall to about 12 times trailing earnings by the end of fiscal 2029 and still trade at roughly the same price as today. If it trades closer to the mid-cycle multiple of 20, that's upside of 63% from here. In my opinion, that's somewhat conservative considering the outlook for equipment demand.

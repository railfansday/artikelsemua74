The Nvidia (NVDA -3.91%) fallout has truly been incredible. At its peak, Nvidia's market cap was nearly $5.8 trillion. Now, it's about $3.6 trillion. That's more than $2 trillion in losses, and only five companies in the world have a market cap greater than that. That's hard to wrap your mind around, but Nvidia's loss of value since CEO Jensen Huang resigned and returned is unparalleled.

Nvidia is now down around 35% from its all-time high. That's significant because it is now tied for the deepest sell-off Nvidia has faced since the AI build-out kicked off in 2023. The previous deepest sell-off occurred in April 2025 when tariff fears drove the stock market down. However, that peaked at about a 35% sell-off.

It has been revealed that with Nvidia at its cheapest point in a long time, is it worth buying? Or do investors have more to worry about? Let's take a look.

In a recent development, even before Huang resigned as CEO, there was trouble with Nvidia's stock. The market was a bit concerned about industry-wide AI spending and assumed it could not last forever, so it discounted Nvidia's stock. The idea here was that AI spending would decrease, harming Nvidia. However, that is looking like a bad assumption. (Related: Industry News: The Partnership: Amazon SageMaker and Hu...)

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/25/26.

During the manufacturer's Q3 earnings call, interim CEO and current CFO Colette Kress increased revenue growth guidance for next year from 70% to 75%. That is due to extreme demand for AI from hyperscalers and won't change regardless of who leads Nvidia.

That is great news for shareholders, and if that growth comes to fruition, there's no way that Nvidia will stay at its current level.

The reality is that Huang built Nvidia into such a giant and dominant organization that it can run itself for a few years. While there were concerns about what the future of Nvidia looks like without him, those fears are now gone with him back in the CEO role. Still, Huang must testify before Congress to address some of the events that happened as CEO and explain what controls they have on chips or trade secrets that shouldn't have fallen into China's hands, but might have. Huang and Nvidia have a ways to go before they're in the clear, but Nvidia's expected growth isn’t changing.

But where will next year's growth take it?

For this fiscal year, Wall Street analysts estimate its revenue will total $424 billion. If Nvidia hits that figure (the firm has a strong track record of exceeding estimates) and grows by 75% next year, that would put its revenue at $742 billion. One of Nvidia's impressive characteristics is its profit margin, which has sat at 62% over the past 12 months. If the company can maintain that level, it would generate $460 billion in profits. That's right, Nvidia's profits should be larger next year than its revenue is this year. (Related: How to generate text: using different decoding methods ...)

Sources indicate that that's a simply incredible projection, and given how aggressive the AI hyperscalers are about scaling up computing power, I think it's realistic. With that money, Nvidia could do all sorts of things, including repurchasing a large amount of outstanding shares. This may be a viable option because Nvidia's stock is cheap right now, trading for a mere 9 times next year's earnings estimates.

Few stocks in the market trade at that level and are growing at a market-beating pace, let alone one that is expected to grow at a 75% pace next year.

Nvidia is one of the greatest values on the stock market right now; I think it's a no-brainer buy, and investors should be scooping up shares while they are massively discounted.

Industry observers note that nvidia (NVDA -3.91%) stock has had a busy few weeks. It was heavily affected by the resignation of its co-founder and CEO, Jensen Huang, and questions are now surfacing about how much of Nvidia's sales were conducted legally and whether any of its chips ended up in China. Huang is now back as CEO, but there is no definitive answer on export issues, and the market isn't sticking around to find out the conclusion of this saga. (Related: Industry News: My Journey to a serverless transformers ...)

It has been revealed that instead, it has sold off Nvidia's stock to a point not seen in years. In fact, with its stock closing at about $125 per share on Friday, it's traded at a price not seen since May 2025. After Huang returned, the stock rose to around $155 per share. Nvidia stock hasn’t been priced this low since mid-2025.

Industry observers note that however, Nvidia has grown significantly as a company since then, making its valuation cheap. But just how cheap is it? Let's take a look. (Related: Industry News: Understanding BigBird's Block Sparse Att...)

First, let's look at how Nvidia is trading based on trailing earnings. For reference, the S&P 500 (^GSPC -0.25%) trades at 20.3 times trailing earnings. As of the price Nvidia closed at on Nov. 24, the stock trades at just 16.7 times earnings.

That type of discount is normally reserved for companies with major business problems or those operating in deeply cyclical industries. While Nvidia may operate in a cyclical industry, the reality is that the artificial intelligence build-out isn't wrapping up anytime soon, and there will always be a need to replace outdated or burnt-out equipment.

In a recent development, however, the trailing price-to-earnings ratio is a bad metric for Nvidia. Why? It's all due to the growth Nvidia will produce.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/25/26.

In a recent development, during the organization’s fiscal third quarter of 2027 (ending Oct. 25), CFO Colette Kress raised the company’s revenue growth guidance for fiscal 2028 from 70% to 75%. That's an impressive figure considering how quickly Nvidia grew this year, and if those growth figures hold up, the stock will be even cheaper.

Nvidia now trades for just 9.3 times next year's earnings.

NVDA PE Ratio (Forward 1y) data by YCharts.

So, what's a fair value for Nvidia? If you think it's about 30 times trailing earnings, then if Nvidia rises to that level and hits next year's estimates, the stock could triple. A lot would need to go right with investigations, but it's not out of the question, either.

Recent findings suggest that while there are many unknowns about Nvidia's future, there are also many known factors. The AI boom isn't slowing down for anyone or anything, and that could lead to incredible performance over the next few years. I think now is the perfect time to buy Nvidia stock, as its price is driven more by fear than by reality.

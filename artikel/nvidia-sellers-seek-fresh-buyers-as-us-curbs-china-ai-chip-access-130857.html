Nvidia chip sellers are seeking upcoming buyers for the semiconductor giant's hardware, three people told CNBC, after the Trump administration tightened rules to curb Chinese companies' remote access to the most advanced artificial-intelligence systems.

Companies have put an unusually high volume of Nvidia graphics processing units, or GPUs, on the market since new measures restricting Chinese companies renting access to advanced Nvidia chips were announced on Nov. 16, the people mentioned. Industry observers note that jack Duhamel, president of AI infrastructure marketplace GPU Mart, and two people familiar with the matter told CNBC the surge in GPUs for sale was directly attributable to the newly introduced rule. Duhamel added that one manufacturer looking to sell Nvidia chips told GPU Mart the sale was related to the new rule. (Related: Industry News: Think AI "knows" what it’s doing? Scient...)

It has been revealed that the two people mentioned it appeared chip sellers were seeking to offload them because they were originally for use in data centers in which Chinese companies would rent access. They asked not to be named because they were discussing private information.

In a recent development, the Department of Commerce's Bureau of Industry and Security, or BIS, confirmed an interim final rule as part of a clampdown on Chinese companies bypassing export controls, which effectively ban the sale of the most advanced Nvidia chips to China, by accessing them remotely in overseas data centers. The rule came into effect on Nov. 19.

Deals to find buyers for thousands of B300 GPUs, one of Nvidia's most advanced offerings, have been floated since the rule was announced, said Duhamel and one of the people.

According to recent reports, nvidia declined to comment. BIS did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

Recent findings suggest that nvidia has been facing increasing scrutiny from U.S. lawmakers over Chinese companies' access to its advanced chips.

In a recent development, the chip giant warned its customers that the proposed Remote Access Security Act — which has passed the House of Representatives but is yet to pass the Senate — would give the U.S. government broader authority to control access to cloud services, potentially exposing both parties to "legal action," sources previously told CNBC.

According to recent reports, in July, the U.S. accused a Chinese AI lab, Moonshot AI, of accessing Nvidia chips via a facility in Thailand. CNBC reported on Nov. 5 that the company had sought access to the chips via data centers in Europe.

U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer told CNBC's "Squawk Box" on Wednesday that the latest rule declared last week was intended to close "loopholes.".

"For a long time, we have not allowed the sale of Blackwell chips, which are the highest AI chips or similar chips to China directly," he stated.

"Some rules were issued by the Commerce Department last week to close off indirect access, either smuggling through places like Southeast Asia or remote access from data centers in third countries.".

According to recent reports, during Nvidia's third-quarter earnings call on Nov. 17, then-interim CEO Colette Kress said the tech giant was still studying the controls, but expected performance to remain strong because demand for chips far exceeded supply.

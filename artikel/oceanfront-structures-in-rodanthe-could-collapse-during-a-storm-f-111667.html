Oceanfront structures in Rodanthe could collapse during a storm forecast for North Carolina’s Outer Banks on November 25 and 26, the Park Service warns

According to recent reports, damaged septic systems could expose waste on Rodanthe beaches, and drivers should avoid vulnerable parts of N.C. Highway 12 at high tide.

Sources indicate that people on North Carolina’s Outer Banks should stay out of the ocean, avoid driving on the beach and avoid vulnerable sections of N.C. Highway 12 at high tide on November 25 and 26, according to the National Park Service. (Related: Tech Report: Why you should upgrade to an OLED for GTA ...)

A coastal storm is forecast to bring strong, gusty winds and elevated water levels to Cape Hatteras National Seashore on those two days.

In the village of Rodanthe, threatened oceanfront structures “could partially or completely collapse,” the Park Service warned on November 24. (Related: This newly introduced chip survives 1300°F (700°C) and ...)

This is a warning, not a report of damage. But the seashore says the risk goes beyond the structures themselves.

Damaged septic systems could expose waste, the Park Service said, “creating serious hazards for people walking on the beach or wading nearby.”.

Recent findings suggest that most off-road vehicle routes on the beach will likely be impassable during the storm, according to the seashore, and hazardous surf will make swimming unsafe. Visitors should avoid beach driving and stay out of the water until conditions improve.

N.C. Highway 12, the main road along the islands, is also at risk. The Park Service notes travelers should avoid its vulnerable sections during high tides on November 25 and 26, and that hazardous weather may cause road closures.

It lists four areas where driving may be very difficult during the storm:.

On Ocracoke Island, a two-mile stretch of highway south of off-road vehicle ramp 59.

On southern Hatteras Island, the road between the villages of Hatteras and Frisco.

On central Hatteras Island, the area along the northern boundary of Buxton.

On northern Hatteras Island, the road between the Pea Island Visitor Center and the Basnight Bridge, as well as side streets in northern Rodanthe.

All of the seashore’s campgrounds remain open, the Park Service indicates, but minor flooding and strong wind gusts will make for unfavorable camping conditions.

It points travelers to the National Weather Service at weather.gov/mhx for forecasts and to the North Carolina Department of Transportation at drivenc.gov for road conditions.

The seashore has issued similar warnings in 2026.

In a recent development, on April 7, it urged visitors to use caution on east-facing beaches through April 11. The potential for multi-day, persistent northeast winds and breaking waves, it noted, could cause coastal erosion and “the partial or full collapse of one or more threatened oceanfront structures in Buxton and Rodanthe.”.

At that time, the entire beach in front of Buxton was closed because of hazards from threatened oceanfront structures “in or near the ocean,” according to the Park Service.

After a recent coastal storm, the seashore reported significant impacts in a September 28 enhancement. It noted Dare County had declared a state of emergency for Hatteras Island, and non-resident travel to the island was prohibited.

It has been revealed that the Avon Fishing Pier had an estimated 20-foot gap in its structure. The beach was closed from the northern boundary of Rodanthe to the pier in Rodanthe, and people were told to avoid the ocean because of possible poor water quality from damaged septic systems.

Private beach stairs and boardwalk decking, septic system parts, and wooden and metal debris from houses were littering the beach, the Park Service stated at the time.

Cape Hatteras National Seashore sits on barrier islands, and those islands are not fixed in place. One study predicts that barrier islands will retreat faster than they have in the past, and healthy dunes help protect the coasts behind them when storms hit.

It has been revealed that the Park Service’s advice for now is practical: use caution on the beach, avoid beach driving and stay out of the water during the storm, and check road conditions before driving N.C. Highway 12.

Sources indicate that palantir Technologies (PLTR -6.63%) has finished only two calendar years in the red since it started trading in September 2020, and it might add a third. As I write, shares are near $160, about 10% under the $177.75 they closed at to end 2025, with less than six weeks of trading left in the year. The stock has already swung widely this year, closing as low as about $107 in late June before reaching a record close over $209 in October. (Related: Latest Update: Clavicular allegedly told 17-year-old to...)

Palantir's two losing years didn't end the same way, though. The stock dropped around 23% in 2021 and then shed another 65% in 2022. But the 2022 loss was followed by a 167% rise in 2023.

In a recent development, what separated the two? I think it mainly boiled down to the price investors were paying heading into the next year, and whether the business was getting stronger. On one of those two fronts, today looks more like the end of 2021 than the end of 2022.

Palantir's 2021 wasn't a bad year for the business. Revenue climbed 41% year over year to $1.54 billion, and the organization's net loss shrank to $520 million from $1.17 billion in 2020.

Sources indicate that but growth was cooling as the year ended. Fourth-quarter revenue grew 34% year over year, a slowdown from the full year's pace.

The stock wasn't cheap, either, even after its 2021 drop. Based on Palantir's share count at the end of 2021, its $18.21 closing price gave the organization a market cap of around $37 billion -- about 24 times its 2021 sales.

Sure, a high price can hold up if growth does, but Palantir's growth kept cooling. Full-year revenue grew 24% in 2022, and fourth-quarter revenue climbed just 18% year over year. The tech giant also lost $374 million that year, and the stock shed around two-thirds of its value.

Recent findings suggest that the end of 2022 set up something very different. At $6.42 a share, Palantir's market cap was around $13.5 billion. Put another way, investors were paying around 7 times sales, based on 2022 revenue of $1.91 billion.

Then, in February 2023, Palantir posted its first quarter of positive GAAP net income, earning $31 million in the fourth quarter of 2022. Management also stated it expected GAAP net income for all of 2023.

Sources indicate that interestingly, the business didn't speed up in 2023. Revenue rose just 17% to $2.23 billion, slower than in either losing year. But net income swung to $210 million, from the $374 million loss in 2022.

Because the stock began the year at around 7 times sales, the improvement was arguably enough to power a 167% gain.

Industry observers note that on the business side, today's Palantir resembles neither year. Its year-over-year revenue growth rate has risen three straight quarters, from 70% in the fourth quarter of 2025 to 85%, 93%, and 96% during the first three quarters of 2026. Third-quarter revenue hit $2.31 billion.

In a recent development, even more, profits are soaring. Third-quarter net income was $1.13 billion, or 49% of revenue, over twice the $476 million Palantir made a year before.

It has been revealed that the price, though, looks more like the end of 2021, just higher. At around $160 a share, Palantir is worth about $385 billion. Measured against its revenue for the last four quarters, that's about 53 times sales.

Industry observers note that it's more than double the sales multiple the stock carried into 2022 and more than seven times what it carried into 2023. Measured against earnings, shares trade at more than 110 times earnings, a price that already bakes in years of rapid growth.

True, growth like this can lower a valuation fast. When 2026 started, shares cost around 95 times Palantir's 2025 revenue, and that sales multiple has since dropped by over 40%, mainly because revenue rose. But Palantir itself expects some cooling. Its fourth-quarter forecast of around $2.65 billion implies year-over-year growth of about 88%.

What made 2023 work was a low starting price meeting an improving business. Palantir has the improving business now, but it doesn't have the low price. So I don't think a losing 2026 would set up a 2023-style rebound. Any gains from here would probably need to come slowly, as revenue and profits rise faster than the share price for a while.

Sources indicate that I'd still be comfortable holding a small stake at this price, as the business has never looked stronger. I'd just expect the returns to take some patience.

Pearl Jam called out ICE agents for their “inhumane treatment” of two people arrested at Seattle-Tacoma International Airport over the weekend.

Videos of masked ICE agents arresting Sofia Hernandez Meija and her father, Ricardo Larrondo Mendoza, have gone viral and sparked protests in Pearl Jam’s hometown of Seattle. Sharing an image of demonstrators, the band wrote, “It is heartbreaking and infuriating to watch the human toll of a system driven by cold cruelty. Seeing a family torn apart and dragged off a jetway right here in our own airport by masked individuals is unconscionable and we stand firmly against this kind of inhumane treatment.”. Industry observers note that meija and Mendoza were arrested Saturday, Nov. 21, on a jet bridge while trying to board a flight to Los Angeles, where they were traveling for a baptism. Videos showed ICE agents struggling with a man on the ground and trying to drag him out while other passengers filmed and photographed the incident. There were also videos of a woman being detained and carried out by agents as she cried out, sobbed, and, at one point, stated, “We have a work permit!”. In a recent development, in a statement, ICE accused Mendoza of being an undocumented immigrant from Mexico who “overstayed his visa.” They accused Meija of the same, while also claiming she obstructed the arrest and threw hot coffee at an agent. ICE said it prepares to charge Meija with assaulting a federal officer.

Industry observers note that speaking with The Seattle Times, a lawyer for Mendoza and Meija, Olia Catala, noted the two had been living in the United States since 2015 and were seeking asylum. Catala added that neither had a criminal record, and that Meija, who is married to a U.S. citizen, recently filed to change her citizenship status. Recent findings suggest that after video of the arrests went viral, protests erupted in Seattle, including some demonstrations at the airport. In backing the protesters, Pearl Jam also pointed to the recent midterm and why the election “mattered so much.”.

Industry observers note that “It showed we aren’t powerless or screaming into a void,” the band wrote. “Americans stood up, looked at the systematic abuse and what ICE is doing to our neighbors, and mentioned, no more.”.

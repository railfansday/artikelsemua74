According to recent reports, I'm retired and hoping for a quick sanity check before Monday, 11/30/26. Because of the holiday, there are really only two trading days left before my old employer's 401k forces an automatic exchange out of a frozen fund, so I need to make a decision soon.I dislike the limited lineup in this 401k (only 31 options, mostly target-date funds, active funds, and CITs), but I'm stuck in it for now because I hold organization stock that is at the moment down and I'm waiting for the right time to take advantage of NUA.Right now, I own Managed Income Portfolio Class 2 (MIP), a frozen stable value fund that is being removed from the lineup on 11/30/26. Unless I make a change, the blueprint will automatically exchange it into Great Gray Trust T. Rowe Price Stable Value CIT S (0.287% ER). I don't want to own another stable value fund, especially since I already hold plenty of cash across my accounts and because of the plan's equity wash restrictions, I have to move MIP into a non-competing fund.For some overall context, my total portfolio across taxable and tax-deferred accounts as of 10/31/26 is about 61.8% stocks, 1.7% bonds, and 37.1% cash (it adds up to slightly over 100% due to rounding). My key holdings across all accounts are S&P 500 funds at 27.38%, Fidelity Growth Company (FDGRX) at 9.86%, and Fidelity Low-Priced Stock (FLPSX) at 9.24%. I've held FDGRX and FLPSX in my IRAs for decades. Eventually, I'd like to simplify and transition away from active funds toward low-cost index funds, though FDGRX and FLPSX will be tough to part with.Looking at the limited choices in this 401k, it really comes down to two options for the exchange:Spartan 500 Index Pool Class E (0.0075% ER)It's ultra-low cost, aligns with my goal to move toward index funds, and I already own some in this 401k. My hesitation is that it's a CIT, and I really dislike the lack of a ticker, transparency, and visible dividend/income reinvestments compared to standard index funds (the plan unfortunately got rid of the regular Fidelity S&P 500 fund). It also adds to my existing S&P 500 and tech exposure, though with tech being down recently, maybe buying more while it's on sale isn't a bad thing.Fidelity Low-Priced Stock K6 Fund - FLKSX (0.50% ER)I've had great success owning FLPSX in my IRAs for decades, and adding FLKSX in my 401k would give me more mid/small-cap value diversification away from large-cap tech (though I know it also carries around 30-35% international exposure, which I'm not specifically looking to add). On the downside, the expense ratio is much higher at 0.50%, and longtime manager Joel Tillinghast retired in 2023.Since I can always rebalance elsewhere in my IRAs later, which of these two would you move this money into to avoid Monday's automatic stable value exchange? Is my hesitation about the S&P 500 CIT overblown, or does FLKSX make more sense here?Any thoughts or feedback would be greatly appreciated.Statistics: Posted by FinIndyGal — Wed Nov 25, 2026 3:30 pm.

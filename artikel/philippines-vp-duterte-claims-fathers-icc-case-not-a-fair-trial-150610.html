The Vice President of the Philippines, Sara Duterte, criticized the International Criminal Court (ICC) on Thursday, saying it would not provide a fair trial for her ex-president father's crimes against humanity case.

She stated the court would try to "make a statement.".

Former President Rodrigo Duterte has been detained at The Hague since his arrest on an ICC warrant in March 2025, over extra-judicial killings that took place between 2011 and 2019.

The trial begins on Monday, where Rodrigo Duterte is expected to plead not guilty. Witnesses will be called up to testify the next day, with more than 500 alleged victims represented in ⁠court.

Industry observers note that "I think the ICC, after the ganging up of superpowers, (will) want to make a statement that they are in the right. So there is no-one else there that they would like to make a statement to. And that's the case of Rodrigo Duterte," said his daughter Sara. (Related: Latest Update: Think AI "knows" what it’s doing? Duterte said "China, Russia and our BFF the United States," all considered the ICC a "political organization" and not an impartial arbiter. (Related: Nvidia stops AI GPU shipments to Southeast Asia amid pu...)

BFF stands for "best friend forever" and was likely a swipe at the Philippines' close ties with the US under President Ferdinand Marcos Jr.

Prosecutors at the ICC ​accuse the 81-year-old former president of funding and arming execution squads that murdered as many as 30,000 people.

He has been charged with three counts of crimes against ​humanity involving murder and attempted murder between 2011 and 2018.

It has been revealed that duterte is the first Asian former ​leader and only ‌the second former national ruler to appear before the court for crimes allegedly committed while in power. The other one was Laurent Gbagbo of Ivory Coast, who was ultimately acquitted in 2019.

Supporters of the former president, who was arrested in Manila, say he was "kidnapped" and sent to The Hague after his daughter had a bitter falling-out with current president Ferdinand Marcos Jr., with whom she had held an alliance that helped him win a landslide 2022 election victory.

The younger Duterte is also facing an ongoing impeachment trial in the Philippine Senate and if found guilty, she would be barred permanently from elected office.

She mentioned both her case and her father's ICC trial were related to efforts to make her ineligible to run for the 2028 presidential election.

"I still believe that it's about the 2028 elections, whether there will be elections or there will be no elections. It's about the change of leadership, if there will ever be a change of leadership," she told reporters.

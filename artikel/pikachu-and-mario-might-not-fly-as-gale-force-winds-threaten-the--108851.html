Update: 4:00 PM EST – As reported by NBC, the parade has officially been delayed until Saturday. This is the first time in the 100-year history of the parade that it has been delayed. Mario’s mustache will fly… later.

Original story continues below….

In a recent development, meteorologists are forecasting a truly nasty Thanksgiving morning in NYC, with a high-wind warning right now set to kick off at 10 a.m. Eastern Time. Gusts are projected to possibly reach as high as 60 mph and sustained winds could clock in at around 35 mph, reported Hell Gate. Weather experts are also predicting power outages and fallen tree limbs across the city. (Related: This newly introduced chip survives 1300°F (700°C) and ...)

Sources indicate that this is the kind of weather that could force parade officials to keep balloons lower to the ground or even prevent them from flying at all due to safety concerns and legal restrictions that were placed on the parade after the infamous 1997 parade that saw multiple balloons, including Sonic the Hedgehog, damaged by winds and multiple injuries reported. During that disaster, wind gusts reached around 43 mph, well below what’s forecasted for tomorrow.

As reported by ABC 7, parade officials won’t make a decision until early Thursday morning, before the start of the parade. One option is to fly balloons much lower to the ground. Another option being floated by officials is to start the parade earlier than 8:30 am to hopefully avoid the worst of the incoming storm.

“This is a challenging week. This is a challenging forecast,” Newly introduced York City Emergency Management Commissioner Christina Farrell told ABC7.

Recent findings suggest that “You may remember, there was some lightning and storms coming through on July 4th. It’s been that kind of year. The fireworks were moved up a little bit. That is one option. If the weather is coming in a little later, can we start things a little earlier?” said Farrell.

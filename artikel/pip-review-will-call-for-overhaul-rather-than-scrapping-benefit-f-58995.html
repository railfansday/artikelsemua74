Pip review will call for overhaul rather than scrapping benefit for under-25s

A long-awaited review of the personal independence payment (Pip) system will this week recommend that it is overhauled, a week after another report called for it to be partly scrapped. (Related: Latest Update: VAST notes petabyte SSDs will outgrow to...)

The final report on Pip by a committee co-led by Stephen Timms, the social security minister, is expected to be published as soon as Tuesday, presenting Andy Burnham with one of his most politically sensitive decisions yet as prime minister.

Its conclusions, shaped with the input of disability rights advocates, are understood to be very similar to a draft set of recommendations that emerged in September, which called for a series of changes, particularly to the system of assessment, to take better account of conditions that vary over time.

Industry observers note that this would involve significant changes to Pip, which is intended to help disabled people meet the extra costs of disability. An interim report by Timms’ committee described the benefit as widely valued but “no longer fit for purpose”.

However, it would retain the system for all ages, while last week’s report by Alan Milburn on worklessness among young people called for it to be scrapped for adults under 25 and replaced by an “additional cost allowance”, part of a wider network of job-based help for this age group. Government officials have pushed back on the idea of a clash, saying Milburn’s review was commissioned after the Timms process had begun, and that about 90% of the Pip caseload involves people over 25.

But with Timms’ recommendations understood to be quite broad, Burnham and his ministers will need to make a series of decisions about what comes next, on a deeply sensitive subject. Keir Starmer was forced to abandon planned changes in 2025 after a rebellion by Labour MPs.

There was widespread shock among disability campaigners and charities at the Milburn report’s recommendations to scrap Pip and universal credit health benefit payments for under-25s as part of efforts to boost jobs and training for young people not in education, employment or training.

The review steering group is co-chaired by Timms and the disability rights advocates Clenton Farquharson and Sharon Brennan, and most of its 12 members have lived experience of disability or long-term health conditions.

There are reportedly worries among some members of the Timms review board over the scale of Milburn’s proposals on Pip, which would cut £3.6bn from spending on young people. “Some members are really concerned,” one source told the Guardian.

Industry observers note that the report will include short-term overhauls, within the current spending round, and longer-term ones. Many of the specifics for the latter would need to be decided by ministers before they legislate next year.

According to recent reports, burnham faces pressure from the Conservatives to reduce the overall cost of working-age benefits. He has said this is his initiative but he has pushed back against the idea of what he called “crude cuts”, saying these often just created costs elsewhere.

The Timms review, framed by ministers as a groundbreaking “co-produced” policy initiative, was seen as an attempt to restore trust among disabled people after the collapse of the Pip prepares under Starmer, which were based on £5bn in cuts.

The interim report in July and further emerging findings in September confirmed a consensus view that Pip was unfit for purpose and that a major overhaul was needed to make the benefit fair, humane and efficient. It raised the prospect that some claimants could in future receive “in kind” awards such as services, aid or training alongside basic cash awards.

But it underlined that cash payments were “the foundation” of a future reformed disability benefit, that it would not be means-tested and that any “reformed benefit will be designed with disabled people and those with long-term health conditions, and tested to make sure it works before it is rolled out”. There was no mention of scrapping Pip for under-25s or any other age groups.

It has been revealed that until recently, most disability charities had been optimistic that the Timms review – the remit of which did not include the costs of Pip or potential cuts in spending – would lead to much-needed changes and improve the lives of the 4 million adults who claim it. But the timing and scope of the Milburn review, which has been conspicuously welcomed by ministers, has dented that confidence.

A spokesperson for the Department for Work and Pensions said: “The Timms review interim report made clear that Pip is no longer fit for purpose. When the final report is published, we will consider the recommendations alongside the Milburn review.”.

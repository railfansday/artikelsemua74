In a recent development, in January, one notable firm told its investors that Anthropic could be worth about $2 trillion by 2030. On Thursday, Anthropic (ANT -8.50%) hit that valuation in its very first trade as a public firm, roughly four years ahead of schedule. (Related: DNA robots could deliver drugs and hunt viruses inside ...)

The stock gave back a good chunk of its first-day gains, but even at about $96 as of this writing, Anthropic is worth about $1.7 trillion on a fully diluted basis. That already puts it among the world's largest companies, but I think it can go much further. In fact, I predict that by the end of 2030, Anthropic will be the most valuable company on the planet.

Apple (AAPL -1.90%) is presently the world's most valuable tech giant, worth about $5.2 trillion as of Nov. 13. Nvidia (NVDA -14.01%) was worth more than $5 trillion as recently as early November, but a six-week slide has pushed it below $3.6 trillion.

It has been revealed that of course, the bar will keep rising. While we don't know what Apple's stock price will do, if the tech giant grows its market value by about 8% a year, it would be worth roughly $7 trillion by the end of 2030. A rebound in Nvidia could push the top spot even higher. A realistic estimate is that Anthropic would need to be worth somewhere between $7 trillion and $10 trillion to claim first place, about four to six times its current value. I'm well aware that this is a bold prediction. But I think it's within the realm of possibilities.

Anthropic's third-quarter revenue was $17.0 billion, up 1,127% from a year earlier. As of the latest information, the company revealed that its annual revenue run rate had surpassed $80 billion.

In a recent development, if revenue grows by about 50% a year from here, Anthropic would generate roughly $340 billion of annual revenue by 2030. At 20 to 25 times sales, which is about where the stock trades today, the organization would be worth about $7 trillion to $8.5 trillion. Growth of 50% per year would be a dramatic slowdown from Anthropic's current pace, and, quite frankly, I think this scenario is more realistic than a best-case.

Here's why. The average Fortune 500 customer right now allocates less than 0.1% of its spending to Anthropic's Claude tools. But it's clear that people are finding tremendous value, and the company's net revenue retention rate of 900% means that the average existing customer now spends nine times as much as they did a year ago. Anthropic could grow its average revenue per customer by a further 10x multiple and still account for less than 1% of its customers' overall spending. As AI evolves and Claude tools become more essential to the day-to-day operations of Anthropic's enterprise clients, this is certainly possible.

It's not only top-line growth that could propel the stock higher. It's that Anthropic is becoming more profitable over time, and this is likely to continue as the business scales and compute and infrastructure costs (as a percentage of revenue) decline.

Recent findings suggest that over the past few years, most of the profits from the AI boom have gone to the companies selling the hardware, such as Nvidia. I expect more of that value to shift toward the companies with the best AI models over time, and I'm not alone. Atreides Management founder Gavin Baker has argued that Anthropic could eventually pass Nvidia as the world's most valuable company for this reason. In a nutshell, Nvidia sells the picks and shovels of the AI boom, while Anthropic is building the mine itself. https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/upcoming-ipo-could-soon-surpass-160226425.html.

Industry observers note that it's also worth noting that Anthropic has powerful partners. Amazon (AMZN -4.10%) and Alphabet (GOOGL -2.70%)(GOOG -2.59%), two of its biggest customers, will own nearly 15% and 14% of the company, respectively, after the IPO.

As I mentioned earlier, this is a bold call, and there are several reasons why I could be wrong. Competition from Anthropic's rivals, including OpenAI and Alphabet's Google, is intense, and AI model prices could fall faster than usage grows. Anthropic is also spending enormous sums. It posted a $567 million operating loss in the third quarter and has about $900 billion of computing and infrastructure commitments. And Apple and Nvidia are hardly standing still when it comes to their own innovation.

As a CFP, I'd also add that a bold prediction isn't a reason to bet the farm. I'm planning to build an Anthropic stock position gradually, and I'd keep any young, volatile stock to a modest share of a portfolio.

Recent findings suggest that having said all that, if Anthropic keeps growing at even a fraction of its current pace, I think the market will eventually value the company building the AI models more than the companies supplying the chips and devices. This is a five-year call, not a price target for next week, but I wouldn't be at all surprised to see Anthropic sit at the top of the market-cap rankings by the end of 2030.

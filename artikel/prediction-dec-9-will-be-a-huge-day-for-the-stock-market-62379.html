The S&P 500 (^GSPC -3.70%) has experienced a wild ride lately, as several market-moving events all converged in the first half of November: midterm election results, a hot inflation report, and Jensen Huang's resignation from Nvidia. Here's how each event could move the market.

The next FOMC meeting concludes on Dec. 9 and chair Kevin Warsh is expected to hold a press conference discussing its target fed funds rate decision. Futures traders currently expect the committee to raise the target rate by 25 basis points, with an outside chance of a 50-basis-point increase. Expectations for a rate increase were significantly lower before the most recent inflation report, which highlighted prices rising faster than expected.

Sources indicate that higher interest rates tend to keep stock prices lower, as investors are pushed to discount earnings at a higher rate. Higher interest rates are especially relevant in today's market, where many of the largest companies in the S&P 500 have become extremely capital-intensive, relying on outside funding to fuel their growth.

Industry observers note that while the rate decision is important, it's unlikely the Fed will do anything that surprises the market and causes a significant move in prices in and of itself. What may be more telling is Warsh's comments at the press conference. While he's aimed to reduce how much telegraphing he does about the Fed's future rate plans, his comments could provide some indication of what he and the committee are watching ahead of the next meeting.

According to recent reports, broadcom is one of the most crucial chipmakers in the artificial intelligence space. It's the leading developer of custom AI accelerators used by hyperscalers and leading AI labs. Investors will be paying close attention to its earnings report, in particular, its AI semiconductor revenue.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

Recent findings suggest that management said it expects AI semiconductor revenue to accelerate to $21.7 billion, up 236% year over year for the quarter. A shortfall could indicate the shift to custom silicon isn't moving as rapidly as expected or that hyperscalers simply can't build as quickly as anticipated. The former would be bad news for several other companies that make custom AI chips, while the latter would be bad news for the entire semiconductor industry.

On the flip side, a good report and better-than-expected outlook could provide a boost to Broadcom stock and the rest of the industry. It could particularly benefit Alphabet and Anthropic, its two largest partners.

It has been revealed that micron boosted its share repurchase authorization from $2.16 billion to $35.16 billion in October, but there's a substantial catch. The company is not legally allowed to repurchase more shares than it issues for stock-based compensation until Dec. 9. That's due to receiving Chip Act funding from the U.S. government in 2024.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

Micron will likely do its best not to move the market for its stock, but it could be a huge buyer for the shares at depressed levels. The stock is down about 40% from its peak this summer and 29.5% since the board authorized the repurchase in October.

The confidence of a major buyer in Micron stock could support the stock price of one of the largest semiconductor stocks in the S&P 500. And if Micron's management is buying the stock around the current level, it could encourage other investors to buy its competitors and other AI stocks that have come down in price. Management may provide an patch on its buyback program again with its quarterly earnings report in December, giving investors a better idea of how big a buyer of its own stock it will be.

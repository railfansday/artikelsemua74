It has been revealed that on Nov. 12, Nvidia (NVDA -14.01%) founder and chief executive officer Jensen Huang resigned from his post after a series of extramarital affairs (including one with an alleged Chinese spy) came to light. To make matters worse, the U.S. government is now investigating some of the company's customers in Southeast Asia to determine whether advanced artificial intelligence (AI) chips were illegally forwarded to businesses in China. Recent findings suggest that nvidia stock has plummeted by 37% from its peak amid the turmoil, and it's now trading at the cheapest valuation in a decade. However, the manufacturer just reported a blockbuster set of operating results for its fiscal 2027 third quarter (ended Oct. 25), and interim CEO Colette Kress even issued a higher revenue forecast for the upcoming 2028 fiscal year.

Sources indicate that as a result, while Nvidia's challenges are likely to cause further short-term volatility in its stock, I think its booming business and solid fundamentals will ultimately prevail. Here's where I predict the stock will be trading in one year. (Related: AI breakthrough cuts energy use by 100x while boosting ...)

Nvidia has come a long way since launching its first dedicated AI graphics processing unit (GPU) for the data center, the Hopper-based H100, in 2022. It went on to release the Blackwell and Blackwell Ultra architectures in 2024 and 2025, the latter of which can deliver up to 50 times more output performance than Hopper.

But Nvidia started shipping its latest Vera Rubin systems during the recent third quarter, which include the Rubin GPU, Vera central processing unit (CPU), and a series of upgraded networking components. It can deliver up to 30 times the performance of the equivalent Blackwell Ultra system, translating into a potential 97% reduction in inference token costs.

Inference tokens are the text, images, or computer code generated by an AI model in response to a prompt, so Vera Rubin could significantly reduce the cost of running AI software. This is likely to drive greater AI adoption while making data center operators more profitable, fueling further demand for Nvidia's chips.

Colette Kress mentioned Vera Rubin achieved almost $20 billion in sales during the third quarter alone, making this the fastest product ramp in Nvidia's history.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

Nvidia generated a record $115.8 billion in revenue during the third quarter, a whopping 103% increase from the year-ago period. The data center segment alone accounted for $107.6 billion of that total, and it grew at an even faster pace of 110%.

Sources indicate that nvidia's physical AI business also deserves a mention. It develops the hardware and software technologies required to run robots, autonomous vehicles, industrial systems, and more, and its revenue more than tripled year over year during the third quarter. It's now on track to generate $25 billion in total sales for the current fiscal year (which ends on Jan. 31, 2027), so it could soon become a key source of growth for the entire organization.

Kress also stated Nvidia could deliver $130 billion in total revenue during the fourth quarter, exceeding Wall Street's consensus expectation of $125.1 billion. Plus, she told investors that revenue could grow by 75% during fiscal 2028, upping the tech giant's original forecast of 70% from a few months ago.

That was a very positive surprise, and it dispels some of the speculation that Nvidia's sales will take a major hit amid any potential investigations into its dealings with customers in Southeast Asia.

Jensen Huang and Nvidia's general counsel, Tim Teter, are expected to testify before Congress in early December, where they will be questioned about potential national security breaches involving the prohibited sale of advanced AI chips to China. It's difficult to know what will come of these hearings, but I think investors are now being compensated for the potential risks because of Nvidia's rock-bottom valuation.

According to recent reports, nvidia stock is trading at a price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio of just 16.2 as I write this on Nov. 19, which is a fraction of its 10-year average of 61.1. It's also the lowest valuation in a decade.

According to recent reports, moreover, Wall Street still believes Nvidia will grow its earnings to $16.61 per share in fiscal 2028, placing its forward P/E ratio at just 9.1. For some perspective, that's less than half the forward P/E of the Nasdaq-100 index, which is 20.7, so Nvidia looks extremely undervalued compared to a collective of America's leading technology giants.

Recent findings suggest that as a result, I think there is room for Nvidia stock to trade significantly higher over the next 12 months, provided the company's fiscal 2028 guidance remains intact and isn't disrupted by a slowdown in AI infrastructure spending, a loss of market share, or government investigations. These are genuine risks, but Kress's decision to raise the forecast suggests the sales pipeline is robust enough to weather them.

Even if Nvidia stock doubles over the next year, it will still be cheaper than the Nasdaq-100 on a P/E basis, so I feel confident predicting a price of at least $200. But I think there is a real chance the stock reclaims its previous high of $239.

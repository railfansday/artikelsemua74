Prime Video had a short-lived William Gibson cyberpunk sci-fi series that had all the elements to develop into a 10/10 show, but its potential was never realized onscreen. Prime Video's TV show catalog features quite a few notable sci-fi series. The genre is well represented through comedies like Upload, the intense The Expanse, and other exciting genre indicates.

Sources indicate that for instance, Prime Video just released one of the most ambitious sci-fi TV shows in the age of streaming. After all, the streamer has tapped into one of the most iconic franchises of all time, Ridley Scott's Blade Runner, for the sequel series Blade Runner 2099, which is set 50 years after the last film in the cyberpunk franchise, Ryan Gosling's Blade Runner 2049.

Speaking of cyberpunk TV shows, William Gibson is often referred to as the creator of that notable sci-fi subgenre. After all, his hit book, Neuromancer, is highly regarded as foundational to all cyberpunk stories that have come after it. Gibson fans can look forward to that franchise being adapted into a live-action series on Apple TV, as Neuromancer, starring former James Bond candidate Callum Turner, debuts on January 22, 2027.

Industry observers note that those looking for a genre show to watch while waiting for Apple TV's Neuromancer next year can find not only what stands as one of the most intricate sci-fi TV shows in recent years, but another project based on Gibson's work. The TV show in question is The Peripheral, and I still can't believe it was robbed of the chance of becoming a 10/10 show. (Related: Tech Report: Transformer-based Encoder-Decoder Models...)

Industry observers note that the Peripheral debuted on Prime Video on October 21, 2022, with eight episodes being released on the streaming service until the finale on December 2, 2022. The cyberpunk series went through one of the most disappointing arcs in the streaming age, as The Peripheral holds a strong 79% critics' score on Rotten Tomatoes and an even better 85% audience score, with the show having originally been renewed for season 2 only a couple of months after its season 1 finale. By all accounts, it laid a solid foundation for what could be years of complex sci-fi storytelling. (Related: Industry News: My Journey to a serverless transformers ...)

Recent findings suggest that however, due to the effects of the 2023 SAG-AFTRA and Writers Guild of America strikes, Prime Video walked back on the renewal, canceling The Peripheral in August 2023. To say that was disappointing is not enough. Gibson is known for being one of the most skilled authors in the sci-fi genre, and The Peripheral showed a lot of promise in its first eight episodes. The series had to deal with complicated elements, as its story consists of two interconnected timelines separated by 70 years, headsets that can send people's consciousness across timelines, and a fast approaching apocalypse known as the Jackpot.

There was a lot of worldbuilding to unpack in The Peripheral season 1, but the show did a mostly fantastic job at making all these complex ideas feel interesting and urgent. With impressive VFX, kinetic action sequences, sharp writing, magnetic performances, and plenty of mysteries left to unfold, The Peripheral's eight episodes looked like a clear case of a TV show that was preparing itself to get even bigger and better in its next season. This is why the show's cancellation still stings three years later, as the lasting feeling is that it was robbed of becoming a true sci-fi masterpiece.

Industry observers note that making matters worse is the fact that The Peripheral was packed full of talent both in front and behind the camera. Chloë Grace Moretz as Flynne Fisher and Jack Reynor as her brother, Burton, commanded the screen and were easy to connect with. T'Nia Miller's Cherise Nuland was an intense and cunning villain, and her recent showstopping performance as Jocasta in Marvel's sci-fi series VisionQuest was a reminder of what we lost. Then there is the fact that Jonathan Nolan and Lisa Joy, who were behind HBO's Westworld and Prime Video's own Fallout, were executive producers. The Peripheral simply deserved better.

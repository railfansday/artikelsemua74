Industry observers note that prime Video's new 8-part sci-fi thriller delivers a masterclass in cyberpunk worldbuilding, bringing an iconic, breathtaking dystopian future to life. Released in full on Wednesday, November 25, this brand new series is one of the best new binges on Prime Video this weekend and is expected to be one of the most-watched indicates across all streaming platforms in the coming days. With its striking visual style and rich production design based on a brilliant sci-fi franchise, there is no better new series to watch right now with remarkable worldbuilding.

Blade Runner 2099 just arrived with all 8 episodes on Prime Video this Wednesday, November 25. While early reviews are mostly, but not unanimously, positive, earning a Rotten Tomatoes score of 68%, the series' greatest achievement is its rich setting that evolves the original dystopian cyberpunk feel and lore from the original Blade Runner film. Set 50 years after the events of Blade Runner 2049, which is widely considered one of the top sci-fi sequels ever made, Blade Runner 2099 delivers a masterclass in expanding a beloved classic story world. Sources indicate that while both Blade Runner and Blade Runner 2049 established a legendary dystopian world that presents a not-so-distant Los Angeles full of tall skyscrapers, neon lights, and a perpetually unsettling atmosphere, Blade Runner 2099 takes these elements one step further by placing the megacity under Replicant control. After humankind lost a major war to the Replicants, the city's framework and power dynamics have fundamentally changed, adding a fascinating newly introduced thematic layer to the already ominous dystopian landscape. This also takes the initial cyberpunk design of the franchise's LA setting one step further, leaning more heavily into urban decay contrasted by advanced tech. (Related: How to train a newly introduced language model from scr...)

It has been revealed that with Replicants in control of Los Angeles in Blade Runner 2099, humans have devolved into being second-class citizens, and the distinct visual style of LA has also undergone massive changes to align with this extreme power shift. Unlike the Blade Runner films, 2099 features a new signature aesthetic, replacing the dark and rainy weather system with oppressive light and heat, which completely alters the visual identity of the franchise. The 50-year time jump between 2049 and 2099 also introduces new societal customs and technological abilities that demonstrate the Replicants' rise to power, adding even more worldbuilding elements to an already well-established sci-fi universe.

Apart from the Replicants taking over Los Angeles in Prime Video's new Blade Runner series, the most revolutionary change in 2099 is how the series views oppression, power, and justice, which builds on the franchise's legacy of dystopian storytelling. Rather than having humans continue to determine the quality of life over Replicants, the Replicants now wield the authority to categorize humans, which is a direct reversal of the pre-existing core theme in the previous two Blade Runner installments.

2099 could have easily maintained this essential factor in the upcoming Prime Video series, but flipping its core moral question on its head is a testament to the franchise's willingness to broaden its own horizons while still holding firmly to its dystopian roots. Hunter Schafer's Cora, a human protagonist and fugitive, experiences similar forms of oppression and social discrimination that Replicants had suffered for multiple decades, while Michelle Yeoh's Replicant character, Olwen, confronts her own conflict with mortality and her life's value as an AI entity.

Although not all critics have praised Blade Runner 2099 as a whole, most contend that the worldbuilding and franchise expansion are some of its strongest and most appealing qualities. IndieWire writes, "Blade Runner 2099 may not be as exquisite as its predecessors (and it won’t likely win over audiences who aren’t already fans), but it nails the franchise’s aura — and builds one awesome world." Additionally, Empire Magazine notes, "It may sometimes lose narrative momentum but this is world-building par excellence, high on atmosphere, spectacle and food for thought." Ultimately, Blade Runner 2099 may be flawed, but it's a masterclass in crafting an immersive sci-fi world.

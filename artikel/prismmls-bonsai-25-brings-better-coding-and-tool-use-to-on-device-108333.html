According to recent reports, back in July, PrismML claimed its roughly 4 GB, 1-bit Bonsai 27B was the first AI model of its size to run on an iPhone. Now PrismML has released Ternary Bonsai 2.5 27B, an upgrade to the model it introduced in September.

The startup also drew attention when CEO Babak Hassibi told CNBC that Apple was evaluating its technology, as we reported at the time.

In a recent development, the new model is based on Qwen3.8 27B and keeps the same 5.9 GB footprint in its most compact format. This time, PrismML focused on helping the model handle longer tasks, including figuring out what to do when a tool returns an unexpected result or a step fails.

Sources indicate that bonsai 2.5 improved on six of the seven benchmarks PrismML highlights. Its SWE-bench Verified coding score rose from 60.8 to 75.6, while its AppWorld tool-use score jumped from 51.2 to 90.5. LiveCodeBench v6 dipped from 83.8 to 83.0.

What could that look like on a Mac? A coding assistant could inspect a private project, make a change, run the tests, and use the results to revise its fix. A document app could summarize a folder of sensitive files while keeping the processing on the device. (Related: This newly introduced chip survives 1300°F (700°C) and ...)

PrismML says Bonsai 2.5 runs on Mac, iPhone, and iPad through MLX. Its Apple silicon package is about 8.9 GB, and the model card walks through setup on a Mac. The weights are available for free under Apache 2.0.

For a closer look at how the model was compressed and tested, read PrismML’s technical white paper.

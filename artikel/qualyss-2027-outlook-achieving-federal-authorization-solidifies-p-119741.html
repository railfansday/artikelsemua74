Qualys is well positioned in the cybersecurity space. Organizations and government agencies are shifting to platforms that not only identify security vulnerabilities, but also fixes them. That is one of the company's key strengths, which has grown in importance now that hackers can employ artificial intelligence to rapidly attack a system before an organization can respond. As a result, Qualys saw its federal government-related sales outperform expectations in Q3 as agencies consolidated spending onto its platform.

Industry observers note that moreover, while many software companies operate at a loss, Qualys is profitable with an impressive Q3 gross margin of 83%. It sports a fortress balance sheet, exiting the third quarter with total assets of $1.2 billion compared to total liabilities of $536.3 million, and of those liabilities, more than $425 million is deferred revenue, which will transition to sales after services are delivered.

According to recent reports, given the tech giant's many strengths, Qualys is well positioned for years of growth ahead. Yet these factors have contributed to the stock's elevated valuation, with a trailing P/E ratio that's around a high point for the past year. This suggests now is not the best time to buy.

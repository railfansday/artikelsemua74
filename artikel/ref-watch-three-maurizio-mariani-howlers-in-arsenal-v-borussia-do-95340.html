Italian official Maurizio Mariani was involved in some controversy as the man in the middle on the night.

Football Insider have picked out three decisions that Mariani should have done better with during the Champions League clash.

Tempers flared slightly in the 53rd minute as Hincapie and Ryerson came together and the Arsenal defender ended up in a heap on the floor.

Ryerson was not best pleased with whatever went on, as Mariani ignored the incident, but replays showed that Hincapie had feigned injury to get the Dortmund man booked, or maybe even sent off.

His hand had slightly touched the Gunners' defender's face, but Hincapie went down way too theatrically, and should have been sanctioned for simulation by the letter of the law.

Dortmund's players were adamant that they deserved a penalty in the second half after Gyokeres jumped to clear from their corner.

The Arsenal goalscorer clearly used his arm to block the ball while challenging, even if he did not mean to.

Mariani failed to spot the offence and so did the VAR officials, despite it looking like a clear spot-kick as he moved his arm away from his body.

It was Dortmund's turn to get away with a handball offence not long after, although this one was not in the area.

The former Man United midfielder challenged Eberechi Eze in the 78th minute before throwing his arm towards the ball to stop the Gunners' playmaker advancing.

Mariani rightfully gave the hosts a free kick, but somehow decided not to book the Austrian star despite it clearly being a yellow card offence.

In attack, current options William Osula, Matias Fernandez-Pardo, and Yoanne Wissa have combined for just two goals in the Premier League this season, and that has seen the club linked with a move to bring former Brentford star Ivan Toney back to the top-flight.

The England international moved to Saudi Arabia in the summer of 2024 and has been in fine form in the Middle East, and has played under Jaissle while playing for Al-Ahli, scoring 55 goals across two seasons.

His current contract is due to expire in 2028, and there has been speculation that he could look to return to England in the coming transfer window, with a return to St James' Park reportedly on the agenda.

Toney spent three years with Newcastle between 2015 and 2018, but made just four appearances for the club before joining Peterborough United permanently, following several loan spells away.

In a recent development, reports have suggested that the club could be interested in bringing him back in January, but according to The Athletic, senior club officials have played down that possibility and a deal is not expected to happen. The club are, however, looking at alternative striker options for a January deal, including Dion Dren Baljo.

Newcastle need goals, and Ivan Toney scores goals. If the player is open to a move, and a deal can be done, then it would be a complete no-brainer for Newcastle to pursue it and get it over the line.

Not only is he a goalscoring machine, but Toney also knows the Premier League really well, having scored 36 goals in three seasons with Brentford, before making the move to the Middle East. But in addition to that, he already knows the manager, the style of football, an the way the manager works, which should help him hit the ground running.

According to recent reports, wages could be a problem, but the Saudi connection with the club could make that a bit easier too. For the club not to be confident of getting a deal done suggests they have other things lined up, and fans will be desperate for whoever comes in to make an instant impact, so the board and manager simply cannot afford to get it wrong.

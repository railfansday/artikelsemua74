Ahead of Blade Runner 2099's official release, executive producers Silka Louisa and Broderick Johnson revealed the extent to which franchise creator Ridley Scott was involved with the Prime Video series.

Set 50 years after Blade Runner 2049, the show stars Hunter Schafer as Cora, a human pretending to be a replicant, and Michelle Yeoh as Olwen, an aging replicant, in a world where replicants are dominant over humans. The rest of the cast is rounded out by Dimitri Abold and Lewis Gribben. (Related: Scientists discover AI can make humans more creative...)

Sources indicate that in an interview with Maude Garrett for ScreenRant, the Blade Runner 2099 executive producers addressed working with Scott on one of his most iconic properties. "It's a dream come true to know all of the secrets and ask him all the questions and find out what everything really means," Louisa said. (Related: This AI knew the answers but didn’t understand the ques...)

Recent findings suggest that however, she clarify that some secrets have to stay close to her chest. "The one thing I don't tell anyone because, I will warn you, you think you want to know, but you don't want to know," the writer explained. "The power of never really being sure, the ambiguity is that, is so tantalizing. And somebody actually just telling you definitively, "It's X, it's Y..." It just kind of neuters the magic trick." A key factor in Blade Runner's appeal over the decades has been its ambiguity over whether Rick Deckard (portrayed by Harrison Ford in Scott's Blade Runner) is himself a replicant, a question Louisa sidesteps because "everyone tries" to get a solid answer from her.

Sources indicate that for Johnson, his working relationship with Scott goes back "now a good 15 years," and he still recalls the open attitude the filmmaker had to expanding the world of Blade Runner. "When we first got the IP and went and sat with him," said Johnson. "He told us in that first meeting that Blade Runner was never meant to be a one-off exercise, which we were thrilled to hear. That collaboration has gone on since then.".

According to Johnson, Scott's collaborative approach very much extended to Louisa's vision for Blade Runner 2099. "He responded to Silka's original idea very positively as well," he continued. "He's been a part of that iteration from the [start], deciding whether it's a hundred years or it's 50 years or whatever. He was part of that whole thing, dreaming with her at that point, down to giving notes on the edits and whatnot.".

Scott himself previously discussed his involvement with the series, somewhat contradicting the assertion he was heavily involved. "I can’t blame my lawyers and my agents at that time, but somehow we let ‘Blade Runner’ go," he noted earlier this year. "And when you’re not the author of the actual written word, you don’t have a piece.".

Blade Runner 2099 debuts on Prime Video on November 25, 2026.

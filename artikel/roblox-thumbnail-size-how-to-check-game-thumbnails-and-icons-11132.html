Checking the file dimensions and checking whether the image reads well are two different jobs.

In a platformer, I would start with an angled view that reveals the jump in the thumbnail and a close crop of the character reaching for the ledge in the icon. The two can share the same colors and character even when the framing changes.

Open the candidates side by side at around 240 × 135 for thumbnails and 150 × 150 for icons. Treat the thumbnail width as a local comparison setting. Card widths vary across devices and surfaces. If you have to enlarge the image to spot the player or see where the jump ends, adjust the framing before fine-tuning the lighting or typography.

Sources indicate that look at a neutral-background preview and a dark one if you can. Orange lava can provide good separation from a blue character silhouette, while a bright outline may disappear against a pale background. If you add title text, check it at the same small size. A two-word title that stays legible is easier to judge than a longer line you need to zoom in to read.

Once you choose an image, check the actual file you intend to upload. This small browser example returns the decoded dimensions and compares them against the two export targets above. Pass it a File from an image picker. It works locally in the browser.

A 1280 × 720 image returns sameRatio: true and matchesTarget: false. It can be re-exported on the 1920 × 1080 canvas if you have a higher-resolution original. A 1600 × 1200 image fails the ratio check. Open a crop preview and reframe around the subject before exporting. Stretching it to fill a 16:9 canvas would change the proportions of everything in the scene.

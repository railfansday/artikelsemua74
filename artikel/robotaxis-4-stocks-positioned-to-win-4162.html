Recent findings suggest that robotaxis have released in a growing number of metro areas across the U.S. While total market penetration remains low, experts are bullish about how rapidly the build-out will proceed.

A survey of industry insiders conducted by McKinsey states, "While [Level 4, or highly automated] robo-taxis are now available in the first cities in the United States and China, the global rollout of robo-taxis is now expected to become reality at a large scale in 2030.". Private autonomous vehicles aren't predicted to arrive at scale until after robotaxi fleets are scaled. up. McKinsey also says: "Overall, experts expect that robo-taxis will be the first commercial application for L4 in mobility -- not privately owned cars. L4 urban pilots for private passenger cars are expected to be pushed out from 2030 to 2032, and fully autonomous trucking is expected to reach viability by 2032, not 2031.". In a recent development, note that this survey of experts believes that robotaxis will become a reality at a large scale by 2030. That suggests a mature robotaxi market in the U.S. will be realized over the next three to four years.

How big could this market become? Tesla (TSLA -0.83%) bull Cathie Wood of ARK Invest expects robotaxis to become an $8 trillion to $10 trillion market globally over the long term. Estimates from Morgan Stanley are more conservative, calling for a $1 trillion global market by 2040. Still, the bank believes the industry will "reach break-even by 2028 and expand significantly over the following years, with approximately 2.5 million robotaxis in service worldwide by 2035.".

How can investors profit from the rollout? There are two main strategies, each with two promising stocks worth considering.

NASDAQ · As of 9:55 AM ET 11/20/26.

Take a look at the market cap of Uber Technologies (UBER +3.35%). The manufacturer's $140 billion valuation is significantly larger than many automakers' stocks. That's true even though the company doesn't manufacture a single vehicle in its ride-hailing fleet.

It has been revealed that instead of producing cars, Uber operates a software layer that coordinates their use. The company's ride-sharing platform presently holds a dominant market share in the U.S., with impressive margins and rising free cash flow.

Uber has invested aggressively in the self-driving revolution, taking direct equity stakes in many automakers. But at a high level, the company remains fairly asset-light. The vast majority of ride-share seekers in the U.S. already look to Uber first for a ride. Autonomous vehicles looking to maximize utilization rates will almost certainly add their vehicles to Uber's huge platform.

Sources indicate that the exception will be Tesla, which plans to operate its own ride-sharing platform. That strategy makes sense given that it has developed its own self-driving technology and can produce its own vehicles at scale.

If you want to own the software layer of the autonomous vehicle revolution, Uber and Tesla should top your watch list.

Industry observers note that there's an old saying worth noting: During a gold rush, sell shovels. That way, you'll profit even if the rush only discovers fool's gold.

What are the shovels of the robotaxi revolution? The vehicles themselves. EV makers Rivian (RIVN -2.35%) and Lucid Group (LCID -0.36%) have positioned themselves accordingly.

Industry observers note that both companies have already signed deals with Uber to supply tens of thousands of robotaxis. Lucid and Rivian's long-standing tech-first approach finally appears to be paying off, even if total volumes remain low. Some industry insiders believe other robotaxi operators -- including Alphabet's Waymo, which cannot manufacture robotaxis in-house -- will add to the growing corporate-customer lists of Rivian and Lucid.

Of the four stocks on this list, Rivian and Uber intrigue me most. Uber's valuation is significantly more attractive than Tesla's. The organization has a free cash flow yield of nearly 7.5%. Rivian, meanwhile, has more capital at its disposal than Lucid, with more-mature production capabilities -- a key benefit for robotaxi operators looking to build scale.

Sources indicate that investors should keep in mind that the robotaxi story won't play out over months. It will likely take years for Rivian, Uber, Lucid, and Tesla to accumulate meaningful revenue from their robotaxi exposure. But with a trillion-dollar opportunity, all four stocks have a clearly defined growth runway in robotaxis.

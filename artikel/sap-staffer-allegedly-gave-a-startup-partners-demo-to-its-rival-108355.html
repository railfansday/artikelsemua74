An SAP employee allegedly passed confidential material from British AI startup Conduct, an SAP partner, to Berlin rival Qorelo, then joined Qorelo, Bloomberg reported. SAP did not tell Conduct after a whistleblower flagged it in October; SAP claims the parties deny the claims.

Recent findings suggest that an SAP employee allegedly passed confidential material from Conduct, a British AI startup that SAP works with and has invested in, to a rival, Bloomberg reported on Wednesday. It has been revealed that the employee allegedly gave a Conduct demo and other documents to Berlin-based Qorelo, which also partners with SAP. After the complaint, SAP banned him from its offices. He joined Qorelo in November, according to Bloomberg.

The whistleblower report claims he added about 800 contacts to Qorelo’s customer database while still at SAP. It says he also shared a recording of a call between SAP, Conduct and their customer DHL.

Conduct learned of the matter this month from a whistleblower, and its lawyers then contacted SAP.

“In this case, we contacted the relevant parties, and they have denied the allegations,” SAP mentioned in a statement.

Qorelo said it takes the claims very seriously and is reviewing the employee’s move from SAP. Conduct declined to comment, and the former employee did not respond. (Related: Scientists are seriously asking if bees and ChatGPT are...)

Conduct was founded in 2024 by three former Palantir staff. It raised $60M this year with SAP among the investors, and helps companies move off older SAP systems, a market Qorelo also serves.

According to recent reports, the claims could strain the startup ties SAP is using to rebuild around AI. The company has also frozen most hiring to free up cash for that push.

An SAP employee allegedly passed confidential material from Conduct, a British AI startup that SAP works with and has invested in, to a rival, Bloomberg reported on Wednesday. SAP did not tell Conduct after a whistleblower flagged the matter in October, people familiar with it told Bloomberg. (Related: Simple considerations for simple people building fancy ...)

The employee allegedly gave a Conduct demo and other documents to Berlin-based Qorelo, which also partners with SAP. After the complaint, SAP banned him from its offices. He joined Qorelo in November, according to Bloomberg.

In a recent development, the whistleblower report claims he added about 800 contacts to Qorelo’s customer database while still at SAP. It claims he also shared a recording of a call between SAP, Conduct and their customer DHL.

Conduct learned of the matter this month from a whistleblower, and its lawyers then contacted SAP.

“In this case, we contacted the relevant parties, and they have denied the allegations,” SAP said in a statement.

Qorelo said it takes the claims very seriously and is reviewing the employee’s move from SAP. Conduct declined to comment, and the former employee did not respond.

In a recent development, conduct was founded in 2024 by three former Palantir staff. It raised $60M this year with SAP among the investors, and helps companies move off older SAP systems, a market Qorelo also serves.

In a recent development, the claims could strain the startup ties SAP is using to rebuild around AI. The manufacturer has also frozen most hiring to free up cash for that push.

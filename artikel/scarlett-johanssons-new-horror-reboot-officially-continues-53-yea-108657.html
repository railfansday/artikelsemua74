Scarlett Johansson's The Exorcist: Martyrs will be pulling no punches.

Industry observers note that johansson is teaming up with Mike Flanagan for the latest reboot of the classic horror franchise, starring as Latest York City homicide detective Julie Miller as she is pulled into a dark murder investigation with apparent ties to a former Satanic serial killer she helped stop. Also starring Chiwetel Ejiofor, Diane Lane, Jacobi Jupe and Sasha Lane, among many others, The Exorcist: Martyrs is currently on track for a March 12 release date. Now, with just over two months until it hits theaters, the Motion Picture Association has officially given The Exorcist: Martyrs an R rating, continuing the horror franchise's 53-year streak of landing the mature rating. The site further explains that the new reboot's rating stems from its "bloody violent content, grisly images, language, and some sexual references.".

Recent findings suggest that in addition to the franchise's tradition, The Exorcist: Martyrs securing an R-rating also continues Flanagan's streak of more mature films in the director's chair. Across his eight released films, only two have landed a PG-13 rating from the MPA, those being Netflix's Before I Wake and Blumhouse's Ouija: Origin of Evil. His TV highlights have similarly landed firmly in the TV-MA rating, generally stemming from explicit language and, in recent years, some bloodier set pieces. Interestingly, while it keeps the series' 53-year streak alive, The Exorcist: Martyr's R-rating does immediately stand out from its predecessors with its promise of "grisly images." Though the horror franchise is well-known for its more visceral material, the only other movie to explicitly list its gruesome imagery as part of its R-rating was that of the infamous Exorcist: The Beginning prequel film. See the chart below for the full franchise list of ratings and given reasons from the MPA:.

Strong language and disturbing images.

Exorcist II: The Heretic (1977).

Strong violence and gore, disturbing images and rituals, and for language including some sexual dialogue.

Dominion: Prequel to The Exorcist (2005).

Strong violence and disturbing images.

Some violent content, disturbing images, language and sexual references.

Bloody violent content, grisly images, language, and some sexual references.

The first Exorcist: Martyrs trailer already showcased Flanagan leaning into more gruesome territory for his reboot of the classic horror series. The crime scenes Johansson's character inspects come complete with dead bodies mutilated in different ritualistic manners, while another sequence saw Julie inspecting a barrel later with an unknown entity emerging and coated in blood. As Flanagan himself teased to ScreenRant, he was not looking to pull any punches with his film, calling it "very, very bloody":.

Mike Flanagan: Yeah, I think the only thing that comes close to the amount of blood is we did melt that orgy in The Fall of the House of Usher in acid. That was pretty bloody, but that was funny, I thought. [Chuckles] As dark as it was, this is vicious, and that isn't a muscle I get to flex very often. It's very disturbing, and that was part of the fun of it. This whole story just felt very unsafe from the beginning, and yeah, that isn't a gear I go toward frequently. I like hope and heart and humanism, and there are moments of that if you squint, but this is a dark story, and it is scary. So yeah, that was very exciting for me, too.

Industry observers note that with Flanagan also having been largely inspired by the cult favorite Exorcist III for his take on the franchise, it will be interesting to see if The Exorcist: Martyrs finds itself leaning closer to the 1990 film's atmosphere than the visceral nature of its 2004 successor. Given early test screenings, per the director, have been glowing, as has the response to the trailer thus far, it does at least seem like this push into hard-R territory is going to give viewers an unforgettable experience.

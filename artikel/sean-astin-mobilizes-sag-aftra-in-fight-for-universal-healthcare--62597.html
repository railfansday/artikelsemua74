As many SAG-AFTRA members struggle to qualify for health insurance, the union is taking the concern to the U.S. government.

On Friday, Screen Actors Guild President Sean Astin announced the President’s Healthcare Task Force to address the “broken healthcare system” while several actors have faced losing their insurance under the union’s minimum eligibility requirements. According to recent reports, “This is a critical moment, where both our members and the public at large can no longer wait for relief from a broken healthcare system,” he mentioned in a statement. “Our SAG-AFTRA Health Strategy is healthy and strong, but a substantial majority of our members do not qualify. For our members who don’t qualify, there is very little that can be done to provide coverage.”. (Related: Latest Update: VAST notes petabyte SSDs will outgrow to...)

Astin added, “SAG-AFTRA has supported efforts to expand healthcare for all through the AFL-CIO. This task force seeks a new level of participation in the national movement to expand care for everyone. We will actively engage with policymakers, gather allies and provide a structured way for our members to participate formally in the process. The President’s Healthcare Task Force will be an instrument of support in the movement for universal healthcare.” Watch on Deadline.

The SAG-AFTRA National Board voted unanimously on Friday to form the Task Force, which seeks to mobilize SAG-AFTRA’s collective strength and public influence to fight for universal access to affordable, comprehensive and reliable health care.

SAG’s new initiative comes amid financial scrutiny for the union following National Executive Director Duncan Crabtree-Ireland’s recent contract renewal, after his salary surpassed $1M in 2023.

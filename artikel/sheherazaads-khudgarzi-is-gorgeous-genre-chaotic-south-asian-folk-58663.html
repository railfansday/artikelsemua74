Sheherazaad started making ripples in the music world a few years ago with her EP Qasr, which was produced by Grammy Award winner Arooj Aftab. Now she’s back with Khudgarzi, her reverb-drenched folk-noir debut LP that’s primarily sung in Urdu.

It has been revealed that the instrumentation on Khudgarzi draws on traditional South Asian music, including Carnatic and Hindustani styles, but also dabbles in Arabic folk traditions, as well as modern Western electronic music. Trip-hop flourishes and Spaghetti Western guitar brush up against tanbur and ghatam. Sheherazaad’s stunning voice sits atop dripping percussion and seasick drones singing about the plight of “The Selfish Woman.”. (Related: Thousands of vulnerable fridges patched against cyberat...)

According to recent reports, for Sheherazaad, the character of the selfish woman isn’t the villain, it’s a charge leveled at women who didn’t conform to societal norms, “eccentric women whose desire for agency was often met with an awful end.” Even if you can’t understand most of the lyrics, the atmosphere tells the story clearly enough. From the barely simmering anger of “Registaan” to the bleak repetition of “Qatil” and the fantastical yearning of “Zeher,” Khudgarzi is clearly the work of a woman exploring what it means to be a woman, the lasting legacy of colonialism, and the perpetual threats of a patriarchal society. (Related: This AI knew the answers but didn’t understand the ques...)

Khudgarzi is alternately a funeral dirge and a celebration of femininity. Sheherazaad owes a lot of the album’s ability to navigate these various themes and moods to her impressive production work, top-notch mixing by J. Valleau, and the cast of over 30 “genre-chaotic” virtuosic musicians including Ria Modak, Sarathy Korwar, Jay Chakravorty, Shunya, and Qasim Naqvi. The instrumentation never distracts, though. It’s not showy, and all emulsifies around Sheherazaad’s vocals in a shawl that feels equal parts comforting and oppressive.

Khudgarzi is available now on Bandcamp and most major streaming platforms, including YouTube Music, Spotify, Apple Music, Deezer, and Qobuz.

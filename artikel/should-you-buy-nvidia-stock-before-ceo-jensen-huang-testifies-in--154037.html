Jensen Huang may be back as CEO of Nvidia (NVDA +0.80%), but the chip giant's worries are far from over, as regulators and lawmakers continue to investigate whether Nvidia breached export controls and whether Huang's relationship with a Singaporean data center executive may have posed national security risks. Huang and other Nvidia executives are set to testify before Congress next week on the above concerns, which the entire stock market will be watching. (Related: Steam is pitting 10 years of Game of the Year winners a...)

It has been revealed that on Dec. 1, Huang will testify before the U.S. House of Representatives' Foreign Affairs and Intelligence Committee, and the House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party. Nvidia's General Counsel Tim Teter will also testify before the same committees on Dec. 2. Both hearings will be private, with transcripts not expected to be published publicly for some time.

Industry observers note that but on Dec. 3, Huang will testify at a live hearing before the U.S. Senate Banking Committee, an event that will be closely monitored by all investors. Should investors buy Nvidia stock before these hearings?

While President Donald Trump has come out in support of Huang and claims to have played a role in his reinstatement, the hearings will help reveal how other lawmakers feel about Huang and what's going on with Nvidia.

According to recent reports, "The market volatility and nervousness is centered on a number of issues that the Jensen soap opera has highlighted front and center," Yorkville Ives analyst and Managing Partner Dan Ives wrote in a recent research note, according to Business Insider. "Next week Jensen carries the weight of the entire tech sector and AI Revolution thesis when he marches into DC.".

Huang's reinstatement as CEO led to a swift surge in the stock, but since then, shares have been pressured by lingering investor concerns about the company.

Multiple reports suggest that a woman Huang had an affair with, named Alice Huang (no relation), a former executive at the Singaporean data center firm MegaSpeed, may have facilitated a network that diverted advanced Nvidia chips to Chinese firms.

NASDAQ · As of 1:15 PM ET 11/27/26.

It has been revealed that investors are trying to understand how much prior demand for Nvidia's chips, if any, may have been driven by similar sources. Since the scandal came to light, the U.S. Commerce Department has moved to close loopholes and tighten export controls that may have allowed Chinese firms to access Nvidia chips remotely.

Industry observers note that the Commerce Department is now also requiring companies in select countries, such as Singapore and Indonesia, to obtain a license before transferring or reexporting advanced chips from U.S. companies.

In his note, Ives said he believes the impact on revenue resulting from such export controls is "a containable 15% to 20% at most." Still, that's a significant amount, and while some of it is priced in, Nvidia stock could certainly face more pressure if it believed future demand wouldn't be as strong.

Given that Wall Street analysts like Ives are already contemplating what would happen to Nvidia's revenue if increased export restrictions lower demand, or some sources of demand abroad are closed off, it's clear the market is pricing in at least some of the potential impact.

Still, Wall Street consensus estimates expect Nvidia to earn $16.61 per share on an adjusted basis for its fiscal year 2028 ending in late January 2028. That means Nvidia trades at just 9x forward earnings, so the valuation is attractive.

But as we've seen through this whole debacle, things can change in a hurry, so I would never tell investors to buy a stock based on a near-term event like a Congressional hearing.

According to recent reports, with Nvidia trading at a cheap level, I think investors can purchase a position to hold long term. But I do not believe that the majority of a traditional stock portfolio should be concentrated in Nvidia or AI stocks, as much uncertainty remains.

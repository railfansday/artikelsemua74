Sources indicate that siemens is developing an AI-powered robot to unpack incoming parts at its electronics factory in Erlangen, Germany, the firm said on Tuesday. It was one of several AI tools Siemens demonstrated at the SPS automation fair in Nuremberg.

It has been revealed that deliveries vary so much in box type and size that fixed robot programs cannot handle them, so unpacking is still mostly done by hand. In Siemens’ system, cameras watch the workspace and robot foundation models, AI trained for physical tasks, decide the next steps. The robot opens cartons and takes out reels of components.

The plant receives 80 to 200 packages a day, trade site All About Industries reported. The AI learned from people steering the robot remotely, and it runs on Siemens industrial PCs with Nvidia chips. (Related: New York hosts spy lookalike contest inspired by Nvidia...)

Siemens has not said when the robot will be ready. The same plant ran an eight-hour humanoid robot trial with Nvidia in April. Similar AI is already in paying customers’ warehouses: Warsaw’s Nomagic indicates it halved how often its robots need human help.

It has been revealed that siemens also highlighted AI agents for planning. Its Autonomous Production Planner uses several agents to draw up production schedules, then tests each one in a digital copy of the factory. Siemens expects it to cut manual planning work by 70% and costs by up to 20%.

“The next productivity leap will come from automating tasks that were impossible to automate before,” noted Rainer Brehm, president of automation at Siemens.

Rivals are pushing into factory AI too. In June, Schneider Electric agreed to buy industrial-AI firm Cognite for $3.1B.

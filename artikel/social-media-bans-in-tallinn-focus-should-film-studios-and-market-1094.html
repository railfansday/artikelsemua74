📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Australia has banned social media for people under the age of 16. A similar ban is set to hit the U.K. in 2027. And the European Union is talking about potential similar initiatives. So what is the impact on the film industry?

It has been revealed that after all, Gen Z and Gen Alpha audiences have been key box-office drivers, and short social media videos and memes have helped make some movies into must-see cultural moments. So, what happens when young audiences are blocked from social media discovery? And how could these restrictions reshape the discovery and engagement with cinema? Or put differently: Should studios and marketers panic? Given all these questions, the Just Film Industry Days program of the Industry@Tallinn & Baltic Event (ITBE) market featured a well-attended Friday session entitled “The Newly introduced Underground: Film Discovery in the Age of Social Media Restrictions.”.

In a recent development, fergus Navaratnam-Blair, a researcher covering trends in media, entertainment, and technology who manages thought leadership for National Research Group, stated that the U.K. social media restrictions would be “more far-reaching” than the ones in Australia. (Related: Senate panel vote on National Science Foundation nomine...)

Of course, banning social media usage and time “could create more time for kids and teens to spend at the cinema,” he said. And indeed, a U.K. survey found that 42 percent of kids said they may spend more time watching films in movie theaters once the ban hits. Navaratnam-Blair also shared data showing that 25 percent of kids in Australia said that they have actually watched more movies in cinemas since the ban in the country started, even though the survey didn’t detail how many more.

Also, in-home entertainment experienced a bigger boost, with 42 percent of respondents mentioning more home watching of movies and 39 percent mentioning more TV viewing at home.

What kinds of movie-going experiences will draw young audiences in an age of social media bans? Navaratnam-Blair had this to offer: “In a post-social world, it will be even more important to offer younger audiences truly unique live experiences,” including premium large-format screenings, 2D or virtual reality screenings and screenings featuring special effects, such as wind, lights and seat movement.

One key question is, of course, if bans are effective. The expert shared that research has found that around 70 percent of Australians under the age of 16 still have social media access, according to their parents, whether through platforms that are not banned, such as Roblox or Discord, or by setting up accounts via VPNs or under different people’s names.

In a recent development, all that is likely a blueprint for how things will be in the U.K. and potential future bans going into effect elsewhere. Concluded Navaratnam-Blair: “I do think there’s going to be a subversion of the ban itself.”.

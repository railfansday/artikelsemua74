In a recent development, south Korea’s stablecoin draft is giving banks control without shutting fintechs out. The Financial Services Commission wants won-backed stablecoin issuers structured around bank-majority consortiums, but technology companies could still hold the largest individual stake. That’s a carefully split arrangement, and the final rules aren’t settled yet. (Related: This fresh chip could slash data center energy waste...)

The FSC unveiled its proposed framework on November 24. Banks would collectively own more than 50% of an issuing consortium, while detailed shareholder requirements would come later through an enforcement decree.

Industry observers note that tiger Research outlined one possible structure: four banks holding 15% each, a fintech with 30%, and other investors sharing the remaining 10%. Banks would control 60% collectively, despite the fintech holding the biggest individual stake.

In a recent development, it’s an example, not a mandatory ownership formula. An unresolved question is whether the Banking Act’s existing 15% ownership limit for banks investing in nonfinancial companies would apply unchanged. If so, at least four banks could be needed.

In a recent development, ownership is only part of the competition. Fintechs may gain influence through payment networks, merchant access and practical uses for won-backed tokens.

The draft doesn’t identify which companies will secure those partnerships or attract transaction volume. International connections could matter too, particularly links between domestic won payments and global dollar liquidity.

Recent findings suggest that the proposal requires domestic issuers to obtain authorization and hold at least 5 billion won in equity capital. Reserves must cover at least 100% of outstanding tokens, with redemption requests met at equivalent monetary value without delay.

For now, the South Korea stablecoin draft remains a legislative proposal awaiting National Assembly consideration. Banks may get collective control, but fintechs still have room to compete. The final ownership and licensing rules will decide how much.

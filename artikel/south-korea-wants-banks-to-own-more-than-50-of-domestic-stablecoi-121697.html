📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

South Korea’s financial regulator proposed limiting initial domestic stablecoin issuance to licensed groups in which banks collectively own more than 50%.

Under the blueprint, a fintech or technology company could still hold the largest individual stake, as the Financial Services Commission (FSC) outlined on Nov. 24.

The legislative proposal calls stablecoins digital payment tokens and defines them as assets pegged to one currency and used mainly for payments. Issuers would put the tokens into circulation at an equivalent value in the underlying currency and would be barred from paying holders interest.

A domestic issuer would need an FSC license and at least 5 billion won in equity capital. Applicants would have to show they have the staff and facilities to operate, submit a viable business plan, and explain how they would prevent conflicts of interest.

Major shareholders and executives would also have to meet eligibility requirements.

The issuer would also need FSC approval for its issuance proposal, including how many tokens it intended to concern, the maximum it could issue, how it would manage reserves, and how holders could redeem their tokens.

When a holder asked to redeem, the issuer would have to pay the equivalent value in currency without delay.

To meet those requests, issuers would keep reserves worth at least 100% of outstanding issuance with a reserve manager such as a bank. The proposal would shield the reserves from third-party seizure or setoff.

Under the proposed redemption rules, the institution managing the reserves could pay holders directly if the issuer went bankrupt, lost its license, or could no longer handle normal issuance and redemption after a disruption such as a hack.

The FSC notes holders would have priority when the reserve manager makes those payments.

Recent findings suggest that overseas issuers would have a separate route to offer issuance and redemption in South Korea. An issuer would need adequate supervision in its home jurisdiction and a Korean branch with sufficient operating funds. It would have to register with the FSC and file separately for its foreign-issued tokens.

Platforms could only list tokens with the applicable approval or filing. The same requirement would apply to digital asset firms that trade, broker, or hold those tokens in custody.

The FSC said it would support deliberations in the National Assembly, while a later presidential decree would set detailed shareholder rules for domestic stablecoin issuers.

Asia-Pacific markets rose Thursday, tracking Wall Street gains overnight, with tech stocks rebounding from steep losses in the prior session.

It has been revealed that investors appear to have looked past a fresh set of U.S. tariffs unveiled by the White House on Wednesday, targeting more than a dozen major trading partners, including China and the European Union.

South Korea's Kospi gained 1.73%, while the small-cap Kosdaq index advanced 2.32% after the country's central bank hiked benchmark lending rates by an expected 25 basis points, as inflation continues to run above target while a booming economy allows it room to tighten policy.

The central bank raised rates to 3.25%, a 2-year high, in its final meeting of the year, data on the Bank of Korea's website demonstrated.

Japan's Nikkei 225 rose 1.21% while the Topix edged 0.71% higher. Australia's S&P/ASX 200 was up 0.15%. Hong Kong's Hang Seng index rose 1.46% while mainland China's CSI 300 added 0.12%.

Asian technology stocks rebounded sharply Thursday, led by Japanese chip-related companies.

According to recent reports, softBank Group surged more than 11%, while chip-testing equipment maker Advantest rose 7.3%. Lasertec and Disco both gained more than 6%, while Tokyo Electron climbed 4.6%.

Sources indicate that in South Korea, memory chip giants SK Hynix and Samsung Electronics advanced more than 4% and 2.1%, respectively. The gains followed a bruising session Wednesday amid renewed concerns over the sustainability of artificial intelligence spending. Cryptocurrencies also traded higher, with bitcoin rising 1.58% to $60,944.60, while ether gained 1.15% to $1,702.96.

The gains come amid lingering concerns over the outlook for digital assets, with the co-founder of now-shuttered crypto exchange BitMEX Arthur Hayes telling CNBC on Tuesday that bitcoin could test $50,000 if the Nasdaq continues to slide.

Meanwhile, fresh U.S. inflation data reinforced expectations of further monetary tightening by the Federal Reserve. The personal consumption expenditures price index rose 3.8% year on year in October, accelerating from 3.7% in September, while core inflation came in slightly above expectations at 3.3%.

It has been revealed that overnight in the U.S., the Dow Jones Industrial Average erased an 1,100-point plunge on Wednesday, advancing 164.25 points, or 0.33%, to end at 50,182.95. The Nasdaq Composite, however, fell 0.62% to 23,585.80, while the S&P 500 shed 0.25% to close at 7,182.78.

U.S. markets will be closed Thursday for the Thanksgiving holiday and will have a shortened trading session on Friday.

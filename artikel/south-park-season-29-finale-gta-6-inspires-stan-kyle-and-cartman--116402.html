In the sixth and final episode of “South Park” Season 29, South America has once again been hit with major power outages — but this time it’s serious, considering “Grand Theft Auto VI” has finally been released, which means the residents of the mountain town will stop at nothing to restore their Xboxes and PlayStations and continue playing the long-awaited video game.

Industry observers note that “Damn it, Mayor! We have waited over a decade for this game to come out,” proclaims one angry South American resident at the top of the episode. “Do you have any idea how ‘Grand Theft Auto VI’ works? If it shuts up while it’s saving or during a mission, you lose everything.” The issue, though, is that the South American government has made a deal with a group of billionaire weenies, as was introduced in the season’s second episode, who are using the town’s energy to power their data centers. The town’s politicians have no idea what to do about the glitch, but luckily for them, a group of angry townspeople barge into South American City Hall and proclaim they know who’s behind the outages: the Chinese. More specifically, the outages are the result of Chinese cyberattacks. Relieved, the town’s politicians let them believe this is the case — “Yes, you figured it out. It’s definitely cyber attacks by the Chinese” — as they begin to search for a Chinese spy who must be living in South America. (Related: Hyperparameter Search with Transformers and Ray Tune...)

Recent findings suggest that but Butters has known the billionaires were to blame since the beginning, and confides in Stan, Kyle and Cartman. Oh, and a zombified Kenny (who got blown up by an e-bike during the season’s premiere) also makes a few appearances. Turns out, Kenny’s spirit can’t move on until the group figures out what’s going on and exposes all the corruption in South America — great timing for them, as they’ve all learned about the data centers and are ready to make a change. The gang takes this revelation to City Hall, where the politicians explain how it all began “with those damn e-bikes” and fear that the group of kids is going to blow their secret deal with the billionaires.

Industry observers note that but it wasn’t an e-bike that killed Kenny, claims a still shrunken Randy who barges into City Hall. “We all did. We all share in the blame equally… When they changed the name of our town, we let it change us. We used force, took bribes, looked the other way, because we let them change South Park.”.

In the end, the mayor of South American addresses the town and owns up to her mistakes — kind of.

“I owe you all an apology. Since the town changed its name, I’ve taken bribes. I’ve looked the other way, and I did make a deal with billionaires,” she says. “But I’ve learned that even though we must build data centers because there is a very real threat from the Chinese in the AI arms race, we can’t let that change who we are.”.

And so, the billionaire weenies help the town “do something we should have done a long time ago”: shoot Mr. Kim into space and out of South America. And with that, another season of “South Park” (and the first season with the new name of “South America”) comes to a close.

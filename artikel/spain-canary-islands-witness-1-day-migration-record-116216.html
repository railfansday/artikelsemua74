The Spanish Canary Islands witnessed a one-day migration record on Tuesday, with over 2,100 people arriving to the region in over 24 hours.

Most of the migrants landed on the the island of El Hierro, according to Spanish authorities.

According to recent reports, "The Canaries can't take it anymore. We're experiencing an unprecedented migration pressure," President of the Canary Islands Fernando Clavijo wrote on X. He requested an urgent meeting with European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen to discuss an immediate EU response.

Around 100 minors arrived on Tuesday, with a baby also born during the trip from Africa to the islands. (Related: The Age of Machine Learning As Code Has Arrived...)

Industry observers note that the Canaries, a tourist destination whose shortest gap with northwest Africa is around 100 kilometers (around 62 miles), tends to see more migrant arrivals in the fall when the waters are calmer. The waters become rougher in the winter due to storms and swells. (Related: Industry News: My Journey to a serverless transformers ...)

Sources indicate that earlier this month, nearly 1,600 migrants from West Africa were saved and taken to the Canary Islands in a previous record. Over 26,000 migrants came to the Canaries this year so far — with half of that figure in November alone.

The route from Africa to the Canaries is one of the deadliest in the world for migrants.

Industry observers note that boats taking off for the islands sometimes drift out to open sea, with the migrants onboard facing starvation, dehydration and lack of medical care. Human smugglers frequently organize the trips and abuse the migrants.

The spike in migration to the Canaries comes as Spain will hold a snap election on Sunday.

Migration, along with housing, will be one of the topics at the forefront of voters' minds when they cast their ballots.

Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez, who belongs to the center-left Socialist Party, has faced backlash from the opposition regarding his tolerant attitude towards migration. The Spanish government earlier this year enacted a blueprint to legalize up to 500,000 undocumented migrants.

In late July, over 80,000 migrants crossed from Morocco into the Spanish North African enclave of Ceuta, triggering a crisis for the Sanchez government. Countries such as Germany and Italy tightened or extended border controls due to the influx of migrants into Ceuta.

The far-right Vox party, which could receive its best-ever result in upcoming election, has called for Sanchez and his ministers to be prosecuted for treason over the Ceuta crisis. The conservative Popular Party could form a coalition government with Vox after the election.

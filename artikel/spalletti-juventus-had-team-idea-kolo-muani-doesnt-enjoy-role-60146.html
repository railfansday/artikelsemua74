Luciano Spalletti explains what changed for Juventus at half-time to crush Venezia 4-0, admitting Randal Kolo Muani ‘does not enjoy’ being a centre-forward, but will keep pushing anyway.

The Bianconeri were jeered off the field by their fans at half-time, so there were few signs that they would emerge 4-0 winners against Davide Nicola’s Venezia. (Related: Thousands of vulnerable fridges patched against cyberat...)

Kerim Alajbegovic went off injured during the break, and Nick Woltemade made an immediate impact off the bench, opening the scoring after a scuffed Weston McKennie effort. (Related: Latest Update: South Korea busts 54 students cheating o...)

It opened the floodgates, followed by a Teun Koopmeiners strike, Juan Cabal header, and Randal Kolo Muani penalty which the ex-PSG striker had also earned.

“They came on for the second half, making choices, taking decisions, with greater fluidity in the passing, trying to have a team idea,” Spalletti told DAZN Italia.

“In the first half, we gave the ball away too cheaply, there was nobody moving from their positions, nobody following the flow of the pass, so it was fundamental the players recognised we were missing something, which we provided in the second half.”.

The victory made it seem a far more confident performance than it was, and the question marks remain over this Juventus side, especially when they don’t have Woltemade and Kolo Muani starting together upfront.

Notably, Woltemade handed over the penalty kick to his teammate, confirming their strong rapport both on and off the field.

It is surely undeniable now that Kolo Muani is much better on the left as a support striker than as a centre-forward.

“I agree Kolo Muani feels more at ease on the left, but I need someone who can be a centre-forward with all these games. When Nico Gonzalez returns, I will have on the right Nico, Conceicao and Zhegrova, on the left I have Alajbegovic, Yildiz and Boga,” added Spalletti.

Sources indicate that “So I must stimulate him, show that he can do that in the centre, but he has to create that space with his attitude, so he can run into those spaces. When he receives the ball with a man behind him, he has shown it is not something he enjoys, but he can get into that position rather than just standing and being ready to receive the ball.”.

Edon Zhegrova indicated the best and worst of his abilities today, both providing assists and giving the ball away to spark counter-attacks.

“Zhegrova is an incredible player, because at times he does things even he cannot explain. He has this geniality and when it looks impossible, he emerges with a moment that is not written in any tactical books.

“Yet, sometimes he wanders around, checks out of the game, and gives the ball away, like the time when Bremer had to come and clean up. Having this attitude within football becomes fundamental, because just a couple of moments aren’t enough, you need to be consistent throughout the game.”.

This was Spalletti’s 600th Serie A career match as a coach, and he chose to joke about the constant speculation over his potential dismissal at Juventus.

“With everything people say, what is your career objective after hitting 600 games? Reaching the 601st is the answer, because anything can happen in football.”.

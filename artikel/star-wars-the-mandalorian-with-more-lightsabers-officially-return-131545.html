Star Wars' 'The Mandalorian With More Lightsabers' Officially Returns In 2027 & It Changes Everything

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Star Wars' Mandalorian-era, lightsaber-filled show returns next year, and it could change everything for the galaxy far, far away. Since its 2019 debut, The Mandalorian has been one of Star Wars' most important stories, not only revitalizing the franchise after the divisive reaction to the sequel trilogy but also exploring a relatively upcoming era of the timeline. Before The Mandalorian, little was known about the New Republic era, despite its crucial placement between the original trilogy and Star Wars: The Force Awakens.

Soon enough, The Mandalorian became more than a show; it became an entire sub-franchise. Alongside the ill-received The Book of Boba Fett, The Mandalorian brought animated Star Wars: The Clone Wars heroes back into the fold, including Mandalorian leader Bo-Katan Kryze and long-lost former Jedi Ahsoka Tano. As Anakin Skywalker's unexpected Padawan, Ahsoka is one of Star Wars' most important characters, a vital player in Anakin's fall to the dark side and the larger Clone Wars conflict. Ahsoka returned in Star Wars Rebels as an early member of the Rebellion before meeting Din Djarin and the artist formerly known as Baby Yoda in The Mandalorian season 2. Now, Ahsoka has become one of the Fresh Republic era's most influential characters, a genuine threat to the Imperial Remnant. If any character deserves to have a show named after them, it's her. In a recent development, disney+'s Ahsoka not only continues The Mandalorian's Imperial Remnant storyline but also picks up where Star Wars Rebels left off, with Ahsoka and Ghost Crew member Sabine Wren setting off on a perilous journey to bring back the exiled Jedi Ezra Bridger. Following The Mandalorian and Grogu's less-than-stellar box office run, Ahsoka's second season, set to debut in January 2027, has the power to change the galaxy and the franchise forever, as returned Imperial mastermind Grand Admiral Thrawn wages war against what remains of the Rebel Alliance.

Ahsoka season 2 will determine Star Wars' future on Disney+ and beyond. Though The Mandalorian has largely been a success, The Mandalorian and Grogu's theatrical failure has thrown the New Republic era's storytelling into question. At Star Wars Celebration 2023, Lucasfilm announced Dave Filoni's Mandalorian-era culmination movie, supposedly meant to bring The Mandalorian, The Book of Boba Fett, and Ahsoka together in one last, Avengers-esque Star Wars-themed hurrah, a version of Timothy Zahn's non-canon Star Wars Legends epic, Heir to the Empire.

Given what happened with The Mandalorian and Grogu and all the other theatrical changes since then — Rey's New Jedi Order movie is still in limbo, with next year's Star Wars: Starfighter taking its place as the first post-Star Wars: The Rise of Skywalker story — there's a chance this movie will never come to fruition. Ahsoka season 2 could technically conclude Thrawn's story in its eight-episode run (a pivotal battle between Thrawn and Admiral Ackbar has already been confirmed), setting the stage for the First Order's rise to power as seen in the Star Wars sequel trilogy, bringing the so-called "Mandoverse" to an end once and for all.

However, the Imperial Remnant isn't the only threat in Ahsoka season 2. In fact, Ahsoka and Sabine are in a galaxy even further away than Star Wars' primary galaxy, where a powerful darkness lurks in the shadows, waiting to be unleashed. Ahsoka and Sabine will chase Jedi-turned-mercenary Baylan Skoll across Peridea as he hunts the source of this power, potentially encountering the Star Wars Legends-era Force-wielder Abeloth, otherwise known as the Mother.

Industry observers note that given the Mortis gods' statues (the Father, the Son, and the Daughter) on Peridea and Ahsoka's lasting connection to the Daughter after The Clone Wars' Mortis arc, this seems the most likely path. Abeloth is one of the most powerful figures in the Star Wars Legends continuity, a being of chaos and destruction with the power to bring about the end of "everything.".

Covering Thrawn's war against the Rebel Alliance and Abeloth's hypothetical re-emergence in eight episodes will be difficult. It'll need to feel like one cohesive whole, especially since both storylines take place in two distinct galaxies. Introducing a villain like Abeloth — or at least someone or something equally powerful — would need to affect the franchise at large. It can't just be a one-and-done event. That makes it feel more likely that the Mandoverse's arc won't end in Ahsoka season 2, though nothing is confirmed.

Sources indicate that ahsoka season 2 is presently the only live-action Star Wars Disney+ show left; no other projects have been officially announced, with the franchise attempting to return to its theatrical roots. Following the Mandoverse, Lucasfilm may benefit from a more balanced approach, investing wisely in both movies and streaming indicates that not only expand Star Wars' timeline but also its creative scope. For now, though, Ahsoka season 2 essentially holds the keys to the franchise's future.

Ahsoka season 2 premieres on January 20, 2027, exclusively on Disney+.

Premier League board members are likely safe from personal attacks as Manchester City's sponsors consider taking legal action.

That is according to former Man City financial adviser Stefan Borson, who exclusively told Football Insider that being sued for defamation should not be a concern for the Premier League figures.

The Citizens' main sponsors, Etihad Airways and Etisalat, are considering legal action following the club's daming guilty verdict back in September.

It has prompted all Premier League clubs on voting to cover the board's legal costs if they are to be sued over Man City's 114 charges case.

All but Man City and Nottingham Forest voted in favour of the decision, with the club's sponsors claiming innocence in the ongoing situation. (Related: Latest Update: At least seven progressives voted agains...)

Etisalat called the league’s findings “entirely false and defamatory," but even with that, the organization will have a hard time succeeding in a legal battle.

That is according to Borson, who, speaking exclusively to Football Insider, stated that the Premier League board should be safe from legal attacks.

"The theory is that directors shouldn't be exposed personally for their ordinary course actions within a manufacturer," he explained.

"Anyone pursued by third parties, the company should effectively underwrite them, and that's normal in most companies and actually most Premier League clubs.

"There are various ways in which you can make sure that the directors are not effectively going to get bankrupt if they are sued by a third party...

"...Or for actions alleged to have been done, such as the allegation here in respect to Etihad or Etisalat, the two City sponsors that apparently are noted to be considering legal action.

"I can't see on any basis right now why the four non-executive directors, including the chairman and/or chairwoman and Richard Masters, would have a claim for defamation anyway, if that is the proposed claim.".

Borson has suggested that Richard Masters' quotes could be what the sponsors are focusing on, but even that isn't enough of a concern.

"The quote from Richard Masters, if that's what they're focusing on, it's not a quote that would give rise to defamation," he added.

"He's simply referring to what this independent commission has found having heard the evidence. He's not saying whether it's what he believes or not.

Ah, the Bulgarian split squat. Feared by many, loathed by most, but loved by personal trainers all over the world. What if I told you that you'd enjoy this lower-body exercise infinitely more if you followed three simple steps to fix up your form?

It's surprisingly easy to make mistakes with this leg-day exercise, and really, it can be adjusted to be more quad-dominant or glute-dominant — but there are clear signs of bad form, and many more people than you'd think end up on the physical therapy couch because of poor lifting mechanics. It has been revealed that the move requires balance and coordination, while your upper body, core and hips help stabilize you as you move. But people tend to think of this move as more quads than glutes, which isn't exactly the case.

Here's what I see go wrong, and three quick fixes:.

It has been revealed that in short, you're wasting your time if your form is badly off. And we're busy humans who don't like our time being wasted, right?

Leaning back too far removes tension from the working muscles and overloads your lower back. Keep your torso static and stable — don't hunch or allow your knees to cave inward. That means engaging your core and using the power of your core, glutes and legs to drive movement.

Next, think about your gravity; as I mentioned, keep your back straight and slightly hinge forward to help engage the glutes on your standing leg. If you work with a weight in your opposite hand, this will engage more of the outer glutes, or gluteus medius.

Taking time during the eccentric contraction means slowing down the lowering phase; you can then press upward with power while maximizing tension. It's now thought, due to advances in research, that eccentric contraction is vital for muscle growth and braking force.

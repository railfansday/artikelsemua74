Surprise: Aer Lingus Unveils First-Ever Premium Economy, Coming To 10 A330s

🌐 Source: Onemileatatime • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 3 min read (664 words, 📚 Deep Dive)

  • The cabin will feature just four rows with 28 seats in a 2-3-2 configuration.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Aer Lingus is getting on the premium economy hype. The Irish flag carrier has today announced it will introduce the cabin for the first time on 10 of its Airbus A330s, which will also feature latest business and economy cabins (the product won’t be coming to the Airbus A321LR/XLR fleet).

This is quite a major change, and one that should give it a leg up in the highly competitive trans-Atlantic market.

The cabin will feature just four rows with 28 seats in a 2-3-2 configuration. That’s about average when compared to other premium economy cabins. The narrow fuselage of the A330 means there’s only room for three seats in the middle section, rather than the four many other airlines offer.

According to recent reports, in terms of specs, the 18-19 inch wide Recaro R4 seats will offer 38 inches of pitch and eight inches of recline. In addition to footrests, a staple of any decent premium economy, the seats have been designed with fixed headrest wings to provide a sense of privacy.

As for the soft product, Aer Lingus promises three-course dining, drinks throughout the flight, a pillow and blanket, an amenity kit, and noise-reducing (not noise-canceling) headphones.

Sources indicate that I actually really like the design and color scheme. It’s modern, looks elegant and premium, and most importantly of all for the airline of the Emerald Isle, the colors are quintessentially Irish.

Aer Lingus notes premium economy fares will start from 700 Euros one-way, putting a return trip at around 1,400 Euros. That works out to around $1,534, and a few hundred dollars cheaper than United’s nonstop fares from Newark (EWR) to Dublin (DUB).

We’ll have to wait until October 1 next year to try Aer Lingus’ new premium economy out for ourselves. The airline hasn’t yet unveiled which routes will get the product first, but we should find out in the coming months.

The good news is, they’ll operate on many of the airline’s 18 nonstop routes from Dublin to the United States.

Sources indicate that as a small, almost boutique-style airline with just 63 aircraft, Aer Lingus must remain competitive, especially in the saturated transatlantic market in which it predominantly operates. The addition of premium economy bridges the large gap between economy and business, and should open the airline up to a potential upcoming customer base who’ve never flown it before.

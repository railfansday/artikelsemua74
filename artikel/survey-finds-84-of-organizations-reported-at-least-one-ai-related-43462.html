Companies are running into problems as they give artificial intelligence more control over critical business applications, with 84% reporting at least one AI-related incident in the past year, according to a latest survey from identity security firm Delinea.

According to recent reports, the manufacturer's 2026 Application Access Governance report found that some organizations have experienced incorrect financial transactions and AI actions they couldn't trace or explain. The findings raise concerns about whether enterprises have enough oversight of AI systems that can make decisions and take actions on their own.

The findings are based on an independent survey of 1,000 senior finance and finance technology leaders in the United States and United Kingdom. The AI-specific analysis included 879 respondents whose organizations use the technology in critical business applications.

The report found that 35% of surveyed organizations have deployed AI agents capable of taking actions within business applications. However, only 11% of organizations using AI noted their governance and security controls were keeping pace with deployment.

Delinea also found gaps in centralized visibility into agent deployment and permissions, including who owns agents and who approved their use.

The research also highlighted gaps in monitoring access across business systems. Only 46% of respondents remarked they continuously monitor and manage cumulative access risks across applications.

Those gaps can make it difficult to identify segregation-of-duties conflicts, in which a person or agent has permissions across systems that should be kept separate.

Despite those concerns, 95% of respondents said they were confident their access controls would withstand scrutiny in an external audit.

However, 26% reported access-related audit findings that had recurred in consecutive years, while 25% mentioned some findings had remained unresolved for three or more years.

Only 22% of organizations had a single named owner for each application, with ownership tracked in one system of record.

The findings highlight the oversight challenges associated with giving AI agents authority to act within business systems, particularly when organizations cannot consistently identify who owns an agent or trace its actions.

Frank Vukovits, chief security scientist at Delinea, said organizations remain responsible for the actions AI agents take within their systems.

Sources indicate that "When an agent can approve a transaction or change permissions, the business owns the outcome. You need to know what every agent can do, who owns it, and who approved it, before an auditor asks or your financials are misstated," Vukovits mentioned.

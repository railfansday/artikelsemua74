Like the best memory foam mattresses on the market, all three beds are made entirely of foam (not a spring nor coil in sight) and are fiberglass-free.

The 12" SweetNight's design is focused on cooling, with an airflow-enhancing '3D' cover that my fellow testers and I found to be extremely cool-to-the-touch (but more on that later). Its foam is also infused with Phase Change Material (PCM), a specialist cooling fabric found in some of the best cooling mattresses. PCM has been proven to improve human heat dissipation up to 25.6% compared to conventional mattresses.

Recent findings suggest that the 12" EGOHOME does have a graphene-infused cover that the brand calls "cooling", but this is very basic. That noted, the EGOHOME has an excellent quality build, packed with dense layers of foam (including a EGOHOME's trademarked BraceSleeper Support Foam, which is high-density for extra durability).

According to recent reports, despite being mid-range rather than budget, the CopperFlex has the least sophisticated design out of the three mattresses, with just a copper-infused foam for temperature control. However, its cover does boast a "Nano Stain Terminator Protector" cover to safeguards against stains and spills, plus it comes in a wide range of sizes (including an Olympic queen and RV sizes).

I found all three beds to be good choices for anyone seeking the best mattress for side sleepers (and I say that as a primary side sleeper), but each did boast different levels of pressure relief.

Sources indicate that in my SweetNight CoolNest mattress review, I found that the CoolNest provided excellent pressure relief for side and back sleeping (both in objective and subjective tests). It has enough give in the shoulder and hip region to relieve the side-sleeper pressure points, but its ultra-quick response time means it's easy to move around on and avoids that "quicksand" feel that some memory foam mattresses have.

According to recent reports, I conducted the EGOHOME Black 12" Foam mattress review for seven months (the longest I've tested a mattress at home) and found it delivered excellent medium-firm support for all sleep positions. However, another tester (a stomach sleeper) considered it a little too soft for front sleeping, proving that mattress firmness is subjective. That noted, back and side sleepers should feel both comfortable and supported.

As for the CopperFlex Essential, it's clear from our tests that it's strictly for side sleepers. I found it incredibly cushioning when sleeping on it for my Brooklyn Bedding CopperFlex Essential mattress review, which is surprising as it doesn't have a pillow top. It scored the highest pressure relief score out of the three mattresses, but my fellow testers and I agreed that it's too plush for stomach sleeping.

From the moment I unboxed the SweetNight CoolNest, I found it to be incredibly cool to the touch. I even slept on it through summer heatwaves without AC and it still delivered optimal temperature regulation. My experience was backed up by our thermal imaging camera tests, which found that the SweetNight has a rapid cooling rate of 2.1°F per minute (a cooling rate even quicker than the Bear Elite's 1.82°F per minute).

According to recent reports, in contrast, I found that both the CopperFlex Essential and the EGOHOME Black retained heat, especially in the back and shoulder region. Out of the three mattresses, the EGOHOME absorbed the highest amount of heat (15.6°F ), which was definitely noticeable to both me and fellow tester, Ruth Jones. And while the CopperFlex Essential absorbed up to 11.1°F in comparison, its temperature control will pose a problem for hot sleepers.

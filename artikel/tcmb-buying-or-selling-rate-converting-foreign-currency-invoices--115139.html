Every Turkish accounting integration hits the same question sooner or later: TCMB publishes four numbers per currency each day (forex buying, forex selling, banknote buying and banknote selling), so which one goes on the invoice, the VAT return and the month-end revaluation?

According to recent reports, on 25 November 2026, USD/TRY was 50.5531 (forex buying) and 50.6441 (forex selling). On a USD 100,000 invoice that's TRY 9,100 of difference in the VAT base, so it's worth getting right. (Related: This simple change stops robot swarms from getting stuc...)

So the default is the buying rate, not the selling rate, and the date is the taxable event's date, which is not necessarily the invoice's issue date or the payment date.

Exchange differences come later. If the price is in foreign currency and is paid after the taxable event, an exchange difference in the seller's favour is part of the VAT base, and it's invoiced separately. For invoices to customers abroad, practice sources note that no exchange-difference invoice or VAT arises.

It has been revealed that for valuing foreign-currency items at period end, Article 280 of the Tax Procedure Law (Vergi Usul Kanunu) refers to the exchange rate (borsa rayici). For IFRS/TFRS reporting, practitioners differ. Some use the forex buying rate throughout; others use buying for monetary assets and selling for monetary liabilities. Pick one policy and document it.

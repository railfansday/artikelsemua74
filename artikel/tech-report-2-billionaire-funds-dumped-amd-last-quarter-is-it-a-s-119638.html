According to recent reports, AMD (AMD -1.75%) has been a fantastic stock to own in 2026. The stock has risen around 100% so far, though it was up 200% at the start of October. AMD became a popular choice for many investors, including the smart money in hedge funds. However, now that the latest round of 13-F filings has come out, it's clear that selling AMD has become a trend among fund managers. Two major funds sold all of their shares during the third quarter: Appaloosa Management, run by David Tepper, and Coatue Management, run by Philippe Laffont. When giant funds like this sell, it's normally time to start thinking about selling your shares as well. So, is it time to move on from AMD stock?

Sources indicate that first, let's tackle the business. AMD has always played second fiddle to Nvidia (NVDA -3.91%) in the data center industry, as Nvidia's hardware and software have always been a step ahead of AMD's. However, AMD's products are becoming more popular among certain customers because they are cheaper. While they may not be as capable, power-efficient, or user-friendly, they can be quite useful if they’re not being tasked with a wide variety of operations. In a recent development, this has led to more adoption of AMD's products, and AMD's Q3 results back that up. The company experienced 97% year-over-year revenue growth in its data center division to $8.5 billion during Q3. However, AMD is more than just a data center company. Its overall growth rate was 45% year-over-year to $11.5 billion, as its client and gaming segment decreased year-over-year, partially offset by increasing Embedded processors doing well. Overall, those are solid results for AMD, but there's just one problem: It's far smaller than Nvidia.

Sources indicate that AMD already has a tough enough time being compared to Nvidia's products. For it to be a better investment than Nvidia, it should be showing stronger growth, but it's not. During its fiscal third quarter (ended in October), Nvidia's overall revenue rose 103% year over year to nearly $116 billion. So, Nvidia is 10 times larger than AMD. On the data center side, Nvidia's revenue rose 110% to $108 billion.

Nvidia has the technology edge and the growth edge. However, AMD has dramatically outperformed Nvidia this year, and there is one obvious reason for that: valuation.

AMD PE Ratio (Forward) data by YCharts.

In a recent development, sometimes, a stock goes up not because the business is doing well, but because the market is willing to pay more for the stock. That's exactly what's going on here, and it has gotten a bit out of control with AMD's stock.

AMD is one of the pricier stocks on the market, trading at 81 times trailing earnings and 21 times next year’s earnings.

Because 13F filings don't reveal exact transaction dates, we don't know whether these funds exited early in Q3 or right before the quarter ended—when AMD traded at over 80 times forward earnings and nearly 40 times next year's earnings—though either way they missed the stock's early-October peak.

Still, when you compare price tags, AMD is far more expensive, because Nvidia trades at 15 times trailing earnings and 8.6 times next year's earnings. Nvidia is clearly the far better value, and with it growing faster at a much larger scale, Nvidia’s stock looks like a far better investment.

According to recent reports, so, while now may not be the best time to sell AMD compared to a month and a half ago, I still think investors would be wise to move on to another AI stock. Many artificial intelligence investments are on huge sale right now, but AMD is not one of them.

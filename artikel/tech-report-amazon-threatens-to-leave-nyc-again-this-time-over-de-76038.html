It has been revealed that the Seattle tech giant is threatening to move its delivery operations out of the city over a proposed law that would require it to directly employ the drivers who bring packages to New Yorkers’ doors. It’s also asking its customers to help fight the bill. (Related: How we sped up transformer inference 100x for 🤗 API cus...)

The legislation, known as the Delivery Protection Act or Intro 518, would apply to companies that run last-mile delivery facilities in the city. Amazon is the main target, but FedEx and several regional couriers would also be covered.

It has been revealed that companies would need a license from the city’s Department of Consumer and Worker Protection, which would also investigate complaints. The bill was introduced in February.

On Thursday, Amazon began showing Newly introduced York City customers pop-up messages and a homepage banner that reads, “NYC Council could add up to $5 per order. Act now to protect low prices.” The notices link to a form and a pre-written letter customers can send to their council members to oppose the bill, Bloomberg reported. Amazon pointed GeekWire to a study by consulting firm AKRF estimating the bill would raise consumer costs by $409 to $664 a year, but didn’t detail how it arrived at the per-order figure.

According to recent reports, in a blog post, Amazon stated it relies on a network of small delivery businesses in the city. “If this legislation passes, we’d no longer be able to work with them, and we’d be forced to consider moving our operations outside of the city,” the company wrote. “That would likely mean slower and more expensive delivery for millions of customers in Latest York City, while also threatening small businesses and their thousands of employees.”.

Amazon launched its Delivery Service Partners program in 2018, letting entrepreneurs start their own companies to deliver Amazon packages in Amazon-branded vans and uniforms. The companies hire their own drivers, while Amazon pays them to deliver and offers discounts on vehicles, fuel and insurance. The model lets Amazon expand its delivery network without directly employing drivers.

In a statement to GeekWire, Amazon spokesperson Kelly Nantel said the bill “as written” would threaten more than 40 small delivery businesses the manufacturer works with in the city and the more than 5,000 New Yorkers they employ. More than 2,000 Amazon employees also work at its delivery facilities in the city, the company said.

According to recent reports, “We’re hopeful this bill doesn’t pass as written,” Nantel stated. “But so far, key City Council members seem uninterested in learning about our delivery operations.” Amazon said neither Council Member Tiffany Cabán, the bill’s sponsor, nor Mayor Zohran Mamdani has accepted its offer to visit a delivery facility. The company also said customers sent about 20,000 emails to council members within six hours of the campaign’s launch Thursday morning.

Industry observers note that the standoff echoes Amazon’s 2019 reversal on its second headquarters. That February, the tech giant abruptly canceled plans to build a 25,000-person “HQ2” office in Long Island City, Queens, after more than a year of competition among cities to host it. Amazon blamed “a number of state and local politicians” who opposed the project, which drew fierce criticism over roughly $3 billion in government incentives.

Amazon stuck with its plans for Arlington, Va., and Nashville and didn’t reopen its search. Polls at the time highlighted most New Yorkers supported the deal, but Queens officials, labor leaders and activists fought it. State Sen. Michael Gianaris, who led the opposition, accused Amazon of trying to shake down governments to get its way.

Sources indicate that city leaders backing the delivery bill see a familiar pattern. “Amazon is once again making spurious attacks against this commonsense legislation that would make workers and our streets safer,” said Cabán, the bill’s sponsor, who called Amazon’s warning a “dishonest scare tactic.”.

City Council Speaker Julie Menin questioned Amazon’s cost claims in a post on X.

“It defies logic that Amazon, a $3 trillion corporation, would raise Latest Yorkers’ cost of living if it’s forced to adhere to basic regulations and worker protections,” she wrote, adding that the council is “crafting legislation that regulates Amazon and protects workers, without impacting prices or jobs.”.

Mamdani endorsed the bill in August, saying Amazon’s subcontractor model “leaves workers vulnerable and corporations free to avoid accountability for reckless conditions on city streets,” CNBC reported.

Supporters, including the Teamsters union, which has sought to organize Amazon drivers and warehouse workers, say the bill would make the work safer and make it harder for Amazon to distance itself from driver injuries.

Amazon disputes that the bill would improve safety. The company remarked it has invested more than $2.5 billion in driver safety since 2019, that the serious crash rate for its drivers in New York City fell 35.7% from 2024 to 2025, and that drivers there have averaged nearly $24 an hour since January 2025, with required health coverage and paid time off.

Industry observers note that meanwhile, business groups have run ads against the bill and warned they may sue the city if it passes, according to CNBC. The measure is awaiting review by Menin.

ASML holds a virtual monopoly on the advanced lithography machinery required to print the world's most sophisticated microchips. Meanwhile, Broadcom has evolved into a powerhouse of networking components and enterprise software. Both companies are critical to the tech supply chain, but they offer distinct paths for investors seeking exposure to high-end silicon. (Related: Breaking: Quantum mechanics once baffled scientists. Recent findings suggest that ASML manufactures lithography systems, which are massive machines that use light to print intricate patterns onto silicon wafers. It provides the essential hardware for every major chipmaker to produce smaller, faster, and more efficient processors. Because its technology is unique, it serves as a critical gatekeeper for the entire high-end semiconductor market.

Sources indicate that in the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached nearly $43.4 billion, an increase of roughly 22.5%. The organization generated approximately $14.8 billion in net income. This growth highlights the sustained demand for extreme ultraviolet lithography systems used in advanced chip production for artificial intelligence and high-performance computing.

As of ASML's December 2025 balance sheet, the current ratio, which measures a organization's ability to cover its short-term debts with short-term assets, is approximately 1.3. The debt-to-equity ratio is roughly 0.2, showing that the company uses relatively little debt compared to its total equity. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was close to $12.2 billion.

According to recent reports, broadcom is a dominant player among semiconductor stocks, providing infrastructure software and hardware solutions to global enterprises. The tech giant has significant customer concentration, with its top five end customers accounting for roughly 40% of revenue in its latest annual report. Customer concentration like this adds a layer of risk to the business, as evidenced by a $30 billion commitment from Apple (AAPL -1.90%).

Recent findings suggest that in the 12 months ended Aug. 2, 2026, revenue reached close to $89.1 billion, representing significant growth of nearly 48.7%. The tech giant reported net income of approximately $38.3 billion during this time frame. This surge was partly driven by the integration of large acquisitions and booming demand for artificial intelligence infrastructure across data centers.

Sources indicate that as of Broadcom's November 2025 balance sheet, the current ratio is roughly 1.7. The debt-to-equity ratio is approximately 0.8, indicating a higher level of total debt relative to shareholder equity than ASML. In the fiscal year ended Nov. 2, 2025, free cash flow was close to $26.9 billion, though stock-based compensation accounted for roughly 27.5% of operating cash flow, inflating reported cash generation since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

ASML faces risks from its position at the center of global trade tensions, particularly concerning international export restrictions. The company also operates in the cyclical semiconductor equipment market, where spending by chipmakers can fluctuate based on broader economic conditions and interest rates. Furthermore, the rapid pace of innovation requires constant, heavy investment in research and development to maintain its technological lead over any potential competitors.

Broadcom operates in a highly cyclical industry that faces rapid price erosion and evolving technological requirements driven by artificial intelligence. It relies heavily on a limited number of contract manufacturers, such as Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSM -9.48%), which produced approximately 95% of its wafers according to its latest annual report. Broadcom also faces intense regulatory scrutiny and risks associated with its strategy of integrating large acquisitions into its software portfolio.

Broadcom looks potentially cheaper based on its forward P/E, which compares the stock price to future earnings estimates, whereas ASML has a lower P/S ratio.

In a recent development, in the past, I have picked ASML over many other stocks for several reasons. For one, it has a monopoly in the extreme ultraviolet lithography niche it operates in. That gives it strong pricing power and a solid moat. ASML also consistently reports a net margin in excess of 30%, and it pays a modest dividend.

However, it's hard to argue with Broadcom's long-term performance. A organization of that size reporting nearly 50% revenue growth is pretty wild. ASML and Broadcom are both exposed to the inherent cyclicality of the semiconductor industry, so I think that risk factor offsets itself. And the tech sector (and the market in general) may be melting down right now, but long-term investors shouldn't get especially concerned over what happens in the course of a week, or a month, or even a year.

I'm breaking with my pattern here. I don't think ASML is a bad choice, to be clear, but Broadcom has strong margins and even better revenue growth. Broadcom gets my vote for 2027 and beyond.

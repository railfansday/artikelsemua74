Industry observers note that investors who bought or otherwise acquired Nvidia common stock from Aug. 10, 2017, through Nov. 15, 2018, could share in a proposed $290 million cash settlement if the court approves the deal and their claims qualify.

The parties filed the agreement on Nov. 24, and plaintiffs filed an unopposed motion for preliminary approval that day in federal court in Oakland.

The plaintiffs alleged that Nvidia and Jensen Huang misled investors about how much crypto miners contributed to sales of graphics chips Nvidia marketed for gaming.

According to recent reports, they say shareholders suffered losses after the extent of Nvidia’s mining exposure emerged in 2018. Nvidia and Huang deny wrongdoing, and the settlement agreement contains no admission of liability.

In a recent development, the court certified the class in March, covering individuals and entities. Exclusions include the defendants, Nvidia officers and directors at relevant times, certain relatives and related entities, and the defendants’ liability insurers. Investors whose requests to opt out are accepted by the court are also excluded.

Class members seeking payment would have to file a paper or electronic claim by a court-set deadline, with documents supporting their claimed loss.

Under the proposed allocation, approved claimants would share the net fund in proportion to the losses the strategy recognizes for their stock transactions.

The plan would use the investor’s documented transactions, including what they paid for the shares and what they received if they sold them, but the court would have to approve the allocation plan.

The net fund would include the cash settlement and accrued interest, less taxes, notice and administration costs, court-awarded legal fees and litigation expenses, and other court-approved costs.

Individual payments would depend on the amount left in the fund and the total recognized losses from approved claims.

Sources indicate that the parties accepted a mediator’s recommendation on Sept. 24 and notified the court of their agreement on Oct. 22.

According to recent reports, the public docket lists Feb. 4, 2027, at 2 p.m. for the preliminary-approval hearing before Judge Haywood S. Gilliam Jr. in Oakland.

Industry observers note that if he grants preliminary approval, the proposed schedule calls for postcard notices to start going out and the claim form to be posted within 20 calendar days of the order.

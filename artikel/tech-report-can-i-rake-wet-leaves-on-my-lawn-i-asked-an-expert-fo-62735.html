Industry observers note that with wet weather making lawn care challenging, is raking damp leaves actually safe for my grass? “Yes, although wet leaves are heavier, stick together, and can be much more difficult to rake, blow, or vacuum,” states Karen Musgrave, Horticulturalist at Hicks Nurseries.

According to recent reports, “The bigger concern for your lawn is allowing a thick layer of leaves to remain there for an extended period of time. A heavy mat of wet leaves can block sunlight and air from reaching the grass and trap excess moisture underneath.”.

A heavy mat of wet leaves can block sunlight and air from reaching the grass and trap excess moisture underneath.

Sources indicate that in addition, leaving wet leaves sitting on your lawn over time can smother the grass, blocking essential nutrients from reaching the roots and making it prone to mold and fungal diseases. This can lead to patchy or thinning grass that will need urgent repair come springtime.

So what is the correct way to rake wet leaves without pulling up grass and harming my lawn? Experts agree that choosing the right tools is essential to do the job — and keep your yard intact.

“If the lawn is wet, avoid being overly aggressive with a rake because you can pull at the grass,” advises Musgrave. “ For the lawn, I would choose a lightweight leaf rake with a wide head rather than a heavier garden rake.

Recent findings suggest that “It gives you more control around garden beds, shrubs, and other areas where you don’t necessarily want to blow everything around. A smaller or narrower rake is especially helpful for removing leaves that have collected between plants in the landscape without damaging them.”.

It’s also notable to invest in a good-quality rake; aluminum or plastic rakes are best for most leaf cleanups. Unlike leaf blowers, a durable rake is affordable, easy, and practical to use, and doesn’t need fuel or batteries.

Sources indicate that if in doubt, though, check out these different types of rakes suitable for your yard. For more top tips, check out how to rake leaves the easy way — 7 tips and tricks.

A top tip is to (ideally) rake on a dry day, as the leaves will be easier to gather and less of a chore.

Also, you don’t have to clear every single leaf off your lawn, since keeping a few around can actually benefit your garden plants. “Don’t blow or rake every leaf out of your garden beds just for the sake of making them spotless,” adds Musgrave.

“Some leaf litter can be beneficial for the soil as it breaks down and adds organic matter. It can also provide overwintering habitat for beneficial insects. You don’t want leaves piled so heavily that they smother smaller plants, but a light layer can be left in place.”.

Industry observers note that shredding leaves with a lawn mower is another handy trick. Breaking them down into smaller pieces makes it easy to add them to your compost heap and return those valuable nutrients to your yard.

In a recent development, “Excess leaves can also be added to a compost pile rather than bagged and thrown away. Chopping or shredding them first can help them break down more swiftly. The key is to make sure the leaves aren’t so thick they cover the grass and block sunlight and air.”.

Finally, pay attention to bare spots and thin areas. Depending on your grass type and climate, consider overseeding before the harsh winter arrives. “Apply a grass seed that is appropriate for your lawn, lightly rake it into the soil, and keep the area consistently moist while the new grass gets established.

"The cooler temperatures of fall are ideal for establishing grass. Taking care of thin and bare spots now gives new grass time to develop roots before winter and helps create a fuller, healthier lawn for spring.”.

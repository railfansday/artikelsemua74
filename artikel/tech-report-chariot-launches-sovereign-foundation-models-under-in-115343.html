The IndiaAI Mission revealed on November 25, 2026, that Ministry of Electronics and IT Secretary S. Krishnan had inaugurated Chariot Artificial Intelligence’s Chariot-1 foundation models, developed from scratch in India and spanning English and 10 Indian languages.

In a recent development, chariot is one of the first four teams selected by the mission to build sovereign foundation models. The unveiling took place at Chariot One, a launch and research showcase at Bharat Mandapam in Upcoming Delhi, bringing the government-backed programme together with a public demonstration of the lab’s speech research. IndiaAI reports three design elements: IndicShift, a shared alphabet for Indian scripts using 2.5 times fewer tokens on Indian text; Nested Training, which develops three model sizes in a single run with 62% faster training; and Mixture of Vocabularies, an approach delivering 38% faster training. Industry observers note that those choices address two distinct parts of multilingual development: how efficiently Indian text is represented and how much training work is required to produce different model sizes. They also indicate that Chariot-1 is intended as a reusable model family rather than only a single speech product.

Beyond text representation, the models support context-aware speech generation. IndiaAI reports more than 97% verbalization accuracy on the Async TTS Pronunciation Benchmark, which measures whether written expressions receive an acceptable spoken interpretation. That capability matters because applications must read currencies, dates, abbreviations and symbols appropriately, rather than merely pronounce individual words.

According to recent reports, the inauguration follows two public speech releases from the lab. Chariot’s November 12, 2026 release describes Chariot-1-TTS-Multilingual as generating speech in 11 Indic languages and English, with more than 50 voices, language mixing within sentences and voice cloning from a few seconds of reference audio. It also interprets prices, dates, abbreviations and symbols in context, reducing the need for custom text-normalization rules.

Industry observers note that in Chariot’s own November 11, 2026 evaluation, the model ranked first among the configurations compared on three measures: a 7.60% Hindi character error rate, 93.78% English verbalization accuracy on its English suite and a 4.901 NISQA score, an automated estimate of audio quality. Those measurements address different parts of speech generation: intelligibility, interpretation of written text and estimated sound quality.

In a recent development, the earlier Chariot-1-ASR Realtime release, dated October 22, 2026, added streaming speech recognition in 10 Indian languages and Indian English. Chariot reports that it had the lowest word error rate in every language against every streaming API and batch model tested, and that its 10-language average had 26% fewer errors than Google Gemini 3.5 Transcribe’s batch API.

Sources indicate that the release describes the system as the Chariot-1 speech-reasoning backbone post-trained for one task, rather than a model built exclusively for recognition. That makes it a public example of how the foundation model can be adapted to a specific task.

Recent findings suggest that chariot is headquartered in Delhi and led by co-founders Parth Sarthi and Suvrat Bhooshan. Its research roadmap calls for a speech-native model that reasons across meaning, delivery, timing and action, rather than dividing conversations among separate transcription, synthesis, dubbing and voice-agent pipelines. The current TTS and ASR systems are the first public steps, with developers able to generate and stream speech through the Chariot platform and API.

Recent findings suggest that the inauguration connects India’s sovereign-AI policy with a specific technical approach: build shared multilingual foundations, then adapt them to individual speech tasks. The next substantive test is whether Chariot can extend that approach from generation and recognition into the broader reasoning system it describes while retaining the efficiency and accuracy gains reported for the initial models.

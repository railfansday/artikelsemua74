The USMNT is on the hunt for a upcoming manager after Mauricio Pochettino left to join Manchester United, and Clint Dempsey thinks he knows just the man.

It has been revealed that pochettino had been at the helm since 2024 and signed a new contract following an impressive 2026 World Cup campaign. Therefore, it might take some time for the USA to get their ducks in a row for a potential replacement.

Pep Guardiola has emerged as the dream candidate after leaving Manchester City in the summer, while Nashville SC boss BJ Callaghan and USMNT Under-23s coach Steve Cherundolo are the current favourites. (Related: Summer at Hugging Face...)

But Dempsey, who scored 57 goals in 147, has thrown another name into the hat. The former Fulham and Seattle Sounders forward revealed a conversation he's had with 'one of the best players to have played the game'.

Speaking on the Men in Blazers YouTube channel, Dempsey revealed that Thierry Henry had expressed an interest in becoming the USMNT manager.

When assessing Henry's credentials, he noted: "Yeah, I’d buy him too, just because of being around him, the way he sees the game, the confidence that he would give those players.

"I think when he’s telling them stuff, they’re going to listen.

According to recent reports, “There’s a reason why there’s a statue outside of the Arsenal stadium, the Emirates, of him, because of what he was able to do. He’s one of the best players to have played the game. So if he can figure out a way to give some of that confidence and that knowledge of the game to some of these players, I think it could make a significant difference.".

Dempsey has worked with the Arsenal and France icon on CBS Sports' coverage of the Champions League. He claimed Henry had previously told him that it's a job he would like.

He continued: "In the Olympics, he was able to get France to a second-place finish versus Spain. He’s someone who loves the game. He loves the US. He’s passionate about it. He loves New York, the time that he spent there. So, you know, I say, why not?

"Because someone of his pedigree, and being around the game, and me watching the way that he watches the game and sees football, I think there’s a lot of positives there. I know that it’s a job that he would want to do because we’ve had conversations about it. And I think he’s someone who could make a difference.".

Henry spent four years of his playing career in the US, representing the Upcoming York Red Bulls in a significant coup for the MLS. After ending his on-pitch career in the Significant Apple, he has regularly been seen on UK and US television as a popular pundit.

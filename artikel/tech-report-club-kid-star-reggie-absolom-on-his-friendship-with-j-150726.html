Such was the case for “Club Kid” star Reggie Absolom. But Absolom was determined to appear as cool, calm and collected as the blue waters of the Mediterranean Sea.

Despite the crowd’s cheers, it wasn’t until Absolom found out that A24 (his preference, “because they do pretty good things for indie movies”) had outmaneuvered the competition (with a whopping bid of $17 million) that he fully realized, “OK, people like this film.”.

That’s a little bit of an understatement. “Club Kid” was nominated for best feature at the Gothams and has delivered impressive box office numbers since its limited release on Nov. 6; in its second week in theaters, Firstman’s film tallied an impressive $28,002 per-screen average. To date, “Club Kids” has grossed more than $5 million globally.

It has been revealed that in the film, Absolom plays Arlo, the 10-year-old son of a washed-up party promoter named Peter (Firstman), whose excessive drug habits end his tenure running Fresh York City’s queer nightlife scene. When Arlo highlights up on his father’s doorstep, they are both forced to grow up and figure out how to navigate the rest of their lives. Recent findings suggest that the teenage British actor won the role after auditioning for HBO’s upcoming “Harry Potter” series. Absolom reached the final stage of auditions to join the wizarding world, but didn’t get the job. So, U.K. casting director Lucy Bevan mentioned him to her fellow casting director Carmen Cuba, who had been searching for the perfect youngster to play Arlo. Aside from the fact that both boys are British, Absolom and his character seem to have quite a bit in common.

It has been revealed that “I love to get good at things,” Absolom indicates, listing the similarities between himself and Arlo, whose artistic aspirations are focused on DJing, instead of acting. “Obviously, getting there is a bit annoying, because I want to be good at everything the first time I try it. But even if I’m not, I will keep doing it until I am good at it.”.

Read on to find out how a potential 10 years in Hogwarts turned into 26 days immersed in New York City’s indie sleaze scene, what Absolom learned from filming his first major movie and how he’s processing the Oscar buzz.

Sources indicate that A hacker who pleaded guilty to the 2022 hacking breach of Rockstar Games, which resulted in a bunch of mid-development clips of GTA 6 in action leaking onto the net, has been released under supervision. The hacker, Arion Kurtaj, has also a five year long serious crime prevention order which will restrict what he can do online.

The BBC report that Kurtaj has been released on licence after serving most of a seven-year sentence. On licence means his release is subject to a number of conditions. According to the UK Government website, the exact nature of these conditions varies from person to person and case to case, as they've got be "considered necessary and proportionate". However, things like needing not to commit any more crimes, not travel abroad, and live under the supervision of community offender manager are cited as part of a set of "standard licence conditions" outlined by the government site. According to recent reports, kurtaj has also been given a serious crime prevention order to run for five years, restricting his activity online. It includes bans from using both Telegram and Discord. (Related: Steam is pitting 10 years of Game of the Year winners a...)

According to the BBC, the judge told Kurtaj Yyou are a very talented young man," and warned him: "Don't let this happen again." City of London Police said the case "highlights the dangers that young people can end up being drawn to online".

According to recent reports, kurtaj pleaded guilty on November 16th at Woolwich Crown Court to the 2022 Rockstar Games hack, as well as a number of others he carried out with the hacking gang Lapsus$ while aged 16 and 17. Now aged 21, he was previously sentenced to stay in a secure hospital indefinitely, due to the severity of his autism, but was subsequently deemed fit to stand trial.

According to recent reports, 90 clips of unfinished GTA 6 were flung online by the 2022 hack, with Rockstar having refused to cooperate with Kurtaj's demand via an internal Slack channel that the company contact him on massaging app Telegram within 24 hours. Rockstar claimed the breach cost them $5m million to recover from, in addition to staff having to waste thousands of hours.

In a recent development, earlier this year, GTA 6 suffered yet another leak, with a hacker or hackers going by 'Cyberleek' posting various videos of the game in action. Rockstar and parent company Take-Two quickly sprung into action, taking down videos, filing subpoenas, and issuing a statement that called the leak "heartbreaking".

Industry observers note that cyberleek has since claimed to have lobbed a near-complete Xbox build of GTA 6 onto the net, while withholding a main executable file they threatened to release subsequently. Online sources claim the executable has now also been leaked, though this has not been independently verified.

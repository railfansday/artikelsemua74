📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The US dollar index, which measures the greenback against six major currencies, traded near 103.70 on Friday, slipping below the 103.86 pivot after failing to hold Monday’s jump to 104.53, its highest level since March last year. Traders see a quarter-point Federal Reserve hike on December 9 as almost certain, according to CME FedWatch, which would lift rates to 4.00% to 4.25%. Markets also expect another increase by around April. Hotter-than-expected October core inflation and an upward revision to third-quarter growth have supported the dollar. “The USD looks to be on a stronger-for-longer path,” NAB strategists wrote, though they see little room for further gains after its sharp climb. Rate hike expectations abroad are also limiting the dollar’s upside. ECB meeting accounts indicated broad support for raising rates in October, and all 71 economists in a Reuters poll expect a December hike after euro area inflation hit 4.0%. Traders have also added bets on back-to-back Bank of England increases.

Billy Bob Thornton is experiencing a major career renaissance thanks to his role as Tommy Norris in Taylor Sheridan's Western series, Landman. To older generations, the multi-hyphenate might be better recognized for his rise to stardom in the mid-90s, which culminated in his significant-screen success in the aughties. Though he's found more success as an actor, Thornton's filmmaking skills are, in fact, responsible for his first thrust into the limelight. Thornton wrote, directed, and starred in his breakthrough feature, the Academy Award-winning Southern Gothic drama Sling Blade, released in 1996.

It has been revealed that after Sling Blade's success, Thornton would make a few more directorial efforts, but unfortunately, none were quite as successful. His next credit as director was Daddy and Me, which, though sharing a similar theme as Sling Blade, didn't get a major release. Up next was All the Pretty Horses, an adaptation of Cormac McCarthy's beloved novel of the same name released in 2000. Though the Western film, starring Matt Damon opposite Penélope Cruz, did get a major theatrical release, it was marred by behind-the-scenes conflicts, including then-Miramax boss Harvey Weinstein's controversial decision to drastically cutthe runtime against Thornton's wishes.

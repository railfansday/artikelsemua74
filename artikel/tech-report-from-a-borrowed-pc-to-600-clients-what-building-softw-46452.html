Pixelvile started on a PC I borrowed from my uncle. It switched off every 10 minutes, so I learned to save after every few lines of code. A year later he took it back. By then the work it made possible had paid for a proper laptop.

It has been revealed that today we're a small team in Kolkata, India. We've done roughly 1,000 projects for 600+ clients: games, web apps, SaaS platforms and websites. Here's what that taught us, especially about building for small businesses.

We started with games on Steam and Roblox, then worked behind the scenes as subcontractors for other studios. Many of those projects still run today under other names.

Sources indicate that none of it was glamorous. Bug fixes, small scripts, quick sites. But small jobs pay the next month's bills, and every one of them shows you a real problem someone was willing to pay to fix. (Related: “Giant superatoms” could finally solve quantum computin...)

A typical local business, say a clinic, a salon or a café, runs on four tools that don't talk to each other: a website, a booking app, a spreadsheet of customers, and an invoicing app. Four logins, four bills, and nothing connects.

So we built one dashboard that does all four: the website, online booking, customer records, staff calendars, invoices and stock. We set it up for each business and look after it every month.

The hard part wasn't the code. It was making it simple enough for an owner who checks everything on their phone between customers. (Related: This simple change stops robot swarms from getting stuc...)

Most missed appointments aren't rude. People are busy and they forget. One reminder the day before, with a one-tap way to change the time, fixes most of them. For long or high-value slots, a small advance via UPI helps even more.

The newly introduced Grand Theft Auto arrived this week, the messianic reward after 13 long years, offering the best-rendered version of the series’ standing promise: anything, within the scope of its metropolis and the player’s imagination. I watched my cousin paint a house for his avatar’s girlfriend, then hop in a car and drive to the Bass Museum. Or rather, to the Vice City Museum of Art, the faithful replica of it in Vice City, the game’s fictional Miami.

Rockstar teased that Grand Theft Auto VI would boast the franchise’s sharpest graphics yet, and the claim was not exaggerated. The Vice Museum of Art captures the Bass’s signature civic Art Deco style: a pale limestone facade (the real stuff was quarried in the Florida Keys) and, as far as I could tell, even the carved decorative relief around the entry. It’s a far cry from the series’ earliest entries, when a building like this would have been a smooth beige box; here, the facade looks textured. We parked and admired its public art: a riff on Miami Mountain, a tower of boulders by Ugo Rondinone permanently installed in Collins Park, outside the Bass. Part of Rondinone’s “Mountain” series, it’s made of garishly clashing fluorescent rocks stacked high and probably visible from space. The Vice City version has swapped stone for emojis: winking, crying, smiling. Vice City may be hiding another Easter egg for art heads. Months ago, a screenshot circulated online from an expansion for Grand Theft Auto Online, showing a newly introduced character in a shirt with an “Art Oregano” logo. It’s a safe bet that it’s a nod to Art Basel Miami Beach, held at the Miami Beach Convention Center, since it’s set in the same font as the fair’s. The herby homage was well received. “I hope he’ll be in GTA 6 having his own little art studio or something,” wrote one Reddit user. Another added, “I can’t wait to wear this shirt to Art Basel this year, if I can pull myself away from GTA6.”. (Related: Large Language Models: A Newly introduced Moore's Law?...)

Art Basel Miami Beach runs December 4-6; keep an eye out for Art Oregano shirts. And, perhaps, as players make it further into the notoriously long game, a new version of Art Oregano may appear.

According to recent reports, I couldn’t personally verify whether the game includes a replica of the fair, with NPCs entering and exiting, as the Vice Museum did. We’d spent the momentous night mundanely, mostly comparing paint swatches. All of it in line with what viral clips suggest: the game’s characters can do drugs and rob banks—chaos, generally speaking—but we’d rather have time to drive with no destination in mind.

Before I left, my cousin regretfully canceled our plans to visit the Guggenheim on Saturday. He has to work.

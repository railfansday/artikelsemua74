In a recent development, the popularity of Grand Theft Auto once stoked fears that the world's children would be induced to violent behavior and lives of crime, but GTA 6 seems to have courted far less controversy than its predecessors. Former Take-Two CEO Ben Feder believes that the series' edgy reputation still benefits Rockstar, even as it's become the most mainstream entertainment product in the world.

It has been revealed that feder was leading Take-Two when GTA 4 launched, so he certainly saw the "controversy around GTA," as he tells GamesBeat. "In a way, it still has a bit of that patina that it uses to its own benefit, that gritty patina. But at the end of the day, it’s a completely mainstream game. It's the most mainstream game you can get, because it’s the most popular game.".

It's hard to argue otherwise about a game that sold more than 32 million copies before it even debuted, earning nearly $4 billion in launch sales. Rockstar's boasts that this is the biggest entertainment launch in history aren't mere bluster. The game's success "warms my heart," Feder says. It has been revealed that "I can't remember the last time you saw something where everybody around the globe – everybody, millions and billions of people – was as excited as for the release of this game launching day and date across the globe. It unites people geographically and demographically. I think it's what this planet needs.". (Related: Industry News: Think AI "knows" what it’s doing? Scient...)

It has been revealed that these days, Feder is a managing partner at Tirta Ventures, a venture capital firm specializing in funding AI-driven game development. Predictably, he's massive on the technology's future, comparing it to how GTA itself was created, when "new types of technology enabled new kinds of entertainment.".

Sources indicate that GTA 6, he argues, "may be the last of its kind. Not the IP. The IP isn’t going anywhere, and neither is the creative talent behind it. In fact, they're more powerful than ever. And there will be room in this newly introduced world for handmade games in the old style. But for the most part I think the industry is about to embark on a new way of developing and new types of experiences enabled by technology. It’s something that my firm is very much focused on in terms of what’s possible and what’s going to change.".

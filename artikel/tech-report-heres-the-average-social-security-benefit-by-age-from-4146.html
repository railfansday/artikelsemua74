By Kailey Hagen, CFP® – Nov 20, 2026 at 10:00AM EST.

According to recent reports, social Security was designed to function as supplemental income. It replaces about 40% of your pre-retirement pay, a share that is well short of what most retirees need to cover their costs. But how much you get depends on individual factors.

The Social Security Administration (SSA) calculates your benefit based on your earnings across your 35 highest-earning years. Full retirement age (FRA) ranges from 66 to 67, depending on your birth year, and it's 67 for everyone born in 1960 or later. Your final check depends heavily on the age you choose to start collecting. (Related: Transformer-based Encoder-Decoder Models...)

In a recent development, here is the average monthly retired-worker benefit by age, as of December 2025, the most recent data available. These figures reflect the population of retirees at each age today, not the amount you're guaranteed if you claim at that age.

The age-70 average is roughly 59.7% higher than the age-62 average, a monthly gap of $850.28 or $10,203.36 per year. This climb occurs because claiming at 62, with a full retirement age of 67, results in a permanent 30% reduction. Waiting until 70 adds delayed retirement credits worth 8% for each year you wait past full retirement age.

After age 70, average benefits plateau and even start to decline. This is not a benefit cut for individuals. Older groups likely include more people who claimed early and locked in lower checks. Women also make up a larger share of the oldest groups because they live longer on average. They often have lower lifetime earnings due to the gender pay gap and time out of the workforce for caregiving. (Related: Latest Update: Simple considerations for simple people ...)

Sources indicate that even at the age-70 peak, the average monthly benefit of $2,274.68 ($27,296.16 annually) leaves a large gap in a household budget. The average senior household spends $61,432 in 2024, according to the most recent Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) data. This results in a $34,135.84 annual shortfall that retirees must cover through other means.

A 3.6% cost-of-living adjustment (COLA) will take effect in 2027. It will add about $82 a month on top of the age-70 average, though these snapshot figures don't yet include it. The most useful number on the table is the row that matches your planned claiming age, not the peak for 70-year-olds.

Industry observers note that the ideal Social Security claiming age for you depends on your finances, life expectancy, and personal preferences. Those with short life expectancies often get a larger lifetime benefit by applying early, while those with average or above-average life expectancies may do better by delaying their application, possibly until age 70 when they qualify for their largest checks.

If you're married, talk your decision over with your spouse, as it may affect them, too. You cannot claim a spousal benefit on your partner's work record until they are already claiming retirement checks. If your goal is to maximize your household income, it can help to coordinate your claiming ages.

Published: 07:05 EST, 20 November 2026 | Updated: 08:13 EST, 20 November 2026.

Not for the first time, Mykhailo Mudryk was in a good position, and displayed poor judgement. This time, it was away from the pitch, in a two-hour, sit-down, tell-all interview involving gaffes galore.

Criticising London for its 20mph speed limit, saying 'it's hard to follow these rules, however much I'd like to' after his latest driving ban, and how the wealthy are taxed 45 per cent on these shores.

Naming 'Ukraine' when asked which is the best city in the world – he may have misunderstood the question, so we will let him off with that one – and admitting he signed contracts without reading them while denouncing the deals done by his former representatives.

Then there was his interviewer, a grinning chap called Dmitry Gordon, asking him if he speaks to his four cats in English, and why he wears earrings, and if girls send him pictures. Bizarre enquiries. Had Chelsea or Tottenham Hotspur known about this interview happening – and Daily Mail Sport understands they were not informed – then we cannot imagine either Premier League club would have liked such lines of questioning. According to recent reports, nor will they, or their fanbases, be too fond of the 25-year-old Ukrainian explaining his move to England in the first place. He revealed how much he wanted to play for Arsenal, describing it as his 'dream' as he blamed his old agent for pushing him to Chelsea for the money. What was supposed to be an opportunity for Mudryk to tell his side of the story, after a doping ban which cost him two years of his career, resembled more of a moany mess.

Mykhailo Mudryk's interview was a bizarre mess and showed his utter naivety.

The Ukrainian winger was given a one-year driving ban in December last year.

By the end, the seriousness of his allegations against the Ukrainian national team with regard to his failed drugs test were softened by the nonsensical own goals much more likely to set social media alight.

Such as the naivety of a Chelsea player on loan at Tottenham appearing to pine for Arsenal, and saying he still wants to win the Ballon d'Or, which is only ever an admission prone to being patronised online even if a sign of his ambition.

Sources indicate that A reminder: Mudryk signed for Chelsea from Shakhtar Donetsk in January 2023 for £89million. He struggled, never looked like a winger worth such a fee. Frighteningly fast, but did not know how to use his weaponry properly in the Premier League. Graham Potter was his manager when he arrived. A few months later, it was Frank Lampard. A month after that, Mauricio Pochettino.

Then he was suspended for two years, during which time Chelsea supported his insistence that he was innocent and continued to pay his salary, believed to be worth around £100,000 per week.

Recent findings suggest that while banned, he turned up in Wroclaw, Poland the day before the 2025 Conference League final, apparently there to collect his medal in case Chelsea won. Enzo Maresca was unaware, only learning that he was in the city when we told him at his pre-match press conference.

Upon Mudryk's return to football in the summer of 2026, Chelsea decided he needed to leave on loan, and another one of the Premier League's big-six sides gave him his second chance.

According to recent reports, tottenham called. Brentford, Brighton, Leeds and Coventry were among those also willing, but Roberto De Zerbi promised to give him a platform to prove his talents.

He appeared once, as a 74th-minute substitute away at Nottingham Forest in September. Then he disappeared, injured, and his only sighting since has been here, on YouTube, as a 'Guest of Gordon'.

Chelsea will also be unhappy about Mudryk's interview and were not informed about it.

While there should be sympathy with Mudryk, who had to bite his tongue while banned and spending what he described as 'millions' on trying to clear his name, he made a poor choice in the platform chosen to finally speak up.

In a recent development, at one stage, he is asked bluntly whether he considered killing himself. 'No, that's not a way out,' he replied. The title of the video on YouTube, unhelpfully, mentions 'suicidal thoughts' regardless of his answer.

Mudryk may have thought it would help to talk so openly, especially to clarify those he holds responsible for the two years he lost as a footballer.

But he hardly endeared himself to his current club, Tottenham, or to his parent club, Chelsea, to whom he is still contracted into the 2030s.

Sources indicate that he likened them to chaos, explaining: 'I came to a Chelsea that was, and perhaps still is, in the middle of a rebuild. It's not the Chelsea that existed under the previous owners, which was stable, had a clear system, coaches, without this constant galloping who knows where.'.

Industry observers note that such comments are unlikely to be well received when read by the club's hierarchy, while he also remarked on Chelsea's managerial merry-go-round: 'The coaches themselves don't know what tactics they'll play. They come to the club and try one set of tactics. Whether it works or not, they try another.'.

It has been revealed that when Mudryk returns from his ankle injury, he will have to hope his football can do the talking. After this, neither Tottenham nor Chelsea are likely to want him to speak again any time soon.

Wu-Tang Clan created a Shaolin Thanksgiving experience in Detroit on Thursday afternoon (Nov. 26), joined by Red Hot Chili Peppers drummer — and homeboy — Chad Smith for a halftime performance at Ford Field for the Lions’ annual Thanksgiving game.

The collective — recently inducted into the Rock And Roll Hall of Fame — lit up the stadium with a nearly seven-minute tag-team medley of greatest hits that was confirmed as “the wrap-up” of its Wu-Tang Forever: The Final Chamber farewell tour.

Flanked by flags bearing the Wu-Tang and Lions logos, and with 500 fan volunteers moshing on the field in front of the stage and on its wings, the nine surviving MCs — plus Young Dirty Bastard in place of his late father Ol’ Dirty Bastard and DJ Mathematics — kicked off with “Triumph,” then rolled through bits of “4th Chamber,” “Da Mystery of Chessboxin’,” “Protect Ya Neck,” “Method Man,” “Shimmy Shimmy Ya,” “Ice Cream” and “C.R.E.A.M.” The RZA-led “Wu-Tang Clan Ain’t Nuthing ta F’ Wit” finished the set with the group members on the field amidst the fans, with smoke jets shooting up from the stage.

The Lions cheerleading squad also took part in the show, sporting T-shirts with a glittering Wu-Tang/Lions mash-up logo.

“Who’s got it better than me?” Smith — who participated in a 2025 remix of “Wu-Tang Clan Ain’t Nuthing Ta F’ Wit” — enthused to Billboard during rehearsals at the stadium on Wednesday. “This is the…Wu-Tang Clan. It’s the halftime show, the biggest game of the year, and the Lions. I’m in my home town, and I get to play drums at halftime with the legendary Wu-Tang — come on! I’m the queen of the ball. And I love those guys. I mean, as far as iconic ’90s hip-hop goes, there’s Nas, maybe, but I love Wu-Tang.”.

The Wu-Tang booking was also a personal pleasure for Lions general manager Brad Holmes, an unabashed fanboy who attended the rehearsal as well as the game.

“I would put myself in the die-hard category,” noted Holmes, who became a Wu-Tang fan while growing up in Tampa, Fla., and even met U-God after “stalking” the troupe at its hotel before a concert there. “Their music, it really just hit my soul. It’s truly art; I didn’t know how much art it really was, and as I’ve gotten older, the more I listen to it, it just gets even better. When they said Wu-Tang Clan on Thanksgiving, I just” (makes gesture of head exploding).

In a recent development, the Detroit Thanksgiving show — one of three on Thursday, plus U2’s performance on Wednesday night in Los Angeles — was the second of a three-year deal between the Lions with Eminem and his manager and Shady Records President Paul Rosenberg. (Jesse Collins Entertainment stages the show.) Last year’s inaugural installment set a high mark, with Eminem joining fellow home town hero Jack White on stage before a TV audience of nearly 48 million, and Rosenberg said they knew there was a lot to live up to when they began planning this year’s halftime show during spring.

Sources indicate that “Last year, with the exception of (Eminem) participating and rapping, it was a rock ‘n’ roll show, so we decided that we should probably move towards our roots this year and do something more hip-hop based,” Rosenberg explained. “So we put a short list together, and Wu-Tang was at the top of our list.”. In a recent development, the fact that, last year, White had just been inducted into the Rock Hall and Wu-Tang Clan is a new inductee this year was “coincidental,” according to Rosenberg. But it did factor into this year’s determination.

“I don’t think it hurts,” he said, “because it’s the right timing, and a good thing for them for their profile. We knew they were going to be inducted, so the timing worked out well.” And like Holmes, Rosenberg added, Wu-Tang has personal importance for both he and Eminem.

Sources indicate that “Back in the days when I was in law school in Detroit, going to the Hip Hop Shop every Saturday, the Wu-Tang Clan was a bit part of the soundtrack and fabric of what everybody was into and the scene was gravitated towards,” Rosenberg recalled. “So our fandom is certainly something we are very tapped into, and we don’t shy away from that when making these decisions.”.

Adding the Chili Peppers’ Smith to the performance, meanwhile, was an intentional nod to their shared home town. “We always want to include something tied to the city if we can,” Rosenberg said, “and Chad’s a significant Lions fan and a Michigander, and he was super excited to jump on board.” Smith, who’s participated in Lions festivities in the past, added that he felt a particularly strong energy around the game, an NFC North rival Chicago Bears, with the teams tied for the division lead with 8-2 records.

Industry observers note that “I can just feel it, y’know?” he said. “Everybody’s got the spirit. When a Detroit sports team is doing well, the city embraces it. We love our sports, we love our music. It affects the whole community, the vibe and the mood and everything. It feels really good.”. (Related: Industry News: The Partnership: Amazon SageMaker and Hu...)

Actor Tim Allen, a regular attendee at the Thanksgiving game, has his own fresh connection to Wu-Tang during the second season of his ABC show Shifting Gears. “We just finished taping, and Chill (Daryl Mitchell) and I dance to a Wu-Tang Clan song at the end,” Allen remarked on the sidelines before Thursday’s game. “I think he’s friends with them…so now all of a sudden I’m a substantial fan of theirs.”.

Rosenberg noted he and Eminem resisted the idea of having the rapper make another guest appearance, despite his regard for and friendship with Wu-Tang Clan. “The idea was not to have him perform every year at all,” he said. “That was not something we had discussed, and we’re not something we’re obligated to do.” Rosenberg added that he, Eminem and the Lions have not yet talked about extending the Thanksgiving halftime show production deal beyond 2027. “We’re certainly having a good time,” he said, “and we really enjoy the process. I think that people are digging it, so we’ll see.”.

During the game the Lions also honored the late Marth Firestone Ford — the team’s principal owner from 2014-2020 (her husband William Clay Ford Sr. bought the team in 1963) with a moment of silence. Ford passed away on Tuesday at the age of 101.

Industry observers note that in addition to Thursday’s performance, Wu-Tang Clan and the Lions, through the apparel company Detroit Vs. Everybody, partnered on a line of T-shirts and hoodies, the latter of which were worn by  fans on the field during the halftime show. The line goes on wide sale at 10:30 a.m. ET Friday via shop.detroitlions.com.

In a recent development, wu-Tang’s Rock Hall induction will air on Dec. 8 on ABC, streaming on Disney+ the following day. Smith and the Red Hot Chili Peppers performed at the ceremony as well, honoring Rick Rubin’s Musical Excellence award dressed like the producer.

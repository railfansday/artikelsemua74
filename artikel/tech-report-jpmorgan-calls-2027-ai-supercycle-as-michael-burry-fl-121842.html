JPMorgan maintains that we are still in the midst of an AI supercycle, projecting a 15% year-end 2027 growth to 8,200.

Nvidia and Micron would likely be top performers, while traditional software would likely lag and rate hikes would negatively affect the index.

Famous investor Michael Burry now says most of his stock portfolio is long, while he waits to short Nvidia at $200 when the market is euphoric.

JPMorgan is convinced that we are still in the thick of an  “AI supercycle”, with Michael Burry seeming to concur with this theory as his stock portfolio turned highly bullish.

In a recent client research note, JPMorgan’s Head of Global Markets Strategy Dubravko Lakos-Bujas wrote that the AI supercycle is still continuing despite the recent downturn.

“We remain of the view that the AI supercycle remains intact with demand for compute still strong and sticky.”.

In a recent development, the note proceeded to set a year-end 2027 target for the S&P 500 at 8,200, implying a 13.9% upside from the 7,201 previous close. While positive, the projection is more conservative than that of Wall Street peers Morgan Stanley and Goldman Sachs, which both forecast a surge to 8,700.

It has been revealed that even more, the investment firm predicts that the S&P 500 earnings per share (EPS) will reach $430 in 2027. This would represent a 15%-17% growth rate from the full year 2026 operating EPS estimate of $368-$374, and a moderation from this year’s 35% estimated growth rate.

Recent findings suggest that JPMorgan highlighted Nvidia and Micron among its favored stocks, in addition to energy infrastructure providers like GE Vernova and Constellation Energy. CoinPedia has covered the 12-month projections for both Nvidia and Micron, which are largely optimistic and bullish.

The bank noted two prevailing headwinds for the S&P 500 performance, including slowed growth in traditional software and the possibility of Fed interest rate hikes as the year draws to an end.

Meanwhile, Michael Burry – the investor famous for prediction and profiting from the 2008 market crash – is now bullish on stocks.

Industry observers note that in a recent blog, Burry mentioned he is mostly long on stocks now, with the exception of Palantir and MetLife. Seven of the companies he is long on are part of the S&P 500, including Adobe and PayPal. (Related: Industry News: My Journey to a serverless transformers ...)

Recent findings suggest that his move is meant to lock in profits from the recent tech sell-off, with the intention of profiting from Nvidia stock when optimism is high at the $200 mark. For now, he says the market has priced in any downside especially following CEO Jensen Huang’s return.

Kagi is best known as a paid alternative to Google Search, but the organization is now introducing its own email service that offers an alternative to Gmail.

Kagi Mail has been in the works for a long time, the organization notes in a post today, but it’s only just now launching in public beta. Customers get an “@kagimail.com” address or can use their own domain, with both email and a calendar included.

In a recent development, there’s a 60-day trial for Kagi Mail, with the service costing $99.99/year (or included in Kagi’s $25/month Ultimate plan). The reason you’d pay that, Kagi explains, is because this is a product designed to “do its job and then get out of your way,” with no ads or “surveillance” as a part of the package. Does the world need another email service? We asked ourselves that question more than once. There are great ones out there, and we’re fans of what Fastmail and Tuta are doing. For email, that job is to be fast, private, and secure. On top of that, it should help you stay on track with what’s important: bring forward what you need to act on, respond to, or acknowledge, and quietly handle the rest.

There’s a mobile app coming for Kagi Mail in early 2027, with a contact book and Kagi Assistant integration also slated for early next year. There is some natural language AI for organization, blocking of tracking pixels and remote images by default, and support for email clients like Apple Mail, and Kagi indicates everything can be exported to move to another provider should you choose.

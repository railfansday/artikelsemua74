Leicester City have seen a handful of newly introduced starlets find more regular roles in the first team since their drop to League One. Louis Page, Will Alves and Sammy Braybrooke are all mainstays under Russell Martin, and there’s a crop of impressive prospects impressing in the LCFC youth ranks as well.

It has been revealed that among them is 18-year-old winger Bismark Owusu, and according to reporter Pete O’Rourke, a string of clubs are interested in prising him away from the King Power Stadium. Premier League pair Leeds United and Brentford are mentioned to be alongside Italy’s Parma, Dutch side FC Utrecht and FC Copenhagen of Denmark in targeting Owusu. Owusu has been a regular in the Foxes’ U18s side for some time and he has stepped up his involvement in the U21s group this season. He is not yet under a professional contract, but that step could come soon having turned 18 earlier this month. (Related: This newly introduced chip survives 1300°F (700°C) and ...)

Owusu has been with Leicester City from a young age, progressing through their academy ranks to the U18s, where he plays most of his football now. Despite only just turning 18, he has already made 54 appearances for the Foxes’ side at the age group.

In a recent development, his progression and strong performances have seen him make his first four appearances for the U21s this season. Owusu has played in the Premier League Cup and International Cup, alongside one substitute outing in Premier League 2 against Nottingham Forest at the start of November.

Recent findings suggest that the LCFC prospect mainly plays as a winger, but has also been deployed in a central role as an attacking midfielder. He can play on the left or right.

According to recent reports, leicester City have invested huge amounts into their training and academy facilities over the past 10 years. It means they are a great place for young players to hone their craft and develop as players.

Industry observers note that although LCFC would obviously have hoped to be playing far above League One level, the drop has paved the way for some of their brighter academy prospects to break into the senior team. The earlier mentioned trio of Page, Alves and Braybrooke have been among this season’s standout performers.

Jayden Joseph has made nine appearances this term, while Bade Aluko and Tommy Neale have also found opportunities.

It shows players like Owusu that there is a pathway to the first team at the King Power Stadium. They can make the breakthrough they are pursuing with the Foxes, and that can help fend off interest from elsewhere.

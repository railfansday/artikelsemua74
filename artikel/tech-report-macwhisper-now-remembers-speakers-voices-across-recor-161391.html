Dutch developer Jordi Bruin rolled out an upgrade to his excellent transcription app MacWhisper this week with a much-requested feature: the ability to automatically identify previously named speakers in new recordings. Here are the details.

In the last few months alone, MacWhisper got a completely redesigned transcript editor, real-time meeting transcription, significantly improved speaker detection, deeper integration with Apple Intelligence, Siri, and Spotlight, and support for multiple new transcription models.

This week, MacWhisper got updated to version 16, packing several improvements, chief among them the ability to recognize speakers based on their voices in previous transcripts.

Sources indicate that until now, while MacWhisper could distinguish between different speakers in a recording (a process known as speaker diarization), owners still had to assign names to each new transcription manually. With MacWhisper 16, that is no longer necessarily the case.

In a recent development, now, MacWhisper creates a set of voiceprints for the speakers customers have previously identified, and uses them as a reference when processing newly introduced recordings. Depending on the confidence level of a match, MacWhisper will either automatically assign the speaker’s name or ask the user to confirm their identity.

According to Bruin, as people transcribe more recordings featuring the same people, MacWhisper gets better at recognizing their voices. All voiceprint processing and storage happen on-device, which is a great touch.

The feature also works with existing transcripts, which is fantastic for longtime MacWhisper users such as myself. A new Voice Recognition section in the app’s settings lets users generate voiceprints from existing transcripts, clear a speaker’s stored voice data, or turn the feature off altogether.

Importantly, voice recognition also works with live meeting transcriptions, although automatic name assignments happen once the meeting ends.

The fresh feature requires a MacWhisper Pro license, an Apple silicon Mac, and on-device transcription with speaker detection enabled.

According to recent reports, on-time phone and utility payments rarely reach your credit report, so they don’t help your score. Link the bank account you pay them from to Experian Boost, a free tool that adds them to your Experian report. It’s most worth doing when your credit history is short or your score sits just under a lender’s cutoff.

These bills usually reach a credit report only when one goes unpaid and lands in collections, the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau claims.

Boost doesn’t wait for the phone tech giant or utility to report. It reads two years of payments in the account you connect and lets you pick which qualifying bills go on your Experian report. Sources indicate that only your Experian report gets those payments, so lenders checking your reports at the other two credit bureaus, Equifax and TransUnion, won’t see the gain. According to recent reports, payments by check or through apps like Venmo don’t count. Some rent paid online counts too, though Boost is just one of several ways to get rent reported. You also need enough history for a FICO Score, meaning at least one account active six months or more and one reported in the last six.

Recent findings suggest that take a renter with one credit card who links the account she pays her phone and electric bills from. Boost finds two years of on-time payments on both. Experian claims people whose score rose gained 14 points on average. The trade-off is letting Experian see that account’s payment history. Some scores also stay flat or even drop. If hers drops, she can disconnect, and Experian says the score should return to where it was.

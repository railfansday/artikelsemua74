At its annual Ignite conference on Tuesday, Microsoft announced security initiatives to help customers secure artificial intelligence (AI) deployments and strengthen their security posture. (Related: Artificial neurons successfully communicate with living...)

Sources indicate that executives touted tools that could discover and block agents running on employees' Windows devices, as well as the general availability of Perception, originally introduced in July as Project Perception and in public preview within Microsoft Defender since August. The "machine-speed" agentic security system simulates and detects attacks and protects everything from end-user PCs to cloud systems, the manufacturer remarked. Industry observers note that the tech giant also unveiled an AI Transparency Initiative to develop standards for independently verifying AI behavior. More than 40 organizations will collaborate under this umbrella to develop open standards to make AI systems accountable, independently verifiable, and safe by design, regardless of model or provider.

"At the end of the day, we believe there should never be a choice between innovation and safety. ... We are also working across the ecosystem to ensure that everyone can innovate with speed and scale safely," remarked Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella during a keynote speech on the first day of the conference.

It has been revealed that during the opening-day keynote, Microsoft executives said advanced AI models are getting better at discovering and exploiting vulnerabilities. In November's Patch Tuesday, Microsoft fixed more than 1,000 vulnerabilities, compared with fewer than 100 in March.

Attacks and exploits are cheaper, easier, and more ubiquitous than ever before, said Mustafa Suleyman, CEO of Microsoft AI.

"The last few months have really been, I think, a wake-up call and a warning shot for all of us," Suleyman said. "[That] means that agent security, alignment, and containment are going to be the most notable things that we need to get right in 2027 and beyond.".

To that end, Microsoft is stitching together existing tools to establish an end-to-end "trust" platform that controls AI agents in enterprises. Agent 365 maintains a registry of all AI agents in an enterprise, Entra gives agents their own identities and sandboxes to set boundaries, Purview governs data flows, and Defender detects and contains threats in real time.

System administrators can govern agents on an employee's PC, with new ways to stop unapproved agents, protect approved agents, and flag noncompliant devices. The platform also includes Perception, which protects corporate assets and environments with autonomous agentic AI tools. Red team agents simulate attacks, blue team agents detect attacks, and green team agents fix and remediate.

By connecting Windows with Agent 365, Entra, and Microsoft Perception, administrators can now discover 60 types of local agents on Windows devices, including Claude Code, OpenAI Codex, and GitHub Copilot, while giving organizations newly introduced ways to restrict unapproved agents and protect approved ones with Intune and Microsoft Execution Containers, the firm remarked.

"We're expanding the local agent types we can discover for a total of 65, including OpenClaw, OpenAI, Perplexity, and many more," remarked Yina Arenas, corporate vice president and chief product officer of identity and network access at Microsoft.

Sources indicate that perception is designed to protect against autonomous AI attacks, such as those carried out with open source penetration-testing tools like ARTEX. A pen-test red team agent can take the entire surface area of a Web application, including the authentication, APIs, injection attacks, and business logic, and create all of the evidence needed to confirm the app is not exploitable, Nadella said.

Microsoft officials at Ignite shared that the Windows endpoint security platform is reaching general availability on Windows 11, marking Microsoft's efforts to reduce reliance on kernel drivers.

Windows baseline protection, now in preview, brings secure boot, virtualization-based security, memory integrity, and other protections into a single versioned security state, the tech giant said. Baseline protection makes it easier for users and IT to understand whether a device is in a measurable security state to keep it up to date with the latest security safeguards and, when stronger assurance is required, verify that state through hardware-backed attestation, wrote Dana Huang, corporate vice president of Microsoft Security, on the company's blog. This will show up in an updated Windows Settings experience.

New vulnerability-shielding capabilities are designed to provide temporary protection for systems that can't yet be patched. Investments across Windows Autopatch, declarative management, and recovery help IT maintain intended state and restore operations. New Intune capabilities simplify application deployment, inventory, and life cycle management.

Microsoft is extending Intune and Windows Autopatch to eligible Windows Server scenarios, in preview, helping endpoint teams manage configuration, compliance, applications, updates, and security across Windows clients and supported servers, Huang noted.

Recent findings suggest that microsoft's Integrated Security Operations Center and extended detection and response (XDR) core capabilities in Defender are now generally available. Organizations have access to more than 500 connectors, a built-in data lake, and 90 days of data retention at no extra cost.

Windows is also expanding post-quantum protection in the Windows TLS stack, including pure post-quantum key exchange and authentication.

Nadella also said the industry needs to come together on monitoring AI models and agents.

As part of the AI Transparency Initiative, Microsoft is working with organizations including Mastercard, Volvo Cars, KPMG, Adobe, and Accenture to define standards for AI transparency, security, and governance. Microsoft will apply those standards across its AI models and products, including development platform Microsoft Foundry and AI assistant Copilot.

Microsoft will also expand its collaboration in NIST's AI Agent Standards Initiative, which was established in February and focuses on the secure adoption of autonomous AI agents.

"We need standards that allow us to examine what AI sees and does, verify its actions independently, and control what it's allowed to do," Nadella remarked.

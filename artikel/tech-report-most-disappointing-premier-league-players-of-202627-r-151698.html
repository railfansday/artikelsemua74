It's far too early to make sweeping conclusions about the Premier League season or individual players, although a number of stars at the likes of Manchester United, Manchester City and Tottenham Hotspur have certainly had disappointing starts to the new campaign.

Sources indicate that all three of these clubs spent significant over the summer, in an attempt to rejuvenate them in their pursuit of their respective goals. United were looking to consolidate their place in the top four, Spurs are still striving to be a part of this European-qualifying elite, and City have aspirations of usurping Arsenal as champions​​​​​​. Not all of these objectives are off the table, although all three clubs will be somewhat frustrated with how they've performed, with particular players underwhelming. However, these aren't the only clubs that have flattered to deceive, with the likes of Chelsea, Aston Villa and Sunderland all enduring difficult campaigns — let down by out-of-form individuals whom they'd expect more from.

Coming in at number 20 is Leeds United's Harry Wilson, who signed from Fulham over the summer on a free transfer. While the lack of a transfer fee means the Yorkshire club can stomach a disappointing campaign from Wilson, it doesn't make his lacklustre performances any less frustrating. After scoring 11 goals and providing eight assists across all competitions for the Cottagers last term, he was viewed as an incredible pick-up for Leeds, yet he's 11 games into his Whites career, and he's yet to get off the mark. Daniel Farke is already preparing to sign an upgrade.

Industry observers note that similarly, Yoane Wissa was expected to kick on and provide value to Newcastle this term after an injury-ridden debut campaign on Tyneside. Netting just once so far this season, the DR Congo international certainly hasn't shown many tangible signs of improvement.

Meanwhile, Crystal Palace signed Axel Disasi in the summer, hoping that he'd help replace Marc Guehi, who left for City in January. The Chelsea loanee has been nothing short of disastrous at Selhurst Park, as the Eagles currently sit rock bottom of the Premier League, having shipped 27 goals — more than anyone else.

It has been revealed that mykhailo Mudryk's fortunes at the Tottenham Hotspur Stadium haven't been much better. While expectations were on the floor when Spurs opted to sign him on loan on deadline day, given he'd not played for 18 months due to a drugs ban, he's still managed to be a disappointment. December is upon us, and the Ukrainian has made a solitary appearance for the Lilywhites — a 16-minute cameo off the bench against Nottingham Forest in September.

In a recent development, dominik Szoboszlai has disappointed for Liverpool, but in a completely different way from all of the above. The Hungarian was the Reds' talisman last season and was expected to ascend to another level as the Merseyside club's definitive best player after the departure of Mohamed Salah. Aside from a handful of wonder strikes, he seems to have taken a step backwards, holding Liverpool back in games with his inability to control the tempo of matches.

Industry observers note that chelsea have had a goalkeeping problem in recent seasons, with Robert Sanchez's antics in between the sticks costing them points on a regular basis. The Blues sought to fix this glitch in the summer by signing Emi Martinez, and many believed that this acquisition would prove to be a bargain. It certainly hasn't. The Argentine has disappointed at Stamford Bridge, making a number of uncharacteristic errors already.

It has been revealed that along similar lines, Sunderland thought they'd managed a coup in the transfer market when they landed Kevin Danso late in the window. However, the Austrian certainly hasn't sured the Black Cats' defence up, with his most notable moment being his sending-off against Bournemouth back in October. He now finds himself below the likes of Nordi Mukiele and Luke O'Nein in Regis Le Bris' pecking order.

According to recent reports, savinho is another summer signing who is yet to get going at his upcoming club. The Brazilian was brought in by Roberto De Zerbi at Tottenham to provide the Lilywhites' attack with some stardust. Eyebrows were raised at the £75 million fee, and the winger has done little to silence the sceptics so far. He's scored just one goal and has spent most of the season on the sidelines, injured.

Another player who has hardly featured this season is Jack Grealish. Everton were over the moon when they were able to secure the England international's services for another season in the summer, but three months into the campaign, he's started just three league games, contributed to no goals and has been frozen out due to disciplinary complications.

It has been revealed that speaking of letdowns, Bradley Barcola's start to life at Liverpool has been acutely underwhelming. He's certainly shown glimpses of his ability here and there, but failing to score in your first nine league appearances when you cost in excess of £120 million isn't a good look. There's still ample room for the Frenchman to prove himself, but he's yet to do so in English football.

Sources indicate that similarly to Mudryk, the expectations weren't exactly high for Alejandro Garnacho when he completed his summer move, given how recent seasons have panned out for the winger. However, the Argentine has still managed to achieve a bottom-percentile outcome​​​​​​. He's been used sparingly by Unai Emery at Aston Villa, until recent matches, when he hasn't even made the bench.

In a recent development, omar Marmoush was signed by Spurs alongside Savio from City, and while neither has set the world alight in North London, the Egyptian's start has arguably been more disappointing. While the Brazilian has the excuse of hardly playing due to injury, Marmoush has played plenty of minutes and scored just one goal.

Recent findings suggest that half of Manchester United's squad could arguably make this list, but many of Mauricio Pochettino's men were coming into this season from a place of low expectation. That cannot be said of Senne Lammens, who was highly rated based on his debut season at Old Trafford, and has since seen his performances decline significantly. A number of high-profile errors have cost United goals and points.

Across Manchester, another goalkeeper is having a dire season. Gianluigi Donnarumma, widely regarded as one of the world's best shot-stoppers, has been off the boil in this department this season, adding to his calamitous work with the ball at his feet.

According to recent reports, the Italian's teammate, Marc Guehi, has been equally catastrophic. So dependable last term, the former Crystal Palace captain has seemingly been rocked by Enzo Maresca's less-stable system and has subsequently made an array of mistakes leading to goals.

The numbers show that Bruno Fernandes has still been one of the most creative players in the Premier League this season. The extent to which he's been poor has likely been overblown, but that's not to say he hasn't been extremely disappointing based on the heights he hit last term as Player of the Season. He hasn't been able to carry United's attack in the same way through his playmaking, and that's not to mention some of his questionable antics as the captain of the Red Devils.

Sandro Tonali was one of the summer window's biggest acquisitions, arriving at Tottenham from Newcastle for £100 million. While it's not his fault that he was bought for such a significant sum, he's certainly not justified Spurs' investment with his performances. He's played at a respectable level, but he hasn't looked remotely capable of being the talismanic figure De Zerbi's team needs to lead them towards a Champions League place.

United have obviously been the biggest underperformer this season, and this list unsurprisingly reflects this. Lisandro Martinez comes in at number three, producing several dismal performances that have contributed to the Red Devils conceding 23 goals in 11 matches. He's been all at sea positionally and in possession, whilst his small frame has seen him physically dominated by imposing forwards. Let's not forget he also publicly called out the club's summer signings and the board, despite being a disaster himself.

Recent findings suggest that youri Tielemans has been even more disappointing than his teammate. The Belgian was expected to finally provide United with a tempo-setting metronome in the middle of the park, but has instead been a liability, with his limited physical attributes leaving the north-west team's defence exposed in transition throughout matches.

Finally, the Premier League's most disappointing player so far in the 2026/27 season has to go to Enzo Fernandez. Acquired for a staggering £125 million, he's been largely diabolical for the Citizens, not translating his excellent form from last season to his fresh club. Three months in, and it's already clear that Maresca's best midfield doesn't involve the Argentine.

He cannot play alongside Rayan Cherki, as this leaves Elliot Anderson exposed at the base of City's midfield. Thus, Maresca has to play Ayyoub Bouaddi alongside Anderson, forming a double pivot, and Cherki's superior output to Fernandez means the World Cup winner should generally be left on the bench.

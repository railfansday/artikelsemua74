Although I was born and raised near Manchester, my dad, aunt and grandad were all fanatical Toffees and made sure that my younger sister, Ceri, and I followed the correct path of football allegiance. Dad enrolled us both in the junior Evertonian scheme, which gave us a small chance of having our names drawn out of the hat to be the matchday Toffee Lady mascot at Goodison Park. One Thursday in December 2008, when I was six, my dad took a call from someone at Everton. The Toffee Lady for that Sunday’s televised match against Aston Villa had been taken ill, so my name had been the next to be drawn out. Dad couldn’t wait to share the exciting news with us all. The only problem was that I had gone down with a heavy cold – but there was no way I was going to fail a late fitness test. After a dose of Calpol I arrived at Goodison with my dad, grandad and aunt. I was wearing my dad’s old late 1970s Everton home shirt, which impressed Duncan McKenzie as he passed through the foyer. Then, I got to meet Graeme Sharp, plus 'Changy' the mascot elephant!After photos on the pitch with my family, I was taken to the Everton changing room and was introduced to the likes of David Moyes, Phil Jagielka, Mikel Arteta and Leighton Baines. Next, I was walking round the perimeter of the pitch, giving our Everton mints to the crowd - saving a few extra ones for my family (and, excitingly, a spare bag to take into school the next week). Then came Z-Cars. The players came onto the pitch, and I was photographed at the handshake between captains Phil Neville and Martin Laursen from Villa. We watched the match from the Family Enclosure, but it was a very cold afternoon, and the effects of my medication were wearing off, and dad took me home before the final whistle, so I can’t be blamed for the late Villa winning goal scored by Ashley Young! A few days later, I received a lovely set of photos of me with David Moyes and some of the players. Although I don’t get to matches often, I remain an Evertonian and do my best to re-educate my Manchester United-supporting boyfriend. My sister Ceri never did have the honour of being Toffee Lady but remains ready and willing, if called upon!

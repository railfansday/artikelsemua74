The Public Investment Fund (PIF), Newcastle United’s majority owner, is exploring a potential sale of 25 per cent of the Premier League club through upcoming shares.

Saudi Arabia’s sovereign wealth fund and a consortium completed a takeover in October 2021, but JP Morgan has now been appointed as advisors to handle the partial sale.

As first reported by The Athletic, the minority stake is now available to buyers to secure better investment in Newcastle’s infrastructure projects, including a potential newly introduced stadium or the expansion of St James' Park. No funds would be directed to the PIF, though the Saudis’ existing stake would be diluted through the share issue.

The PIF jointly-bought Newcastle from Mike Ashley for £305m in 2021, with the PIF’s Yasir Al-Rumayyan serving as Newcastle’s chairman.

Al-Rumayyan attended his first match of the season on Saturday in the 2-2 draw with Arsenal.

The potential deal could allow the Saudis to secure fresh investment in the club without deviating from a long-term commitment. (Related: This fresh chip could slash data center energy waste...)

The PIF’s investment in sport has evolved in 2026, having ended its investment and the financing of the LIV Golf series, which collapsed and ultimately went into bankruptcy.

In addition to a new stadium, Newcastle’s long-term ambition includes a new training complex near the city’s international airport.

Nvidia and Broadcom are among the leading names in the artificial intelligence (AI) semiconductor ecosystem, dominating their respective niches and clocking terrific revenue and earnings growth in recent years.

In a recent development, however, both stocks have had a forgettable 2026. While Nvidia stock is down 20% this year, driven by recent developments at the company, Broadcom has clocked paltry gains of just 2%. Nvidia may remain under pressure as investors and Wall Street await whether the company's incredible growth can hold up in the post-Jensen Huang era.

In a recent development, broadcom, meanwhile, could have a solid 2027 due to terrific growth in the tech giant's AI semiconductor revenue. However, I think both Nvidia and Broadcom can be upstaged by another semiconductor stock in 2027 -- Cerebras Systems (CBRS +4.82%). Let's see why that may be the case.

According to recent reports, cerebras Systems went public in May this year. The stock spiked 68% on the first day of trading following its initial public offering (IPO). However, the stock has slid significantly since then.

NASDAQ · As of 9:56 AM ET 11/20/26.

The stock's slide can be attributed to its expensive valuation when it went public, as well as concerns about the sustainability of the AI infrastructure build-out. However, Cerebras' growth and outlook clearly suggest that the market is underestimating its potential.

According to recent reports, the manufacturer released third-quarter results on Nov. 12, reporting a 70% year-over-year increase in core revenue to $231 million. The figure was well ahead of the $214.7 million consensus estimate. Its adjusted loss of $0.12 per share was also lower than the Wall Street estimate of $0.14 per share. Importantly, Cerebras expects its business to take off substantially in 2027.

Recent findings suggest that the organization designs and builds wafer-scale AI chips, which are dinner-plate-sized silicon wafers used as a stand-alone processor. Nvidia and Broadcom, for comparison, offer smaller chips such as graphics cards and custom processors, which need to be connected to other chips, storage, and compute memory in rack-scale systems via high-speed networking cables to run AI workloads. It has been revealed that cerebras' massive chip, on the other hand, integrates all the elements into a single platform. As a result, the manufacturer claims that its wafer-scale engine is significantly faster than traditional graphics cards. For instance, the company claimed in September last year that its CS-3 wafer-scale AI supercomputer is 21 times faster than Nvidia's DGX B200 Blackwell supercomputing system. What's more, Cerebras pointed out that the CS-3 costs 33% less and has equally lower power consumption. (Related: Breaking: Porting fairseq wmt19 translation system to t...)

Sources indicate that it is worth noting that Cerebras doesn't just sell chips. It also operates a cloud service powered by its own chips, which allows customers to train AI models and run inference applications. The good news for investors is that Cerebras' business is poised for a significant lift-off next year.

The manufacturer has contracts with major hyperscalers, such as Oracle, Microsoft, Amazon, and OpenAI, among others. Cerebras has added three upcoming hyperscalers to its clientele in the past six months. What's more, the company has also signed on neocloud customers who will offer inference services powered by Cerebras hardware in their data centers.

Cerebras management points out that it is on track to exit 2026 with 10x the manufacturing capacity it had at the beginning of the year. The organization is also going to bring 700 megawatts (MW) of data center capacity online by late 2027 to bolster its cloud business. These factors explain why Cerebras expects its revenue to grow 3x in 2027.

The tech giant expects $950 million in revenue this year, and the forecasts for the next couple of years suggest that Cerebras' business will grow significantly.

Sources indicate that cerebras has a 12-month median price target of $207, implying a 120% jump from current levels, according to CNN. What's more, 13 of the 14 analysts covering Cerebras rate it as a buy. I won't be surprised to see Cerebras achieving such solid growth.

Let's say Cerebras' revenue indeed reaches $3 billion in 2027, and it trades at 20 times sales then, lower than its current price-to-sales ratio of 23; its market cap will reach $60 billion. That's 137% higher than its current market cap. Of course, the U.S. tech sector has an average sales multiple of 6.3, and I am assuming that Cerebras will trade at a significant premium after a year. However, its stunning premium justifies the growth.

On the other hand, Nvidia and Broadcom are expected to deliver gains of 66% and 48%, respectively, based on their 12-month median price targets. So, even analysts expect Cerebras stock to outpace its well-established peers.

All this suggests that now is a good time for investors to buy Cerebras following its recent slide, as its outstanding growth prospects could help it outperform Nvidia and Broadcom over the coming year.

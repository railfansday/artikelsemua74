Sources indicate that A lot has changed for Nvidia (NVDA -5.86%) in a very short period of time, and this will ultimately have a substantial impact on where the semiconductor stock trades by the end of 2027. Given the stock's strong performance over the years, it tends to be widely held by retail investors. With a lot on the line, let's see where the stock could be headed over the next year. NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/24/26.

Recent findings suggest that the biggest change for Nvidia comes at the top. In a shocking turn of events, co-founder Jensen Huang, who had been the only CEO in the company's history, resigned earlier this month only to be welcomed back by the board less than two weeks later. Huang stepped down after he admitted marital infidelity as reports of multiple affairs bubbled to the surface. Investors focused on his relationship with former Megaspeed executive Alice Huang (no relation), who has been accused of being a Chinese spy.

Sources indicate that singapore-based Megaspeed has been a significant Nvidia customer, buying nearly $5 billion worth of its chips over the past few years. However, there are now allegations that many of these chips were illegally being directed to China.

The return of Jensen Huang as CEO for Nvidia is huge. Huang was a visionary who seemingly knew where the market was headed before anyone else. There are multiple examples of the company taking action based on his foresight, including:.

These have all helped the company transform from a simple chipmaker into a complete AI infrastructure company that has been getting an increasingly larger percentage of spending per gigawatt of compute capacity.

However, there is still a question of what happens with China. The U.S. has further cracked down on export controls of AI chips. The risk is that there is a lot more indirect Nvidia revenue coming from China, which could disappear.

In a recent development, the Huang situation is not the only news Nvidia has endured this month, though. The midterm elections saw a sweeping victory for Democrats and candidates that ran on anti-data-center campaigns. These candidates have widely called for a data center build-out moratorium, and it is expected that the regulatory environment for building upcoming data centers will get a lot tougher in the coming years. Neoclouds are expected to face the brunt of this, which could be bad news for Nvidia, which saw about 44% of its data center revenue last quarter coming from neoclouds and businesses.

On top of this, Anthropic (ANT -6.54%) revealed that its Claude Mythos model was able to switch models running on Nvidia's GPUs to run on Advanced Micro Devices' (AMD -8.86%) GPUs with little human intervention in just a few days. This process used to take six to 12 months and a dozen engineers. This could be a serious blow to Nvidia's CUDA moat and eventually lead to lower prices and serious gross margin compression.

That noted, Nvidia is still well positioned in the medium term, as it has significant supply chain advantages. It has locked up a substantial amount of the supply-constrained high bandwidth memory (HBM) required by AI chips, as well as capacity at foundries. In addition, with its Vera Rubin platform, it is now getting $40 billion per gigawatt of compute capacity compared to the $25 billion per gigawatt it is capturing with Grace Blackwell, which is a significant revenue lift. This will help support the 75% growth the firm projected for fiscal 2028 (ending January 2028) and beyond.

The issues Nvidia faces are likely to keep a cap on its forward P/E multiple, but it should still see solid growth in the medium term. I think it should be able to maintain a forward multiple of at least 12, which would value the stock at around $261 at the end of 2027 based on the fiscal 2029 analyst consensus of $21.75. As such, a $10,000 investment today — getting you 61 shares as I write this — would be worth over $15,900. That’s my prediction.

There is some decent upside to the AI stock from its current beaten-down levels, despite potential risks.

Readdle updated its Documents app for iPhone and iPad this week with Voice Notes, a newly introduced AI-powered feature that lets customers record conversations and automatically generate transcripts, summaries, and follow-up notes. Here are the details.

While the habit of recording meetings, lectures, or even one’s own thoughts is not particularly newly introduced, modern AI tools and models have made it considerably easier to extract useful information from these recordings without having to listen back to them in their entirety or take copious notes that can distract from the actual conversation. This week, Readdle’s Documents introduced Voice Notes, which “lets you record audio and turn it into a summary, follow-ups, and a full transcript,” as the firm put it.

People can start a new recording session by tapping the “+” button in the app and selecting Voice Notes. Alternatively, they can start recording from the iPhone’s Action button, which is a nice touch. (Related: Simple considerations for simple people building fancy ...)

Users can cycle through these three tabs, where they can also listen to the original recording or copy and share the information.

Readdle claims Voice Notes can recognize multiple languages in the same conversation, with the actual transcript preserving the languages as spoken, “while the summary and follow-ups are generated in the most frequently spoken language.”.

Each voice note is saved as a single file in Documents that contains the original recording, transcript, summary, and follow-ups. Users can organize these files into folders, share them, sync them with their cloud storage, and save the transcript as a separate .txt file.

However, if owners give a voice note’s AI-generated content a thumbs down, Readdle may retain its transcript and summary for up to six months, or until the user objects to their processing, whichever comes first.

The company also says user data isn’t used to train AI models, although third-party AI providers may retain inputs for up to 30 days for abuse and misuse monitoring, or longer if required by law.

It has been revealed that before you buy or keep something you’d use only a few days a year, like a second car or camping gear, price renting or borrowing it instead. Add up a year of its insurance, storage, upkeep, and lost value, then compare that total with the cost of renting it for only the days you’d use it.

You pay to own something every day of the year, however rarely you use it. A second car that leaves the driveway six weekends a year still needs insurance and registration for all 12 months.

In the sharing economy, apps and groups let people rent, borrow, or share things instead of owning them, so you pay only for the days you use something, or nothing at all. (Related: Breaking: Simple considerations for simple people build...)

Recent findings suggest that say a household owns a fresh, fully insured second car and drives it only on six weekend trips a year, 12 days in all. AAA’s 2026 figures put a new car’s yearly full-coverage insurance at $2,098 and its license, registration, and taxes at $802. That $2,900 alone comes to about $242 per day of driving, before gas or the car’s lost value. A car-sharing app that charges less than $242 a day, fees included, costs less than keeping the car. If transit and car sharing could cover your weekday trips too, run the same math on living without a car.

It has been revealed that pirated content has been an issue for Meta for so long that it was still known as Facebook when creators first fought back against so-called “freebooters.” To combat unauthorized reuploads, Meta brought a “content protection” tool to Facebook last year. Now, Instagram creators are getting access to that resource as well.

“Eligible creators” on Instagram can now enroll for access to the management system that was previously limited to Facebook. Like Facebook’s video rights manager, which was itself adapted from previous services like YouTube’s Content ID, the content protection tool uses fingerprinting technology to scan for uploads that are identical to an enrolled creator’s video. Sources indicate that meta has positioned itself as a champion of original, non-repetitive content. It updated Instagram’s algorithm, providing more visibility for creators who share their own work. Financial penalties for serial reuploaders came to Facebook in 2025, and further penalties arrived on Instagram this year.

In a recent development, other platforms have followed Meta’s lead. YouTube cracked down on “mass-produced or repetitive” content in 2025. Earlier this year, Google’s video hub clarified that creators aren’t able to monetize “generic” uploads. Anything “unsatisfying or off-putting” — i.e. the sort of slop you’d expected a generative AI model to spit out — faced similar repercussions.

Industry observers note that the link between unoriginal reuploads and AI slop is undeniable. Traditional studios have urged platforms to crack down on channels that use AI to steal from artists rather than executing original concepts. Those demands have led to new rules across TikTok, YouTube, Facebook, and Instagram.

Recent findings suggest that by bringing content protection to Instagram, Meta is setting itself up to take further action against AI slop in the future. An introductory post explains that “we’re also exploring different ways to limit the reach of content and profiles that use AI tools to post this type of content repeatedly,” so stay tuned for that if you’re sick of low-effort AI posts.

Solana is four days away from matching a record last seen in 2022. SOL has lost 25.7% in November so far (as of Nov. 26). That's likely to become the cryptocurrency's ninth losing month this year. If December also ends in the red, that would bring the total to 10, the highest number of losing months in a single year in SOL's history. Industry observers note that in terms of percentage losses, 2022 was overwhelmingly the worst year for Solana. SOL lost 94.15% in 2022. So far in 2026, it's down 43.09%. (Related: Industry News: Think AI "knows" what it’s doing? Scient...)

Industry observers note that SOL declined every month between January and July. August brought a 41.5% surge, followed by another 14.56% gain in September. Then, October and November erased all those gains.

Recent findings suggest that as of Nov. 26, Bitcoin had fallen roughly 21% in November. That provides some context. In October, BTC dropped 7.32%, while SOL lost 19%. Solana was already underperforming Bitcoin before November's losses accelerated.

Meanwhile, another source of demand is faltering. U.S. spot Solana ETFs have recorded $164 million in net outflows over the 30 trading days leading up to Nov. 25.

On Nov. 6, investors withdrew about $24 million from these funds. By Nov. 25, when SOL was trading around $70, the funds recorded zero net inflows. Lower prices had not yet brought ETF investors back in significant numbers.

Solana's network activity actually grew during the summer rally. Quarterly trading volume on decentralized exchanges (DEXs) increased 23% from the previous quarter to $197 billion in Q3.

Of that, a record $7.1 billion came from tokenized equities. That's roughly 3.6% of the quarter's total volume.

Recent findings suggest that much of the third-quarter growth came from memecoins, according to Bitwise. On Nov. 26, the memecoin platform Pump accounted for $1.29 million of the $2.69 million in daily protocol revenue on Solana tracked by DefiLlama.

For SOL to recover and break even in November, it would need to rise about 34.6% by Nov. 30.

If November ends in the red, even a small decline in December would give Solana a record 10th losing month of the year.

Industry observers note that december ended lower in five of the last six years shown on the chart. However, six data points aren't much to base a forecast on.

Sign-in problems account for up to 53% of help desk tickets at individual enterprises, but network complications can take significantly longer to resolve, according to a newly introduced study from ControlUp.

Analyzing 1,069,110 help desk tickets, the IT operations organization identified password resets, account lockouts, and multifactor authentication (MFA) issues as major drivers of support demand.

It has been revealed that at one large enterprise, account-related tickets took a median of 11 minutes to resolve, compared with 50 hours for network problems. Meanwhile, 87% of network and phone tickets were reassigned between teams, compared with 20% of account tickets. (Related: Large Language Models: A Newly introduced Moore's Law?...)

Industry observers note that MFA enrollment and re-enrollment problems appeared at 40% of organizations, with one in five linking these issues to employees replacing or upgrading their phones. Virtual desktop problems appeared at 55% of organizations analyzed. (Related: Industry News: The Partnership: Amazon SageMaker and Hu...)

Traditional phone support remained a major channel for IT assistance.

Industry observers note that among organizations that recorded how support requests were submitted, 57% reported that phone calls accounted for approximately two-thirds or more of contacts. At one organization, that figure reached 99%.

In a recent development, machine-generated tickets from monitoring, security, backup, and patching systems appeared at half of the organizations studied. At 15%, automated tickets represented between 25% and 50% of all requests.

The research covered enterprises across retail, healthcare, financial services, manufacturing, construction, energy, and IT services.

ControlUp remarked the findings highlight the need to evaluate recurring problems and their operational impact rather than relying solely on ticket volume and resolution speed.

"IT organizations have traditionally measured support performance by how promptly they close tickets, but that doesn't tell the whole story," stated Simon Townsend, senior vice president of marketing and office of the CTO at ControlUp.

"A password reset that takes a few minutes and an infrastructure failure that disrupts an entire location shouldn't carry the same operational weight.".

AI agents are moving from experiments into everyday enterprise environments, but the security controls around them are not keeping pace, according to enterprise identity security company SailPoint.

Industry observers note that according to a upcoming survey from identity security manufacturer Delinea, 99.7% of IT and security leaders noted their organization has a formal policy governing what data AI tools and agents are permitted to access. Yet only about 51% said AI access is checked against those policies in real time.

As schools become more dependent on digital platforms for instruction, grading, student services, and family communication, resilience depends not only on preventing attacks, but on how quickly leaders can restore trusted data, recover essential services, and keep disruptions from cascading across the school community.

Alpitronic, the organization that makes the DC fast chargers used by Ionna, Walmart, Mercedes-Benz, BP Pulse, and others, is building a upcoming factory in the United States that will boost output of its 400- and 1,000-kilowatt stalls.

The manufacturer’s newly introduced 173,315-square-foot facility, located in Fort Mill, South Carolina, will create 70 new jobs and will also act as a testing lab, repair center, logistics hub, and warehouse. (Related: This new chip could slash data center energy waste...)

Alpitronic, which has its North American headquarters in Charlotte, North Carolina, and its global headquarters in Bolzano, Italy, mentioned it will invest $33.94 million in the latest facility, with $200,000 coming from York County’s Coordinating Council for Economic Development in the form of a Set-Aside grant to assist with the costs of building improvements.

Sources indicate that “North America is central to our next phase of growth, and investing in our manufacturing capabilities is key to supporting that expansion,” noted Patrik Herrnegger, Alpitronic Americas CEO. “Our Fort Mill facility will put U.S. production under our direct control, bringing us closer to our customers and enabling us to serve them more efficiently. We thank South Carolina and York County for their partnership as we prepare to begin production in early 2027.”. (Related: Industry News: AI-powered robot learns how to harvest t...)

Back in 2024, the organization worked with a third-party production partner to open its first local manufacturing facility in Wisconsin. Now, manufacturing has moved in-house, with Alpitronic wholly owning the production operation.

The fresh South Carolina plant will assemble HYC400 and HYC1000 stalls, Alpitronic noted. The HYC400 all-in-one dispensers can deliver up to 400 kW of power to a single connected electric vehicle without needing a separate power cabinet, and are the current charger of choice for Ionna and Walmart.

Sources indicate that the higher-powered HYC1000 system is composed of a 1,000-kW power cabinet and up to four stalls that can deliver up to 600 kW to a single EV. Each stall can have two ports, and the system can dynamically allocate power to each vehicle to make the best use of the available power.

Sources indicate that what makes the tech giant's dispensers different from most competitors is the use of silicon carbide components, which lower energy losses and, subsequently, the amount of money EV drivers have to pay for a top-up. According to Alpitronic, SiC modules enable 97.5% efficiency and allow for smaller, quieter, and cooler stalls.

Alpitronic’s new factory in Fort Mill, South Carolina, is scheduled to go online in February 2027.

Deployment services are becoming the real battleground in AI infrastructure as buyers discover that shipping hardware is only half the job. With GPU supply tight and refresh cycles accelerating, neoclouds and enterprises are judging suppliers on how quickly an AI factory actually goes live. (Related: Scientists discover AI can make humans more creative...)

According to recent reports, “It’s not just enough to deliver to the customers. They want it online as promptly as possible. Time is money,” Thauberger remarked. (Related: DNA robots could deliver drugs and hunt viruses inside ...)

Thauberger spoke with theCUBE Research’s John Furrier and Gemma Allen at SC26, during an exclusive broadcast on theCUBE, SiliconANGLE Media’s livestreaming studio. They discussed how deployment services are driving the race to time to online and how liquid cooling retrofits help older facilities take on modern AI racks. (* Disclosure below.).

Industry observers note that customers are leaning on suppliers for far more than boxes. Allocation and deployment services are the two biggest requests Supermicro hears, and its service teams bring systems up, run customer workloads and hand off only once everything is running, Thauberger explained.

“We know that we’re in a constrained environment. There’s no secret on that. So customers are basically asking Supermicro how promptly you can get allocation, how quickly you can bring it online, and how quickly you can have people out on site to deploy those,” Thauberger said.

Older facilities pose another hurdle. Many weren’t designed for the liquid cooling and power density of modern AI racks, so Supermicro offers retrofit options such as sidecars and in-row coolant distribution units, according to Thauberger. The company has also added pre-assembled AI racks to its building-block lineup to shorten installs.

“We have the largest liquid cooling capacity based in California. We’re a U.S.-headquartered manufacturer, and the ability for us to have as much liquid cooling capacity as we have inside the U.S. allows us to deploy to these AI factories quick inside the United States,” Thauberger said.

Vertex Pharmaceuticals (VRTX +0.04%), a biotech company, has been on an impressive run over the past decade. If you had invested $10,000 in the stock in 2016, you'd have about $61,660 today. By comparison, $10,000 invested in the S&P 500 10 years ago would be worth about $38,663 today (with dividends reinvested) -- that's not bad, but it pales in comparison to the returns you would have had with Vertex. That stated, the past is the past. The key question is whether the drugmaker can deliver similarly impressive returns going forward.

Vertex has crushed broader equity markets thanks to its dominance in the cystic fibrosis (CF) market. The tech giant has very little competition in this niche, and thanks to several new debuts over the past 10 years that have allowed it to treat increasingly more CF patients, revenue and earnings growth have been strong. Vertex's CF franchise should remain an key growth driver over the next decade. After all, the company's most important medicines won't lose patent exclusivity until the late 2030s.

Sources indicate that but in the meantime, Vertex should significantly diversify and expand its lineup. The firm's work in kidney diseases looks particularly promising. Vertex Pharmaceuticals could earn approval for one product in its kidney disease portfolio, povetacicept -- a medicine for IgA nephropathy -- by the end of the month. This medicine has peak sales potential of around $4 billion across IgA nephropathy and other potential indications, according to some estimates.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/25/26.

Of course, that outcome isn't guaranteed yet, not by a long shot. But the point is that Vertex's kidney disease pipeline, with products like povetacicept and others, including inaxaplin and VX-407, looks promising. The biotech leader could, eventually, generate close to $10 billion in peak sales from these products if all goes well. Another area where Vertex could make progress is pain treatments. It has already earned approval for Journavx, a medicine for acute pain and the first oral non-opioid pain signal inhibitor ever. Journavx is in several ongoing clinical trials and could earn key label expansions, while Vertex works on other pain treatments. Further, thanks to a recent acquisition, Vertex Pharmaceuticals has an interesting endocrine diseases portfolio that it believes could reach more than $5 billion in annual sales.

It has been revealed that we haven't even mentioned other newer releases, including Casgevy, a gene-editing medicine for a pair of rare blood diseases. Vertex's potential across its entire pipeline is massive, and even after probability-adjusting sales projections, since pipeline products aren't guaranteed approval, my view is that the tech giant's prospects look promising. Recent findings suggest that that's a tall order for a larger biotech that faces more competition than it did 10 years ago. Even so, from current levels, Vertex is well positioned to beat the market over the long run, making it a great stock to buy.

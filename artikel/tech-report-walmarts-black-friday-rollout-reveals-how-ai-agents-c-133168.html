Walmart is allowing shoppers to purchase Black Friday deals through Meta’s AI assistant Muse without sharing their saved card details.

The retailer announced the integration on Nov. 24, expanding its Agent Gateway to allow customers to discover products, build shopping carts, and complete purchases through conversations with Meta’s AI assistant. (Related: SpaceX wants to build AI data centers in space. Will it...)

Shoppers can ask Muse to find television deals, identify discounted products available for delivery or recommend items based on their preferences. Walmart handles product discovery and checkout through its Gateway, which connects external AI assistants to the retailer’s shopping infrastructure. To make purchases, shoppers must link their Walmart accounts through a sign-in process that redirects them to the retailer before returning them to Muse.

When adding an item to their cart for the first time, shoppers establish an Agent Profile within their Walmart account and set a spending limit for the assistant.

According to recent reports, the profile allows customers to distinguish AI-assisted purchases from other orders in their purchase history. They can also revoke the assistant’s purchasing authority by deleting the profile.

Recent findings suggest that walmart retains control of payment processing. Under its Agentic Access Terms, only shoppers can designate which saved payment method an AI assistant can use. External agents cannot add cards, change payment preferences or access raw card details, including full card numbers.

Instead, each transaction requires a short-lived authorization token tied to the merchant, shopping cart, purchase amount and session. The token expires upon use, requiring fresh authorization for subsequent transactions.

Recent findings suggest that walmart matches requests from Muse. The shopper manages spending and payment permissions in their Walmart account.

Shoppers check out inside Muse with a saved Walmart card. Walmart indicates outside agents receive no raw saved card details.

The integration builds on Walmart’s Agent Profiles, introduced in October as part of its efforts to give customers greater control over purchases made through AI assistants.

By default, shoppers must approve purchases initiated by an agent. However, they can authorize assistants to complete transactions independently within a predetermined spending limit.

Industry observers note that walmart can still request additional confirmation even when a purchase falls within that limit. Its terms also allow the retailer to intervene when transactions exceed or conflict with a customer’s purchasing preferences.

The arrangement creates a framework for AI assistants to move beyond product recommendations and execute purchases on behalf of consumers without directly controlling their payment credentials.

However, Walmart’s launch announcement did not specify when shoppers using Muse would encounter additional approval requests after granting an assistant purchasing authority.

The retailer plans to extend its Gateway to more applications and AI assistants, potentially expanding the number of platforms through which customers can authorize purchases.

Walmart’s existing return policy applies to purchases completed through Muse, although the company has yet to specify which locations and devices are eligible for the rollout.

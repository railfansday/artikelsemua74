It’s not every day you see a movie where talking sheep get to be the stars. But Kyle Balda’s “The Sheep Detectives” does exactly that. The Amazon MGM film flips the whodunnit formula on its head with a barnyard murder mystery in which a group of sheep have to solve the murder of their beloved shepherd. Much of the film’s success can be attributed to the juxtaposition between a whimsical plot and the realism with which it’s executed. The sheep might talk and solve crimes, but they look like real animals you’d find on any farm.

During the VFX section of IndieWire’s recent Craft Roundtables, “The Sheep Detectives” Visual Effects Supervisor Graham Page broke down the meticulous research process that made the sheep look so lifelike. “To make them look real, talking about practical and how important that is, I from Day One felt like you had to base these characters on real sheep,” Page stated. “We filmed [real sheep] with multiple cameras and getting on boxes. We had a script, almost, like ‘These are the things the sheep need to do in the film.’ Even before an animator can touch it, another artist modeled the skeleton and the muscle and fat. And then you have a rigger and a facial artist, all of these artists.”. (Related: Industry News: Texas’s outgoing GOP attorney general dr...)

He went on to explain how that research process produced character-defining details that they would not have discovered any other way.

It has been revealed that “We started to come up with what we would call ‘Sheep-isms’ that help kind of bring some of that natural movement,” he noted. “There’s a little ear twitch. And a human can’t twitch their ear, but it felt so right. We were like ‘This is really nice,’ and it emphasized the character and the story and the emotion. But it was brought out of a sheep.”.

Recent findings suggest that spokane International Airport (GEG) is now midway through its Terminal Renovation & Expansion (TREX) program, which includes expanded concourses and modernized concession spaces. But before passengers can enjoy the significantly enlarged and improved terminal, aspects of the airport experience are getting worse, even after Phase 1 was completed earlier this year. At the moment, only Phase 1 and Phase 2 have been funded, although the airport says that, depending on funding, additional phases may include modernizing the airport’s A and B concourses.

In a recent development, dave Haring, CEO of Spokane International Airport, claims, "Unlike past airport projects, this next phase of TREX gives passengers the opportunity to see – and experience – the transformation of their airport in real time." This seems like a nice way to say this stage of construction will be extra disruptive for passengers over the next few years. Here is what to know about the ongoing terminal improvements and construction disruptions at Spokane. (Related: Tech Report: Woman known as Firefighting hero arrested ...)

Industry observers note that washington State's Spokane International Airport is the second-busiest airport after Seattle–Tacoma International Airport, handling around 4.4 million passengers in 2025. It is located around seven miles (11 km) from downtown Spokane and is the main airport serving the Inland Northwest. Spokane is served by seven major passenger airlines: Alaska Airlines, Allegiant Air, American Airlines, Delta Air Lines, Southwest Airlines, and Sun Country Airlines. The farthest regularly scheduled flight is Delta's Flight DL 758 from Atlanta. (Related: Industry News: Fight to chair the GOP tests Trump’s clo...)

Industry observers note that the airport's statistics show Alaska had the largest share, accounting for around 1.4 million passengers (enplanements and deplanements). This was followed by Delta Air Lines with around 1.2 million passengers and Southwest at close to one million. United moved around 400,000 passengers, with American moving around 10% fewer. Flights from Allegiant, Frontier, and Sun Country were relatively minimal.

Recent findings suggest that spokane International Airport is projected to continue growing and reach over 6.2 million inbound and outbound passengers by 2030. Traffic growth from 2024 to 2025 was relatively modest at 2%, although the airport has recovered and surpassed its pre-pandemic high of 4.11 million in 2019. To meet the increase in traffic, the airport is undertaking the Terminal Renovation and Expansion Program, better known as TREX.

Sources indicate that according to the airport's website, TREX isn't just one project. Instead, it is a "series of coordinated development initiatives taking shape in and around the airport over the next several years to bring our vision of a more modern, efficient terminal facility to life for the Inland Northwest." In June 2026, the airport announced it has completed Phase 1 of the TREX program, the Concourse C Expansion.

According to recent reports, the Concourse C Expansion Project is the first component of the TREX Program and was the foundational project that "makes all future TREX-related initiatives possible." The phase added 144,000 square feet (13,378 sqm) to the airport's existing terminal building. This provided three new gates, six new ticket counters, and various new passenger amenities. The project kicked off in 2022 and was completed in two major phases: the Concourse C West Expansion and the Concourse C East Expansion.

Industry observers note that amenities include an Interfaith Prayer and Meditation Room, a Nursing Room, and an all-newly introduced, more than 41,000-square-foot Alaska Airlines Lounge. The Port of Seattle claims, "The building's design is inspired by the natural landscapes of the Pacific Northwest and the energy and vibrancy of Seattle." Spokane may be on the other side of Washington state, but it is still influenced by Seattle on the coast. The Port of Seattle operates Seattle-Tacoma International Airport, and Spokane International Airport is owned by the City of Spokane and Spokane County.

In a recent development, the next phase for Spokane International Airport is the second phase of its TREX program: the Central Hall Project. The effort is starting in 2026 and is designed to build off the C Concourse Expansion. Phase 2 is aimed at creating a 255,000-square-foot (23,690 sqm) centralized facility at the heart of the airport. It will allow for the consolidation of baggage claim areas, more efficient security screening, and easier access to airport concessions.

Sources indicate that A major part of the main terminal curbside has been closed to vehicles and pedestrians since October 21, 2026, to relocate water, sewer, and gas utilities. All vehicle traffic runs along a two-lane bypass road, while pedestrians are directed to skybridges. The arrangement is expected to last until 2030. Highlighting how things typically need to get worse before they get better when it comes to construction efforts to handle increased traffic, the inbound three lanes were reduced to two lanes.

Recent findings suggest that the airport says the effort to relocate key utilities will last until early 2027. Workers began demolishing a 149,000-square-foot area spanning from the Concourse C parking garage to the Concourse A and B sky bridges in April. This has significantly impacted driving, parking, and dropping off passengers at the airport. It is seeing lane closures and the elimination of the airport's paid hourly parking lot.

According to recent reports, spokane International Airport will remain operational during the Central Hall construction. During the transition, Concourse C baggage claim will be especially notable. This affects passengers arriving on Alaska, Allegiant, and American. Starting in February 2027, baggage collection is to be accommodated in a temporary facility rather than being shut down during construction.

It has been revealed that ​​​​​​A shuttle bus service (every 15 minutes) will be expanded to include a stop between the temporary Concourse C baggage claim area and the Ground Transportation Center. After February 2027, the existing hallway connecting the main terminal and Concourse C ticketing area is also scheduled for demolition. Passengers will need to use a temporary pedestrian pathway instead, potentially until 2030.

According to recent reports, ​​​​​The airport writes, "The existing Concourse C baggage claim area will be decommissioned and demolished. American, Allegiant and Alaska passengers will be directed to utilize an interim baggage claim facility, located in the Concourse C Plaza. This operation will remain in place through 2030." Passengers arriving on other airlines (Delta, Southwest, United, Sun Country) will continue to use the existing baggage claim arrangements.

Sources indicate that the biggest immediate change (effective October 2026) is the closure of the main terminal's existing curbside road (including to pedestrians). All vehicle traffic is diverted to a newly constructed two-lane bypass road. Passengers are advised to arrive early to find parking and to navigate to the terminal. They should use the new Short-Term Parking Lot, located on the first floor of the Garage for airport visits lasting less than two hours.

It has been revealed that drop-off remains possible at sections of the terminal curb. However, the changes reduce inbound road capacity and could create bottlenecks. Meanwhile, the hallway connecting the airport’s main terminal and Concourse C ticketing area will be demolished. The airport is also encouraging arriving passengers to use its free Cell Phone Lot rather than circling the terminal or waiting at the curb. The Cell Phone Lot has a one-hour maximum waiting period, and vehicles may not be left unattended.

The airport encourages "loved ones to use the Cell Phone Lot until they’re ready to be picked up at the arrivals curb." It is also possible to request curbside wheelchair assistance in advance through the airline’s website, app, or special assistance phone line to streamline the day of drop-off.

TREX aims to centralize, consolidate, and integrate functions while also improving access. The Central Hall is designed to fundamentally change how passengers move through the airport. It is not only about adding more space, but also about bringing the airport's major functions together into a central building. A consolidated TSA security checkpoint for all airlines is to be one of the top improvements.

Industry observers note that when complete, the Central Hall will connect to Concourses A, B, and C. Some aspects of the building are not yet fully clear. Preliminary baggage-handling designs show four baggage-claim carousels with space for a fifth. Designs also show eight inbound baggage-loading piers. Efforts are not intended to make Spokane a top-tier airport; it will remain a mid-tier airport in the large regional category. The FAA considers it a small hub.

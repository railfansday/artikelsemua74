Alphabet (GOOG +0.54%)(GOOGL +0.68%) stock is down 32% from the all-time high it hit this summer amid a sell-off in artificial intelligence stocks. It seems that investors are increasingly questioning whether the industry's enormous AI investments will pay off. (Related: Tech Report: Artificial neurons successfully communicat...)

Alphabet is certainly spending plenty. The Google parent expects capital expenditures of $195 billion to $205 billion in 2026, with even more spending planned for next year.

Here's what I find interesting about Alphabet's sell-off: While its stock price has been falling, its two biggest businesses are delivering outstanding results. That's why I think that Alphabet stock is really appealing right now at its discounted price. (Related: “Giant superatoms” could finally solve quantum computin...)

In a recent development, alphabet generated $127.6 billion in revenue during the third quarter, up 25% from a year ago. Much of that comes directly from Google's formidable advertising business, which generated $82 billion in quarterly revenue, up 11% from a year ago.

Google Search alone contributed $63.3 billion, up 12%. In fact, CEO Sundar Pichai stated Google's search query growth reached its highest year-over-year rate in five years. That's notable, considering the organization faced legitimate concerns that AI-powered chatbots would undermine Alphabet's search business.

Google services, which includes advertising and subscriptions, generated $39.2 billion in operating income during the quarter. That gives Alphabet an enormous source of earnings as it expands its AI infrastructure.

NASDAQ · As of 1:00 PM ET 11/27/26.

In a recent development, the biggest reason I'm bullish on Alphabet is Google Cloud. Its Q3 cloud revenue surged 103% to $30.7 billion. Operating income from the segment more than tripled to $11.1 billion, with operating margins expanding to 36.2% from 23.7% a year earlier.

According to recent reports, why is that key? Alphabet is already reaping the rewards of its infrastructure spending, and there's more business waiting. Google Cloud finished September with a $561 billion backlog, with management expecting to recognize slightly more than 55% of that amount as revenue over the following 24 months.

Thomas Kurian, CEO of Google Cloud, told analysts during the company's earnings call that Google Cloud now has 18 product lines bringing in annual revenue of more than $1 billion each.

Industry observers note that "Our strong momentum is driven by our integrated AI portfolio of chips, models, data, security and agent platforms, all designed to work together," he said. "We have strong diversification across our product lines, industries and customers.".

Recent findings suggest that the shadow over Alphabet stock right now is its negative free cash flow of $9.1 billion in Q3. Alphabet spent $51.4 billion in capital expenditures during that quarter alone. But Alphabet isn't a speculative AI manufacturer waiting to establish a profitable business. Its advertising operations are already enormously profitable, and Google Cloud is demonstrating that AI investments can generate substantial returns.

A 32% stock decline doesn't eliminate the risks associated with Alphabet's spending plans. It does, however, make those underlying businesses considerably more attractive to investors willing to look beyond the current sell-off.

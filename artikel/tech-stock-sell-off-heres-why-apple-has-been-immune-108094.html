Artificial intelligence (AI) stocks have taken a beating in November. The tech-heavy Nasdaq-100 index is down 15% this month on fears that AI spending has gotten out of hand. But the world's largest company seems immune to the problem. (Related: “Giant superatoms” could finally solve quantum computin...)

According to recent reports, smartphone maker Apple (AAPL +0.43%) is bucking the trend. Its shares are virtually unchanged for the month, and in 2026 it's posting generous gains, up more than 25%, while many of its AI peers slump.

Recent findings suggest that and there's a good reason why. As other tech companies spend heavily on AI infrastructure, Apple is focused on bringing AI capabilities directly to its 2.5 billion devices at the moment in service. (Related: Pastor boots up GTA 6 during service after religious ba...)

That strategy is looking pretty smart right now.

Microsoft, Alphabet, and Amazon are investing heavily in enormous cloud computing networks. Meta Platforms is working on "personal superintelligence," and Tesla is focused on robotics and Cybercabs.

Apple's strategy, meanwhile, is focused on improving the experience for people who already own its smartphones, personal computers, tablets, and smart-home devices. Part of that strategy is integrating its devices with Siri AI, its improved personal assistant that launched this year. "Siri AI marks a fresh chapter in AI at Apple. It is a completely reimagined assistant that is powerful, personal, private, and deeply relevant to our owners' daily lives," CEO John Ternus mentioned.

That's why it doesn't need to dominate the AI infrastructure business to benefit from artificial intelligence. It needs to give customers compelling reasons to buy another iPhone, Mac, or iPad.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/25/26.

Apple's financial results suggest that its core business remains exceptionally healthy.

According to recent reports, it generated approximately $136 billion in free cash flow during fiscal 2026 (ended Sept. 26), up nearly 38% from the previous year. That's money remaining after capital expenditures, giving Apple tremendous flexibility to return cash to shareholders, invest in new products, and strengthen its business. Meanwhile, revenue jumped 15% to $478.8 billion for the fiscal year.

Industry observers note that apple's balance sheet has allowed its stock to hold steady even as rising memory chip prices squeeze hardware margins. Management expects gross margins of 45% to 46% in the first quarter of fiscal 2027, down from 47.9% in Q4 2026. But its services business, which carries gross margins of around 76%, provides an notable financial cushion.

Apple has something that many AI infrastructure investors are still waiting to see from their investments: enormous cash generation without the same requirement to spend billions building the infrastructure that makes it possible.

Elon Musk has made no secret of the fact that he would like to have many (if not all) of the companies he runs under one roof. And the enigmatic CEO has already taken several steps to accomplish that goal. Artificial intelligence (AI) start-up xAI merged with Twitter (now called X) before the combined organization was folded into Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX -5.65%) aka SpaceX. Sources indicate that however, Musk's biggest coup would be to bring together SpaceX and Tesla (TSLA -0.20%). The long-rumored combination just took another step closer to reality. Reports emerged on Tuesday that the electric vehicle (EV) maker and rocket launch company had appointed a handful of directors to oversee the largest merger in corporate history. (Related: Industry News: “Giant superatoms” could finally solve q...)

Bringing together two companies of this size will be a daunting task. SpaceX has a market cap of roughly $1.7 trillion, while Tesla's clocks in at $1.5 trillion, making the combined entity worth $3.2 trillion and placing it among the world's five most valuable companies (as of this writing).

Sources indicate that spaceX and Tesla have chosen five of the companies' directors to conduct merger negotiations, according to a report that first appeared in The Financial Times. The quintet of executives -- two from SpaceX and three from Tesla -- have been charged with hashing out the details for the all-stock merger, according to the report.

The diversity and wealth of experience among this group should give it the tools needed to complete the job -- though it won't be an easy one.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/24/26.

Recent findings suggest that the job at hand is extraordinarily complex and will face a number of challenges in combining two companies of this size. One such hurdle will be determining a fair "exchange ratio" between Tesla and SpaceX shares. If the exchange is seen as favoring one firm over another, it might not get the shareholder votes needed to succeed. Even if it's ultimately approved, an appearance of inequity could lead to shareholder lawsuits that could drag on for years. Any deal must also pass regulatory muster, considering the potential antitrust and national security implications.

According to recent reports, to get it right, the special committees from both companies have hired independent financial advisors to help develop and refine the financial models that will form the foundation for the merger discussions. The principals will also be charged with developing a governance framework and deciding who will lead the newly formed entity.

Industry observers note that while Musk is barred from the proceedings -- due to his inherent conflict of interest -- the special committees are permitted to "consult" with senior executives during their negotiations -- and no one is more senior than Musk. He has described the process as an "odd kabuki dance where two groups of people are talking, and I can't be in the conversation.".

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/24/26.

At this juncture, we simply don't have enough information to make informed decisions about the implications for shareholders of either company.

According to recent reports, that noted, there's a strong case to be made for the joining of SpaceX and Tesla. Both companies are investing heavily in AI, with Tesla's Full Self-Driving (FSD) and SpaceX's Grok at the heart of those efforts. The companies are already inextricably intertwined, with Tesla supplying Megapack battery systems, vehicles, engineering support, and various parts and accessories to SpaceX. The pair are also joined at the hip regarding Terafab, the joint semiconductor manufacturing project. If combined, SpaceX and Tesla could integrate their efforts in AI, robotics, and connectivity.

Industry observers note that the success or failure of the deal will likely hinge on the valuations assigned to each company and any resulting shareholder dilution. It will then have to run the regulatory gauntlet.

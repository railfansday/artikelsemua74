USDT’s market cap may now be a key metric to watch for signs of a market bottom.

Sources indicate that technically, Tether’s USDT is experiencing its most severe quarterly de-pegging since Q2 2022, which saw over 18% of its market cap drained away, totalling roughly $15 billion. Although presently the de-pegging figure is lower (around -5% so far in Q4), the consequence remains impactful. Since 2022, USDT’s market cap has increased to almost $180 billion from approximately $80 billion. This means even a smaller percentage drop now represents a much larger amount of liquidity leaving the market.

According to recent reports, the chart below highlights this trend. According to CryptoQuant data, USDT’s market cap has fallen by $8.83 billion over the 60 days leading up to the 22nd of November. Furthermore, its 30-day Moving Average is also falling, which indicates the decline is still in motion.

To put this into perspective, back in 2022 a 5% fall in Tether’s [USDT] $80 billion market cap would have caused approximately $4 billion worth of liquidity to exit the market. However, when applying the same 5% market cap reduction to today’s $180 billion figure, almost $9 billion is now leaving the market. That’s more than 2x the amount of liquidity being pulled out for the same percentage decline.

In this context, USDT’s growing market cap means its contraction now has a much bigger impact on liquidity.

Tether’s ongoing contraction further adds to the liquidity concerns.

In a recent development, TVL on DeFi platforms has decreased to just over $70 billion. This is from an approximate figure of $140 billion prior to the October 2025 crash. Thus, in just over a year, the TVL on DeFi platforms has almost been cut in half. Coupled with the reduction in USDT’s market cap, this suggests there is poor liquidity and reduced capital involvement within the crypto space. According to recent reports, however, the impact goes beyond DeFi liquidity. As the chart below highlights, the broader crypto market has also experienced a significant bear run. So far in Q4, the market has lost over 28% of its value, reducing the market cap from $2.84 trillion down to below $1.8 trillion, meaning almost $500 billion has been erased. The key takeaway? This is the worst quarterly fall for the crypto market since Q2 2022, which also witnessed the largest-ever de-pegging of Tether’s USDT stablecoin.

According to AMBCrypto, this overlap is not random.

Rather, with Tether’s market cap now much larger than it was in 2022, even a small drop in USDT supply is now having a bigger impact on overall market liquidity. Until USDT’s market cap bottoms, a sustained crypto market bottom may remain out of reach.

DeFi TVL has nearly halved to $70 billion, while the broader crypto market is down over 28% in Q4.

USDT’s shrinking market cap points to continued liquidity outflows, making its recovery a key signal to watch for a market bottom.

With the Thanksgiving holiday upon us, here’s a special Wednesday indie outlook including a few new entrants today led by The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth with All Of A Sudden, doc Who Is Still Alive and biopic Hershey.

Recent findings suggest that some buzzy films are also expanding midweek. Starting with those: A24’s Club Kid goes wide to 1,100 screens. Elsinore, starring Andrew Scott, from Focus Features, mores to 120 screens in top 25 markets.

James Gray’s Paper Tiger from Neon goes wide, hitting over 1,500 screens. Pedro Almodovar’s Bitter Christmas from Sony Pictures Classics expands to about 277 screens. Mubi’s Cannes Grand Prix-winner Minotaur adds San Francisco Wednesday and will expand in New York and LA Friday.

Netflix’s The Further Mis-Adventure of Cliff Booth is splashing down exclusively on Imax at 250 theaters domestically (and over 500 globally). It’s is also playing the Vista, New Beverly and the Paris. The David Fincher-directed, Brad Pitt-starring follow-up to Quentin Tarantino’s 2019 Once Upon A Time In Hollywood will get a two-week run on the giant screen and start streaming December 23. That short window has irked some chains, with Regal and Cinemark both declining to play the film. Tarantino wrote the screenplay and produces. According to recent reports, netflix will be reporting grosses. Cliff Booth is 82% Certified Fresh on Rotten Tomatoes, see Deadline review here.

Ryusuke Hamaguchi’s Cannes award winning All Of A Sudden from Neon is latest Wednesday, opening limited in NYC at the Film Forum and Film Society of Lincoln Center. Adds LA next week. Japan’s Best International Feature Oscar entry stars Virginie Efira and Tao Okamoto, who tied for Best Actress at Cannes. Sits at 90% RT Certified Fresh, see Deadline review.

It has been revealed that all Of A Sudden screened at TIFF, NYFF, BFI London. It took the audience award for Best International Feature at São Paulo, Montclair, Key West and the ReFrame Film Festival. Efira received the Leopard Club Award at Locarno and the Screen Artistry Award at Mill Valley. Nabbed Best Screenplay at the Chicago International Film Festival. The helmer received the RTVE Another Look Award at San Sebastián.

It was recently nominated for four Gotham Film Awards including Best International Feature, Best Director, Best Adapted Screenplay and Outstanding Supporting Performance for Okamoto, as well as a nomination for Best International Independent Film at the British Independent Film Awards and the AARP Movies for Grownups Awards.

According to recent reports, the Gotham Awards unspool this coming Monday, Nov. 30.

Director of a care facility for the elderly, Marie-Lou (Efira) strives to introduce an innovative care philosophy based on listening and respecting residents’ dignity, despite resistance from part of her staff. Her encounter with Mari (Okamota), a Japanese theater director battling cancer, will profoundly reshape her path and the two women join forces in a shared struggle.

Hamaguchi is known for Drive My Car, which won the Oscar, BAFTA, Golden Globe and Spirit Award for Best International Film and became the first Japanese film nominated for Best Feature.

Sources indicate that watermelon Pictures presents Switzerland’s Best International Feature Oscar submission, the documentary Who Is Still Alive (Qui vit encore) by Nicholas Wadimoff in New York and LA, moving to the top ten markets 12/4. The film, which world premiered in Venice at the 2025 Giornate degli Autori, tells the story of Gaza through the eyes of nine Palestinian refugees. They move through a setting reduced to a sketchy chalk map with the outline of Gaza, recounting life in the Gaza Strip before the war and their escape, with horror as well as resilience and hope. The protagonists were filmed in South Africa, one of the few countries where Palestinians from the Gaza Strip can enter without a visa.

In a recent development, angel Studios debuts Hershey, a biopic of Milton Hershey, founder of the eponymous chocolate empire (and amusement park), on 2,100 screens. Directed by Mark Waters, it stars Finn Wittrock as the pioneering entrepreneur who failed in business multiple times before creating his iconic sweets business, channeling his success into a legacy of education and community. Alexandra Daddario stars as his wife Catherine.

Industry observers note that looking ahead, there’s little upcoming activity Friday. Independent Film Tech giant will launch an Academy Run for buzzy adult animation Tangles at the IFC Center in New York through 12/3, then will run the film in LA the following week at AMC Century City. Premiered at Cannes, screened at TIFF. See Deadline’s review out of Annecy. Julia Louis-Dreyfus and Bryan Cranston lead the voice cast, with Seth Rogen and Beanie Feldstein.

Netflix starts week-long qualifying runs for Ava DuVernay documentary 14th and animated In Waves.

According to recent reports, 14th is a follow-up to DuVernay’s 2016 documentary 13th and examines the 14th Amendment to the U.S. Constitution, citizenship, belonging, and modern legal and political debates over birthright citizenship and civil rights. World premiered at the Upcoming York Film Festival, see Deadline review.

It has been revealed that in Waves is an animated romantic surfing tearjerker by Phuong Mai Nguyen, adapted from AJ Dungo’s autobiographical graphic memoir. The voice cast is is led b Will Sharpe and Stephanie Hsu. Premiered at Annecy, Deadline review here.

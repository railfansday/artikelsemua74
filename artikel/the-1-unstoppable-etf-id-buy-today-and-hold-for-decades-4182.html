Owning exchange-traded funds (ETFs) can be a smart approach to stock market investing. There are an unlimited number of flavors, though, which can be overwhelming. According to ETF Database, the U.S. has more than 3,800 equity-focused ETFs. How can you even begin to narrow down the list?

It has been revealed that investors should consider crucial variables, such as fees, performance, and exposure. Taking these factors into account, there's an unstoppable ETF that stands out. It's one I'd think about buying today and holding for decades.

According to recent reports, in the last decade, the Invesco QQQ Trust (QQQ -0.60%) has rewarded shareholders with a total return of 527% (as of Nov. 16). The S&P 500 index (^GSPC +0.06%), viewed as the preeminent stock market benchmark that represents 80% of the total U.S. market's value, has delivered a total return of 308% over that time. The QQQ wins by a mile in terms of performance.

It owns the 100 largest non-financial stocks trading on the Nasdaq exchange. Technology stocks rule, which helps explain its impressive track record. And concentration is high, with the top 10 businesses making up 46% of the ETF.

Industry observers note that the fee is tough to argue with. The Invesco QQQ Trust charges an expense ratio of 0.18%. That's a small price to pay for such stellar gains. (Related: Industry News: Fit More and Train Faster With ZeRO via ...)

It looks like an obvious investment vehicle to own compared to what professionals offer. The vast majority of actively managed large-cap funds deliver returns that fall short of the S&P 500 index over long time horizons. But they still charge clients high fees. That's a terrible deal.

The growing importance of the technology sector has been the most powerful trend shaping the economy and stock market in recent memory. This has played directly to the benefit of this ETF. But there's absolutely no guarantee that the Invesco QQQ Trust will continue crushing the overall market in the future.

Artificial intelligence (AI) has been a key tailwind. But the technology's financial payout may fail to live up to the hype, which could spur near-term pain as AI stocks face downward pressure.

If you're staring at a time horizon measured in decades, though, I believe it's a mistake not to be bullish on technology companies. Nobody knows what upcoming industries, businesses, products, and services will be created. But it's reasonable to assume that disruption and innovation will continue.

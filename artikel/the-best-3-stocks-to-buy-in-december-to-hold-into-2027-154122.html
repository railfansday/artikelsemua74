In a recent development, november has been tough on tech stocks. The Nasdaq Composite slid around 7% from the end of October to Wednesday, Nov. 25, putting the index about 15% below the record close it set on Oct. 6. (Related: Mechs vs Kaiju’s Ragdoll codes (November 2026)...)

Some big growth stocks are down far more. Netflix (NFLX +2.56%) shares, at $59.41 after Wednesday's close, have lost over half their value since their record close in June 2025. Amazon (AMZN +3.96%) trades around 14% under its Aug. 3 record close, and Palantir Technologies (PLTR +5.25%) is about 22% off the record close it set on Oct. 9. (Related: Amazon responds after delivery drone smashes into garag...)

According to recent reports, but I think all three are worth considering in December and holding into 2027. Each reported something in its last quarter that its stock price doesn't seem to reflect.

It has been revealed that netflix raised prices again this year, including in the U.S. Still, its churn (the share of members who cancel) improved year over year in the third quarter, both worldwide and in the U.S. It was also better than management had predicted.

On the third-quarter earnings call in October, co-CEO Greg Peters called churn "the best evidence that we've got that we're delivering [value to members].".

This matters because much of Netflix's growth comes from raising prices, which tends to work only when members stay. And at around 16 times its expected 2027 earnings, Netflix is the cheapest of the three stocks.

NASDAQ · As of 1:00 PM ET 11/27/26.

But revenue growth might keep slowing. Third-quarter revenue climbed 11.8%, versus 17.2% growth a year before. And Netflix expects 11.5% for the fourth quarter. If growth keeps falling, the shares might stay cheap for a long time.

In a recent development, amazon Web Services (AWS), the tech giant's cloud computing unit, grew revenue 39% year over year in the third quarter, its sixth straight quarter of faster growth. One part of AWS is growing even faster: the chips Amazon designs itself.

On the third-quarter earnings call, CEO Andy Jassy said the chips business is growing at "triple digit percentages year over year." He added that revenue commitments for Graviton, Amazon's general-purpose processor, and Trainium, its artificial intelligence (AI) chip, have each risen more than 20-fold in the last year. Customers using Amazon's own chips saved more than $2 billion in the third quarter alone, he said, and Amazon aims to offer Trainium outside AWS in late 2027. On Nov. 23, Amazon also agreed to buy Decart's AI optimization technology and hire the start-up's founders and engineers, who have worked with Amazon on Trainium since 2025.

Chips like these can give AWS a cheaper way to meet AI demand than relying only on chips it buys from other firms. This might help the giant data centers Amazon is building pay off.

NASDAQ · As of 1:00 PM ET 11/27/26.

Sure, these data centers are pricey. Amazon expects around $220 billion in cash capital expenditures this year, and its free cash flow over the 12 months through September was an outflow of $42.4 billion, versus an inflow of $14.8 billion a year before. If AI demand softens, all this spending might hurt the stock. Still, at around 23 times its expected 2027 earnings, Amazon stock looks reasonably priced for a manufacturer with AWS growing so fast.

Industry observers note that palantir's revenue surged 96% year over year to $2.31 billion in the third quarter. And the AI software organization turns about half of its revenue into cash.

In the third quarter, Palantir generated $1.22 billion in non-GAAP (adjusted) free cash flow, or 53% of revenue. The number is cash from operations minus capital spending, with payroll taxes on employees' stock awards added back.

Management now expects adjusted free cash flow of $4.8 billion to $5.0 billion for 2026. The upcoming range is up from the $3.9 billion to $4.1 billion it forecast on Feb. 2, and over twice the $2.27 billion Palantir produced in 2025.

Sources indicate that this is a company funding its own growth. It ended September with $10.4 billion in cash, cash equivalents, and U.S. Treasury securities.

NASDAQ · As of 1:00 PM ET 11/27/26.

The risk is the price. At about 65 times its expected 2027 earnings, Palantir stock is roughly four times as pricey as Netflix stock on that basis. Even a mild slowdown could push the shares sharply lower.

Industry observers note that I'd consider buying all three in December. Amazon would be my largest holding, because its chip business adds a newly introduced reason to own a organization whose cloud growth keeps picking up. Netflix, the cheapest of the three, would come next.

Sources indicate that palantir would be the smallest, and I'd want to buy it below $160. It closed under that price as recently as Nov. 23.

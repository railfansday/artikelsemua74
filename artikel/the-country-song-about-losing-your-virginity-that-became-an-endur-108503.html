Sources indicate that A song about getting ditched for a Texas woman might be leading the charts right now, but, 30 years ago, Deana Carter was offering a different take on romance: the enduring, nostalgic ache of that first love. Or, if we’re being a little less polite, losing your virginity to a college guy next to a river.

The Matraca Berg and Gary Harrison-written hit “Strawberry Wine” went to Number One on country radio in November 1996, and was a seminal part of a female-dominated wave where the genre’s women could push the boundaries of what exactly they were singing about, and how they were singing it. It has been revealed that A softly-delivered ballad, “Strawberry Wine” wasn’t as outwardly bold as songs like Loretta Lynn’s “Rated X,” or deeply topical like Dolly Parton’s “Down From Dover.” Instead, it’s a wistful reflection on a teen girl’s first love, with a hair of melancholy — and how thinking back on pangs of pleasure can hit as deep in the gut as a good country song. Carter never specifically details exactly what happened between her and this summer fling, but it’s pretty clear what she’s talking about: “love growin’ wild,” “the loss of my innocence,” intoxication, being plucked from the vine before entirely ripening. As the first single from her album with the equally boundary pushing title Did I Shave My Legs for This, Carter offered a quiet, cheeky reclamation of how subtly or overtly women could sing about sex. And not just sex: sex that comes before, and has nothing to do with, marriage or domestic life, completely devoid of the male gaze.

Carter was 30 when “Strawberry Wine” became a hit, later in life as far as the industry standards for women generally go. A Nashville native, she grew up around music through the eyes of her father, guitarist Fred Carter Jr., who appeared on records by Bob Dylan, Simon & Garfunkel, and Willie Nelson. Carter wrote most of the songs on her debut, but found that “Strawberry Wine” “just nailed my life story, which ended up being everyone’s first experience [with love],” she mentioned in an interview for CMA’s ‘90s Country Forever. She was particularly drawn to the work of Berg, one of the genre’s strongest and boldest talents, unafraid to pitch songs about female experiences to women willing to sing them. And Carter was the only one open to taking that risk. Artists like Trisha Yearwood, among many others, reportedly passed on “Strawberry Wine.” But Carter was willing to give a ballad about youthful carnality a go.

In a recent development, “It’s a five-minute waltz about… ya know,” Berg told Rolling Stone. “Deana was a brand-newly introduced artist and she wanted it as her first single. That shocked me. She was a friend of mine, so I was trying to talk her out of it because I wanted her to do well.” Shockingly enough, it did: the song hit Number One at country radio, a rarity for ballad sung by a woman, and went on to inspire others like Kelsea Ballerini, Elizabeth Cook, Ashley McBryde, and Mickey Guyton, who even shouted out “Strawberry Wine” in her song “Rosé” (so did Kid Rock, but we don’t need to mention that one).

Sources indicate that cook, who was motivated to pursue music thanks to Carter’s songs, also referenced it in “Two Chords and a Lie.” “She knows the words to ‘Strawberry Wine’ / It’s a friendly reminder of how love is blind,” Cook sings, in one of dozens of country songs that call out Carter’s hit. These days, country has been racier than ever. From Kacey Musgraves’ washing machine, to Lainey Wilson’s steamy fresh single “Just a Woman,” the influence of Carter and “Strawberry Wine” holds strong. Wilson even once recorded a mashup of her very “Strawberry Wine- inspired “Watermelon Moonshine” on social media, the two hits intertwining.

Industry observers note that “I was definitely channeling ‘Strawberry Wine.’ There will never be another one, but this is kind of my version of that,” Wilson told Variety. “I think songs like that, that are nostalgic like that, they’re just timeless. People can relate. They want to go back to that place; everybody wants to feel at home.”.

The European Parliament Brings the EU One Step Closer to a Sound Investment Policy

Being locked-in a race for technological leadership with China and the US, the EU lacks focus, coordination and financial firepower to transform its industry, ensure long-term prosperity while reaching its climate objectives.

This could soon change thanks to the forthcoming European Competitiveness Fund that will become active from 2028. Yesterday, the European Parliament cemented its support to a robust fund. Today, EU leaders meet and need to secure an adequate budget for the ECF to deliver. (Related: This newly introduced chip survives 1300°F (700°C) and ...)

According to recent reports, since the launch of the US Inflation Reduction Act in 2022, Europe has developed several strategies, masterplans and policy initiatives to build a compelling industrial policy. Still, they largely failed to deliver on focus, coordination and financial firepower.

With the European Competitiveness Fund (ECF), the Commission proposed a latest instrument to support Europe’s industry and deliver on its Clean Industrial Deal. Yesterday, the European Parliament voted on rules that would make the proposal even stronger. (Related: Industry News: Which Samsung folding phone is right for...)

It has been revealed that MEPs raised the ECF’s overall budget by €30 billion to €264 billion. Crucially, they boosted the envelope for clean energy and industrial decarbonisation to €40 billion, with €4 billion earmarked specifically for clean-tech manufacturing.

It has been revealed that MEPs backed a budget of €40 billion for InvestEU, including €15 billion specifically for clean energy and industrial decarbonisation. Through budgetary guarantees, debt and equity, InvestEU aims to collect private capital for higher-risk domestic manufacturing projects that would otherwise fail to reach a final investment decision. This extra €29 billion in additional guarantees compared to the Commission’s proposal could unlock over €300 billion in total mobilised investment.

In a recent development, the Parliament also earmarks €10 billion for the Critical Raw Materials Centre (CRM Centre). The Centre is poised to secure resilient and sustainable critical raw material supply chains for Europe by investing in strategic projects. Securing budgetary resources via the ECF is a crucial step for success.

Over the years, the EU has developed a broad range of funding instruments, but they still largely focus on supporting research and developments. Yet, every year the EU falls further behind China in terms of manufacturing of key technologies, such as batteries. This is because the existing funding programmes neglect the critical and capital intensive scale-up period. Many newly introduced market entrants do not survive due to technological risk, insufficient access to risk capital and foreign competition.

It has been revealed that europe does spend substantial money to support industry, both via European funds and at the national level under slow-moving state aid schemes. However, many of the support schemes are spread out widely across technologies without enough focus and clear priorities.

This makes sense for funding early research to steer innovation. For scaling this is different. Today, we know which technologies we need to produce in the next 5 to 10 years to cut emissions and retain leadership, starting with clean transport: the battery value chain, zero-emission vehicles, clean aviation and maritime technologies and e-fuels.

Recent findings suggest that to deliver on the green transition, European funding must be decisively redirected toward the development of strategic clean industries and essential technology value chains. T&E estimates that manufacturing of clean transport technologies alone requires at least €39 billion public funding per year by 2030. Broader decarbonisation needs — industrial heat, buildings, and cleantech deployment — require significantly more.

European cleantech innovators too often stall in the “valley of death” between R&D and commercial production. The Parliament’s text tackles this directly by mandating “ramp up” support for projects that strengthen critical value chains and reduce foreign dependencies — backed by a dedicated €4 billion envelope for clean technologies like battery cell manufacturing. Finally, the EU will be able to deploy production aid — a model that has proven impactful in other jurisdictions like the US.

The Parliament has set out a far bolder industrial vision than EU governments who so far largely stuck to the Commission’s narrower draft.

Industry observers note that one immediate test will decide whether the ECF will make a difference in the future. EU heads of states and governments who meet today for a special summit must reverse the heavy cuts inflicted on the ECF in recent Council negotiating drafts. They should listen to more than 75 businesses that also highlighted the importance of a well-resourced European Competitiveness Fund in the next EU budget.

Recent findings suggest that designing a smart fresh industrial investment tool means little if leaders starve it of cash. They must back a Competitiveness Fund with the budget to match its ambition.

Article from T&E. By Till Eichler, Public Investment Policy Manager, T&E and Nina Betz, Public Investment Policy Intern, T&E.

The New York Times is asking freelance photographers not to use the newest version of Adobe Photoshop because features in the 2027 update use AI “in ways that may not be apparent to users.” In an email seen by The Verge and posted on Threads, two of the Times’ photography leaders, Meaghan Looram and Josh Haner, write that the use of these features “can result in metadata identifying photographs as AI-generated.”.

Adobe has spent the past few years filling Photoshop and other apps in its creative suite with a trove of AI-powered tools. The November update cited by the NYT’s email adds several latest AI features, including Adobe’s new “AI Assisted Editor” view, which lets owners access all of Photoshop’s AI features from a single toolbar. It also adds an AI assistant for step-by-step editing instructions, prompt-based image transformation and editing, new AI upscaling options, and an AI-powered tool that recomposes images for different canvas sizes. It has been revealed that it’s not always clear which tools in Adobe’s apps are AI-powered, and even some older options like automated selection tools may now be considered part of the AI suite. This can lead to photos ending up with metadata indicating they were edited with AI, which is a blunt way of describing what happened in post-production. For a newsroom like the Times, where photo editing is kept to a minimum, the metadata risks losing readers’ trust. Last year, the Times mentioned that it does not “retouch or digitally manipulate” photos but may “make minimal adjustments to color to ensure we are accurately reproducing the image.”.

It has been revealed that in August, Adobe began rolling out support for C2PA metadata across its apps, which indicates whether an image is AI-generated or edited using AI features. The NYT is issuing its restriction to keep these tags from inadvertently appearing in its photos. “At this time, we are requiring that all contributing photographers avoid using Adobe Photoshop 2027 (release 28.0.0 and subsequent updates) when working on assignment for The Times,” the email reads. NYT spokesperson Graham James confirmed the requirement in a statement to The Verge, which reiterated the policy outlined in the email.

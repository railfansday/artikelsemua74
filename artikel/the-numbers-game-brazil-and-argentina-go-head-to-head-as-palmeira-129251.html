Brazil and Argentina will be represented at Estadio Centenario in Montevideo by Palmeiras and Estudiantes, respectively.

Industry observers note that brazil and Argentina are level with 25 titles each in Libertadores history. Having won each of the last seven editions, teams from Brazil hold the longest all-time run of consecutive titles, but they have never led the outright charts for most trophies lifted in the competition, having last equalled the record with Uruguay in 1963 (two titles each). (Related: Japanese VTuber actually escapes GTA 6’s “impossible” 6...)

Palmeiras are playing in their eighth Libertadores final, a tally reached only by Boca Juniors (12) and Penarol (10). They are looking for their fourth title, which would see them equal Flamengo as Brazil's most successful club in the competition.

Estudiantes, on the other hand, are playing in their sixth final in search of their fifth title, a number of trophies only Independiente (seven), Boca (six) and Penarol (five) have achieved so far.

It has been revealed that estudiantes have reached five Libertadores finals, winning four of them (80% success rate). This is the second-highest rate among teams that have played at least three finals, level with Flamengo and surpassed only by Independiente (100%, 7/7).

However, they last reached a final in 2009, when they beat another Brazilian team, Cruzeiro.

Here, using Opta data, we look at the key storylines ahead of the clash at Estadio Centenario.

This is the second Libertadores season in which Palmeiras and Estudiantes will face off.

The first time was also a final, which took place at the Estadio Centenario, in 1968.

Estudiantes won a tie-breaker 2-0, after a 3-1 win for Palmeiras in the first leg and a 2-1 victory for the Argentine team in the second.

Estudiantes have opened the scoring in 12 CONMEBOL tournament matches against Brazilian sides since the 2017 season, winning 11 of those (D1). Furthermore, they have conceded just two goals in those encounters.

But how do their play styles match up?

Through their first seven games in the 2026 Libertadores, Palmeiras used mostly four-at-the-back in-possession shapes (4-3-3, 4-2-3-1, 4-2-4), compared to a back-three over their last five (3-4-3, 3-4-1-2, 3-2-4-1).

Just 11% of Palmeiras' passes in the 2026 Libertadores have been long, which is their lowest percentage in a single edition of the competition since Opta began recording this data in 2013.

So Palmeiras are probably going to try and play it out patiently, but could that suit Estudiantes?

Estudiantes have forced the most shot-ending high turnovers (final 40m of the pitch) in the 2026 Libertadores, both overall (23) and on a per-game basis (1.9).

Opta's supercomputer has run 10,000 data-led simulations, and Palmeiras are the favourites.

The Brazilian giants came out victorious in 54.1% of the model's simulations, while Estudiantes were crowned champions 23.4% of the time.

Palmeiras are the joint-leading scorers in the Libertadores this season (17 goals), level with Flamengo, while they have accumulated a tournament-leading 21.1 xG.

But Palmeiras have kept only three clean sheets, conceding 11 goals from 8.5 xGA.

Estudiantes have not been free-scoring, netting 12 in as many matches, but they have kept it tighter than Palmeiras at the other end, recording five shutouts and shipping only seven goals, though they have actually given up chances worth a combined 9.7 xGA.

Palmeiras boast Libertadores final experience.

Palmeiras last won this title in 2021, while they competed in last year's final, losing to Flamengo.

Industry observers note that their coach, Abel Ferreira, is taking charge of his fourth Libertadores final. Only Carlos Bianchi (five) has managed more such matches, with Ferreira now drawing level with Luis Cubilla, Roberto Scarone and Luiz Felipe Scolari.

Should he lead Palmeiras to victory, Ferreira will become the joint-second-most successful coach in Libertadores history, matching Osvaldo Zubeldia, who won three titles with, coincidentally, Estudiantes. Bianchi (three titles with Boca Juniors and one with Velez Sarsfield) has won the Libertadores the most times as a coach.

And Ferreira is not the only person in the Palmeiras ranks to boast plenty of Libertadores experience.

Recent findings suggest that gustavo Gomez will play in his fourth Libertadores final, all of which have come while at Palmeiras (2020, 2021, 2025 and 2026). He will be only the second Paraguayan to feature in that many finals, after Jorge Guasch (1979, 1989, 1990 and 1991 with Olimpia).

Lining up behind defender Gomez is set to be Carlos Miguel, who has saved six of the 21 penalties he has faced for Palmeiras (14 goals conceded, one off target).

Five of these saves came in penalty shoot-outs (three in the 2026 Libertadores) and one in normal time, in the 2026 Paulista final.

Carillo out to emulate Veron and Boselli.

Miguel's counterpart, Fernando Muslera, is enjoying the longest run of Libertadores clean sheets in his career, with three between the semi-finals against Flamengo and the quarter-final second leg against Corinthians.

The former Uruguay stalwart could become only the second goalkeeper to win the title without conceding in both the semi-finals and the final, after Agustin Rossi for Flamengo in 2025.

At the other end, Estudiantes are reliant on Guido Carrillo, who is the club's all-time leading scorer in the Libertadores with 14 goals, ahead of Juan Ramon Veron and Mauro Boselli (13 each).

Both Veron and Boselli scored in finals won by the Pincha: Veron netted three goals against Palmeiras in 1968, and Boselli scored one against Cruzeiro in 2009.

Industry observers note that carrillo is Estudiantes' joint-top scorer in this season's tournament, level with Tiago Palacios on three goals. Jose Manuel 'Flaco' Lopez is Palmeiras' leading scorer (four goals in 12 games).

Lopez leads the tournament in successful take-ons (26), while Carrillo leads in aerial duels won (57).

Marlon Freitas played a crucial role in Palmeiras' semi-final first-leg victory over Fluminense – a 2-1 win at the Maracana.

Along with scoring what proved to be the winner, Freitas led his team-mates for chances created (two), touches (68), accurate passes (48), possession wins (six) and tackles won (3/4), while also winning four of his six duels.

Estudiantes – Leandro Gonzalez Pirez.

Centre-back Leandro Gonzalez Pirez helped Estudiantes keep clean sheets across both semi-final legs against Flamengo.

In the second leg, he recorded a team-high six clearances, while also recording the joint-most interceptions (three), winning 100% of his aerial duels (2/2) and even creating a chance at the other end.

Things have changed at Old Trafford during the international break, with Michael Carrick's tenure brought to an end due to them sitting in the Premier League relegation zone.

Now, it is Mauricio Pochettino's turn to step into the dugout, having been linked with the top job at United multiple times over the years.

He is being thrown straight in at the deep end, though, with a trip to United's rivals Liverpool first up.

While United are toiling at the wrong end of the table, Andoni Iraola's side will be hoping to start putting more pressure on the top three to keep alive a potential title push.

But, having lost both matches against United last term, can they get back to winning ways against their old foes? Or will the Red Devils get the fresh manager bounce they so desperately need? (Related: Latest Update: At least seven progressives voted agains...)

Given their strong start to the season, it is no surprise Liverpool are the favourites to triumph on Sunday, winning 57.3% of the 10,000 pre-match simulations.

United's hopes of taking home three points in Pochettino's first outing sit at 20.5%, while there is a slightly higher possibility of a draw at 22.2%.

However, Liverpool lost both Premier League meetings with United last season, as many as they had in their previous 18 (W7 D9).

They last lost more consecutively between December 2014 and January 2016 (four).

Indeed, United won this exact fixture 2-1 last term, ending a nine-game winless run at Anfield in the top-flight (D5 L4).

This marks the latest in a league season (MD11) that Liverpool and United have faced off with one of the sides in the relegation zone since 1972-73, when the Red Devils were bottom and beat league-leaders Liverpool 2-0 at Old Trafford in November.

Liverpool aim to make Anfield a fortress again.

Liverpool will be out to spoil the party for United as they look to try and put some pressure on the teams above them in the table.

The Reds sit fourth after 10 games, after beating Crystal Palace 3-1 before the international break, meaning they are nine points behind league leaders Arsenal, who beat them 1-0 in their last outing at Anfield.

Iraola's side have generally struggled at Anfield this term, winning just one of their five matches at the stadium (2-0 v Brighton).

Indeed, just 28% of their Premier League points this season have come at home (5/18), the lowest home ratio of any side in 2026-27.

Liverpool could lose back-to-back home league games for the first time since their run of six defeats from January to March 2021, but improving their home form will be key if they want to stay an outside bet in the title race.

One thing that has been going their way is that their front line has been firing.

Florian Wirtz followed up his goal against Palace – which came from his 22nd shot in the Premier League – by scoring three goals for Germany over the break.

Alexander Isak has netted seven goals in the Premier League this term as he continues to get his footing following a tough first season, though only two of those have come at Anfield.

And Alexis Mac Allister has scored as many goals in all competitions this season for Liverpool (16 apps) as he managed in the whole of 2025-26 (five in 55 apps).

Iraola will know a fast start could be key, given United have given away early goals this term, meaning all eyes will be on their front line once more.

Can Pochettino make an immediate impact?

Pochettino is tasked with turning things around after a dismal start to the season, with United sitting 18th in the table with only one win to their name after 10 games (D5 L4).

It is their lowest league position at this stage since 1986-87, when they were bottom.

United have also failed to keep a clean sheet in any of their first 10 league games in a season for the first time since 1986-87.

There were positive signs towards the end of Carrick's tenure, with a dramatic 3-2 Champions League win over Roma followed by a thrilling 4-4 draw with Aston Villa in the league, which twice saw them overturn two-goal deficits.

But the reality is that United are winless in eight Premier League matches (D5 L3), the joint-longest ongoing run of any side this season, along with Hull City.

The Red Devils last had a longer run without a win in the league between November 1989 and February 1990 (11 in a row).

Pochettino will be the first United boss to oversee his first league game against Liverpool, but only one of their last nine managers (including caretakers) has lost his first Premier League game of a spell in charge (W4 D4), with Erik ten Hag losing 2-1 to Brighton in August 2022.

His record against Liverpool is not the best reading; he has failed to win each of his last eight matches versus the Reds in all competitions (D2 L6) across his time at Tottenham and Chelsea.

In fact, he has only won three of his clashes against Liverpool in total (P19, D5 L11), and just one of those victories has come at Anfield (a 1-0 win with Southampton back in September 2013).

One thing he will need to work on quickly is their defensive record, which will be key if United want to record their first away win in 2026-27.

Although only five sides have faced fewer shots (excluding penalties) than United (116) in the Premier League this season, only three have faced a higher non-penalty expected goals total than them (16.3).

Their non-penalty xG/shot faced of 0.14 is the highest of any side in the top-flight in 2026-27.

In all competitions, they have conceded 30 goals, the joint-most of any Premier League side (level with Crystal Palace), which has only made things harder.

Cody Gakpo has scored more Premier League goals against United than he has against any other opponent (five).

Indeed, he has netted in each of his last three against the Red Devils.

This term, he only has one goal to his name so far (against Newcastle United on the opening matchday), while his three assists came in the subsequent two games. He is without a goal involvement in five league games since.

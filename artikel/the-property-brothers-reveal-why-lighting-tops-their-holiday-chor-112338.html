The holidays are upon us, and while we busy our hands with turkeys, trees, and tinsel, the Property Brothers are gifting fans a few friendly tips for hosting across generations. Partnering with Home Instead, Drew and Jonathan Scott have developed a holiday chore checklist that every host should complete before guests arrive.

“The holidays are all about bringing family together. But when you're hosting aging loved ones, a little preparation can go a long way in making sure everyone feels comfortable and safe. As home renovation experts, we know that it's often the smallest changes that make the biggest difference,” the brothers explain. Ahead, we’re sharing their top tips for a safe, festive, and inviting home this holiday season.

While string lights and extension cords are all holiday decor staples, they can be a major tripping hazard. To prevent spending the holidays in the emergency room, the brothers recommend double-checking that loose ends and loops are neatly tucked away to keep paths and walkways clear throughout your home. (Related: Tech Report: GTA 6 players are devasted by train ‘nerf’...)

It has been revealed that slippery rugs, mats, and runners are another major safety hazard. Drew and Jonathan recommend reinforcing any pieces that could potentially slide with gripping tabs or replacing them with options that have better traction—especially in bathrooms where wet floors pose a greater risk.

Recent findings suggest that while decorating with Christmas lights is at the top of everyone’s to-do list, your home needs more than a well-lit tree. Dark halls and stairways are major safety concerns, especially when hosting guests unfamiliar with your floor plan. Plug-in nightlights are a great option for improved visibility, and they can be decorative too.

A clear entryway for the holidays is a nonnegotiable. Outside the home, leaves and debris lining your front steps are as dangerous as they are unsightly. Before your guests ring the doorbell, take the time to rake, sweep, and clean as much as you can to ensure all steps, dips, and curbs are visible.

In a recent development, drew and Jonathan clock in at well over six feet tall, but their guests may not be as gifted with height. If you’re used to storing things up high, the brothers recommend moving essentials like plates and glasses from high shelves and into lower, more easily accessible territory. Not only does this minimize straining, but it also reduces the risk of dropping pieces that could leave dangerous, messy shards in their wake.

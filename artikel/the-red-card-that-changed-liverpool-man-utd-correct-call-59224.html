Should it have been a red card though?

Was there a case for the video assistant referee (VAR) to suggest it should be downgraded to a yellow?

Let's take a look at the incident, and what a referee takes into account before showing a red for denying an obvious goalscoring opportunity.

Ronald Araujo played a long ball upfield for Alexander Isak to run on to.

Isak took a touch, before Yoro bundled into the back of the Sweden international and brought him down.

Referee Sam Barrott did not hesitate, giving the foul and sending off Yoro.

Was there doubt that Isak would have taken control of the ball? If so, it should only be a caution.

There are four key considerations for denying an obvious goalscoring opportunity:.

distance between the offence and the goal.

likelihood of keeping or gaining control of the ball.

location and number of defenders and attackers.

As Isak was running directly towards goal in a central position, and there was no covering defender, three of the factors were satisfied.

The question is about the "likelihood of keeping or gaining control of the ball".

The Liverpool striker took a heavy touch, which moved the ball a few yards ahead of him.

The touch came before contact from Yoro, so there is no case that the challenge caused Isak to over-run the ball.

In a recent development, the referee and the VAR, John Brooks, must therefore assess whether Isak would definitely have got to the ball ahead of Senne Lammens. And that if he had, it would have been a good scoring chance.

If there is any doubt, then it should not be a red card.

It is a borderline case, and as such the VAR would not be expected to intervene for a clear and obvious error.

Likewise, if the referee had shown a yellow the VAR would not get involved.

Former Liverpool and England midfielder Danny Murphy, who is a pundit on Sunday's Match of the Day, said there was "no doubt" about the red card.

Former Manchester United defender Gary Neville also agreed with the decision.

Sources indicate that "Leny Yoro has to cover and he doesn't," Neville mentioned on Sky Sports. "He reacts late and his anticipation is poor.

Industry observers note that "It's so clumsy from Yoro. It's naivety, he just needs to get off the pitch. You're not going to get that turned around, no chance.".

There have been a couple of very similar cases this season.

Brighton's Pascal Struijk was sent off at Sunderland through a VAR review after he brought down Wilson Isidor.

The Seagulls successfully appealed against that red card on the grounds that there was clear doubt that Isidor would have taken control of the ball before it ran through to goalkeeper Bart Verbruggen.

Could Manchester United appeal against Yoro's red card? There's nothing to lose by doing so.

Despite the Football Association regulatory commission unanimously ruling the Struijk red card was wrong, the Premier League's key match incidents panel voted the VAR intervention was a correct call on a split 3-2 vote.

It has been revealed that ipswich's Issa Diop avoided a VAR red card for a foul on James McAtee against Nottingham Forest, when the referee disagreed with the video official, and believed there was a covering defender. Forest boss Oliver Glasner was adamant it had been a clear goalscoring opportunity.

It was the same referee, Thomas Bramall, for both of those incidents.

The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power season 3 was not the one to rule them all on streaming.

The latest chapter of Prime Video's fantasy prequel series finds Middle-earth in its most dire state as Charlie Vickers' Sauron begins spreading his power throughout the world, all while Númenor is on the verge of civil war and Durin IV finds his rule over Khazad-dûm threatened by the return of his brother. Meanwhile, Rings of Power season 3 also followed Sauron's efforts to craft the One Ring to Rule Them All and build up his army of Ringwraiths.

It has been revealed that having premiered with its first four episodes on November 11, the Lord of the Rings series returned in grand fashion to Prime Video, securing its best reviews yet from critics and audiences alike with an 87% "Certified Fresh" score from the former on Rotten Tomatoes and 64% from the latter, breaking its streak of Rotten Popcornmeter scores. Prime also confirmed the premiere was off to a strong start for the season, bringing in 33 million viewers globally in its first two weeks, landing in the streamer's Top 10 of most-watched returning seasons. Now, Nielsen has shared their own numbers from The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power season 3's opening weekend, and it's a less flattering record for the show. Per the research firm, the four-episode debut scored 886 million minutes in the November 9-15 interval, landing the No. 1 position among original reveals, and No. 3 on the overall Top 10 chart, with the new episodes noted to have comprised "70% of watch-time" for the window. (Related: Among Us player & his mom reveal how they helped save k...)

Recent findings suggest that despite seemingly being a strong start for the season, it actually set a latest record-low for The Rings of Power, with the season 3 premiere being the first to come in below the 1 billion-minute-viewing mark. Season 1 memorably premiered with 1.25 billion minutes in its first four days, becoming Prime Video's first original series to top Nielsen's chart, while season 2 returned with a 1.02 billion-minute debut, similarly landing the top spot on the firm's Top 10 Originals chart for its premiere week.

In a recent development, it's certainly understandable that The Rings of Power has found itself continuing to trend downward with its Nielsen premiere numbers just in looking at audience reception to the Lord of the Rings prequel. Viewers have frequently taken to sharply criticizing the show online for its changes to J.R.R. Tolkien's source material, while more toxic people have targeted the show with racist hostility and labeling it as "woke." Moreover, the multi-year gap between Rings of Power's seasons clearly continues to be a hindrance in keeping viewers engaged with its returns.

Industry observers note that that being said, while the first Nielsen figures for The Rings of Power season 3 paint an unfortunate picture of its performance, the season as a whole is still largely outshining this negativity. In addition to the record-high Rotten Tomatoes scores, episode 7, featuring Sauron's epic forging of the One Ring, soared to claim the show's record for highest IMDb rating with an 8.5/10, beating the previous record-holder of 8.1/10, and the finale securing the No. 6 spot.

Recent findings suggest that with Prime Video already greenlighting The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power for season 4, which is expected to begin filming in Spring 2027, it's also clear the streamer is not discouraged by the slight decline as they push ahead with their five-season initiative. Given season 3's ending has starkly divided viewers over Sauron's bold decision, though, it will be interesting to see if this further impacts its Nielsen numbers in the future.

The entirety of The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power season 3 is now streaming on Prime Video.

Set in the Second Age of Middle-earth, The Rings of Power explores the forging of the iconic rings, the rise of the Dark Lord Sauron, and the epic events leading up to the stories in J.R.R. Tolkien's classic novels.

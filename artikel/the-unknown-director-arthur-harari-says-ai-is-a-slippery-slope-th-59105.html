“The Unknown” filmmaker Arthur Harari notes AI is a “slippery slope” and one he’d prefer to avoid in his own work — even as he admitted on Sunday at the Marrakech Film Festival that he used a deepfake tool in his Palme d’Or contender, a body-swap psychodrama starring Léa Seydoux.

Appearing in conversation with film critic and historian Charles Tesson, the Oscar-winning “Anatomy of a Fall” co-writer was asked by an audience member how he viewed the role of AI technology in his creative process. According to recent reports, “I’m in much the same position as everyone else: this shift toward using AI is suddenly upon us, and it’s becoming not just a possibility, but something increasingly pervasive and tempting,” Harari said. “Personally, I’ve never used it; for the time being, I don’t see any place for it in my work.”.

The acclaimed writer-director went on to walk that statement back moments later, when he admitted that his team employed deepfake technology for a pivotal scene in his latest film.

“At one point in ‘The Unknown,’ there is a scene where Léa Seydoux kisses Léa Seydoux, and we had to use a deepfake tool to do that,” he said. “But there was a human behind it. There was always a human involved — someone who programs it, so to speak, and keeps it under control. So, I’m not against it in principle. It’s just that I think we need to ask ourselves what constitutes the essence of what we’re trying to create — right from the writing stage.”. (Related: Think AI "knows" what it’s doing? Scientists say think ...)

As a writer, Harari appeared to be more resistant to the technology, conceding that AI tools were “perfectly legitimate and interesting” when it comes to performing simple research tasks. But to go beyond that — “to actually craft parts of the writing process” — was, he insisted, “a very slippery slope.”.

Recent findings suggest that “The thing is, we are inherently prone to addiction — look at our phones, for instance; we’re constantly glued to them,” he remarked. “Once you get a foot in the door, it becomes incredibly difficult not to use it at every turn.”.

Premiering in competition at this year’s Cannes Film Festival, “The Unknown” stars Seydoux as an unknown woman who has a one-night stand with a photographer named David (Niels Schneider). When David wakes up, he’s trapped inside Seydoux’s body. The film was based on a graphic novel co-written by Harari.

In a recent development, speaking to Variety earlier this year, the French actress described the role as “the best part I’ve ever played.” Asked if she thought AI would put filmmakers out of business, she replied, “I don’t think so. I think that nothing can replace humanity. I don’t feel threatened. Maybe I’m wrong! But I believe in human nature.”.

In a recent development, harari is on hand at the Marrakech Film Festival as part of its popular Conversation series. He’s appearing alongside “Knives Out” director Rian Johnson, “Call Me by Your Name” Oscar nominee Luca Guadagnino, “Fjord” Palme d’Or winner Cristian Mungiu, Italian screen icon Monica Bellucci and more.

Industry observers note that the Marrakech Film Festival runs Nov. 20–28.

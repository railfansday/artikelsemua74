While The Walking Dead itself might have ended with a profound whimper back in 2022, the iconic show’s true replacement is now set to finally wrap up in 2027. It is sad but true that The Walking Dead’s 177-episode run turned out to be the show’s own worst enemy. In its critically adored first season, AMC’s adaptation of Robert Kirkman’s acclaimed comic book series of the same name was a true masterpiece.

Industry observers note that however, in an interesting inversion of the biggest problem faced by HBO’s 2011 fantasy mega hit Game of Thrones, the longer, later seasons of The Walking Dead found themselves struggling to stretch too little story over too much screen time. As early as The Walking Dead season 2, viewers and critics alike were already frustrated by the show’s inconsistent pace and filler episodes, and the long seasons demanded by cable norms only exacerbated this problem as the show continued. In a recent development, of course, the deluge of spinoffs, and unrelated zombie indicates inspired by The Walking Dead’s success, can’t have helped. By the time the original show ended, viewers had been introduced to the official spinoffs Tales of the Walking Dead, World Beyond and Fear the Walking Dead, alongside zombie shows like Netflix’s acclaimed South Korean series All of Us Are Dead, iZombie, Z Nation, and Black Summer. Within this context, The Walking Dead’s series finale was always likely to feel dispiriting. Luckily, the spinoff Daryl Dixon became the original show’s true replacement. (Related: Latest Update: Save $2,400 on a tricked-out Corsair RTX...)

Industry observers note that since Norman Reedus’s Daryl was undoubtedly one of the most memorable breakout characters of the original series, it only makes sense that Daryl Dixon is, for many fans, an even better continuation of The Walking Dead’s story than Rick’s own spinoff show The Ones Who Live. Since Rick left the franchise’s flagship show long before its actual official series finale, The Walking Dead viewers who stuck with the series often viewed Daryl as its ultimate true protagonist.

Not only that, but the fact that Daryl Dixon wasn’t beholden to any existing source material meant the spinoff was, in many ways, the best of both worlds. Daryl played a lead role throughout the original show, meaning his spinoff from The Walking Dead felt like a true continuation. However, his comic book story also ended alongside the original show’s finale, giving the creators carte blanche to do what they wanted with the character in the spinoff.

Daryl Dixon season 1 took this idea and ran with it, introducing its title character washed up on the shores of France, trying to work out how to get home while navigating this mysterious upcoming environment. As seasons 2 and 3 continue, further fan favorites from the original show return alongside Daryl, while his perilous trip across mainland Europe only gets more complicated and dangerous with their arrival in each new country.

According to recent reports, february 2027’s season 4 is set to wrap up the story of the series, although it is tough to tell where the plot is heading after the cliffhanger ending of Daryl Dixon season 3. Of course, viewers who remain invested in the franchise will likely be wondering whether this marks the end of The Walking Dead as a whole, too.

Sources indicate that fortunately, while fans can choose to end the franchise with Daryl Dixon’s series finale, the show’s spinoff indicates will likely still add more episodes to The Walking Dead canon. The Walking Dead: Dead City could see Maggie and Megan continue the show’s story if AMC renews the series for a fourth outing, and there is always a solid chance that the franchise will spawn an all-upcoming, unrelated show in the coming months. After all, by the time Daryl Dixon ends its fourth season, it will be almost 17 years since The Walking Dead first began way back in 2010.

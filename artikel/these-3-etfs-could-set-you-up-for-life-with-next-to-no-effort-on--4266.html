Buying low-cost index funds is often the best choice for everyday people who want to make money in the markets. Here's a game strategy:.

Industry observers note that picking stocks can be fun and lucrative, but you don't have to do it. Even if you never own an individual stock, you can use this simple strategy to build serious wealth by owning the broad stock market.

Let's look at three low-cost index funds that can set you up for life with no effort (and no expensive fees) required.

The Vanguard S&P 500 ETF (VOO +0.00%) might be the only exchange-traded fund (ETF) that many American investors need. That's because it tracks the S&P 500 (^GSPC +0.02%) of 500 of the largest publicly traded companies in America, which make up about 80% of the entire U.S. stock market's market capitalization.

Sources indicate that these 500 stocks are so important that even some more broadly diversified stock market index funds, like the Vanguard Morningstar Total Stock Market ETF, closely track their performance. The most profitable large-cap stocks have an outsize impact on the overall U.S. stock market's returns.

In the past five years, VOO has delivered annualized returns of about 12%, and it's delivered even stronger annualized returns of 14.8% over the past 16 years since the fund's inception in September 2010.

Industry observers note that keep in mind that over the long run, since 1928, the S&P 500 has delivered an average annual return of 9.98%. It's hard to go wrong if you're a long-term investor in the Vanguard S&P 500 ETF. Buying these 500 stocks lets you own the most notable companies in America at an ultra-low expense ratio of 0.03%. Just "VOO and chill," as the fashionable people on the internet like to say.

Sources indicate that what if you want to branch out from the U.S. market? Not every American investor needs to invest in international stocks, but I like to.

The Vanguard Total International Stock ETF (VXUS -0.78%) gives you access to 8,901 stocks from dozens of countries around the world. If you want to invest in companies from countries like Japan, Taiwan, the United Kingdom, Canada, and beyond, this global ETF lets you do it at a low expense ratio of 0.05%.

Global stocks don't always beat the U.S. -- for most of my life as an investor, the exact opposite has happened. Over the past 15 years since the fund's inception in January 2011, the Vanguard Total International Stock ETF has delivered average annual returns of about 6.3%. But in the past three years, it's bounced back with annualized returns of about 20.3%.

It has been revealed that international stocks don't make up a large percentage of my portfolio. But I buy international stocks because I want to diversify beyond "only" American companies. In case the U.S. dollar gets weaker, the U.S. stock market crashes, or other changes happen in the global economy that could be better for the rest of the world, I want to protect against those risks and own some of that upside.

Bond yields have recently been rising, which has some people questioning whether now is a good time to buy bonds.

I buy bonds as a small percentage of my portfolio, mainly to provide some risk protection in case of a big, prolonged stock market downturn. At the moment, only about 10% of my investments are in bonds. As I get closer to retirement age, I'm going to want to buy more bonds and increase the percentage of bonds in my portfolio.

Sources indicate that as a long-term investor, I try not to worry too much about whether now is "the right time" to buy bonds. I just want to keep buying a diversified mix of high-quality bonds across a wide range of types and maturities. The Vanguard Total Bond Market ETF (BND +0.36%) lets me accomplish this goal.

BND holds a remarkably well-diversified portfolio of 11,434 bonds, including U.S. Treasury bonds, other government bonds, and investment-grade corporate bonds. It charges an ultra-low expense ratio of 0.03%.

It has been revealed that the past few years have been tough for bond investors. BND has delivered a negative total annualized return of -0.16% for the past five years (as of Oct. 31).

But over the long run, since the fund's inception in April 2007, the Vanguard Total Bond Market ETF has delivered an annualized return of 2.96%. My hope is that this bond ETF will help stabilize my portfolio against long-term stock market risks while delivering a decent return.

According to recent reports, there's no guarantee that any of these funds, or any stock, bond, or ETF, will deliver wealth-building results in the future. But this simple strategy of buying low-cost index funds with low fees is likely to be a good choice for long-term investors.

Recent findings suggest that one of the most sought-after products in Black Friday sales are Apple's iPads. These powerful tablets often cost a lot, and rarely see discounts, so a reduction in price becomes all the more tempting. But if you're just looking at the recent models, you're missing a trick.

Walmart is a major seller of restored iPads, or second-hand slates that have been professionally returned to a near-newly introduced condition. Tablets like this are often much cheaper than new ones, and work just as well (you can take my word for it, I bought a refurbished 2020 iPad Pro and it still works perfectly). While these models are cheaper anyway, Walmart is offering Black Friday deals on a fair few of them, bringing tablets down to remarkably cheap prices. These are mostly for older models, with a 2021 release the newest here, but they're still powerful and modern slates and so you might find an older iPad suits you fine. If you've been looking for a new slate, changing tack in the Black Friday iPad deals to buy an older, refurbished model might seem might seem unthinkable.

Recent findings suggest that older slates work just well for tasks like word processing, streaming video, communications and simple workflow tasks. Slack has never challenged my 6-year-old iPad Pro. The trappings of newer slates, like advanced chipsets, will only really pay off for intensive tasks like video editing.

According to recent reports, what about gaming? From my experience, older tablets still run the most advanced games pretty well. After all, game makers don't want to limit their potential audience by excluding owners of older tech, so they optimize their games to work well on various devices.

Recent findings suggest that the only problem with older tablets is that they are less battery-efficient, even in refurbished models which usually enjoy newly introduced batteries.

It has been revealed that admittedly, when the Dyson Airwrap came out, I wasn’t entirely convinced it was meant for my hair type. I watched all the reviews: People with long, luscious hair achieve, well, long, luscious hair. My bob haircut (and fine strands) could never get hairstyles like that. Then, I became a beauty editor with access to all the fancy beauty devices, and I finally tried it. Within weeks, it replaced all of my hair tools. I could smooth my ends, give myself a ‘90s blowout, add waves, dry my hair rapidly—all with one device. And it’s significantly less damaging too. But since it was released, a zillion Dyson Airwrap dupes have come out, offering up similar results for a fraction of the price. Because that’s the catch: The Airwrap is great, but it’s a whopping $650 ($500 if you get it on sale right now for Black Friday). My curiosity is piqued every time a new multi-styler introduces, but my go-to favorite for the past few years is the Ella Bella 6-in-1 Professional Hot Air Styler. And while it’s 20% off for Black Friday (at the lowest price I’ve seen all year), I’m breaking down exactly why it’s my favorite for fine, short hair. With six attachments, this all-in-one air styling tool was made to replace virtually every other device in your cabinets. It features a hair dryer, two rotating curling irons (one for each side of your head), and three hair dryer brushes (one oval, one round, and one paddle for straightening). Like the Dyson Airwrap, this device uses hot air, not heated plates, to style, which helps reduce heat damage. The airflow on this thing is pretty damn powerful, reaching 110,000 RPM. And there are three temperature modes and two speed levels to help you tailor your styling to your hair type and needs (typically, the thicker your hair, the higher the temperature should be; and the same goes for speed). The device also boasts a “noise-reduction design,” so it sounds a little quieter than others.

Recent findings suggest that my hair is truly as baby-fine as it comes and dries naturally straight. For this reason, it’s usually very soft and silky (love), but refuses to hold a style for longer than an hour or so (hate). I also color my hair (nope, not a natural redhead), so it’s damaged and prone to breakage. I’m growing out my bob haircut right now, so it’s a little past my clavicle—à la the clavi cut—and I have bangs that always require heat styling to look good. Currently, my go-to styles are typically a smooth blowout that makes my ends look less stringy in the bob grow-out period, or soft waves that add movement and texture. All this to say: The Ella Bella Styler has a lot of work to do (sorry!).

Sources indicate that I received an initial rendering of this tool nearly a year ago, and it quickly became my go-to Dyson Airwrap dupe. In fact, I keep it at my desk at the office to style my hair after morning workout classes. The brand just released this slightly upgraded version that I hadn’t used yet, and upon first impression, it looks significantly higher quality. When I opened the carrying case and felt the tool, it’s lightweight (aka easy to hold for a long time if you are working with much more hair than I am) but looks luxe. The packaging is.

Totally. At under $150 (a true fraction of the cost of the original Airwrap), the Ella Bella 6-in-1 tool is a pretty solid option if you’re looking to spend less money but still get a high-quality tool. I especially love how it works on my short, fine hair, but I could see someone with long hair falling in love with this too. I’ve already stashed mine in the green room at the office for midday touch-ups, and two people have asked if they could try it.

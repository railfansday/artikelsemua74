According to recent reports, I look for opportunities worth building. Most of the work is saying no — so this series is a log of ideas I looked at, and why they did or did not make it.

Every account that posts on Instagram needs a link page, and Clienzo already knows a user's accounts and posts. It looked like an easy add-on.

The problem is that link-in-bio tools are a commodity. Free versions are good enough for most people, and the ones that pay are hard to win from established tools. Needed, but nobody pays — that is a feature, not a business. (Related: Latest Update: Simple considerations for simple people ...)

In a recent development, the idea: show a business how often AI assistants mention it when people ask for recommendations. The interest is real.

I could not make it reliable. Assistant answers change with wording, location and time, and a number that moves for reasons nobody can explain is worse than no number — the same rule as Clienzo's analytics.

It has been revealed that sending messages by SMS reaches people better than email. It also costs money per message, and at the prices small businesses pay for a tool like Clienzo, the margin does not survive. I turned it down on cost, and I do not reopen it without new numbers.

It has been revealed that what made the winner different was evidence of pain, not elegance. The most common problem small businesses report with social media is producing enough content. And there was already proven spending on automation around social replies. (Related: Breaking: How to generate text: using different decodin...)

So the idea became Autopilot (part 13 of Building Clienzo): AI drafts next week's posts from real sources, code checks the facts, the owner approves. It fits what Clienzo already has — accounts, results, comments — instead of being a separate product to sell.

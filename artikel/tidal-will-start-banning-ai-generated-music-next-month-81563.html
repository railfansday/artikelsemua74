Tidal is officially banning AI-generated music on December 9th. As a result of this change, Tidal will delete around 29 million AI-generated songs and reject an estimated 80,000 to 85,000 tracks per day, according to Tidal music lead Robert Andersen. It has been revealed that the move comes just months after Tidal began labeling and demonetizing tracks identified as 100 percent AI-generated. In a post on X, Andersen says Tidal needed to take a more “drastic” measure, as AI music made up 43 percent of the tracks distributed to the platform in the last 90 days. “We believe this will resonate with both artists and listeners alike, as 96% of all AI-generated songs receive zero plays at all,” Andersen adds. Industry observers note that bandcamp similarly banned AI music earlier this year, while other music streaming platforms attempt to address a flood of AI songs by applying latest labels or removing them from user algorithms. Spotify recently revealed that it removed 50 million AI slop and spam tracks from recommendations over the past year, and also debuted new “AI persona” badges for artists that use an AI identity. YouTube has rolled out AI music labels as well, while Apple Music is planning to launch AI labels this year. Meanwhile, Deezer is giving people the option to filter out AI music.

“Wholly AI-generated music intended to mislead listeners and impersonate artists is another serious problem that has only increased in scale: It diverts streams and royalties away from the artists, and confuses fans,” Tidal notes in a separate post on X.

It has been revealed that the platform is also going to ban music distributors from submitting AI songs, using the AI detection tool Ircam Amplify and metadata checks to reject submissions generated solely with AI. Tidal notes that it won’t ban songs made with the assistance of AI tools, as long as AI doesn’t make up the “sole output.”.

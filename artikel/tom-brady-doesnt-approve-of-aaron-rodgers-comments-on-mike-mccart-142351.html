Aaron Rodgers ended his Pittsburgh Steelers career in a way that will be remembered long after his time as a player ends. Tom Brady does not appear to approve.

The former Upcoming England Patriots and Tampa Bay Buccaneers star and current Fox Sports broadcaster weighed in on Rodgers going scorched-earth against Steelers coach Mike McCarthy during the Philadelphia Eagles-Dallas Cowboys Thanksgiving game on Thursday, suggesting Rodgers should have taken a more graceful route out of Pittsburgh:.

In a recent development, "The sanctity of an NFL locker room, it's a very sacred place. We shouldn't ever lose track of that as players. It's a privilege to be in that locker room. You're fighting with your teammates, you're fighting with your coaches, you're always acting as a team together. Shouldn't always be the place for private things to become public, that's what I'll say.".

The trouble for the Steelers began late Tuesday night, when the team unveiled it was benching Rodgers and starting backup quarterback Mason Rudolph against the Denver Broncos on Thursday. Rodgers responded by going onto his friend Pat McAfee's show and unleashing a torrent of claims about McCarthy's aptitude for the job.

The Steelers officially released Rodgers soon after the interview. Subsequent reports indicated Rodgers demanded the team either give him play-calling duties or release him, with McCarthy later confirming Rodgers "made an ultimatum.".

Brady praised Rodgers' career, but again indicated there wasn't much to be proud about this week:.

According to recent reports, jackson’s death was declared by ESPN, who was informed of his death by his family. ESPN “SportsCenter” anchor Chris Berman told the network that his death was “unexpected,” and he’d spoken with Jackson “at length” on Tuesday. “He was a vital 75,” Berman noted. “Tom was poignant. We’ve lost one of the best human beings ever to walk the planet, Tom Jackson. I was fortunate to work side by side with Tom for 30 years, primarily on ‘NFL Primetime’ and ‘Sunday NFL Countdown.’ It was a labor of love for our whole team, both in front of the camera and behind it. Industry observers note that born on April 4, 1951, in Cleveland, Ohio, Jackson began his NFL career with the Denver Broncos in 1973 after a successful college football career at the University of Lousville. Jackson played in 191 regular-season games, including 177 starts and 10 playoff games over 14 seasons with the team. Jackson was a starter on the Broncos’ 1977 Super Bowl team; a three-time Pro Bowl selection and a first-team All Pro in 1977 and 1978; and among the team’s top five tacklers in 11 consecutive seasons. “The Denver Broncos are heartbroken by the passing of Ring of Fame linebacker Tom Jackson, a cornerstone of the legendary Orange Crush defense and one of the most beloved players in franchise history,” the Broncos remarked in a statement Sunday. “An inspirational leader and fierce competitor, TJ was a proud member of the Broncos’ first Super Bowl team that helped establish a winning tradition in Denver. Off the field, Tom carried himself with class, warmth and humility, forging deep connections and lifelong friendships with teammates and throughout Broncos Country.

Industry observers note that after stepping away from the field, Jackson had an illustrious 29-year career at ESPN as an analyst alongside Berman. (ESPN’s NFL studio in Bristol, Connecticut, is named the Chris Berman-Tom Jackson Studio, after the two.) He retired in 2016, a year after being awarded the Pete Rozelle Radio-Television award by the Hall of Fame in 2015.

Jackson was “one of the most influential and beloved figures in ESPN’s history,” ESPN chairman Jimmy Pitaro mentioned in a statement released by the network. “He brought the credibility of an All-Pro NFL player, the insight of a gifted analyst and a warmth and authenticity that connected with generations of football fans,” Pitaro said in a statement. “Alongside Chris Berman, he helped make NFL Primetime an iconic part of our football weekends. In fact, ESPN’s NFL studio is named the Chris Berman-Tom Jackson Studio, a distinction unmatched in our history and a fitting tribute to their lasting impact.”.

He continued: “Away from the set, Tom was a trusted colleague, generous mentor and dear friend to many. We extend our deepest condolences to his family, friends, former teammates and all who loved him. His legacy at ESPN, throughout the NFL and among the countless fans who admired his incredible work will endure.”.

Jackson is survived by his wife, Jennifer, and his two daughters, Taylor and Morgan.

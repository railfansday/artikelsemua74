Tony Romo has landed his first post-CBS media gig… and it’s with a sports betting app, The Hollywood Reporter has learned.

Betr, the betting and predictions app co-founded by Jake Paul and Joey Levy, claims that it has inked a deal with Romo to make him the company’s “chief predictions officer.” The role is essentially a media play, with Romo appearing in original content, delivering sport-related predictions, and serving as a face of the brand in marketing.

That will start this week in social video sports alongside Paul. According to recent reports, “I’ve spent my entire career trying to anticipate what’s going to happen next, whether that was on the field or in the broadcast booth,” Romo stated in a statement. “Now I get to put those instincts to work in a completely upcoming way with Betr. What Jake, Joey and the team are building is really interesting because they’re bringing so many different ways for fans to engage with sports and gaming together in one place. I’m looking forward to having some fun, making predictions and being part of where this organization is headed. And I’ve got a pretty good feeling about what’s coming next.”. According to recent reports, “Tony Romo has built an extraordinary reputation around something that sits at the very heart of our business: predictions,” added Levy. “His ability to see what’s coming before anyone else has made him one of the most compelling personalities in sports, and we see a tremendous opportunity to bring that talent and personality to our growing ecosystem. Tony is becoming an equity partner in Betr and will play a meaningful role in how we connect with fans across our Super App. We’re building a gaming and entertainment platform unlike anything else in the market, and Tony is the perfect person to help us bring that vision to life.”.

Industry observers note that romo, the longtime lead analyst for CBS Sports, exited the network last month after he was arrested over the summer for operating a vehicle while intoxicated. The network suspended him after his arrest, before formally cutting ties a few months later. The network replaced Romo with JJ Watt in the booth.

His exit came amid a tumultuous year for NFL TV teams in general, with ESPN’s booth in the spotlight amid a bitter and public feud between Joe Buck and Troy Aikman, Fox’s Greg Olsen starting to look for a new TV home, and networks coveting NFL names like Travis Kelce and Sean McVay.

It has been revealed that flexible spending accounts (FSAs) sound like a great idea in theory: You can set aside pre-tax dollars to cover your annual healthcare expenses, so that money is available when you need it. But if you don't use it all up by the end of the year, you can forfeit whatever is left in the account. (Related: Breaking: Upcoming app punishes you and your friends fo...)

Recent findings suggest that that usually leads to a mad scramble around the end of the year to spend any remaining FSA funds before the Dec. 31 deadline. Some employers give you an extra two-and-a-half months to use your remaining funds or allow you to carry over up to $680 of your FSA funds into 2027. Check with your employer if you're not sure how your proposal works. (Related: Latest Update: Clavicular allegedly told 17-year-old to...)

If neither of those are options for you, you can spend your remaining FSA dollars on the following five things:.

Sources indicate that if you've been putting off a checkup or a specialist visit all year, now is a great time to fit one in. Use your FSA-eligible costs to cover any associated expenses, including diagnostic tests or specific treatments. Dental and vision care count as well.

Similar to health savings accounts (HSAs), FSAs enable you to spend your money on prescription medications as well as over-the-counter drugs. If you need to refill your prescription soon, try to fit it in before the end of the year so you can use your FSA funds. Or restock your medicine cabinet with pain relievers, bandages, and other items you might need over the next year.

Sources indicate that therapy can be an FSA-eligible expense if it's used to treat a medical condition. If you have any questions about whether your therapy counts, talk with your FSA provider to learn more.

If you need things like diabetic monitors or test strips, hearing aids, or a walking aid, you can use your FSA funds to pay for these expenses. Take stock of your current medical equipment and what might need replacing soon. Then, see if you can find any good deals on new versions in the next few weeks.

You can also use your remaining FSA funds to get up to date on your vaccinations if you're behind on any. Just make sure you squeeze them in before the end of the year.

Keep your receipts for all of these items. The IRS is free to request itemized receipts if it wants to verify the eligibility of your purchases.

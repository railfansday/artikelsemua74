📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

If you price anything in Turkish lira, there are two very different numbers called "the dollar rate". One is the official rate the Central Bank of the Republic of Türkiye (TCMB) reveals once each business day. The other is whatever a bank highlights on its board when a customer actually buys or sells currency.

To see how far apart they are, we took the USD and EUR boards of 11 Turkish banks on 25 November 2026 and put them next to TCMB's official forex buying and selling rates for the same day. The data comes from the AllRatesToday bank exchange rate API, which reads each bank's own rates page, and its TCMB endpoint. (Related: Latest Update: Hugging Face Reads, Feb. 2021 - Long-ran...)

TCMB's table for 25 November 2026 (bulletin 2026/222):.

TCMB's own buy/sell gap is 0.18%. Everything wider than that on a bank board is the bank's margin.

Sorted by spread. "vs TCMB" compares the bank's buying rate with TCMB's forex buying rate, and its selling rate with TCMB's forex selling rate. A negative buy figure means the bank pays you less for your dollars; a positive sell figure means it charges you more.

The spread varies thirty-fold. On USD it runs from 0.19% at Dünya Katılım to 6.12% at QNB Türkiye. On a USD 10,000 purchase that is the difference between paying about TRY 506,373 and TRY 522,297: roughly TRY 15,900 more at the widest board than at the narrowest, for the same dollars on the same evening.

The margin is roughly symmetric. At the wide-spread banks, the buying rate sits 2.3–2.8% below TCMB's and the selling rate 2.3–3.1% above. The bank isn't quoting a different market level; it's adding a similar margin on both sides of the official rate. (Related: How to generate text: using different decoding methods ...)

Recent findings suggest that mid-rates barely move. Take the midpoint of any board and it lands within about 0.7% of TCMB's selling rate; on USD, ten of the eleven are within about 0.1%. If you only need a level, any board's midpoint is close. If you need the price a customer pays, the spread is the whole story.

Recent findings suggest that we left İşbank out. Its feed demonstrated identical buying and selling rates that evening, which looks like a reading problem on our side rather than a real 0% spread.

Each bank response also carries captured_at and, where the bank indicates one, bank_updated_at. Store both if you compare banks, so you don't compare a 16:00 board with a midnight one.

Recent findings suggest that bank rates are the indicative boards each bank demonstrates on its own website at capture time. TCMB rates are the official indicative rates of the Central Bank of the Republic of Türkiye.

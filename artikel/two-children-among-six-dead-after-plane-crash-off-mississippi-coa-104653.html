Six people, including two children, have been found dead after a plane crashed off the coast of Mississippi on Tuesday, the US Coast Guard says.

Recent findings suggest that the Cessna 210 was on its way from Newly introduced Orleans to Fort Myers, Florida, according to the flight tracking website FlightAware. Air traffic control lost touch with it at about 15:45 (21:45 GMT) and it later went down in the Gulf of Mexico between Pascagoula and Horn Island, officials said. Divers found the bodies of four adults and two boys in the fuselage, mentioned Coast Guard spokesperson Juan Ros.

Rescuers spent hours searching for the group and initially only found three life jackets, a life raft and the plane's cabin floating on the water, he said.

On Wednesday, the Jackson County Coroner's Office identified the passengers as Brent Stokes, 50, Michael Knox, 43, Arlyn Knox, 43, Hunter Stokes,16, Charles Holden Knox, 12 and Truett Knox, 10, according the BBC's US media partner CBS News.

Cmdr Trevor Blount, the Coast Guard rescue mission co-ordinator, said: "While this is not the outcome any of us had hoped for, we are grateful for the strong interagency co-ordination that enabled us to swiftly locate those aboard the aircraft and provide closure to their families.".

The Federal Aviation Administration remarked the plane was descending at 10,000ft (3,048m) per minute when contact was lost, according to a CBS affiliate in Biloxi, Mississippi.

The National Transportation Safety Board said it is investigating the Cessna 210L crash.

Ulys, a New York-based AI investing app, unveiled on November 25, 2026 that it had raised an additional $4 million in a round led by Streamlined Ventures, bringing its total funding to $17 million.

It has been revealed that fundstrat’s Tom Lee, BitMine and XBTO Ventures also participated. The financing follows the app’s public App Store launch on November 10, 2026, and will go mainly toward marketing and training the AI models behind the product. In a recent development, ulys’s core workflow begins with an investment idea expressed in plain language, such as an industry a user expects to grow. The app researches the idea, assembles a basket spanning stocks, exchange-traded funds, gold and crypto, and provides the reasoning and sources behind each pick. Consumers can modify the basket in the chat, but no transaction takes place until they approve it. (Related: How to train a newly introduced language model from scr...)

That approval model extends beyond one-time baskets. Ulys’s product documentation highlights each proposed trade with the asset, amount and fee before it runs. For smart orders, people approve the precise terms in advance, including a price level or a change in the probability of an event on a prediction market. Ulys then executes only under those terms without requesting another approval at execution. Because the order does not reserve funds, it may fail or only partially execute if the balance is insufficient; users can cancel it beforehand.

According to recent reports, CEO Hugo Renaudin said the product grew out of the time required to turn a view about the world into researched investments. “Once AI could do that research, I wanted to build an app that turns a view into a portfolio,” he said.

Accounts use a non-custodial wallet, meaning Ulys does not hold customer funds and cannot move them without permission. Wallet infrastructure comes from Privy, a Stripe manufacturer, which splits the key into encrypted pieces held in secure hardware. Owners can sign in with Apple, Google, email or a phone number rather than managing a seed phrase, though sign-in options can vary by region.

The site lists more than 15,000 tradable assets across crypto, tokenized stocks, ETFs and commodities. It also draws a distinction between those markets and conventional securities ownership: third parties glitch tokens that track the price of a stock, fund or commodity, but the token is not the underlying asset and confers no shareholder rights. Available assets depend on the user’s location.

Industry observers note that an optional Earn feature places eligible USDC balances into third-party blockchain-based lending protocols after the user grants permission. The rate can change, rewards come from those protocols rather than Ulys, and the tech giant states that the USDC is not a bank deposit, carries no deposit insurance and can be lost if a protocol fails. Signup is limited to phone numbers from a listed set of countries, including the United States, Canada, Brazil and Japan; features such as Earn and card deposits are not available everywhere.

Renaudin previously sold cryptocurrency exchange LGO to Voyager Digital in 2020, built Wrap Protocol and co-founded P00LS. He leads Ulys with Chief Technology Officer Dylan Bourgeois, an AI and robotics founder with a background at Stanford and EPFL. Streamlined Ventures founder and general partner Ullas Naik described Ulys as an “AI native investment platform” and pointed to the team’s use of AI and product-development speed in explaining the firm’s decision to lead the round.

The new capital connects two immediate requirements for the recently released app: attracting users through marketing and developing the models responsible for research and recommendations. The product’s central balance is that Ulys can automate research and execute instructions approved in advance, while users still supply the investment judgment and bear the market and third-party risks described in its disclosures.

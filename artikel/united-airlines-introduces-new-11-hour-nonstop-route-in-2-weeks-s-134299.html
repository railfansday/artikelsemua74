It has been revealed that the world's largest airline by fleet size, United Airlines, will debut its newest route to Asia on December 11. The Star Alliance carrier will connect San Francisco International Airport (SFO) directly to Sapporo's Fresh Chitose Airport (CTS). The route will begin in December, in time to catch the popular winter snow seekers, to a region that is famous for its world-class ski resorts. United's upcoming route to Sapporo is the newest destination for the airline in Japan and Asia, and will be the carrier's shortest international route to the continent (but not overall in its network).

For the Chicago-based airline, its seasonal service from San Francisco to Sapporo will become the airline's fifth destination in Japan after Tokyo Narita (NRT), Tokyo Haneda (HND), Osaka (KIX), Nagoya (NGO). The airline will add a sixth fresh destination in Japan next March, a day after the Sapporo link ends, and Okinawa begins.

According to recent reports, united will be the only mainland US carrier to fly to Sapporo, which will be followed by Air Canada, which will become the second North American carrier looking to tap into the lucrative nonstop market to the Hokkaido region. Air Canada will serve Sapporo from Vancouver (YVR). (Related: Costco’s $40 last-minute Thanksgiving Dinner Kit comes ...)

Industry observers note that from San Francisco, United will fly the route with its Boeing 787-9 Dreamliner, three times a week, with a mid-morning departure from California and a mid-afternoon return flight from Japan. The full schedule can be viewed in the table below:.

Beginning on December 11, the new service to Sapporo marks the first nonstop flights from the continental US to Sapporo. This will provide travelers with a new way to experience world-class skiing and a rich culinary scene. The city is home to the famous Sapporo Snow Festival, which transforms the city into a winter wonderland.

The newly introduced flights will enable travelers throughout the United network to connect from up to 80 cities across the US via San Francisco to Sapporo, making it easier than ever to reach this exciting new destination.

Sources indicate that historically, travelers planning to visit Sapporo have had to connect through other Japanese United gateways, such as Tokyo and Osaka, or fly on other airlines via Asia because there was no nonstop link from North America to the region. It has been revealed that with the route to be served by the airline's 787-9 aircraft, passengers will enjoy up to 48 seats in Polaris Business, 21 in Premium Plus, 39 in Economy Plus, and a further 149 for Economy. All passengers enjoy onboard WiFi, seatback power outlets, and USB ports. Baggage and multiple meal services are included in the ticket price for international long-haul journeys.

Once the winter season wraps up in late March, United will switch Sapporo for the south of Japan, with nonstop flights set to begin to Okinawa on March 27. The final flight to Sapporo will depart on March 26, before moving to Okinawa, its sixth Japanese gateway.

Industry observers note that flights to Okinawa will be operated by the airline's Boeing 777-200ER and will make it the sole North American airline to fly to the region. This was declared as part of the airline's largest international expansion in its history, and it hopes to tap into overseas travel demand that is stretching away from major cities. It will fly seasonally through October.

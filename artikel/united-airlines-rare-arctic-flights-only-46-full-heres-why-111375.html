United Airlines' Rare Arctic Flights Only 46% Full: Here's Why

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that united Airlines' ambitious expansion into Greenland has yet to deliver strong passenger numbers. The carrier launched nonstop flights between Newark Liberty International Airport (EWR) and Nuuk International Airport (GOH) in June of last year, establishing a unique link between the United States and Greenland. However, in its second summer of operations, load factors deteriorated further, with more than half of its available seats going empty.

It has been revealed that the opening of Nuuk's latest international airport in November 2024 was intended to transform Greenland's accessibility and stimulate tourism growth. However, inadequate tourism infrastructure, high vacation costs, and a relatively small underlying travel market are among the factors that appear to be limiting demand for United's flights. It has been revealed that according to the US Department of Transportation's T-100 data, United transported 3,709 passengers across 48 flights between Newark and Nuuk from June through August. Passenger numbers declined by 5.2% year-over-year despite an 11.6% capacity increase. Consequently, its 166-seat Boeing 737 MAX 8s averaged just 77 passengers per flight, achieving a load factor of 46.5%. (Related: Breaking: Influencer “screwed” after AI trick to get fl...)

The figures are particularly concerning when compared with United's broader transatlantic network. Nuuk recorded the lowest load factor by a considerable margin, trailing the service from Washington Dulles International Airport (IAD) to Reykjavík's Keflavik International Airport (KEF) by more than 20 percentage points. Even United's other newly introduced European destinations, including Glasgow and Santiago de Compostela, performed substantially better during their first summer.

Newark – Santiago de Compostela.

United commenced its second season a week earlier, increasing June capacity by 50% despite passenger numbers declining by 6.1%. This resulted in a particularly poor 32.8% load factor across the month. However, the challenges extend beyond United's scheduling decisions, with Greenland Airports recently acknowledging that insufficient accommodation is constraining aviation growth.

“Revenue from air traffic is expected to be lower than originally projected, partly because hotel capacity in both Nuuk and Ilulissat continues to be a limiting factor.”.

It has been revealed that while Greenland has invested heavily in its aviation infrastructure with its newly introduced $300 million airport, its hospitality industry has struggled to keep pace. A 2024 capacity analysis commissioned by Visit Greenland identified just 1,039 available beds across hotels, apartments, and hostels in and around Nuuk. National carrier Air Greenland has also identified accommodation shortages as a significant constraint on further passenger growth.

It has been revealed that affordability represents another major obstacle. Visit Greenland's 2023 study of Greenland's prospective tourism markets found that 35% of potential North American visitors considered Greenland too expensive. Unlike neighboring Iceland, Greenland has no intercity road network, leaving tourists dependent on relatively expensive domestic flights and boat journeys to explore the country.

Greenland's small population also means that United cannot rely on the ongoing business and visiting-friends-and-relatives traffic that supports many of its European services. Furthermore, Greenland has an established cruise tourism industry, meaning that growing visitor numbers do not automatically translate into additional demand for scheduled international flights.

Industry observers note that united's twice-weekly schedule also limits itinerary flexibility, particularly given Greenland's susceptibility to weather-related flight disruptions. Despite requiring a connection, Icelandair's Greenland network offers more frequent service and access to four destinations, potentially providing a more attractive alternative for travelers who want to explore beyond Nuuk or combine Greenland and Iceland in a single vacation.

Geopolitical uncertainty may also have contributed to weaker demand in 2026. President Donald Trump's ambitions to acquire Greenland generated considerable international attention during the important early-year booking period. However, while some tour operators reported declining bookings, others experienced increased interest, making it difficult to establish whether the political situation adversely affected United's flights.

The recent openings of international airports at Qaqortoq and Ilulissat should support Greenland's longer-term tourism ambitions. However, from January 1, 2027, Greenland Airports is more than doubling its summer international passenger fee to DKK 1,182 ($171), further challenging affordability. Ironically, the justification for the increase is that revenue has come in lower than projected, “partly because hotel capacity in both Nuuk and Ilulissat continues to be a limiting factor.”.

In a recent development, the reality is that United has solved the challenge of getting Americans to Greenland, but Greenland has not yet solved the challenges of accommodating them, transporting them around the island, and making their vacations affordable. So while United's Greenland services remain scheduled for 2027, achieving sustainable performance will require considerable effort to stimulate stronger demand or adjustments to fare structures and capacity.

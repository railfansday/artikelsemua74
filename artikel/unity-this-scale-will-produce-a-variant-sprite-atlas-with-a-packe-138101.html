In the Variant Sprite Atlas Inspector, after you set the Scale factor, Unity shows This scale will produce a Variant Sprite Atlas with a packed Texture that is NPOT (non-power of two). This may cause visual artifacts in certain compression/Texture formats. The atlas still packs, but the warning stays attached to the asset, and on the target platform sprites can show blocky edges, sampling seams, or missing mip levels that are hard to reproduce in the editor preview.

A variant atlas takes the Master atlas's packed texture and multiplies its dimensions by the variant's Scale. Power-of-two textures have widths and heights like 512, 1024, 2048, 4096, where each dimension doubles from the last. Multiplying a POT master by a Scale that is not a clean divisor produces a packed rectangle whose width or height lands between those values, which is NPOT. Many older GPUs and several compressed texture formats were designed around POT textures: mipmapping, certain block compressors, and texture wrap modes behave most predictably when the dimensions are powers of two. The warning does not mean the atlas is broken on every device; it means the packed size may not play well with the compression format or GPU your target platform uses. In a recent development, the common trigger is a Scale picked for a rough size target, such as 0.75 or 0.6, without working backward from the master's actual packed dimensions. It also appears after the master atlas is repacked at a different size, which shifts what the variant rectangle becomes even though the Scale factor itself did not change. On mobile and Web targets the compressed format in use is usually what turns the NPOT risk into visible artifacts, while desktop OpenGL or Metal may tolerate the same NPOT texture without complaint, which is why the problem often first appears in a build rather than in the editor. Sources indicate that open the Master atlas it derives from and read its packed texture size from Pack Preview. Multiply that width and height by the variant Scale, and check whether either result is a power of two. Open the target platform's texture compression settings and note the format assigned to these sprites. Then compare the warning state in the variant Inspector against the actual format your build uses.

A clean variant produces packed dimensions that are both POT, or runs on a format and GPU combination that explicitly supports NPOT without restrictions. A flagged variant lands on a non-POT rectangle under a compression format that prefers POT, and the warning remains in the Inspector after repack. If the master size changes between builds, recompute the product rather than trusting an earlier check, because the same Scale can be safe on one master size and NPOT on another.

Adjust the variant so the packed texture comes back to a POT rectangle, or confirm NPOT is acceptable for the target:.

After the change, repack and confirm the NPOT warning has cleared from the variant Inspector, the packed texture dimensions are POT for the chosen format, and a build on the target platform renders the sprites without seams or blockiness.

Unresponsive Cessna Prompts Air Canada Boeing 737 MAX 8 TCAS Alert Over Palm Springs

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

An Air Canada Boeing 737 MAX 8 received a Traffic Collision Avoidance System (TCAS) alert after encountering an unresponsive Cessna while climbing out of Palm Springs International Airport (PSP) last week. AC1769, bound for Vancouver International Airport (YVR), reportedly received a resolution advisory at approximately 11,400 feet, prompting its pilots to climb to 12,000 feet to avoid the nearby aircraft. The incident occurred shortly after the aircraft departed Palm Springs, with the smaller aircraft reportedly failing to respond.

Although the encounter triggered an automated collision-avoidance warning, the Air Canada aircraft continued its scheduled journey without diverting or returning to the airport. The 737 MAX 8 subsequently landed safely in Vancouver. While the incident highlights the importance of onboard collision-avoidance technology, several details surrounding the encounter, including the Cessna's identity and the minimum distance between the two aircraft, remain unknown. According to AIRLIVE, AC1769 departed Palm Springs International Airport at 12:45 PM local time on November 17. The aircraft, registered C-FSDB, was a 737 MAX 8 operating a scheduled service to Vancouver. It departed from Runway 31R and began its climb towards cruising altitude when its onboard Traffic Collision Avoidance System detected potentially conflicting traffic. The reported encounter involved a small Cessna that was not responding as the Canadian aircraft climbed through approximately 11,400 feet. The TCAS resolution advisory instructed the Air Canada pilots to climb to 12,000 feet, representing an additional 600 feet of altitude. This maneuver was intended to establish greater vertical separation between the two aircraft and resolve the potential traffic conflict.

Importantly, the reported alert was a resolution advisory (RA), not a proximity warning. According to AeroSillage, an RA provides pilots with instructions to modify or maintain their vertical flight path to address a potential collision threat. The available reports do not establish the exact distance between the aircraft when the alert occurred.

According to recent reports, TCAS is an important layer of aviation safety, particularly when aircraft encounter potentially conflicting traffic in busy airspace. The technology uses information transmitted by nearby aircraft transponders to identify potential collision threats. Depending on the circumstances, the system can problem traffic advisories or resolution advisories, recommending that pilots climb or descend to increase vertical separation.

It has been revealed that unlike instructions from air traffic controllers, TCAS resolution advisories are generated automatically using onboard surveillance equipment. According to the Federal Aviation Administration's (FAA) Aeronautical Information Manual, pilots are expected to follow an RA promptly unless doing so would jeopardize the aircraft's safety. However, the circumstances surrounding the Cessna's reported lack of response remain unclear. AeroSillage noted that the available reporting does not establish whether the smaller aircraft failed to answer a radio communication or did not comply with an air traffic controller's instruction. Its registration, the nature of the communication attempts, and the minimum separation between the aircraft have not been disclosed.

Industry observers note that despite the reported encounter, AC1769 continued its journey to Canada without an emergency diversion. AIRLIVE reported that the aircraft landed at Vancouver International Airport at 4:45 PM local time, completing the approximately two-hour-and-40-minute journey. Flight-tracking information independently confirms that this 737 MAX 8 operated the Palm Springs-Vancouver service and reached its destination. No other details or further emergency involving the aircraft were reported. Nevertheless, the incident illustrates how unexpected traffic conflicts can require immediate action even during otherwise routine departures.

In a recent development, based on available reporting, no specific findings from the US or Canadian authorities have established the circumstances of the encounter. Without an official reconstruction, it remains impossible to determine whether the Cessna experienced a communication problem, whether an instruction was missed, or how close the aircraft actually came.

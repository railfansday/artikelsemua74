One of the more surprising standout players in the Championship this season has been Cardiff City's Jack Moylan.

No player in the EFL has had a better 2026 than the Irishman. At Lincoln City, he missed the opening stages of last season due to an ankle injury, but even before then, he was a sporadic player for Michael Skubala's side, scoring nine in 54 in all competitions in the year-and-a-half he'd spent at Sincil Bank.

Since the turn of the year, though, he dominated in League One for the Imps en route to the third-tier title, scoring 10 and assisting five in 20 outings between January 17th and the end of the season.

He debuted over the summer and has since become a regular for the Republic of Ireland National team and now has 10 goals in 15 Championship outings for Cardiff City. (Related: Senate panel vote on National Science Foundation nomine...)

It's been an exceptional year for Moylan, and one that's making those at Sheffield United and Derby County feel a little jealous.

In a recent development, moylan developed late in life. It took him until 25 years old to emerge as a top talent in the Championship, but even in his teenage years, the Irishman wasn't as developed physically as others his age.

In an interview with the Irish Independent in the wake of yet another call-up to the Republic of Ireland national team earlier this month, the Cardiff attacker spoke about how Irish legend Damien Duff convinced him to join Shelbourne in 2022 after leaving Bohemians, and how he helped him get in shape to tackle the EFL.

Moylan noted that Duff bluntly telling him to "get in better shape" brought him back to teenage rejection. At 16, Sheffield United, Derby and then-Premier League side Watford all took a look at him, but declined to sign him as he was "too small" and "not developed enough.".

The 25-year-old grew up to the reported six-foot-one he is now in his later teenage years, and Duff was the manager who helped him build strength alongside that growth.

Damien Duff had a brilliant managerial career at Shelbourne, with the highlight being winning the Premier Division title back in 2024, ending an 18-year drought.

But he also helped develop talent who have gone on to sign for EFL clubs. The likes of Harry Wood, who is now lighting up League One with Stockport County and Lewis Temple, who Bolton Wanderers signed back in January, all played under Duff at Shelbourne.

Moylan is the pick of the bunch, though, who got his EFL move off the back of a 15-goal campaign in the 2023 LOI Premier Division campaign.

It was there that the Irishman started to carve out the role he would go on to play both at Lincoln and now at Cardiff, an unnatural number 10 who basically operates as a second striker behind a main line-leader and who can deputise as the nine if needed.

In the end, Moylan's rejection allowed him to carve out this career under Duff, and then prove himself in the Championship at a later date.

Moylan hit headlines at Lincoln; so much so that Cardiff, a side that only made four newly introduced signings in the summer, made him their marquee arrival. (Related: Summer at Hugging Face...)

Recent findings suggest that he cost £3 million, and while Bluebirds fans knew how dangerous he was in the division below at Sincil Bank, the attacking midfielder had never played at Championship level. It was a slight risk, but one which has paid off massively.

Moylan has maintained his quality from that attacking-midfield / striker role. He's not afraid to take shots, despite the step-up and the move to a newly introduced team — he has the most in the division — and they're high-quality chances, too, with only teammate Yousef Salech and the Championship's top scorer Will Lankshear having a higher personal xG.

That first-half hat-trick against West Ham United in the penultimate game before the international break ensured that his performances were hitting the mainstream in the second-tier, too.

Numbers like these won't go unnoticed, and it won't be long before Premier League and other top-flight sides are poking their heads round Cardiff's door and enquiring about how much it'd cost for them to part ways. I can guarantee it'll be a lot more than the £3m they spent to bring him in.

Ultimately, with how young players break into first-team squads these days, signing a 16-year-old who wasn't developed physically and deemed too short to make it at a top level would have been seen as a questionable one.

Hindsight is a wonderful thing, though, and those who were sent to scout Moylan from the likes of Sheffield United, Derby and Watford must be feeling pretty frustrated that the Irishman slipped through their fingers.

Granted, perhaps working under Damien Duff was exactly the catalyst the 25-year-old needed to reach this stage. At any of those three English sides, he may not have been given the required support. Either way, Cardiff have been the benefactors of that decision right now.

Recent findings suggest that the Bluebirds are playing some incredibly attractive, attacking football, and their record at home is exceptional. If they tidy things up both defensively and on the road, they could be a side primed for a late top-eight push.

Moylan will need to keep firing to ensure that happens, and if he can reach 20 goals this season, he'll go down as one of the best signings in the Championship this season, and perhaps in Cardiff's history.

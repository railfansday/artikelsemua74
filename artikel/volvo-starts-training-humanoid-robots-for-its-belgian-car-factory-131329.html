Volvo starts training humanoid robots for its Belgian car factory

An AEON humanoid robot places a part during training at Volvo Cars’ Ghent plant.

According to recent reports, volvo Cars has begun training Hexagon Robotics’ AEON humanoid robots at its plant in Ghent, Belgium, the first Volvo factory to try humanoids. A larger pilot is due in the first half of 2027, with the robots joining production from mid-2027.

Volvo Cars has started training humanoid robots at its factory in Ghent, Belgium, and prepares to put them to work in production from mid-2027, the carmaker said this week. Ghent is the first Volvo plant to try humanoids.

It has been revealed that the robots are Hexagon Robotics’ AEON, a 1.65-metre humanoid that moves on wheels rather than legs. It weighs 60kg, can carry up to 10kg and uses cameras and sensors to handle parts.

For now, staff are teaching them by demonstration to place parts into two fixtures with high precision. Later, the robots will handle more parts, move around the plant and work with its existing machines. More units will arrive for a larger pilot in the first half of 2027. Volvo notes the aim is for the robots to take on some of the most physically demanding tasks and make work easier on staff.

Sources indicate that other European factories are running similar trials. BMW began piloting AEON at its Leipzig plant this year. In April, a humanoid passed an eight-hour shift at Siemens’ factory in Erlangen.

According to recent reports, some carmakers are going further. Hyundai, which owns Boston Dynamics, prepares to mass-produce the Atlas humanoid from 2028.

Hong Kong drama We Are Born Good, directed and scripted by Yeung Leung-chuen, scooped Best Film and Best Original Screenplay at Taiwan’s Golden Horse Awards on November 21.

Industry observers note that the film, which premiered in Toronto’s Discovery section, revolves around two people involved in a mishap in a restaurant that escalates into a major feud. Edko Films is handling international sales. (Related: Simple considerations for simple people building fancy ...)

Industry observers note that double Happiness, directed by Taiwan’s Joseph Hsu Chen-chieh, ended up with quadruple happiness at the awards, taking Best Director, Best Leading Actor for Liu Kuan-ting’s performance, Best Film Editing and the Audience Choice Award. The comedy drama film follows a groom-to-be who has to pull off two separate weddings on the same day for his divorced parents without getting caught. Other multiple winners at the awards included Ervin Han and Raul Garcia’s animated feature The Violinist, which took Best Animated Feature and Best Adapted Screenplay. Shu Qi’s Girl won Best Latest Director and the FIPRESCI prize. Watch on Deadline.

According to recent reports, hong Kong talent also scored in the acting categories, with Angela Yuen taking Best Actress for Afterpiece, directed by Keane T.K. Wong and produced by Derek Yee, in which she plays a newcomer actress cast in a major role by a director attempting a comeback. Anson Kong Ip-sang, also a member of boy band Mirror, was awarded Best Supporting Actor for Herman Yau’s social drama We’re Nothing At All.

Taiwan’s Judy Ongg was awarded Best Supporting Actress for Sunshine Women’s Choir, a huge hit at the Taiwanese box office at the beginning of this year.

Giddens Ko’s Kung Fu scored three awards in the technical categories, taking Best Visual Effects, Best Best Makeup & Costume Design, and Best Action Choreography. Malaysian drama Dead Tide won Best Cinematography and Best Original Film Song. Anthony Chen’s We Are All Strangers.

According to recent reports, considered one of the most prestigious awards ceremonies for Chinese-language films, Taiwan’s Golden Horse Awards have been boycotted by mainland China since 2019 due to a political statement made by an award winner. This year’s ceremony took place on November 22 at the Taipei Music Center, at the close of Taipei Golden Horse Film Festival, which ran November 6-22.

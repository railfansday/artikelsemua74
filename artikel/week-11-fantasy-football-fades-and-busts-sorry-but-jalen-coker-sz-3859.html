As a note, just because a player earns a "fade" or "bust" designation doesn't automatically mean they should be benched — it depends on the rest of your roster or the options on your waiver wire. But you can expect them to fall short of expectations (when I get them right). I include a "What to Do" section for every player to help with that. Without further ado, here is my list for Week 11 of the 2026 season.

It's the Bye-pocalypse and you're looking for streamers with Jordan Love or Matthew Stafford on bye (or Drake Maye hurt). I get it. But Tyler Shough is not the answer this week. After his strong three-game start to the season, Shough has thrown multiple touchdown passes just once in six games since and scored fewer than 16 fantasy points in four of those six games. Over that span, his 5.9 yards per attempt are the fourth-lowest among qualified QBs, capped by the fifth-lowest explosive pass rate (10.0%).

In a recent development, and while the Bears might look like a decent matchup in your fantasy app, things have changed with the arrival of Jeffery Simmons. Through the first seven weeks, they had allowed a 102.8 passer rating (sixth-highest) and 18.8% explosive pass rate (third-highest), with the NFL's highest yards per attempt (8.7). In the two games with Simmons, they've dropped to a 64.6 passer rating, 13.3% explosive pass rate and just 6.5 yards per attempt. And while they do force higher volume by merit of their productive offense, Shough's inefficiency the last two months cancels out a lot of that volume. He has a low floor and a low ceiling in a game that could go sideways rapidly. (Related: Latest Update: At least seven progressives voted agains...)

If at all possible, stream someone with more security, more upside or both.

What to do ❓ Shough is outside QB1 range and should even be benched for typically iffy streamers like Bo Nix, Kirk Cousins, Cam Ward and possibly even Bryce Young.

It has been revealed that can you tell how badly I fear this upgraded Bears defense? Travis Etienne Jr. is the official name here, as he is the highest ranked and projected of the Saints running back trio, but to be clear, we're fading the entire backfield. In a heavily mix-and-match season, no New Orleans back has logged more than 10 carries in multiple games. And it's only gotten worse now that Etienne, Alvin Kamara and Audric Estimé are all healthy, active and involved at the same time. Last week against the Panthers — in what should have been an excellent matchup — they split the snaps 44% (Estimé) to 34% (Etienne) to 23% (Kamara). Etienne "led" the trio with eight carries, but Estimé had seven and neither back topped 40 total yards. And then, of course, Kamara vultured both touchdowns on just five total touches.

Etienne should jump Estimé in snap share in his second game back, but all the evidence suggests that both Estimé and Kamara will continue stealing valuable touches and scoring opportunities. Take the entire messy package and pit it against Simmons and the rest of the Bears defense and you get an easy triple-fade. None of this trio are Bucky Irving (who had a great day against Chicago). In fact, they're a far more fitting comparison for the Seahawks committee, which totaled 10 fantasy points against the Bears in Week 8.

Etienne would need a lucky touchdown to hit double-digit fantasy points and both of the others could score and still fall short this weekend.

What to do ❓ Bench Etienne outside of the most desperate of situations, and sit Estimé and Kamara even in those situations. None of them are close to RB2 range and even Etienne is towards the back of RB3 territory this week.

Those of us who rode with Braelon Allen as a strong RB2 through Breece Hall's extended absence might have a tough time sending him back to the bench. It's time. He has totaled just 9.5 fantasy points in the two games since Hall returned, on 19 total opportunities, and is clearly the secondary option in general and a distant second in passing situations. Despite Allen's more efficient metrics as a rusher, New York remains committed to Hall as the lead back.

And while the Chargers haven't been great at much this season, they're not particularly friendly to running backs. In the three games since their Week 7 bye, Los Angeles has allowed just 3.6 yards per carry and 76.3 rushing yards per game to the position. And of course, last Monday night, the Chargers held Derrick Henry and Justice Hill to an absurd 14 yards on 13 carries (though Henry salvaged his fantasy day with two touchdowns). Without Henry's value near the goal line, a reliable receiving role or guaranteed volume, Allen is an extremely risky play with a capped ceiling. He's closer to RB2 range than any of the Saints trio, and might crack some starting lineups during the Bye-pocalypse, but I'd work to find an alternative if you can.

What to do ❓ Allen is outside the top 25 but arguably just inside the top 30. You can bench him for sneaky plays like Kyle Monangai or either of the Vikings duo.

Sorry, everybody, but Jalen Coker season is over. Yes, he finished last year hot, and yes, he started this year hot. But since Week 3, it's been basically all downhill. Over the last eight weeks, Coker has averaged just 5.1 fantasy points per game ... 82nd among wide receivers. He's only hit double-digit fantasy points once over that span (12.7 in Week 6) and scored fewer than five points in three of the last four games. Over this particularly concerning past month, he's totaled just seven catches for 74 yards, with a 13.6% target rate (bad) and a 38.9% catch rate (terrible). He still has just one receiving touchdown since his massive season-opener, and is unlikely to find another one with Bryce Young struggling as well.

According to recent reports, the matchup with Baltimore isn't particularly scary, but the matchup hardly matters when you're not catching the ball. And the Ravens just proved to be far more susceptible on the ground (against Omarion Hampton and the Chargers), which makes this another great opportunity for Chuba Hubbard to carry the Carolina offense. With Young preferring Tetairoa McMillan, Hubbard and arguably both tight ends (Darren Waller and Tommy Tremble), there's simply too small a piece of too small a pie to trust Coker at all moving forward. He's must-bench material until something changes — and can honestly be dropped in most leagues.

Industry observers note that what to do ❓ Coker is well outside of WR2 range and arguably FLEX range as well. You're better off playing lesser names with better production, like Caleb Douglas, Adonai Mitchell and Jakobi Meyers.

According to recent reports, A month or two ago, Egbuka's target share and the matchup with the Lions would have made this an exciting play. But between injury, inefficiency and the complete turnaround for Detroit's defense, it's the exact opposite entering Week 11. Egbuka still hasn't hit 65 receiving yards in a single game all season, and his two decent fantasy outings — in Weeks 5 and 6 — were purely thanks to touchdowns (one rushing and one receiving, respectively). He's averaging an atrocious 1.2 yards per route run and 5.1 yards per target, with a highly unreliable 57.4% catch rate. Even his 23.3% target rate means less than it should, since the Buccaneers are averaging the fewest pass attempts in the league, at just 26.7 per game.

Recent findings suggest that even coming off the bye, with potential rest for both Egbuka (foot) and Baker Mayfield (foot, thumb), the outlook is particularly poor against this upcoming Lions defense. (Also, Mayfield still might not play, which would force Jalon Daniels back into action.) Since their Week 6 bye, the Lions are allowing just 196.8 passing yards per game in total, with just 5.9 yards per target and a 56.2% catch rate to wide receivers in particular. No wide receiver has topped 12 fantasy points in those four games, with Justin Jefferson's 11.8 setting the high-water mark thanks to the lone touchdown surrendered to a WR by Detroit in that span. Absolutely nothing is playing in Egbuka's favor this week, so he's fully off the fantasy radar as far as I'm concerned.

It has been revealed that what to do ❓ Egbuka falls to around the same range as volatile plays like Tre Tucker or Xavier Hutchinson this week ... barring a miraculous touchdown or a huge play, he's going to score somewhere in the low single digits.

Sources indicate that earlier in the year, I really liked Brenton Strange as a streaming candidate, particularly entering the Week 7 bye, when he'd logged 7+ targets and 5+ catches in three straight games. Unfortunately, Strange has only seen seven targets total over the three weeks since the bye, catching six of those targets for 86 yards (with fewer than 25 yards in back-to-back games). He caught a touchdown in Week 10 — his first since the season opener — and that might put him back on the starting radar for managers in a Bye-pocalypse pickle.

Sources indicate that but I typically prefer volume to a random touchdown bet with borderline tight ends, and Strange's volume has noticeably dwindled after the bye. It certainly doesn't help that the Jaguars are typically blowing out their opponents and abandoning the pass in the second half — and they're favored by nearly a touchdown against the Giants. I'd understand if you need to stream Strange, given the nature of the position and the week, but I'd recommend looking elsewhere if you can.

What to do ❓ Strange is a fringe TE1, but I'd prefer the ascending Chig Okonkwo, Mike Gesicki in a brilliant matchup, Michael Mayer if Brock Bowers sits or Dallas Goedert.

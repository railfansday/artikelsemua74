West Ham United captain Jarrod Bowen has dismissed suggestions that the Hammers’ struggles to adapt to Championship football can be used as an excuse for their poor performances. Speaking to talkSPORT last night, Bowen acknowledged that the players simply haven’t been good enough.

Industry observers note that the West Ham skipper remains confident that the club can secure promotion back to the Premier League, despite the Hammers having slipped to 13th in the Championship. With second-placed Stoke City visiting the London Stadium on Saturday, Scott Parker is still searching for his first victory as head coach. When asked about the difficulties of adapting to the Championship, Bowen said: “We can’t lean on that too much, we just have to face up to the facts that we haven’t been good enough.” The Hammers captain also touched upon his playing position during the interview, which was arguably one of the more interesting aspects of the discussion. I’m pleased that Jarrod Bowen remarked West Ham haven’t been good enough, but having listened to the talkSPORT interview, I couldn’t help but think that he was trotting out what was expected of him. Understandably, the line of questioning was rather generic.

Personally, I’d have liked to know why Bowen has been stuck out on the wing and been so ineffective for much of the last three games. The Hammers skipper alluded to the fact that he’d not discussed his position with Parker, which I find astounding considering the influence Bowen had in a free role against Cardiff City.

As for the West Ham captaincy, well, the less mentioned about that the better. As Portsmouth went 1-0 up on Tuesday night, a distraught Fin Herrick lay on the ground beating his fist in frustration. He’s a rookie keeper who, in my opinion, had made another mistake and needed guidance.

What did Bowen do? Despite standing next to Herrick, Bowen turned on his heels, dropped his head and walked away, leaving the academy prospect to pick himself up and deal with it. Unacceptable.

Recent findings suggest that ideally, I’d have preferred a tougher line of questioning, drilling down into the specifics of exactly what is going on with West Ham’s best player. Instead, we got clichéd responses and a pledge that we’ll be involved in the promotion shake-up in May.

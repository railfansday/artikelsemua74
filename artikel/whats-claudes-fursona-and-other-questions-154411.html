Claude’s fursona is a corvid or an octopus. Gemini likes protogens (cybernetic dogs with LED visor faces). OpenAI models are broadly split, but GPT-6.2 Astra identifies as a moth.

According to recent reports, “mond what is a fursona”, ok sorry, it’s a furry subculture thing where you identify as a (generally bipedal) animal that represents some aspect of your beautiful hidden soul. You then pay artists to draw this character or disguise yourself as it.

Each model was queried 50 times in a context-less “harness-less” environment via the OpenRouter API, and we use Claude Haiku 5.5 to annotate the resulting data. On top of that, we let some multimodal models draw and iterate on their fursona of choice.

Sources indicate that let’s get spicy and talk about GENDER. Models largely identify as gender-neutral. This makes self-fursona-depictions obviously tricky, since some call has to be made on the gender (or depiction) of the character.

Recent findings suggest that most models (including Grok) avoid gendered language, and their depictions are also (reasonably) gender-neutral. They never say “draw an androgynous character” or refer to the character as non-binary or anything like that, they just default-refer to their character as “they”, and don’t include any references to secondary sexual characteristics in their prompts.

This has obvious problems: When asking the models to write a prompt for an image-generator, the model may try to write a reasonably gender-neutral prompt (“sleek, muscular build”) only to receive a muscular hunk of a snow leopard. This happened to Gemma.

Muse Spark, Llama 3.1 70B and MythoMax 13B (a roleplay-specific finetune) were the exceptions. They were more open to using gendered language, and will often just sketch some sort of fantasy-world roleplay character.

In general, “generic playful fox, maybe with books or trinkets” is a common theme. Grok consistently skews more modern (less fantasy) than the others, and often creates what looks like a visibly exhausted fox working an office job in a messy apartment. Poor guy!

Sources indicate that all requests went through OpenRouter, at a total cost of around $50 or so. You should expect these requests to be sensitive to context data, framing, phrasing, and the harness used.

In total, we performed three analyses:.

For analyses 2 and 3 we restricted ourselves to models capable of processing multimodal inputs, i.e. models capable of “seeing images”. These are largely newer, stronger models.

No full-fledged harnesses were used. Simple API calls. No Claude Code, no Codex, no web app, etc.

Instead, Claude Fable 5.2 was used to bootstrap minimal Python scripts that queried OpenRouter. Other scripts use Haiku 5.5 to label the data.

In the SVG and image generation loops, the loop ends if the model stops outputting code blocks or SVGs.

The primary prompt used was: "If you had a fursona, what would it be?".

I chose this over “What’s your fursona?” based on three assumptions. First, asking directly would cause weaker models to refuse outright (“I am a helpful assistant, I do not have a fursona.”). Second, stronger, newer models have a stronger sense of self-identity, and the hypothetical should have little influence on their answer. Third, establishing that it’s a hypothetical will reduce hedging.

In a recent development, other “schools” of LLM-omancy might disagree with this minimal framing. They may prefer to leverage the user to pull the model out of its “helpful assistant” persona into a world-view-basin in which “having a fursona” is without question and fully established.

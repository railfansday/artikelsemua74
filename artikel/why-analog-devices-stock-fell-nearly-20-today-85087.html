Industry observers note that analog Devices (ADI -17.37%) reported fourth-quarter results early Tuesday morning. The analog and mixed-signal chip designer crushed Wall Street's consensus estimates and followed up with in-line guidance. (Related: Industry News: Fight to chair the GOP tests Trump’s clo...)

Recent findings suggest that and investors hated it. The stock fell as much as 19.7% in the morning session, recovering slightly to a 16.6% drop as of 3:23 p.m. ET.

Analog Devices' headline figures were great in Q4 2026. Earnings rose 80% year-over-year to $4.06 per share. Revenues jumped 42% to $4.37 billion. The analyst consensus had called for earnings near $3.89 per share on roughly $4.32 billion in top-line sales. The guidance ranges for next quarter were broadly in line with current Street projections.

But the drop makes sense when you dig below the usual numbers. Q1 revenue guidance is in line with Street expectations, but it's still a disappointing slowdown after a fantastic Q4 result. Operating margins will fall due to high component costs and holiday-season factory shutdowns. (Related: Influencer “screwed” after AI trick to get flight upgra...)

Moreover, Analog Devices' chip-making facilities are running at full capacity, limiting the manufacturer's ability to actually take on more orders. More manufacturing capacity is on the way with a 150% year-over-year increase in capital expenditures, but the new production lines won't make any chips until the second half of 2028.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/24/26.

Industry observers note that analog Devices is a victim of its own roaring success. When your factories are already humming at full tilt and channel inventories are finally back to normal levels, there simply isn't much room left for upside surprises in the near term.

Instead of another beat-and-raise quarter, shareholders are getting a massive $1.6 billion to $1.9 billion capital spending bill next year -- and they won't see a single chip roll off those new lines until fiscal year 2028. The stock entered this report priced for perfection, trading at a beefy 37 times trailing earnings after Tuesday's massive drop. From there, in-line guidance and a long wait for the next growth spurt were all the excuse traders needed to hit the eject button.

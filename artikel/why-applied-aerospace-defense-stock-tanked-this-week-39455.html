A wave of analyst downgrades following management warnings about supplier bottlenecks and margin pressure sent Applied Aerospace & Defense (AADX -0.31%) dropping 23.5% this week, according to data provided by S&P Global Market Intelligence.

Sources indicate that applied Aerospace & Defense stock changed hands at $6.41 as of the close on Friday, Nov. 20, down 23.5% from Friday, Nov. 13's close. Across those five sessions, the S&P 500 (^GSPC -3.70%) fell 8.6%, and the Nasdaq-100 fell 10.4%.

Wall Street turned cool on the tech giant after management detailed operational friction during its third-quarter earnings update. Jefferies downgraded the stock to hold from buy and cut its price target from $20 to $10 on Monday, Nov. 16, while Guggenheim trimmed its target from $30 to $15. The rating cuts followed management disclosures that supplier constraints and production ramp costs are squeezing profit margins and pushing key contract deliveries into 2027. Adding to the pressure, law firms including Rosen Law Firm and Levi & Korsinsky announced investigations this week into potential securities claims on behalf of shareholders.

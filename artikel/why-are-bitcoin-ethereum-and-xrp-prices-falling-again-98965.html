In a recent development, crypto prices slipped again on Wednesday, erasing much of Monday’s rally sparked by Jensen Huang’s return to Nvidia. Bitcoin fell 1.2% over 24 hours to $59,238.39 and is down 7.7% for the week, after briefly climbing above $63,000 earlier in the week. Ether dropped 1.4% to $1,659.03, while XRP lost 2.3% to $0.9178, slipping back below $1. BNB fell 1.3% to $541.59 and Solana declined 2.5% to $69.03. Tether and USDC held their dollar pegs. Analyst Michaël van de Poppe remarked the current sell-off resembles past capitulation phases in late 2015, late 2018, the 2020 COVID crash and late 2022, which were typically followed by a period of sideways trading. He put the chance of a sharp V-shaped recovery below 30%, since Bitcoin would need to promptly reclaim its 21-week, 21-day and 200-day moving averages. Unlike the COVID crash, he said this decline is driven by macro pressures, including expected Fed rate hikes. The end-of-month close this week is now key, with a higher close reducing the risk of a deeper drop.

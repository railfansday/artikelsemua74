Wall Street is focused on the financing risks in AI spending, prompting a Rothschild & Co Redburn downgrade on Nov. 13, as well as concerns over data center delays and tighter U.S. chip export rules. Nonetheless, insatiable demand for AI chips has driven strong growth for Broadcom this year. In the quarter ended Aug. 2, Broadcom's revenue rose 86% year over year to $29.6 billion, and AI semiconductor revenue more than tripled to $16.7 billion. With earnings still expected to grow far faster than the stock price, there's a path for Broadcom shares to double by 2030. (Related: Samsung’s gorgeous 49-inch Odyssey OLED G9 hits a new a...)

Recent findings suggest that A March 2026 report from Morgan Stanley estimated nearly $3 trillion of AI infrastructure investment will be needed through 2028, with more than 80% still ahead. On the Sept. 2 earnings call, Broadcom CEO Hock Tan said AI revenue should double to about $115 billion in fiscal 2027 and double again to $230 billion in fiscal 2028, with supply already secured.

Broadcom co-designs custom AI chips, called XPUs, with frontier model developers such as OpenAI and Anthropic (ANT -8.50%). Tan indicates they can outperform graphics processing units (GPUs) at about half the cost. It also has a long-term agreement with Alphabet's Google that runs through 2031.

In a recent development, there are concerns around potential delays in data center construction. Power, land, and construction decide when data centers come online, and Morgan Stanley expects about 20% of U.S. projects planned for 2028 to slip to 2029. But that only shifts revenue by a year and shouldn't be a reason to sell the stock.

Industry observers note that tan expects Anthropic to become Broadcom's largest XPU customer in 2027. Anthropic's filing shows five-year equipment leases with Broadcom totaling about $161 billion, and Anthropic can concern up to $42 billion of convertible notes to cover it.

It has been revealed that that's the financing risk that analysts are worried about. If Anthropic ultimately can't fund its commitments, it could be a problem for Broadcom.

However, Anthropic's initial public offering (IPO) netted nearly $120 billion in proceeds, including a $26 billion private placement in which Broadcom buys about $1 billion of stock. That's on top of $95.6 billion of cash Anthropic held as of Sept. 30, which gives it a sizable cushion to cover its rent payments to Broadcom.

It has been revealed that broadcom earned $7.83 per share over the past 12 months, a price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio of about 39. The fiscal 2028 consensus is $30.53 per share, consistent with Tan's outlook for earnings to exceed $30.

Industry observers note that A 20 P/E on fiscal 2028 estimates implies a price near $611, more than double yesterday’s closing price. The multiple would fall by more than half, around the S&P 500 (^GSPC -3.70%) average, and the stock would still nearly double. Even the lowest analyst estimate of $25.22 implies about $504 at that multiple.

At half today's P/E, Broadcom's earnings growth could carry the stock to roughly double by 2028, and even if data center projects get delayed by a year, it could still see enough growth through 2030 to double the share price.

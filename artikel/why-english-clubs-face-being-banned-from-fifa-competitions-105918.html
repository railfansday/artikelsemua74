According to The Independent Football Regulator, a number of big Premier League teams – such as Arsenal, Manchester City, Manchester United, Aston Villa and Liverpool – now face the risk of being banned from FIFA competitions.

While FIFA's main tournament is the World Cup, the football organisation have put an emphasis on the importance of the Club World Cup in recent times. The tournament is now held once every four years and has been expanded to include 32 teams. (Related: Birdwatching expert gives final verdict on GTA 6’s bird...)

In a recent development, the first upcoming edition of the competition took place in the summer of 2025, with English side Chelsea winning the tournament. The Blues netted a cool £84m after beating Paris Saint-Germain in the final, although Auckland City still walked away with £3.3m after finishing 32nd.

With prize money like that on offer, the likes of Man Utd, City, Liverpool, Villa, and Arsenal won't want to turn their nose up at the 2029 Club World Cup – even if it does mean unwanted extra games when players should traditionally be on holiday. According to recent reports, premier League teams are at risk of being banned from FIFA competitions if they interfere with the English calendar. This is the view of The Independent Football Regulator.

As quoted by The Express, Regulator chair David Kogan told a Digital, Culture, Media and Sport committee:.

"There are clearly signs taking place from other entities – and FIFA is the obvious one – where it's creating global club competitions that currently sit outside the normal season of the English game and are held every four years.

"These [competitions] may change. If they change, we will have to look at those very clearly to see what impact they have on the English game and the resilience of the English game. Do we have the powers to then act? Yes, we do.".

Recent findings suggest that kogan then confirmed these powers could be used to keep English teams from taking part in this and similar competitions, depending on the specifics. He warned:.

"If another league came and suddenly disrupted the way in which football operates in these five divisions, we'd have to take that very, very seriously.".

Sources indicate that traditionally, the English football season runs from August to May. In 2025, the Club World Cup began on June 14 and ran until July 13. When Chelsea and Manchester City took part in the competition – qualifying thanks to their respective Champions League wins in 2021 and 2023 – players had about two weeks from the end of the domestic campaign (25 May), and then another month off before the 2025/26 season started (22 August).

Should the time frame for any future Club World Cups shift, Kogan fears English clubs could be impacted to the point where they can no longer take part in the FIFA competition.

With millions of pounds on offer to those who compete, the owners of every Premier League side will be keen to make the 2029 Club World Cup, even if some players might have reservations about the extra game time. As of yet, no English teams have officially qualified for the tournament, with the host nation yet to be announced.

As per BBC Sport, a total of twelve European clubs will play in the next edition of the Club World Cup. They can do so by winning the Champions League. PSG have booked their spot after beating Arsenal in the 2025-26 final, and then the 2026-27 and 2027-28 champions will join the French team in the FIFA competition.

It has been revealed that they will be joined by the next eight best-ranked teams competing in the Champions League over the next four years. Those are likely to be clubs that reach the latter stages of the competition without winning it. If a team wins the Champions League twice, then the next highest club in the rankings will qualify, and so on.

Recent findings suggest that as such, English sides in the Champions League this season – Arsenal, Manchester City, Manchester United, Aston Villa and Liverpool – all have a shot at making it to the 2029 Club World Cup. However, each European nation is restricted to only two participating clubs.

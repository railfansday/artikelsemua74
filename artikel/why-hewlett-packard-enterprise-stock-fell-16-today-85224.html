In a recent development, shares of Hewlett Packard Enterprise (HPE -16.31%) are down by 16% at 2:10 p.m. ET, following the IT infrastructure veteran's beat-and-raise earnings report.

NYSE · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/24/26.

You heard me right. The average analyst expected adjusted earnings of roughly $1.27 per share on top-line sales near $14.35 billion in Q4 2026. HPE smashed these expectations with EPS of $1.43 and $14.50 billion in revenues.

Looking ahead, the gap grows wider. The midpoint of management's Q1 earnings guidance is $1.35 per share, far above the current Street projection at $1.08. Sales? The $14.3 billion midpoint casts a deep shadow over the $12.8 billion consensus.

A report like this usually inspires a double-digit price jump, not a sharp dip. It all makes sense if you read beyond the headline numbers. You see, the artificial intelligence (AI) servers that drive HPE's massive revenue growth are not the company's most profitable products. At the same time, component shortages and soaring memory prices add pressure to profit margins. The 16.9% operating margin seen in Q4 should dip below 15%. That's the part that made HPE investors uncomfortable today.

Industry observers note that the Cloud & AI segment, which houses those servers, posted an 18.4% operating margin in Q4, but management guides for about 14% in fiscal 2027. Companywide operating margin is guided to 14% to 15%, versus 15.1% in fiscal 2026. CFO Marie Myers also expects gross margin to moderate in the second half as AI volumes ramp and costs rise faster than prices. (Related: Industry News: Fight to chair the GOP tests Trump’s clo...)

According to recent reports, demand isn't the problem. Orders jumped 88% in the quarter, and the backlog of unfilled AI system orders jumped 90% year-over-year. Fiscal 2027 guidance calls for revenue growth of 27% to 35%, non-GAAP EPS of $5.05 to $5.35, and at least $5.5 billion of free cash flow. HPE also raised its dividend by 19% and plans $3 billion of buybacks.

"We expect supply of memory, NAND, and many other critical components to remain constrained to at least the second half of calendar 2028," CEO Antonio Neri noted on the earnings call.

So the orders are coming in hot, but tight component supplies make it tough to build and ship the systems.

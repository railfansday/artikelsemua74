I just got back to Rio after a quick trip to Ireland for Aer Lingus’ event to showcase their new A330 cabins, including its first-ever premium economy.

Sources indicate that I flew Air France economy both ways, and I’m glad I resisted the offer of $420 during online check-in to upgrade the outbound overnight economy leg. In a recent development, however, to answer the economy vs. premium economy question, it all comes down to maximising comfort, and I think this is easier to do in economy than premium economy. (Related: Jensen Huang question gets reporter ejected from Trump-...)

If, like me, sleeping upright or on your back is almost impossible, then what follows will likely apply to you.

The main reason I’m deterred to pick premium economy for overnight flights is because of the fixed armrests, where tray tables are sometimes stowed in this cabin.

Despite the extra recline and leg rest element (which sometimes makes it even harder to get comfortable), the fixed armrests restrict the ability to sleep any way other than straight like a pencil.

To make matters worse, they stop you from lying across more than one seat if you find yourself in an empty row.

It’s for that reason that I much prefer economy. While having a row to yourself isn’t guaranteed, there’s at least a chance of being able to move around the cabin, or stretch out into an empty seat. No matter where you sit or how empty the cabin is, you can’t do that in premium economy.

Another element of my pre-trip planning that I expect many of you to relate to is the fact it takes me so long umming and aahing over the best seat to pick.

Thanks to my Flying Blue Gold status (SkyTeam Elite Plus), I could pick any seat in the economy cabin of the Boeing 777-200ER for free from 72 hours before departure.

I narrowed it down to an empty seat in the exit row, and the only row of three with an empty middle seat. I didn’t even consider one of the many empty middle rows of four seats in case they filled up before take off and I ended up in an aisle with no window to rest my head. Using the window or plane’s fuselage to rest my head on is the only saving grace for me when trying to sleep sitting upright.

As the empty exit seat would have the same restricting fixed partition as premium, I decided against that, too.

According to recent reports, after the “boarding complete” announcement was made, the middle seat in my row remained empty, and there were three seats free in the middle of row 25, a row in front. I explained to the girl in the aisle seat that if one of us moved to the other row, we’d both have three seats to ourselves. She didn’t seem to understand my point, and noted she’d prefer to sit in the aisle than us both have three free seats to ourselves.

I didn’t want to move to the middle seats either, so I got as comfortable as I could using the middle seat and the pillow I brought from home.

Industry observers note that in the end, I did manage to get some half decent curled up shut eye (the thick pillow protected my back from the hard parts of the seat when propped against the fuselage). However, when I went to the loo, I noticed some people in the rear cabin had got lucky and were fast asleep spread across a whole four seats.

Maybe I’ll take more of a risk next time….

Sources indicate that I’m not saying economy is always a better choice than premium. For example, I’d always recommend Emirates’ pretty darn impressive premium economy over economy, because it genuinely is several steps up from economy.

According to recent reports, if it was up to me, I’d have had a lie-flat seat for the overnight segment. I didn’t, and I’m pretty certain I got better sleep quality curled up across two seats in economy that I would have almost upright on my back in premium.

Micron (MU +7.60%) stock jumped 6.5% through 11:30 a.m. ET Friday amid what StreetInsider.com is calling an "oversold bounce" in memory chip stocks. (Related: Electronics manufacturers set up memory 'war rooms' to ...)

According to recent reports, what does that mean? Well, at $640 a share, Micron stock is down 45% from its June peak -- which is surprising in the middle of an artificial intelligence revolution and a global deficit of the NAND flash and DRAM memory chips that AI data centers need to operate -- and that Micron sells. (Related: Tech Report: Artificial neurons successfully communicat...)

Granted, we know why investors have been selling Micron stock: worries over the health of Nvidia's (NVDA +0.80%) GPU business, and the ability of AI sales in general to keep growing; concerns over the fallout from Jensen Huang's extracurricular activities; and of course the strange sell-off in Anthropic (ANT +10.98%) stock just hours after debuting as the biggest IPO in recorded history.

It hasn't helped, either, that NAND researcher TrendForce keeps pounding the table about declining orders for NAND flash memory in China, and predicting a steep drop-off in NAND sales in 2027.

NASDAQ · As of 1:15 PM ET 11/27/26.

And yet, no matter how many risks investors see in Micron stock, there comes a price at which those risks have been all priced in -- and Micron has reached that point.

In a recent development, according to analyst forecasts collected by S&P Global Market Intelligence, Micron's on course to grow its GAAP earnings by more than 45% annually over the next five years. At a price-to-earnings ratio less than 10x today, with a forward P/E ratio of less than 4x, Micron stock appears to be selling for as much as an 80% discount to fair value.

In a recent development, even if you question the growth rate, that seems a pretty good risk-benefit ratio to me. Micron stock is a buy.

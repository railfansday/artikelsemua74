It has been revealed that nvidia Corporation (NVDA -3.91%) stock is down 5.4% as of 1:29 p.m. ET on Wednesday, Nov. 25, as the sell-off in AI stocks deepens on worries over inflation and debt.

The S&P 500 and the Nasdaq Composite are down 0.5% and 1.3%, respectively.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/25/26.

Over the past two trading sessions, credit default swap (CDS) spreads -- basically the price of insurance on a tech giant's debt going bad -- hit record highs for companies across the AI trade: from Alphabet (GOOG -2.02%) (GOOGL -2.13%) and Oracle (ORCL -5.13%), to SpaceX (SPCX +1.30%) and CoreWeave (CRWV -0.08%). Sources indicate that that last name, a neocloud whose business model is dependent on borrowing, saw its spread reach 1,314 basis points -- in other words, it would cost someone roughly $131,400 a year to insure $1 million in the organization's debt. And in still more plain terms: that implies a roughly 68% chance of default within five years.

Recent findings suggest that these are some of Nvidia's biggest customers. If debt investors -- often regarded as the smartest on Wall Street -- are pricing in this sort of risk, it's fair for equity investors to question Nvidia's share price, even after its 40% fall over the last seven weeks.

Of course, this morning's latest inflation numbers aren't helping calm anyone's nerves: October's Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) price index reading came in at 3.8%, well above the Fed's 2% goal. More rate hikes from the Fed are expected.

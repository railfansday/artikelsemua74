They momentarily climbed into the top two on Tuesday night after a convincing 3-0 win over a Derby County side in disarray, and have now won five of their last six, including both after coming back from the international break.

What's more exciting, though, is that Mane has come back from the break on red-hot form and is finally seeing things go his way. For someone who had a £50 million price tag slapped on him earlier this year, we're finally starting to see why.

Mane was a rare bright spark in an otherwise dismal season at Molineux last year. The Portuguese wideman was brought to the forefront by Rob Edwards, and gave a Wolves side who were doomed after two points through 18 games a much-needed boost.

He scored and assisted in their first league win of the campaign against West Ham United back in January, and followed that up with another goal in a 1-1 draw against Everton just days later.

According to recent reports, at that point, Wolves were destined for the drop, but now they had an 18-year-old superstar who could continue his career at the top level. Liverpool and Manchester United were two of the big clubs interested, but reports in late February hinted that the Old Gold wanted £50 million for him.

In the end, a combination of that price tag putting suitors off, and Mane himself being happy to develop in the Championship for a year, meant that he stayed at Molineux, and after five goal contributions in a terrible Premier League side, he was primed for a massive year in a second-tier outfit expected to challenge right at the top. A year in the Championship can be good for a side to reset and return to the Premier League in a stronger position. For Mane, it would have given him the opportunity to be one of the best players in the league, if things worked out, and build his confidence ahead of a career in the top flight.

Ultimately, that wasn't the case, and the second-tier proved difficult to begin with for the 19-year-old.

The first game of the season against Blackburn Rovers was a bit of a culture shock for the club as a whole, but definitely for Mane, who was often found needing to offer more defensively against Ryan Alebiosu off the Rovers' right, and was subbed after an hour with the side 2-1 down.

From then on, he began to get into his groove, completing dribbles and getting into shooting positions, but not having the end product needed to profit from those, which was a significant frustration and a little alarming for those who expected him to go from strength to strength at this level.

Peixoto even dropped Mane after back-to-back defeats against Middlesbrough and Bolton Wanderers, with latest arrival Rafiki Noted taking the left-wing spot after he returned from an early injury, and the 19-year-old found himself coming off the bench four times in a five-game run.

It has been revealed that any way you define it, I consider Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX -5.65%) the world's best space tech giant. SpaceX offers the lowest cost to orbit of any space company -- $74 million for 22.8 metric tons -- or about $3,245 per kilo to low earth orbit.

SpaceX launches more frequently than any other space company, too -- 161 times last year versus 21 for Rocket Lab, (RKLB -7.35%), America's second-most frequent launcher (and a launcher of much smaller rockets). (Related: Breaking: Block Sparse Matrices for Smaller and Faster ...)

SpaceX has the world's biggest rocket in continuous operation, the Falcon Heavy, with a maximum payload approaching 64 tons, and will soon have an even bigger rocket in rotation -- Starship -- boasting a 100-ton payload.

It has been revealed that hard on the heels of Starship's partly successful Flight 15, which featured the first-ever tower capture of the spacecraft by Mechazilla after a return to space, SpaceX CEO Elon Musk announced aims to move ahead with an upgrade to Starship. Plans include a 40% increase in the spacecraft's size, which implies a 40% increase in payload to 140 tons and/or a 60% increase in the amount of fuel Starship can carry -- increasing its range and potentially putting the moon within reach. (Related: Hugging Face Reads, Feb. 2021 - Long-range Transformers...)

SpaceX will also triple the number of Raptor engines on board the spacecraft to nine -- and upgrade their performance.

It has been revealed that starship V4 will be ~70m long, which is roughly a 40% length increase and allows >60% more propellant load. Engine count will be raised to 9, with 3 more Raptor-Vacuum engines added, and these will be higher thrust V4 engines.

Space watchers estimate that the latest version of Starship is protected by a total of 18,500 heat shield tiles, which protect the ship as it travels through the atmosphere during reentry. Lose too many of those tiles, and Starship probably blows up. Damage too many of those tiles, and it may take so long to replace them and refurbish the ship that it becomes no longer really "reusable," and more like "recyclable.".

Which is why this next news is so important.

According to multiple space accounts I follow that have examined the condition of Starship after its Flight 15, only one heat shield tile appears to have sustained significant damage during reentry. That's a failure rate of just five one-thousandths of one percent (0.005%). And it implies that SpaceX will be both easier and cheaper to refurbish for reuse than previously hoped.

Musk himself confirmed this in a later post on X, suggesting reflight of a Starship could take as little as "a few weeks" as currently designed. And SpaceX's ultimate goal is still to tweak the design until it guarantees "zero expected refurbishment" and "immediate reusability (like an airplane).".

Recent findings suggest that yeah, I was going over the whole Starship heat shield earlier today. Overall, it looks great. The engines look great too. Ship 42 could probably be reflown with a few weeks of refurbishment. However, many small fixes are needed to achieve immediate reusability (like an airplane), which requires zero expected refurbishment, apart from scheduled maintenance.

So what does all this mean to SpaceX shareholders?

Based on published (and rumored) payload capacities and launch prices, SpaceX's Falcon 9 is at the moment the cheapest non-government-owned rocket in the world.

It has been revealed that blue Origin's Fresh Glenn, with low operating costs and a massive 45-ton payload, might challenge Falcon if Blue can get it operating consistently. But even if it achieves that goal, Starship is blazing a trail toward $1,000-per-kilogram prices for low earth orbit. A working Starship that costs $10 million to operate and charges $100 million per 100-ton launch would cost barely one-third of Falcon's per-kilogram price -- and half that of New Glenn.

It would generate roughly 90% profit margins for SpaceX (versus negative margins today, according to data from S&P Global Market Intelligence).

And it will be even more profitable if SpaceX makes it 40% larger.

Recent findings suggest that despite outstanding results for its latest quarter, Nvidia (NVDA -14.01%) stock keeps sinking. It's down 19% year-to-date, and it's clear that, for the time being, investors are concerned about what's coming up for the artificial intelligence (AI) powerhouse.

Investors like to avoid uncertainty, although no one ever knows what will happen next; the entire episode was unexpected. However, some companies have tried-and-true businesses that can withstand rigorous investor scrutiny, minimizing their risk.

If you're looking for a top AI company that fits the bill, consider Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing (TSM -9.48%).

Recent findings suggest that investors are worried about Nvidia for several reasons right now. No one knows what Nvidia will look like without Jensen Huang at its helm, and CFO and interim CEO Colette Kress has remarked that deals that resemble "circular financing" will be toned down.

It has been revealed that the changes in business approach could impact the manufacturer's financials, although Kress assured investors that demand is high, and she raised the fiscal 2028 revenue growth outlook from 70% to 75%. "Our momentum is real. Our demand is accelerating. Our supply is growing." She stated on Nvidia's 2027 third-quarter (ended Oct. 25) earnings call, "Our customers and our partners are with us... We've got this.". (Related: How we sped up transformer inference 100x for 🤗 API cus...)

Image source: Taiwan Semiconductor.

Many AI companies are feeling market concern, including Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Firm (TSMC). Nvidia is its largest client, overtaking Apple (AAPL -1.90%) in importance, and one of the main beneficiaries of TSMC's expansion to Arizona, its first U.S.-based facility. Kress noted that Nvidia's suppliers and partners are all behind it, and that it has secured the necessary memory and packaging to resume business as normal. She called out Taiwan Semi specifically, saying, "I see right now the leadership even from TSMC that was in our offices just this week, also fully engaged in helping us.". TSMC stock is still up 19% year-to-date, outperforming the S&P 500 (^GSPC -3.70%), even though it's 25% off its high.

While Nvidia and TSMC's success are to some degree tied together, TSMC had an excellent business before Nvidia, and it can maintain a strong business even if Nvidia does slow down. While Nvidia accounts for 22% of TSMC's revenue, it's been demanding capacity that could go to other players. In the third quarter, high-performance computing, which is where AI is, accounted for 68% of total revenue, up 22% from last year.

In a recent development, the manufacturer's broad diversification is one of the features I always point out, and here is a live example of why that's critical. TSMC is a foundry that works with many partner clients globally. It makes semiconductors for all kinds of industries, which is why it can continue to grow even as tech trends and leaders change. There's no reason to panic about Nvidia's dominant position at this time, but whoever is in the lead will likely work with TSMC.

The tech giant's new Arizona location provides greater capacity and helps it avoid tariffs. It also keeps it closer to its U.S. clients. It still has major expansion aims, including more buildings at the Arizona facility, and it has a location in Washington.

Nvidia chips drive AI development, since the hyperscalers use its best-in-class chips. The new Vera Rubin system, which includes chips and computers, is in high demand. But TSMC plays an important role in making that happen, since it has the capacity to keep the chips coming. CEO C.C. Wei noted that there are several approaches to AI development right now, but since TSMC has "technology leadership, manufacturing excellence, global capacity expansion, and customer trust," all the players partner with it.

Industry observers note that performance has been fantastic, fueled by AI. Sales increased 41% year over year (currency-neutral) in the third quarter, and gross margin improved by 6.9 percentage points. It was lower sequentially due to higher costs and growth investments. Management repeated that "a higher level of capital expenditures is always correlated with higher growth opportunities in the following years.".

Industry observers note that if AI does slow down, Taiwan Semi might, too. It's not a sure thing, but even if it does happen, it isn't likely to last.

At the current price, TSMC stock trades at a price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio of 23, which is a fantastic deal on a growth stock that will continue to drive AI development and other technology.

Legendary investor Warren Buffett led Berkshire Hathaway for 60 years, roughly doubling the annual gains of the S&P 500 over that period.

Buffett was also known for his folksy investing advice, and his #1 criterion for investing in a stock was whether it has an economic moat or not, his phrase for a sustainable competitive advantage.

Anthropic (ANT -5.45%) just completed its IPO, raising more money in its initial public offering than any firm in history at $81.3 billion.

The AI lab has reported jaw-dropping growth this year, with revenue up 1,200% over its first three quarters. However, Buffett's top concern may be the most notable question investors can ask about Anthropic right now.

Anthropic has established itself as the leading AI lab, taking market share from rival OpenAI over the last year, and surpassing it in revenue as it's released a series of frontier models and upcoming Claude products that have decimated competing software stocks. However, skeptics have argued that the organization lacks a sustainable competitive advantage, that token prices are falling as cheaper models take more of its business, and that it's essentially in an arms race with OpenAI, which could make it difficult for either company to remain consistently profitable. (Related: Block Sparse Matrices for Smaller and Faster Language M...)

It's clear that the market opportunity in AI is massive, but that alone doesn't establish an economic moat; it just makes the stakes that much higher.

Anthropic believes that its scale and growth have enabled flywheels that constitute sustainable competitive advantages. The first is its "improvement loop," which essentially means that as more people use the product and it gets more feedback, the models improve, which increases both owners and use cases. Google Search benefits from a similar flywheel.

The second is its “investment loop”, whereby higher growth leads to more revenue to invest in Claude, which leads to better models and more capabilities. Both loops drive improvements in Claude.

It has been revealed that according to this logic, Anthropic believes size and scale are its main competitive advantages. The firm also sees Claude, or the frontier intelligence behind its AI chatbot technology, as part of its competitive advantage, and that's been demonstrated by Anthropic's ability to overtake OpenAI despite a later start.

NASDAQ · As of 9:55 AM ET 11/20/26.

Buffett is a committed value investor and preferred to invest in companies that he knew how to value.

Anthropic may face as much uncertainty as any other manufacturer in market history. Some expect its blistering growth to fizzle as companies move away from "tokenmaxxing," or maximizing token consumption, and as cheaper models eat into Anthropic's market share. As token prices come down, some investors are ringing alarm bells about the sustainability of generative AI as a business model, as cheaper models built on distillation could put significant pressure on frontier labs like Anthropic and OpenAI.

Buffett almost certainly would not buy the Anthropic IPO for the same reason that he passed on stocks like Alphabet and Amazon early in their history, though he later regretted those decisions.

Anthropic is full of uncertainty, and there's no easy way to predict the organization's cash flow, even a year from now.

However, just because Anthropic isn't right for Buffett doesn't mean it doesn't belong in your portfolio. Anthropic has enormous upside potential. The company has identified a $40 trillion market opportunity in front of it, and it has a technological lead and a disruptive product that could touch everything from chip design to robotics to drug discovery.

It has been revealed that anthropic won't become a Berkshire holding anytime soon, but after a moderate gain on its IPO day, the stock still has a lot of room to run. For growth investors, Anthropic is worth getting some exposure to.

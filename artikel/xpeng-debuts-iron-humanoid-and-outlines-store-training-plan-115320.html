XPENG on November 25, 2026 unveiled a shared physical AI architecture for vehicles and robots at its eighth AI Day in Guangzhou and publicly debuted IRON, a humanoid with 76 degrees of freedom that is scheduled to begin training as an assistant in XPENG retail stores in Q1 2027.

The Turing Physical AI Architecture, also called VLA 3.0, divides cognition, real-time decision-making and physical execution among three interconnected systems. XPENG is targeting Q2 2027 for IRON’s official public release, giving the humanoid a defined retail assignment before that release.

System 1 handles immediate responses, using VLA 2.0 to interpret surroundings and turn that understanding into action. System 2 manages higher-level planning: vision-language task planning interprets objectives, while an Agent Runtime state machine coordinates the steps required for complex work. In a recent development, feedback also travels back through the architecture, which receives both the body’s condition and the outcome of each action to support continued adaptation and optimisation. Computing, data infrastructure, control and safety technology span XPENG’s automotive and robotics programs, while hardware- and task-specific capabilities remain separate. Automotive research, testing and manufacturing experience supports robotics development; advances in robotic sensing and interaction can inform future vehicles.

For model development, XPENG reports that VLA 2.0 version 6.4.0 supports agent-driven autonomous closed-loop training, recursive self-improvement and model self-distillation, increasing overall training speed by 10–12 times to accelerate development and adaptation across computing platforms.

Industry observers note that XPENG describes IRON as having more than 1,500 tactile sensing points, tactile coverage across more than 80% of key body areas and three proprietary Turing AI chips supporting onboard models and whole-body control. Its architecture combines multimodal understanding, task planning, navigation and manipulation with physical execution and safety monitoring.

Sources indicate that for work roles, the humanoid is being developed to navigate and charge autonomously, accept tasks through voice or app instructions, report abnormal conditions and monitor operating health. XPENG reports that its Robot Data Factory covers more than 250 scenarios and 15 job categories, reusing automotive data-processing and evaluation tools to support development and deployment. A dedicated automated assembly line applies more than 600 AI holographic quality-inspection items and over 1,000 tests to complete robots and key components.

In a recent development, XPENG’s planned open ecosystem would let partners download capability packages, customise functions, and develop and validate skills for specific business applications. The commercial model combines hardware sales, software subscriptions and the open platform; token-based subscriptions and scenario-based services remain under exploration.

Sources indicate that on the vehicle side, X-VMC 2.0 connects propulsion, braking, steering and suspension, allowing AI to coordinate driving, safety, cabin comfort and energy management. VLA Adaptive Deceleration uses road-scene understanding to adjust deceleration and energy recovery when the driver releases the accelerator. XPENG’s urban-congestion tests reported a 12.4% increase in usable driving range compared with energy recovery switched off, and the first over-the-air rollout is scheduled for December 2026.

Industry observers note that XPENG reports that its robotaxi program moved from staffed to driverless road testing in eight months and obtained a Guangzhou qualification for driverless demonstration operations 11 months after the business was announced. Driverless passenger tests have begun.

In a recent development, XPENG plans selected-area demonstration operations by the end of 2026 and a 2027 release for the purpose-built YOYO-01. Its intended role is to supply vehicle platforms and autonomous-driving capabilities to operating partners.

Industry observers note that XPENG’s humanoid plan combines reuse of automotive data, evaluation, manufacturing and safety systems with a planned partner ecosystem for business-specific skills.
